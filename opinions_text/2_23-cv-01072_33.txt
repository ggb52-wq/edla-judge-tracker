II. Law and Analysis
 This Court has addressed this issue before.  In Williams v. State Farm Fire & Casualty
Co., the plaintiff – whose home had been damaged by Hurricane Ida – sued the defendant
for breach of the insurance contract, bad faith damages, and extra- contractual damages
such as mental anguish.  Civ. A. No. 23 -3289 (E.D. La.) (rec. doc. 1 at 6 -8).  After the parties
settled, the plaintiff filed a Motion to Compel Defendant to Issue Split Settlement Checks,
specifically asking “that the settlement be split over two checks: one to Plaintiff and Daly &
Black, P.C., representing the undersigned’s fees and costs; and a second check to Plaintiff,
Daly & Black, P.C., and Plaintiff’s mortgage company.”  Id. (rec. doc. 18-2).2
 This Court tersely denied the motion, holding:
It is axiomatic that “an insurance policy is a conventional obligation that
constitutes the law between the insured and the insurer, and the agreement
governs the nature of their relationship.”  Allen v. Sw. Builders, L.L.C., 372 So.
3d 32, 37 (La. Ct. App. 2023) (citing Supreme Servs. & Specialty Co., Inc. v.
Sonny Greer, Inc. , 958 So. 2d 634, 638 (La. 2007)).  “An insurance policy is a
contract, which must be construed employing the general rules of
interpretation of contracts.”  Id.   Thus, “where the insurance policy's
language clearly expresses the parties’ intent and does not violate a statute
or public policy, the policy must be enforced as written.”  Id.
Here, the clear and unambiguous language of the policy between
Plaintiff and Defendant requires that “any loss payable under Coverage A will
be paid to the mortgagee and the [named insured], as interests appear.”
(Rec. doc. 19-3 at 22).  PennyMac Loan Services, L.L.C. is the mortgagee on
Plaintiff’s property damaged by Hurricane Ida.  (Rec. doc. 19 -2 at 2).

Plaintiff argues that the parties in this lawsuit are unaware of the arguments raised in
Williams and that it appeared that the Williams dispute did not center on the distinction
between contractual and extra-contractual damages.  But that is  a distinction is without a
difference.  Simply put, in a dispute such as this, where the parties did not agree on any
allocation of funds between the coverages under the Policy or between contractual and
extra-contractual damages, there is no basis for the Court to do so by ordering the issuance
of separate checks (particularly on the apparently arbitrary 50/50 basis suggested by
Plaintiff).
