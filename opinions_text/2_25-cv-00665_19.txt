REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

This lawsuit was referred to the undersigned pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1)(B)
for frivolous review and a recommendation on the Motion to Appoint Attorney.  (Rec. doc.
14).  Having reviewed the pleadings, the Court recommends as follows.
I. Background
 Plaintiff, a pro se pauper, sues the Governor and former Attorney General of
Louisiana, Jeff Landry, in his individual and official capacities under 42 U.S.C. § 1983.
Plaintiff alleges that Defendant has “a duty to protect the constitutional rights of Louisiana
citizens, including Plaintiff . . . when reports of abuse within the state” are lodge d by its
citizens against law enforcement.  (Rec. doc. 1 -1 at 3).  In short, Plaintiff alleges that law
enforcement in several Louisiana parishes have beaten and abused him, and Defendant
failed to take any action to curb said abuse.  (Id. at 2).  Plaintiff seeks prospective injunctive
relief and monetary damages.  (Id. at 4).
II. Law and Analysis
 Under federal law, a complaint is frivolous “if it lacks an arguable basis in law or
fact.”  Reeves v. Collins , 27 F.3d 174, 176 (5th Cir. 1994).  A claim lacks an arguable basis in
law if it is based on an indisputably meritless legal theory, such as if the complaint alleges
the violation of a legal interest which clearly does not exist.  Harper v. Showers , 174 F.3d
716, 718 (5th Cir. 1999).  It lacks an arguable factual basis only if the facts alleged are

“clearly baseless,” a category encompassing fanciful, fantastic, and delusional allegations.
Denton v. Hernandez , 504 U.S. 25, 32- 33 (1992); Neitzke  v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319,  327-28
(1989).  Therefore, the Court must determine whether P laintiff's claims are based on an
indisputably meritless legal theory or clearly baseless factual allegations.  Reeves, 27 F.3d at
176; see Jackson v. Vannoy , 49 F.3d 175, 176- 77 (5th Cir. 1995); Moor v. Mabus, 976 F.2d
268, 269 (5th Cir. 1992).   In determining whether a claim is frivolous, the Court has “not
only the authority to dismiss a claim based on an indisputably meritless legal theory, but
also the unusual power to pierce the veil of the complaint’s factual allegations and dismiss
those claims whose factual contentions are clearly baseless.”  Neitzke , 490 U.S.  at 327;
Macias v. Raul A. (Unknown), Badge No. 153, 23 F.3d 94, 97 (5th Cir. 1994).
Plaintiff only names Jeff Landry in his individual and official capacities as a
Defendant in this matter.  But suits against the State and its representatives in their official
capacities implicate the Eleventh Amendment immunity doctrine.  Muhammad v. Louisiana,
Nos. 99-3742 & 99- 2694, 2000 WL 1568210 (E.D. La. Oct. 18, 2000); Citrano v. Allen Corr.
Ctr., 891 F. Supp. 312, 320 (W. D. La. 1995) (citing Anderson v. Phelps , 655 F. Supp. 560, 560
(M.D. La. 1985); Bldg. Eng'g Servs. Co. v. Louisiana , 459 F. Supp. 180 (E.D. La. 1978)).   A
Section 1983 claim against a state official, such as the Governor  or former Attorney
General, in his official capacity for monetary damages, is actually a claim against the State
itself, and such a claim is barred by the Eleventh Amendment.   Edwards v. Louisiana, No. CV
22-2171, 2023 WL 1170309, at *2–3 (E.D. La. Jan. 4, 2023), report and recommendation
adopted, No. CV 22 -2171, 2023 WL 1102567 (E.D. La. Jan. 30, 2023), appeal dismissed sub
nom. Edwards v. Louisiana State, No. 23-30587, 2024 WL 727792 (5th Cir. Jan. 2, 2024), and

The Eleventh Amendment forbids federal courts from entertaining a suit for money
damages brought by a citizen against his own State.  Pennhurst State Sch. v. Halderman, 465
U.S. 89, 98 (1984); Voisin's Oyster House, Inc. v. Guidry , 799 F.2d 183, 185 -86 (5th Cir.
1986).  It also prohibits suits for declaratory and injunctive relief brought against the State.
See Saltz v. Tenn. Dep't of Employment Sec. , 976 F.2d 966, 968 (5th Cir. 1992) ( holding
declaratory and prospective injunctive relief cannot be pursued directly against a
government official unless the official is sued in his individual capacity in federal court); see
also Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho, 521 U.S. 261, 269, 117 S. Ct. 2028, 2034 (1997)
(“The Tribe's suit, accordingly, is barred by Idaho's Eleventh Amendment immunity unless
it falls within the exception this Court has recognized for certain suits seeking declaratory
and injunctive relief against state officers in their individual  capacities.  See Ex parte Young ,
209 U.S. 123, 28 S.Ct. 441, 52 L.Ed. 714 (1908).”) (emphasis added)).   A state may expressly
waive this Eleventh Amendment sovereign immunity.  See Edelman v. Jordan , 415 U.S. 651,
673 (1974) (holding that a state's consent to suit against it in federal court must be
expressed “unequivocally”); Welch v. Dep't of Highways, 780 F.2d 1268, 1271- 73 (5th Cir.
1986).  However, Governor Landry has not done so.
To the contrary, Louisiana Revised Statute § 13:5106(a) provides that “no suit
against the state . . . shall be instituted in any court other than a Louisiana state court.”
Accordingly, the Court is without jurisdiction to entertain Plaintiff's claims against
Governor Jeff Landry for monetary damages in his official capacity .  See Warnock v. Pecos
Cnty., Tex. , 88 F.3d 341, 343 (5th Cir. 1996).  Thus, Plaintiff’s claims against Governor

Landry in his official capacity are frivolous, fail to state a claim for which relief can be
granted, and seek relief from an immune defendant.  Plaintiff’s claims against Governor
Landry must be dismissed without prejudice for lack of jurisdiction.  See Warnock v. Pecos
Cnty., Tex., 88 F.3d 341, 343 (5th Cir. 1996).1
To the extent th at Plaintiff brings a Section 1983 claim against Governor Landry in
his individual capacity, Plaintiff has not alleged Governor Landry was personally involved
in any of the alleged acts or omissions upon which his claims are based.  Neither does
Plaintiff allege that Governor Landry had knowledge of any alleged acts or omissions.
Arcement v. Lopinto, No. CV 22 -2888, 2023 WL 3961134, at *3 (E.D. La. Apr. 28, 2023),
report and recommendation adopted , No. CV 22 -2888, 2023 WL 3949116 (E.D. La. June 12,
2023).
To hold Governor Landry liable, Plaintiff must establish that he was “personally
involved in the acts causing the deprivation of [the Plaintiffs’] constitutional rights or that a
causal connection exists between an act of [Governor Landry] . . . and the alleged
constitutional violation.”  Douthit v. Jones, 641 F.2d 345, 346 (5th Cir. 1981).  The required
causal “connection” is not “merely the general duty to see that the laws of the state are
implemented,” but “the particular duty to enforce the statute in question and a
demonstrated willingness to exercise that duty.”  Okpalobi v. Foster, 244 F.3d 405, 416 (5th
Cir. 2001) (en banc) (quoting Young, 209 U.S. at 157, 158); see Morris v. Livingston, 739 F.3d
740, 746 (5th Cir. 2014).

Plaintiff does not allege, and therefore fails to establish, that Governor Landry was
personally involved in any acts causing the deprivation of his constitutional rights, or that a
causal connection exists between any act of Governor Landry and the alleged constitutional
violations committed by the individual parishes .  Instead, Plaintiff seeks to hold Governor
Landry liable solely because he feels that he was ultimately responsible for his well -being.
Indeed, Plaintiff alleges no more than Governor and former Attorney General Landry’s duty
is “merely the general duty to see that the laws of the state are implemented.”  Opkalobi ,
244 F .3d at 416.  Such an allegation fails on its face in light of Fifth Circuit precedent.
Plaintiff points to no specific statute or state law that renders Governor or Attorney
General Landry individually responsible for the protection of Louisiana individuals’ rights
across a broad swath of parishes governed by their own hierarchical systems and
legislative laws.
III. Conclusion
 Accordingly, and for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS RECOMMENDED that Plaintiff’s claims against Governor and former Attorney
General Jeff Landry in his official capacity be DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE  for lack of
subject matter jurisdiction and  Plaintiff’s claims against Governor and former Attorney
General Jeff Landry in his individual capacity be DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE  as
frivolous.
