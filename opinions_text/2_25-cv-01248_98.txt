ORDER AND REASONS
Before the Court is Plaintiff, Kathy Sherrod’s, Motion to Compel Nexion Health
Management, Inc.’s Complete Responses to Plaintiff’s Request for Production of
Documents (R. Doc. 78). Sherrod filed her motion on August 17, 2026, and sought
expedited consideration in the light of the August 31, 2026 discovery deadline. The
Court granted the request for expedited consideration. For the following reasons, the
Motion to Compel (R. Doc. 78) is DENIED.
Sherrod sought expedited consideration because, she stated, she only recently
received certain discovery responses  from Nexion Health Management, Inc.
(“Nexion”) and because the existence of certain documents and their relevance were
only “first revealed” during Nexion ’s Rule 30(b)(6) deposition on August 6, 2026. 1
After an extensive review of the record, the Court concludes that  Sherrod ’s
explanation for the delay is unpersuasive  such that there is no reason to reach the
merits of her untimely motion.

The substantive attacks on the sufficiency of Nexion’s responses are also stale.

communications between Marrero Healthcare and Nexion Health Management that
she sought in her prior discovery dispute, which resulted in an agreed upon list of
search terms. If Sherrod was dissatisfied with the outcome of that search -term
agreement with Marrero Healthcare, she could have filed an appropriate motion or
otherwise sought judicial intervention. 13 And nothing precluded Sherrod  from
simultaneously or separately challenging the sufficiency of both co -defendants’
discovery responses in a timely manner. Yet Sherrod chose not to take any action as
to Nexion until eight months after receiving Nexion’s allegedly deficient responses.

For the foregoing reasons, Sherrod ’s motion to compel is  untimely, and
Sherrod’s explanation for her delay in filing is unpersuasive, at best. Thus, the Court
need not reach the merits. 14 Pursuant to Rule 37(a), the party that prevails on a
motion to compel is entitled to its reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees.
 Accordingly,
 IT IS ORDERED that the motion (R. Doc. 78) is DENIED.
 IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that , if the parties cannot agree on an
attorney’s fee amount,  Nexion may file a motion within 14 days seeking the
reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, incurred in connection with the
motion to expedite (R. Doc. 81)  and the motion to compel  (R. Doc. 78) . The motion
should address both Nexion’s entitlement to fees and the amount sought and include
supporting documentation.
 IT IS FURTHER ORDERED  that, to the extent Sherrod contests Nexion’s
entitlement to such expenses under Rule 37(a), she may raise that argument, as well
as any challenge to the amount sought, in opposition to Nexion’s fee motion.
