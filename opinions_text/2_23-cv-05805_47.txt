ORDER AND REASONS

Before the Court is the “Motion to Substitute Party Plaintiff via Curator, or Alternatively
via Power of Attorney” filed by Plaintiff, Jamie D’Amico. 1 Defendant, Dover Bay Specialty
Insurance Company, opposes the motion.2 Having considered the parties’ briefing, the record, and
the applicable law, the Court denies Plaintiff’s motion without prejudice, for the following reasons.
I. BACKGROUND
Plaintiff Jamie D’Amico originally filed this action pro se  against Dover Bay on August
28, 2023, in Jefferson Parish, Louisiana. She alleges that property owned by Plaintiff sustained
damage following Hurricane Ida, that the property was insured by Dover Bay under a
homeowner’s insurance policy, and that Dover Bay breached the policy by failing to fairly and
promptly adjust Plaintiff’s claim and to timely tender proceeds due under the policy. 3 Dover Bay
removed the action to this Court on October 5, 2023. 4 Ms. D’Amico has been represented by
counsel since March of 2025. The present motion, filed through counsel, states that Ms. D’Amico
suffers from severe physical and mental disabilities, and due to those disabilities, Ms. D’Amico
moves to substitute her mother, Melvina L. Burnthorne, as the plaintiff in this action. In furtherance
of the substitution, Ms. D’Amico also moves the Court to appoint Ms. Burnthorne as curator ad

II. LAW AND ANALYSIS
Plaintiff’s motion does not cite any legal authority for her requests for an order appointing
Melvina L. Burnthorne as curator ad litem  or to substitute Ms. Burnthorne as party plaintiff. The
Court turns to the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, which contain the following relevant
provisions. First, as to the appointment of a curator ad litem, which the Court construes as a request
to appoint a guardian ad litem, Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 17(c)(2) states that “an incompetent
person who does not have a duly appointed representative may sue by a next friend or by a guardian
ad litem.”5 This rule further states that “[t]he court must appoint a guardian ad litem —or issue
another appropriate order—to protect a minor or  incompetent person who is unrepresented in an
action.”6 Next, as to the substitution of a party due to incompetency, Federal Rule of Civil
Procedure 25(b) states “[i]f a party becomes incompetent, the court may, on motion, permit the
action to be continued by or against the party’s representative.” 7 Because Ms. D’Amico does not
currently have a representative to substitute in her place, the Court must first determine whether
the appointment of a guardian ad litem is appropriate.
As previously stated, “an incompetent person who does not have a duly appointed
representative may sue by a next friend or by a guardian ad litem.”8 “[I]ndividuals are incompetent
for Rule 17 purposes if they lack the capacity to litigate under the law of [their] domicile.”9 There

Procedure article 6 84(A) provides that “[a]  person fully interdicted or a person whose limited
interdiction specifically restricts the procedural capacity to sue does not have the procedural

interdicted, which occurs only by way of  procedure under Louisiana law. 12 The Fifth Circuit has
“reject[ed] the notion that in determining whether a person is competent to sue in federal court a
federal judge must use the state’s procedures for determining competency or capacity. ”13 Rather,
it is the state’s substantive definition or standard for determining competency that applies.14 Article
684(A), read alone, might suggest that a person who has not been interdicted has the capacity to
sue as a matter of Louisiana law. But interdiction is Louisiana’s procedure for determining
incapacity, and the Louisiana Civil Code supplies the substantive standard that this Court applies.15
Louisiana Civil Code article 390 provides that “[a]  court may order the limited interdiction of a
natural person of the age of majority . . . who due to an infirmity is unable consistently to make
reasoned decisions regarding the care of his person or property, or any aspect of either, or to
communicate those decisions, and whose interests cannot be protected by less restrictive means.”
Accordingly, applying Louisiana’s standard in this federal civil action, the Court must determine
whether Ms. D’Amico , due to an infirmity , is unable consistently to make reasoned decisions
regarding her interests in this litigation, or any aspect of those interests, or to communicate either,

On the current record, the Court is unable to determine whether Ms. D’Amico is
incompetent under the applicable standard because Plaintiff has failed to provide any evidence
upon which the Court may properly rely. Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 43(c), “[w]hen a
motion relies on facts outside the record, the court may hear the matter on affidavits or may hear
it wholly or partly on oral testimony or on depositions.”16 Under 28 U.S.C. § 1746, unsworn
declarations carry the same force and effect as a sworn affidavit or declaration if the unsworn
declarations follow the form set forth therein.17 Thus, Plaintiff may carry her burden with unsworn
declarations in the form set out in 28 U.S.C. § 1746, but the arguments and documents currently
provided, on their own, fall short of satisfying any evidentiary standard.
First, in the one-paragraph memorandum in support of the motion, counsel includes a three-
paragraph footnote stating the myriad of mental, physical, and other issues Ms. D’Amico is
currently facing. Statements by counsel in the footnote of a memorandum are argument, not
evidence. Second, counsel attaches a September 21, 2026 note on Dr. John Macgregor’s letterhead
that appears to be Dr. Macgregor’s notes from a visit with Ms. D’Amico, but the note lacks the
requisite declaration and is not signed. Third, counsel attaches what appears to be an email from
Dr. Macgregor to counsel, dated September 28, 2026, that includes comments and opinions by Dr.
Macgregor about Ms. D’Amico’s mental conditions, her competency, and the appointment of a
curator. This document likewise lacks the requisite declaration and is not signed. Accordingly, on
