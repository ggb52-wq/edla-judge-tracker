ORDER & REASONS
Before the Court is a motion for continuance of trial date and to vacate scheduling order
filed by defendant Philip Morris USA Inc. (“PM USA”) .1  Plaintiffs Nancy LeBlanc, Darren
LeBlanc, and Rhonda LeBlanc (collectively, “Plaintiffs”), individually and as the statutory heirs
of decedent Hyson M. LeBlanc, respond in opposition,2 and PM USA replies in further support of
its motion.3  Having considered the parties’ memoranda, the record, and the applicable law, the
Court grants the motion for the following reasons.4
“The [t]rial [c]ourt has, and must have, of course, a wide and flexible discretion in the daily
guidance of a case through the preparatory stages looking toward the climax of a trial.”  Mitchell
v. Johnson, 274 F.2d 394, 401 (5th Cir. 1960).  Under Rule 16(b)(4) of the Federal Rules of Civil
Procedure, a scheduling order “may be modified only for good cause and with the judge's consent.”
Good cause “requires a showing that the relevant scheduling order deadline cannot reasonably be
met despite the diligence of the party needing the extension.”  Garza v. Webb County, 296 F.R.D.
511, 513 (S.D. Tex. 2014) (citing S&W Enters., L.L.C. v. Southtrust Bank of Ala., NA , 315 F.3d

533, 535 (5th Cir. 2003)).  To determine whether good cause exists, courts within the Fifth Circuit
consider four factors: “(1) the explanation for the failure to timely comply with the scheduling
order; (2) the importance of the modification; (3) potential prejudice in allowing the modification;
and (4) the availability of a continuance to cure such prejudice.”  Springboards to Educ., Inc. v.
Hou. Indep. Sch. Dist., 912 F.3d 805, 819 (5th Cir. 2019) ( citation modified) (quoting Squyres v.
Heico Cos., 782 F.3d 224, 237 (5th Cir. 2015)).
Regarding the first factor, PM USA argues that it is unable to comply with the current
scheduling order “not due to lack of diligence” but because it was brought into the case “months
after the operative scheduling order was entered”  and because it could not engage in meaningful
discovery until after the Court ruled on its motion to dismiss. 5  Furthermore, PM USA explains
that cases alleging injuries that stem from cigarette smoking generally involve extensive discovery
that exceeds the amount of time left by the current scheduling order. 6  In response, Plaintiffs
contend that PM USA was not diligent because , prior to filing the instant motion,  it had yet to
participate in  any discovery, despite having been served over a month prior , which, claim
Plaintiffs, PM USA at least should have initiated even while it waited on this Court to rule on its
motion to dismiss.7  In reply, PM USA points out that since filing the instant motion, it “served
discovery requests on Plaintiffs,” who nevertheless, as of the date of the reply, had yet to respond,
and that the motion to dismiss was not its only reason for seeking a continuance.8
 As to the second factor, PM USA argues that its “ability to defend itself” would be impaired
unless this Court grants the instant motion .9  PM USA explains that Plaintiffs’ allegations span

Finally, as to the fourth factor, “PM USA is not aware of any reason why a continuance
would not be an available remedy.”21  And although Plaintiffs do not explicitly address the fourth
factor, they maintain that a modification of the scheduling order would cause unfair prejudice to
them and that PM USA has failed to “demonstrate ‘good cause’ to justify continuance or to vacate
the scheduling order.”22
 The Court finds that good cause exists to modify the scheduling order to continue the trial
and vacate the scheduling order.  The Court acknowledges Plaintiffs’ desire to resolve their claims
as quickly as possible, even after  Hyson Leblanc’s passing, but PM USA  has demonstrated that,
in its experience defending cigarette cases, seven months is not a sufficient amount of time fo r it
to conduct the requisite discovery.  As such, a brief continuance of the trial and the deadlines
imposed in the current scheduling order is appropriate to accommodate the reasons stated for PM
USA’s requested extension.
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS ORDERED that PM USA’s motion for continuance of trial date and to vacate
scheduling order (R. Doc. 174) is GRANTED.
