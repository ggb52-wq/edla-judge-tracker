III. Analysis
 Gaten filed this action under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. In pertinent part, that statute
provides:
Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation,
custom, or usage, of any State or Territory or the District of Columbia,
subjects, or causes to be subjected, any citizen of the United States or
other person within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any
rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws,
shall be liable to the party injured in an action at law, suit in equity, or
other proper proceeding for redress[.]

42 U.S.C. § 1983. Accordingly, “[t]o state a claim under § 1983, a plaintiff must allege
the violation of a right secured by the Constitution and laws of the United States, and
must show that the alleged deprivation was committed by a person acting under color
of state law.” West v. Atkins, 487 U.S. 42, 48 (1988) (citation omitted).
A. “NOPD Strike Unit” & “Sixth District Platoon Officers”
In the caption of the complaint, Gaten lists “NOPD’s Strike Unit ” and “Sixth
District Platoon Officers” as defendants.20 Groups of people or teams are not properly
named defendants. Instead, a § 1983 action must be filed against an actual
identified person who violated a constitutional right, not a department or general
staff group. See, e.g. , Hill v. Strain , No. 08 -4768, 2010 WL 111062, at *3 (E.D. La.
Jan. 11, 2010) (“Plaintiff may not pursue claims against the unidentified medical
personnel and/or sheriff deputies ....”); Banks v. United States, No. 05-6853, 2007 WL

B. Magistrate Judge Lombard and Commissioner Hamilton
Gaten sued Magistrate Judge Lombard and Commissioner Hamilton, who sit
in the Orlea ns Criminal District Court , and signed the related search warrants. 21
Whether named in an individual or official capacity, both a judge and a commissioner
enjoy absolute immunity from suit.
The Eleventh Amendment bars any claim against Magistrate Judge Lombard
and Commissioner Hamilton in their official capacities. See Will v. Mich. Dept. of St.
Police, 491 U.S. 58, 71 (1989); Warnock v. Pecos Cty., Tex., 88 F.3d 341, 343 (5th Cir.
1996); Owings v. Burris, No. 23-487, 2023 WL 5345306, at *3 (E.D. La. June 29, 2023)
(holding that the Eleventh Amendment bars official -capacity claims against a state
court commissioner), adopted, 2023 WL 5333194 (E.D. La. Aug. 18, 2023); Rackley v.
Louisiana, No. 07-504, 2007 WL 1792524, at *3 (E.D. La. June 21, 2007) (finding suit
against Louisiana state judge in an official capacity is suit against the State).
Likewise, absolute judicial immunity bars any claim against Judge Lombard
and Commissioner Hamilton  in their individual capacities. Judges have “complete
protection” from lawsuits based on judicial functions. See Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21,
29 (1991) (quotation omitted); Owings, 2023 WL 5345306, at *3 (finding claim against
commissioner in his official capacity barred by absolute judicial immunity). Absolute
immunity applies regardless of whether the jud icial officer’s actions were allegedly
erroneous, malicious, or in excess of his  or her authority. See Mays v. Sudderth , 97
F.3d 107, 111 (5th Cir. 1996)). “A judge’s immunity is overcome only for actions not

Liberally c onstrued,22 Gaten appears to claim that Hairston falsified affidavits to

their persons ... against unreasonable searches and seizures ... and [that] no warrants

D. Request for Monetary Damages
The only relief Gaten sought in this case is a claim for monetary damages. 25
Section 1997e(e) provides that “[n]o Federal civil action may be brought by a prisoner
confined in a jail, prison, or other correctional facility, for mental or emotional injury
suffered while in custody without a prior showing of physical injury....” 42 U.S.C. §
1997e(e). The Fifth Circuit has held that this requirement “applies to all federal civil
actions in which a prisoner alleges a constitutional violation, making compensatory
