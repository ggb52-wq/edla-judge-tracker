ORDER AND REASONS

Pending before me is Plaintiffs’ Motion for Leave to Serve Supplemental Discovery.  ECF
No. 32; see also ECF No. 34.  In this motion, Plaintiffs seek leave to serve interrogatories and
requests for production, presumably as supplemental to the initial disclosures.  No party filed an
Opposition, nor did any party request oral argument.
Having considered the record and the applicable law,  Plaintiffs’ motion is DENIED as
unnecessary for the reasons stated herein.
I. BACKGROUND
Plaintiffs filed suit alleging that Defendant Navy Federal Credit Union failed to provide
full and accurate information regarding the servicing of their mortgage, in violation of federal (12
U.S.C. § 2605 and related regulations) and state law.  ECF No. 18 ¶¶ 20-34.  The Court’s August
27, 2026 Scheduling Order established a trial date of June 21, 2027, with a discovery deadline of
March 19, 2027.  ECF No. 28, at 3 & 6.  This Order also required the parties to exchange initial
disclosures by September 3, 2027.  Id. at 2.
Shortly after the Scheduling Order, Plaintiffs filed this “Motion for Leave to Serve
Supplemental Discovery.”  The Honorable Anna St. John issued an order clarifying that the parties
discussed initial disclosures, not supplemental discovery, during the scheduling conference.  ECF
No. 34.  Judge St. John granted a 7 -day extension of the deadline to exchange initial disclosures
and referred the remainder of Plaintiffs’ request regarding discovery to the undersigned.  Id.

II. APPLICABLE LAW AND ANALYSIS
In general, discovery in ordinary civil actions governed by the Federal Rules  of Civil
Procedure is initially conducted by the parties without court assistance.  “Unless otherwise limited
by court order . . . [p]arties  may obtain discovery regarding any nonprivileged matter that is
relevant to any party’ s claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case[.]” 1  Absent a
court order, Rule 26(d)(1) prohibits discovery from any source before the Rule 26(f) conference .
When, however, either the Rule 26(f) conference or the Rule 16 scheduling conference has been
held,2 leave of court is not required to conduct discovery during the authorized discovery period
except in limited circumstances. 3  The fact that Plaintiffs are proceeding pro se does not excuse
them for lack of knowledge of the Rules of Civil Procedure  or relieve them of the obligation to
comply with the governing rules addressing the proper means of obtaining discovery.4
A party seeking discovery must present the discovery directly to the appropriate party or
entity, in accordance with Rules 26 through 36 and/or 45 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure.
The responding party is entitled to 30 days within which to provide written responses setting forth
the answers and/or objections.5  If the requesting party deems the responses insufficient, before
seeking court assistance, the requesting party must confer  with the opposing party to resolve any
perceived deficiency in accordance with Rule 37 .  Only after that good faith effort to resolve the
