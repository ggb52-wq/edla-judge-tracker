Before the Court is Plaintiff AC Haynes’s objection1 to the Magistrate Judge’s
Report and Recommendation2 in which the Magistrate Judge recommends dismissal
without prejudice of Plaintiff’s claims ag ainst Defendants Jordan Hollier, Jeremy
Seaux, and Renovation Pro’s, LLC for failure to serve the Defendants, pursuant to
Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 41(b) and 4(m). Performing de novo  review, the
Court overrules Plaintiff’s objection and adopts the Report and Recommendation in
its entirety.
Plaintiff AC Haynes is a sixty-six-year-o ld African American resident of New
Orleans, Louisiana, who was employed by Defendant Renovation Pro’s, LLC. 3
Plaintiff sued Defendants Jordan Hollier, Jeremy Seaux, and Renovation Pro’s, LLC
due to Defendants’ alleged “discriminatory treatment and harassment of Plaintiff due
to his race and [] unlawful retaliation against him after he complained about unlawful
harassment and discrimination in the workplace.”4 Plaintiff alleges that Seaux, the
general manager at Renovation Pro’s, harassed and discriminated against him for his

In the Report and Recommendation dated February 25, 2025,15 the Magistrate
Judge recommends the Court dismiss Plainti ff’s claims against Defendants without
prejudice pursuant to Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 41(b) and 4(m). Plaintiff
timely objects 16 to the Magistrate Judge’s recommendation and contends that
Defendants are avoiding service. 17 Plaintiff asks that his case not be dismissed and
that he be allowed an additional opportunity to serve Defendants. 18 Plaintiff also
points to a state court action he filed against Renovation Pro’s, LLC in the 24th
Judicial District Court for the Parish of Jefferson, in which he claims the company’s
registered agent, Alexis Seaux, was served on March 27, 2024.19 However, according
to Plaintiff, Defendants are no longer associated with that address and their
whereabouts are unknown.20
The Court finds that Plaintiff’s objection does not undermine or call into
question any aspect of the Magistrate Judge’s Report and Recommendation. Federal
Rule of Civil Procedure 4(m) requires that a defendant be served within 90 days after
the complaint is filed. If a plaintiff fails to do so, the court “must dismiss the action
without prejudice against that defendant or  order that service be made within a
specified time,” unless the plaintiff shows good cause for the failure, in which the
court must extend the time for service. FED. R. CIV. P. 4(m). To establish good cause,

of the rules usually does not suffice.” Thrasher v. City of Amarillo, 709 F.3d 509, 511
(5th Cir. 2013) (quoting Winters v. Teledyne Movible Offshore, Inc. , 776 F.2d 1304,

Foremost, Plaintiff has not filed proof th at Defendants were served with the
summons and complaint in this case, despite receiving an extension of time and two
separate notices that failure to effect service could result in dismissal. Nor has
Plaintiff shown good cause for his failure to serve Defendants within the extended
period allowed by the Court. Further, Pl aintiff’s assertion that Defendants are
avoiding service is unsupported. The record contains no exhibits showing Defendants’
evasion of service, much less a record of  when, where, or ho w counsel attempted to
serve Defendants in this case. Instead, Plaintiff’s objection relies entirely on a single
successful service of the corporate defend ant in a separate state court proceeding
almost three months before Plaintiff filed suit here. The state court records have no
bearing on this instant case. Thus, Plaintiff has not shown good cause.
This Court declines to exercise its discretion to extend the time for service.
First, Plaintiff had more than eight months to serve Defendants from the filing of the
complaint (June 19, 2024) to the Magistrate Judge’s Report and Recommendation
(February 25, 2025), far exceeding the 90 days allowed by FED. R. CIV. P. 4(m). Second,
Plaintiff has already received two show cause orders and an extension of time.
Plaintiff did not request an extension until after the original service period expired,

and even then his request did not identify any concrete efforts to serve Defendants
during the 90-day period. Plaintiff did not request another extension. Third, although
Plaintiff pleads with the Court not to dismiss the case, he has not explained how he
has attempted to effect service in this case nor has he shown proof of attempting
service. Fourth, Plaintiff’s objection is internally inconsistent—stating on the one
hand that he “does not know the whereabouts of the Defendants and therefore cannot
serve the defendants,” and then in the next paragraph that he “believes that Jordan
Hollier has started a new business with a new address and believes that he can be
served at that location.”21 Plaintiff’s statements, without supporting facts, fall short
of good cause. Lastly, Plaintiff has been forewarned by the Magistrate Judge that
failure to comply with th e last show cause order 22 could result in dismissal without
further notice. Still, the record reflects no service on any named Defendant.
Having conducted a de novo review of the Report and Recommendation in light
of Plaintiff’s objection, see  28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1), the Court finds Plaintiff’s objection
lacks merit, and therefore, the Court over rules Plaintiff’s objection and adopts the
Report and Recommendation.
Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that Plaintiff’s objection 23 to the Report and
Recommendation is OVERRULED.
