I. BACKGROUND
This case arises out of the alleged wrongful arrest, detention, prosecution, and
ultimate acquittal of a wife for her husband’s murder.
Shantel Parria was arrested, detained, and tried for the second-degree murder
of her husband, Jefferson Parish Sheriff’s Office (“JPSO”) Sergeant Troy Smith, who
died one week after sustaining a gunshot wound to the head . Years after the death

II. LEGAL STANDARD
Summary judgment is appropriate “if the movant shows that there is no
genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a
matter of law.” FED . R.  CIV . P.  56(a). “A dispute is genuine if ‘the evidence is such
that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving party.’” Perry v. VHS
San Antonio Partners, L.L.C., 990 F.3d 918, 926 (5th Cir. 2021) (quoting Anderson v.
Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986)). “A fact is material if it ‘might affect the
outcome of the suit.’” Id. (quoting Anderson, 477 U.S. at 248).
[A] party seeking summary judgment always bears the initial
responsibility of informing the district court of the basis for its motion,
and identifying those portions of the pleadings, depositions, answers to
interrogatories, and admissions on file, together w ith the affidavits, if
any, which it believes demonstrate the absence of a genuine issue of
material fact.
Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 323 (1986) (internal quotations omitted).
III. LAW AND ANALYSIS
A. The Louisiana Direct-Action Statute Merely Confers a Procedural
Right of Action.

Louisiana’s Direct-Action Statute, La.R.S. 22:1269, “grants a procedural right
of action against an insurer where an injured party has a substantive cause of action

against the insured and other statutorily -specified conditions are met.” Marriner v.
Talos Petroleum, LLC, 576 F. Supp. 3d 412, (E.D. La. 2021) (Fallon, J.) (citing Soileau
v. Smith True Value and Rental , 2012 -1711 (La. 6/28/13), 144 So. 3d 771, 775).
However, the Statute “does not create an independent cause of action against the
insurer, it merely grants a procedural right of action against the insurer where the
plaintiff has a substantive cause of acti on against the insured.” Stire v. Watson, No.
12-2982, 2013 WL 3944423, at *6 (E.D. La. July 30, 2013) (Vance, J.) (quoting New
England Ins. Co. v. Barnett, 465 F. App’x 302, 309 (5th Cir. 2012)). Simply put, in the
direct-action context, when the insured is dismissed from the lawsuit, the insurer
likely must be dismissed because there is no underlying liability to support the direct
action. See, e.g., Stire, 2013 WL 3944423, at *6.
B. Parria Has No Substantive Cause of Action Against the JPCO
Defendants’ Insurer.

Underwriters’ liability to Parria under the insurance policy (if any) here is thus
derived from any viable claims Plaintiff has against the JPCO  Defendants. That is,
Parria’s procedural right of action against Underwriters exists only insofar as a
substantive cause of action is maintained against its insureds, the JPCO Defendants.
But Parria’s substantive claims against the JPCO Defendants have been dismissed.11
Accordingly, because Underwriters’ liability is coextensive with the JPCO
Defendants’, Parria’s claims against Underwriters  likewise must be dismissed.
