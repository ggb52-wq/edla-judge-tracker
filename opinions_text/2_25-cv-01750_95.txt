II.  LAW AND APPLICATION
It is a “well-settled rule of law that a corporation cannot appear in federal court
unless represented by a licensed attorney.” 28 U.S.C. § 1654; Memon v. Allied Domecq
QSR, 385 F.  3d 871, 873 (5th Cir. 2004). When confronted with an unrepresented
corporate defendant, the district court has discretion to determine the appropriate
course of action. See F ED. R. CIV. P. 16(f); Memon, 385 F. 3d at 873. The court must
first warn the corporate defendant that it cannot appear unrepresented and explain
the consequences of failing to retain counsel.  See Memon, F. 3d at 875 (reversing
dismissal “because the district court did not first expressly warn the corporation that
it must retain counsel ”); see also Donovan v. Road Rangers Country Junction, Inc. ,
736 F.2d 1004, 1005 (5th Cir. 1984) (per curiam).  “When a corporate defendant fails
to obey orders to obtain counsel after being  warned of the consequences of failing to
do so, the district court may properly strike its defenses or enter default.” G&G Closed
Cir. Events, LLC v. TKO Ram LLC, No. 23-CV-01533, 2025 WL 2612741, at *4 (W.D.
Tex. Aug. 20, 2025).
Pursuant to Rule 55(a), default “must” be entered by the clerk when “a party
against whom a judgment or affirmative relief is sought has failed to plead or

Jurek is instructive. In Jurek, defense counsel filed a motion to withdraw after
responding to the complaint  on behalf of two LLC defendants . Jurek, 2025 WL
1874981, at *1. The district court granted the motion and  advised that, because an
LLC may not proceed  pro se, “the corporate defendants “need[ed] to obtain new
counsel as quickly as possible.” Id. at *2. But over the next several months, the LLC
defendants failed to enroll counsel or appear at scheduled status conferences. Id. The
court noted that “[w]here, as here, a limited liability company previously represented
by counsel becomes unrepresented by reason of counsel ’s withdrawal and fails to

enroll new counsel against the orders of the court, the court may strike that
defendant’s answer and deem all allegations of the complaint admitted by failure to
defend.” Id. at *3. (citing  Donovan, 736 F.  2d at 1005). Therefore, upon Plaintiff’s
motion, the court struck the defendants’ answers. Jurek, 2025 WL 1872978, at *1.
 The Court finds  the same course of action is appropriate  here. On three
occasions, the Court ordered CADA to enroll counsel and explained the consequences
of failing to enroll. See Pipe Hitters Union, LLC v. Pipe Hitters Union MC, LLC , No.
1:20-CV-167, 2020 WL 10692700, at *2 (W.D. Tex. Apr. 27, 2020) (ordering the clerk
to strike the answer of defendant LLC after orders to  enroll counsel were ignored).
Clyne has presented several explanations for CADA’s failure to retain counsel , none
of which apply to CADA. An LLC “is a separate legal entity from its owners.” Axis
Surplus Ins. Co. v. Third Millennium Ins. & Fin. Servs., Inc. , 781 F. Supp. 2d 320,
326 (E.D. La. 2011). Therefore, CADA’s ability to retain counsel is not limited by
Clyne’s personal divorce or bankruptcy proceedings. And the Bankruptcy Court has
confirmed that any automatic stay related to Clyne’s bankruptcy case “does not apply
to persons other than the Debtor, Floyd Gary Clyne.”30 Dragon Limited may therefore
continue to litigate 31  its claims against CADA —claims that CADA has failed to
defend.

The Court also finds that an entry of default is appropriate. The record here is
rife with  examples of delay caused by CADA’s  failure to retain counsel . A fter
permitting counsel to withdraw twice, the Court ordered CADA to retain counsel by
September 14, 2026. Despite this Court’s warning that failing to retain new counsel
could result in a default judgment , no counsel has been enrolled on CADA’s  behalf.
The trial date is quickly approaching, but without legal representation, CADA cannot
appear in federal court to defend this action.  As a result, Dragon Limited has been
prejudiced by its inability  to meaningfully prosecute its case against the
unrepresented LLC. And given CADA’s repeated disregard of prior orders, 32 the
Court is convinced that “a lesser sanction would not have served the interests of
justice.” Kidwell v. Ruby IV, L.L.C., No. CV 18 -2052, 2019 WL 5703240, at *1 (E.D.
