A. Statutorily Required Screening
As soon as practicable after docketing, and regardless of any fee that may have been paid,
the court must review a pris oner’s § 1983 complaint for a cogni zable claim, or dismiss the
complaint if it is frivolous and/or fails to state a claim.7  The court must dismiss the complaint if,
upon review, it is frivolous and/or fails to state a claim.8  A claim is frivolous if it “lacks an arguable
basis in law or fact.” 9  A claim lacks an arguable basis in la w if it is “based on an indisputably
meritless legal theory, such as if the complaint alleges the violation of a legal interest which clearly
does not exist.”10  A factually frivolous claim alleges only facts that are “‘clearly baseless,’ . . . are
‘fanciful,’ ‘fantastic,’ and ‘delusional’ . . .  [or] rise to the level of the irrational or wholly incredible
. . . .”11  A court may not dismiss a claim simply because the facts are “unlikely.”12

allegations do not rise above a speculative level, with the assumption that all factual allegations in

B. Required Elements of a § 1983 Claim
Section 1983 creates a damages remedy for th e violation of federal constitutional or
statutory rights under color of state law:
Every person who, under color of any stat ute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or
usage, of any State . . . subj ects, or causes to be subjec ted, any . . . person within
the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privil eges, or immunities
secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured in an action
at law . . . .21

“The purpose of § 1983 is to de ter state actors from using their badge of authority to deprive
individuals of federally guaranteed rights and to provide relief to victims if such deterrence fails.”22
A plaintiff must satisfy three elements to establish § 1983 liability:
(1) deprivation of a right secured by th e U.S. Constitution or federal law;
(2) that occurred under co lor of state law; and
(3) was caused by a state actor. 23

Because § 1983 merely provides a remedy for de signated rights, rather than creating any
substantive rights, “an underlying constitutional or statutory violation is a predicate to liability.”24
This requires the plaintiff to identify both the constitutional violation and the responsible person
acting under color of state law. 25  “The traditional definition of  acting under color of state law
requires that the defendant in a § 1983 action have  exercised power ‘possessed by virtue of state
law and made possible only because the wrongdoer is clothed with the authority of state law.’”26

C. Subject Matter Jurisdiction
Federal courts are courts of limited jurisdic tion.  “Subject matter jurisdiction may not be
waived, and the district court ‘shall dismiss the action’ whenever ‘it appears by suggestion of the
parties or otherwise that the court lack s jurisdiction of the subject matter.’”27  The party seeking
the federal forum has the burden of establishing jurisdiction. 28  If subject matter jurisdiction over
the complaint is lacking, dismissal is appropriate for that reason and pursuant to § 1915.29
One basis for subject matter jurisdiction is diversity under 28 U.S.C. § 1332.  Federal
diversity jurisdiction requires the party to demons trate that (1) complete diversity of citizenship
exists between the parties and (2) the amoun t in controversy exceeds $75,000.00, exclusive of
interest and costs.30  Complete diversity exists when “no party on one side [is] a citizen of the same
State as any party on the other side.” 31  Another basis for jurisdicti on typically invoked in civil
cases is federal question jurisdiction.  28 U.S.C. § 1331.  Federal question jurisdiction is assessed
under the well-pleaded complaint rule, which requires that a federal question appear on the face of
the well-pleaded complaint.32

IV. CONCLUSION
To the extent Plaintiff seeks to assert a § 1983 claim based on the initial seizure of his
property, that claim is Heck barred.  To the extent he seeks to premise a § 1983 claim on the State’s
subsequent sale of the property in contraventio n of the consent agreement, that claim is not
cognizable under § 1983.  Rather, any relief for the “random and unauthorized” deprivation of
property and breach of contract should be addre ssed through state tort or contract law (i.e.,
conversion or breach of contract claims60), because such claims are not premised on an underlying
constitutional violation as necessary to state a § 1983 claim.
While the court may exercise supplemental jurisdiction over a state law claim where the
court has original jurisdiction over other claims and the supplemental claims are so related to the
original claims that they “derive from a common nucleus of operative fact,” 61 the general rule in
the Fifth Circuit is that the court should declin e to exercise jurisdiction over remaining state-law
claims when all federal-law claims are eliminated before trial. 62  Because I have recommended
that Plaintiff’s § 1983 claims be  dismissed, the court has discre tion to decline or exercise
supplemental jurisdiction over Plaintiff’s state law claims.63
