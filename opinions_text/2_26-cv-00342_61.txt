PARTIAL REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

 Defendant Blue Sky Hospitality Solutions, L.L.C. (“Blue Sky”) removed this lawsuit to
this Court on February  13, 2026.  (Rec. doc. 1 ).  On December 9, 2025, Plaintiff Chelsea
Tamikka May filed a Petition for Damages and Jury Demand in the 24th Judicial District Court
for the Parish of Jefferson naming as Defendants  Blue Sky, Marriott Metairie at Lakeway, 1
Scott Campbell (“Campbell”), Rabia Mehboob (“Mehboob”), Naveen Shah (“Shah”), Berkshire
Hathaway Homestate Insurance Company (“Berkshire”), Erin Ellis (Ellis”), and Nathan
Ganyo (“Ganyo”) for allegedly violating a number of state and federal statutes.
 Since the removal, Plaintiff has filed two A mended Complaints , which this Court
allowed.  (Rec. docs. 13, 19, 26, 29).   After she filed her Fir st Amended Complaint, May
requested service of summons on numerous Defendants, but she DID NOT  request service
of summons on Campbell or Mehboob.  (Rec. doc. 31).  All of the served Defendants waived
service.  (Rec. docs. 33, 34, 40, 41).  After the District Judge adopted this Court’ s Report and
Recommendation that Plaintiff be allowed leave to file a Second Amended Complaint, May
requested service of summons on no Defendant.

 To thi s day, there has been no service and no request for service on Campbell or
Mehboob, and neither Defendant has appeared in this lawsuit.  As this case cannot properly
and efficiently procced without service on all Defendants, this Court issued an Order on June
23, 2026, ordering May or Defendants even to show cause by July 21, 2026 why service had
not been effected on these two Defendants.  (Rec. doc. 47).  This Court warned that “[i]f either
Plaintiff or Defendants fail to comply with this Order, the undersigned [would] issue a report
recommending that this lawsuit be dismissed without prejudice as to these two Defendants
and/or recommend remand for failure to comply with 28 U.S.C. § 1446.”  ( Id.).2  No party
responded to this Court’s Order by the deadline.
 The Federal Rules provide:
If a defendant is not served within 90 days after the complaint is filed, the court
– on motion or on its own after notice to the plaintiff – must dismiss the action
without prejudice against that defendant or order that service be made within
a specified time.  But if the plaintiff shows good cause for the failure, the court
must extend the time for service for an appropriate period.

Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(m).
 “ At a minimum, a plaintiff should request service upon the appropriate defendant and
attempt to remedy any apparent service defects of which a plaintiff has knowledge.”  Rochon v.
Dawson, 828 F.2d 1107, 1110 (5th Cir. 1987) (emphasis added).  Dismissal is appropriate
when service has not been properly effected due to the inaction or dilatoriness of a plaintiff.
Id.

More than 90 days have elapsed since this lawsuit was removed on February 13,
2026.  Despite the passage of that extensive period of time, Plaintiff has not provided this
Court with evidence that either Campbell or Mehboob has been served.  Indeed, Plaintiff
never even requested service on these two Defendants in this federal lawsuit .  The
Court gave P laintiff notice of the fact that Defendants had not been served, as well as an
opportunity to cure that defect.  Nevertheless, Plaintiff has not cured the defect by providing
evidence of service, and, as a result, no service has been effected .  Therefore, the failure to
serve Defendants results not from the actions or inactions of the Court, but rather solely from
the inaction and dilatoriness of Plaintiff.
Despite being given express notice that h er claims against Campbell and Mehboob
could be dismissed unless she provided evidence of service or showed good cause for failing
to do so, P laintiff failed to respond to this Court’ s Order.  Without service on Campbell or
Mehboob, issue cannot be joined, and this case cannot proceed against the properly- served
Defendants.  Accordingly, following Rochon, this Court finds that dismissal of the  claims
against Campbell and Mehboob without prejudice is now appropriate.  See, e.g., Gipson v.
Keith, 678 F. App'x 264, 266 (5th Cir. 2017); Triplett v. LeBlanc , 642 F. App'x 457, 459- 60
(5th Cir. 2016); Armant v. Stalder, 351 F. App'x 958, 959 (5th Cir. 2009); Pines v. St. Tammany
Par. Prison, Civ. Action No. 09-3113, 2009 WL 3347384 (E.D. La. Oct. 14, 2009).  Accordingly,
IT IS RECOMMENDED  that Plaintiff’s claims against Scott Campbell and Rabia
Mehboob be DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE for failure to effect service on Defendants
and failure to prosecute under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(m).
