Court to lift the stay of the above-captioned action. R. Doc. 27. Defendants Certain Underwriters

Having considered the briefing in light of the applicable law and record facts, and having heard

I. BACKGROUND & PRESENT MOTION
Plaintiff filed the above-captioned action, originally in Louisiana state court, asserting that
its property located at 3900 France Road, New Orleans, LA, was damaged by Hurricane Ida in
August of 2021. R. Doc. 1- 1 at 5. Plaintiff alleg es that Defendants, its insurers, failed to make
adequate payment for the damages under the terms of the policy and s eek damages and statutory
penalties. Id. at 5–8. Lloyd’s removed the action to this Court on the basis that this action is subject
to the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards (the

II. DISCUSSION
As recognized in its opinion remanding this action, the Fifth Circuit has previously
addressed the issues raised by the parties in the instant motion in an unpublished opinion relative
to another case presenting nearly identical facts.  See Transportation Consultants, Inc. v. Certain
Underwriters at Lloyd's, London, 190 F.4th 386, 390–91 (5th Cir. 2026). In Crescent City Surgical
Operating Co. v. Interstate Fire & Cas. Co., the Circuit considered whether the district court had
correctly compelled arbitration of the plaintiff’s claims against the foreign insurers, declined to
compel arbitration as to the domestic insurers, and refused to stay the litigation against the
domestic insurers pending resolution of the arbitration against the foreign insurers. No. 25-30044,
2026 WL 1091579, at *1 (5th Cir. Apr. 22, 2026). Applying the recent binding precedent in Town
of Vinton v. Indian Harbor Ins. Co., 161 F.4th 282, 287–88 (5th Cir. 2025), the Crescent City court
affirmed the district court’s order compelling arbitration against the foreign insurers alone but
reversed the district court’s order denying a stay of the litigation against the domestic insurers until
that arbitration was complete. Crescent City, 2026 WL 1091579, at *5. The Circuit explained:
Section 3 of the Federal Arbitration Act “compels [a] court to stay the proceeding”
before it pending arbitration. Smith v. Spizzirri , 601 U.S. 472, 478 (2024)
(discussing 9 U.S.C. § 3). Generally, however, that section “only applies to parties
to an agreement containing an arbitration clause.” Rainier DSC 1, L.L.C. v. Rainier
Cap. Mgmt., L.P., 828 F.3d 356, 360 (5th Cir. 2016). It has been extended to
nonsignatories “only where: (1) the arbitrated and litigated disputes involve the
same operative facts; (2) the claims asserted in the arbitration and litigation are
‘inherently inseparable’; and (3) the litigation has a ‘critical impact’ on the
arbitration.” Id. (quoting Waste Mgmt., Inc. v. Residuos Industriales Multiquim,
S.A. de C.V., 372 F.3d 339, 341 (5th Cir. 2004)). “[W]hile this court generally
review[s] de novo a denial of a motion to stay a proceeding pending arbitration, we
review whether to stay nonsignatories' litigation for an abuse of discretion.” Cure
& Assocs., P.C. v. LPL Fin. L.L.C., 118 F.4th 663, 669 (5th Cir. 2024) (internal
citation omitted). “To constitute an abuse of discretion, the district court's decision
must be either premised on an application of the law that is erroneous, or on an
assessment of the evidence that is clearly erroneous.” Id.

Crescent City, 2026 WL 1091579, at *5. Applying the three factors articulated in Rainier , the

Circuit held that the district court had abused its discretion in declining to the requested stay. Id.
The Circuit explained that the plaintiff’s “claims, in arbitration and in court, ar[o]se from the ‘same
operative facts’ under the Policy, which, though construed as a separate contract between Crescent
City and each insurer, still [wa]s a unified insurance agreement with the aim of providing Crescent
City a seamless level of coverage. ” Id. (quoting Rainier, 828 F.3d at 360). It held that this
arrangement also made the claims against each insurer “inherently inseparable.” Id. (quoting id.).
Finally, the Circuit stated that it was “ difficult to conceive how the litigation could not have a
critical impact on the arbitration, given that both proceedings involve the same coverage questions
and the same facts.” Id. Therefore, it determined, “ the district court should have stayed the
litigation pending resolution of the arbitration” against the foreign insurers. Id.
In its opinion remanding the present action, the Fifth Circuit declined to follow Crescent
City and hold that litigation against the domestic insurers in this case must also be stayed until the
conclusion of arbitration proceedings against the foreign insurers. Transportation Consultants, 190
F.4th 38 at 391. As the Circuit stated, in Crescent City it addressed that issue after the district court
had applied the three factors laid out in Rainier  and Waste Management. Id. In contrast, in this
case, “when the district court lifted the stay of litigation between Transportation Consultants and
the domestic insurers, [Fifth Circuit] law in this area was far less developed —lacking Town of
Vinton and Crescent City—and the parties had not briefed any stay factors. ” Id. Accordingly, the
Fifth Circuit remanded the case to this Court for consideration of that narrow issue —whether,
applying the Rainier factors, litigation against the domestic insurers must remain stayed. Id.
The Court must thus address whether, in this case “ the arbitrated and litigated disputes
involve the same operative facts; (2) the claims asserted in the arbitration and litigation are
‘inherently inseparable’; and (3) the litigation has a ‘critical impact’ on the arbitration.” Rainier,

828 F.3d at 360 (quoting Waste Mgmt., 372 F.3d at 341).
As to the first factor, Plaintiff acknowledges that “ the same hurricane, property, policy
terms, adjustment history, and loss valuation will be material in both forums ” which “strongly
favors [Defendants] on the first Rainier  consideration[.]” R. Doc. 31 at 2. The Court agrees. The
disputes Plaintiffs must arbitrate against the foreign insurers are identical to the disputes they may
litigate against the domestic insurers.  Moreover, as to the second factor, Plaintiff’s insurance
policy, “though construed as a separate contract between [Plaintiff] and each insurer, still  is a
unified insurance agreement with the aim of providing [Plaintiff]  a seamless level of coverage. ”
Crescent City, 2026 WL 1091579, at *5. As recognized by the Cresent City  court, this makes
Plaintiff’s claims against the foreign insurers and the domestic insurers “inherently inseparable. ”
Id. (quoting Rainier, 828 F.3d at 360).1
Plaintiff’s arguments in favor of lifting the stay go primarily to the third Rainier factor:
whether its litigation against the domestic insurers, if not stayed, would have a critical impact on
its arbitration against the foreign insurers. See R. Doc. 27 at 8–9. Plaintiff points out that, in this
case, unlike in Crescent City, arbitration against the foreign insurers has not yet begun. Id.  at 8.
Additionally, in this case, the domestic insurers collectively subscribe to approximately 81% of
the risk covered by the subject insurance policy, with foreign insurers subscribing to only the
remaining approximately 19%. Id. at 9. Plaintiff fails to explain how these differences would
render any determination by this Court in litigation against the domestic insurers any less critical
to arbitration against the foreign insurers than that recognized by the Crescent City court. Plaintiff

III. CONCLUSION
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons;
IT IS ORDERED that Plaintiff Transportation Consultants, Inc.’s Motion to Lift Stay, R.
Doc. 27, is hereby DENIED.
