ORDER AND REASONS
On September 12, 2025, Plaintiff Summer Sears (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint1
for violation of civil rights (42 U.S.C. §  1983), wrongful death, and related state law
claims against Defendants Dr. Gerry Cvitanovich  (“Cvitanovich”), Dr. Charles E.
Eckert, (“Eckert”), Dr. Charles Preston  (“Preston”), Ochsner Medical Center –
Kenner, L.L.C. (“Ochsner”), Dr. Susan Taylor (“Taylor”), Kenner Police Department
(“KPD”), and Keith A. Conley  (“Conley”) . Defendants Conley , K PD, Ochsner,
Cvitanovich, and Eckert filed motions2 to dismiss for failure to state a claim. Plaintiff,
who is proceeding pro se, did not file an opposition to those motions.
On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff moved 3 for leave to file a first amended
complaint. Plaintiff sought to amend her complaint to substitute  the City of Kenner
in place of KPD, to remove Preston as a defendant, and to clarify certain claims . No
defendants filed an opposition to Plaintiff’s motion, which was granted4 on March 16,
2026. Plaintiff filed her first amended complaint 5 on March 16, 2026, naming

Considering the amended complaint, the motions to dismiss Plaintiff ’s initial
complaint filed by Defendants Conley, KPD, Ochsner, Cvitanovich, and Eckert were
denied as moot.7
On March 30, 2026, Defendant City of Kenner filed a motion 8 to dismiss the
amended complaint, which was noticed for submission on August 5, 2026. Then, on
September 16, 2026, Defendant Taylor filed a motion 9 to dismiss the amended
complaint, which was noticed for submission on October 28, 2026.
Local Rule 7.5 of the United States District Court for the Eastern District of
Louisiana requires that an opposition to a motion must be filed no later than eight
days before the noticed submission date. Plaintiff has not filed a memorandum in
opposition to either motion to dismiss the amended complaint. The Court notes that
Plaintiff is proceeding pro se. Although courts construe pro se filings liberally, the
Court still requires pro se parties to “abide by the rules that govern the federal
courts.” E.E.O.C. v. Simbaki, Ltd., 767 F.3d 475, 484 (5th Cir. 2014).
Accordingly, because the motions10 to dismiss the amended complaint filed by
Defendants City of Kenner and Taylor are unopposed, and it appearing to the Court
that the motions have merit,
