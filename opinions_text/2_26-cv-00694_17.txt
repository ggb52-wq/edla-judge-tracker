R AND REASONS
Before the Court is  Plaintiff Danny Trey Crossland’s  Motion for the Appointment of
Counsel to represent him in connection with the instant civil rights suit. R. Doc. 16. Crossland
raises claims relative to the alleged denial of medical care during his incarceration in Ray burn
Correctional Center. R. Doc. 1. Having considered Crossland Motion in light of the applicable law
and record facts, the Court will DENY the Motion for the following reasons.
A civil rights plaintiff has no constitutional or statutory right to appointed counsel. Branch
v. Cole, 686 F.2d 264, 266 (5th Cir. 1981). A court may, however, appoint counsel in exceptional
circumstances. Id. In deciding whether exceptional circumstances exist, courts within the Fifth
Circuit apply the four factors identified in Ulmer v. Chancellor: (1) the type and complexity of the
case; (2) the plaintiff's ability to present his case adequately; (3) his ability to investigate the facts;
and (4) whether the case will likely involve conflicting testimony that requires skilled presentation
of evidence and cross-examination. 691 F.2d 209, 213 (5th Cir. 1982).  Courts may also consider
whether appointing counsel would promote the efficient and equitable resolution of the
case. Jackson v. Dallas Police Dep ’t, 811 F.2d 260, 262 (5th Cir. 1986). Because every pro se
litigant would benefit from counsel, the p laintiff bears the burden of showing that h er
circumstances differ from those of the typical pro se litigant. James v. City of Deer Park, No. 4:18-
CV-646, 2018 WL 11353746, at *2 (S.D. Tex. June 7, 2018).

Applying the Ulmer  factors here, the Court concludes that this case does not present
exceptional circumstances. Crossland’s claims involve issues that courts routinely address in
prisoner civil rights litigation and do not present unusual legal or factual complexity. See Lazard
v. Gusman, No. 14- 2860, 2015 WL 5882984, at *2 (E.D. La. Oct. 8, 2015). Crossland has also
demonstrated that he can articulate h is claims and file appropriate pleadings, and nothing in the
record suggests that he cannot investigate the r elevant facts. Although the Court recognizes the
challenges Crossland faces as an incarcerated pro se  litigant, those challenges do not materially
differ from those that other pro se litigants routinely encounter. Crossland therefore has not shown
the exceptional circumstances necessary to justify appointing counsel.
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons;
IT IS ORDERED that Crossland’s Motion to Appoint Counsel, R. Doc. 16, is DENIED.
