LEGAL STANDARD
To survive a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss, a plaintiff must plead
enough facts “to state a claim for relief that is plausible on its face.”1 A claim is
“plausible on its face” when the pleaded facts allow the court to “draw the
reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.” 2
A court must accept the complaint’s factual allegations as true and must “draw
all reasonable inferences in the plaintiff’s favor.”3 The court need not, however,
accept as true legal conclusions couched as factual allegations. 4 To be legally
sufficient, a complaint must establish more than a “sheer possibility ” that the
plaintiff’s claims are true.5 If it is apparent from the face of the complaint that
an insurmountable bar to relief exists and the plaintiff is not entitled to relief,
the court must dismiss the claim. 6 The court’s review is limited to the
complaint and any documents attached to the motion to dismiss that are
central to the claim and referenced by the complaint.7

LAW AND ANALYSIS
 Defendant moves for dismissal of Plaintiff’s redhibition and breach of
contract claims.
A. Redhibition
Plaintiff alleges that the transformers that it purchased from Defendant
have redhibitory defects. Under Louisiana law, “when a defect in the thing sold
renders it absolutely useless, or so inconvenient in use that the law supposes
a buyer would not have purchased that item had he known of the vice, an action
lies in redhibition.”8 Defendant argues that Plaintiff’s redhibition claim should
be dismissed because it has prescribed. Louisiana Civil Code article 2534
provides, in relevant part, that:
B. The action for redhibition against a seller who knew, or is
presumed to have known, of the existence of a defect in the thing
sold prescribes in one year from the day the defect was discovered
by the buyer or ten years from the perfection of the contract of sale,
whichever occurs first.
C. In any case prescription on an action for redhibition is
interrupted when the seller accepts the thing for repairs and
commences anew from the day he tenders it back to the buyer or
notifies the buyer of his refusal or inability to make the required
repairs.
“Where the petition shows that the claim has prescribed, the buyer must prove
otherwise.”9
In its first consideration of this issue, this Court held that Plaintiff’s
redhibition claim was prescribed on the face of the Second Amended Complaint
where it stated that the SPRs have repeatedly tripped “since installation” in

The threshold question then is whe n Plaintiff knew or should have

Constructive knowledge has been defined by our courts as
whatever notice is enough to excite attention and put the injured
party on guard or call for inquiry. Such notice is tantamount to
knowledge or notice of everything to which a reasonable inquiry
might lead, and such information or knowledge as ought to
reasonably put the injured party on inquiry is sufficient to start
the running of prescription. In assessing whether an injured party
possessed constructive knowledge sufficient to commence the
running of prescription, this court’s ultimate consideration is the
reasonableness of the injured party ’s action or inaction in light of
the surrounding circumstances.11
“The prevailing wisdom is that prescription begins to run when the defect
manifests itself, not on the date the underlying cause of the defect is found.” 12

B. Breach of Contract of Express Warranty
Defendant next moves to dismiss Plaintiff’s breach of contract claim.
Plaintiff’s Third Amended Complaint alleges that Defendant breached its
express contractual obligations by failing to provide transformers that
performed in accordance with the contract and by failing to remedy the covered
defects that occurred during the warranty periods. As this Court previously
held, Plaintiff’s breach of express warranty claim is based on hidden defects
and therefore must be brought as an action for redhibition. “It is ‘fundamental
Louisiana law’ that actions based on breach of warranty against defects are to
be pursued in redhibition instead of as a breach of contract.”16 Accordingly,

C. Breach of Warranty of Fitness
Finally, Defendant moves to dismiss Plaintiff’s claim for breach of
warranty of fitness under Article 2524. Plaintiff argues that, in the event the
transformers are found not to have a redhibitory defect, the transformers were
not fit for their ordinary use and were not capable of performing the task for
which they were sold. Defendant argues that this claim should be dismissed
because (1) it is prescribed and (2) Article 2524 is available only when the thing
is free from redhibitory defects.
At the outset, t he parties disagree about the prescriptive period for a
claim under article 2524. In 2021, article 2534  was amended to provide that
“the action asserting that a thing is not fit for its ordinary or intended use
prescribe in two years from the day of delivery of the thing to the buyer or one
year from the day the defect or unfitness was discovered by the buyer,
whichever occurs first.” “Prior law provided no specific prescriptive period for
breach of the warranty of fitness for use. Consequently, the ten-year
prescription in Article 3499 prevailed.” 17 Plaintiff argues that the ten-year
period applies because the contract of sale was perfected in 2017 and the
transformers were delivered in 2019, prior to the 2021 amendment to Article
2534.
Under Louisiana law,

applied retroactively. However, when the claim has prescribed, the

When granting a Motion to Dismiss, the Court must consider whether
the plaintiff should be given leave to amend the complaint. Though “[l]eave to
amend should be freely given,” a court may decline to give such leave as long
at the denial is justified on grounds of “undue delay, bad faith or dilatory
motive on the part of the movant, repeated failure to cure deficiencies by
amendments previously allowed, undue prejudice to the opposing party by
virtue of allowance of the amendment, futility of amendment, etc.”19 The court
finds that Plaintiff has had multiple chances to amend its Complaint, and
further amendment would be futile.
