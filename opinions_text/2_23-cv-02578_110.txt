ORDER AND REASONS
 Before the Court are Defendant New Orleans Regional Transit Authority’s (“RTA”)
Motion to Declare RTA in Compliance with the Consent Decree1 and Plaintiff O.E.’s (“Plaintiff”)
Motion to Compel Production of Documents. 2 On February 28, 2025, the Court approved the
parties’ proposed Consent Decree.3 The specific provision at issue states, as follows:
Once the three 400 -series streetcars mentioned above are added into active
service—at which point there will be a total of seven ADA -accessible streetcars
available for service—the RTA will update its regular schedule to ensure that the
greatest number of ADA-accessible streetcars feasible are running at any given
time. During Peak Service, there will be at least five ADA -accessible streetcars in
active service on the line, subje ct to unanticipated repairs or maintenance- related
issues.4

Consent decrees are generally referred to as being “hybrids” of contracts and judicial
decrees. Consent decrees have elements of both contracts and judicial decrees.10 A consent decree
“embodies an agreement of the parties” and is also “an agreement that the parties desire and expect
will be reflected in, and be enforceable as, a judicial decree that is subject to the rules generally
applicable to other judgments and decrees.”11
Indeed, the significance of a consent decree’s hybrid status as contract and order is that the

As with any contract, the interpretation begins with looking to the “four corners” of the
decree.14 When a contract is expressed in unambiguous language, its terms will be given their plain
meaning, and enforced as written.15 Likewise, “[t]he entry of a consent decree necessarily implies

presumption that parties to a contract intend every clause to have some effect.”17
“Since a consent decree or order is to be construed for enforcement purposes basically as
a contract, reliance upon certain aids to construction is proper, as with any other contract. Such
aids include the circumstances surrounding the formation of the consent order, any technical
meaning words used may have had to the parties, and any other documents expressly incorporated
in the decree.”18

According to Plaintiff, this provision authorizes only Plaintiff to bring an enforcement motion. The
Court is not persuaded.
Plaintiff’s argument reads the provision in isolation and overlooks other provisions
governing enforcement and this Court’s continuing jurisdiction. The Consent Decree expressly
provides that “this Agreement is enforceable only by the Parties…”20 and “[t]he parties agree to
and shall request that this Court retain jurisdiction over this matter solely for the purposes of
enforcement of the terms and conditions of this Agreement.”21 RTA is a party to the Consent
Decree, and thus, has authority to enforce the Consent Decree. Nothing in the language identified
by Plaintiff expressly prohibits RTA from seeking a determination concerning its compliance with
the Consent Decree. For these reasons, the Court rejects Plaintiff’s argument.
Turning to the merits of RTA’s motion, the Consent Decree expressly contemplated that,
following completion of the modifications to the three 400- series streetcars, RTA would have a
total of seven ADA -accessible streetcars “available for service.” 22 With knowledge that seven
ADA-accessible streetcars would comprise RTA’s fleet, the parties further agreed that RTA would

 These provisions must be read together and in a manner that gives effect to each. The
Consent Decree does not require that RTA place all seven ADA -accessible streetcars into active
service during peak service hours. RTA’s motion explains that it currently maintains seven ADA-
accessible streetcars, with five of those streetcars operating during peak service hours. Of the
remaining two streetcars, one undergoes routine maintenance and the other is “RTA’s sole
operating spare.” Based on this explanation, RTA contends that it has shown that it operates the
highest feasible number of ADA-accessible streetca rs. However, RTA has submitted no
declarations, affidavits, or other competent evidence substantiating these representations.
Statements and arguments of counsel in briefing do not constitute evidence.
 The Consent Decree requires RTA to “ensure that the greatest number of ADA-accessible
streetcars feasible are running at any given time.” Accordingly, although RTA’s operation of five
ADA-accessible streetcars satisfies the Consent Decree’s express require ment that at least five
such streetcars operate during peak service, resolution of RTA’s motion requires the Court to
consider whether five constitutes the greatest number of ADA-accessible streetcars feasible under
RTA’s present operational circumstances.
 RTA’s representations concerning maintenance and the need to maintain an operating
spare, if supported by competent evidence, may demonstrate that its present allocation of seven
ADA-accessible streetcars is consistent with the Consent Decree. On the present record, however,
the Court lacks an evidentiary basis upon which to make that factual determination.

 As the party seeking a declaration that it has complied with the Consent Decree, RTA bears
the burden of establishing an adequate factual basis for the relief it requests. The Court cannot
make the factual findings necessary to declare RTA in compliance based solely on unsupported
representations contained in RTA’s briefing. Accordingly, on the record presently before it, the
Court cannot conclude that RTA has demonstrated compliance with the Consent Decree. RTA’s
motion is therefore denied.
B. Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel
The Consent Decree sets forth the following as it relates to document production:
The RTA shall provide Plaintiff, upon written request, access to all non-privileged
records, plans, or documents relevant to compliance with this Consent Decree. This
includes construction progress reports, procurement contracts, streetcar
maintenance records, and ADA accessibility assessments. The RTA shall  respond
to such requests within 14 days and may only withhold information if it
demonstrates that the material is subject to privilege or other legal protection.24

As it relates to the Motion to Compel, Plaintiff seeks discovery bearing upon RTA’s ability
to operate additional ADA-accessible streetcars, and consequently, whether RTA has satisfied its
obligations under the Consent Decree. On August 11, 2026, Plaintiff’s counsel made a written
request for production of documents to counsel for RTA. 25 In the Motion to Compel, Plaintiff
states that RTA refused to produce the requested documents. In opposition, RTA explains that it
did not ignore Plaintiff’s request, rather RTA asked that Plaintiff narrow the document request and
articulate how the requested materials were “relevant to compliance.”26 RTA argues that Plaintiff’s
motion should be denied because Plaintiff is not in “good faith” compliance with the Consent
Decree’s dispute resolution process, and Plaintiff does not have an absolute informational right to
