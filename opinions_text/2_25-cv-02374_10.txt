SUPPLEMENTAL REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

Plaintiff Charley James, a state inmate incarcerated at Rayburn Correctional Center, filed
this pro se and in forma pauperis  federal civil rights case pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983. James
sued A.R.D.C. Supervisor Carolyn Thomas and Mrs. Ellington, a “ social worker/records ”
employee.1  James takes issue with the February 2025 recalculation of his release date under Act
150.2 According to his complaint and attached exhibits, in approximately 2013, James was
sentenced for first degree robbery and his release date was calculated under Act 1099, which allows
for credit for good time.3 In February 2025, James’s master prison record was audited and, as he
has a prior conviction for armed robbery in 1986, his release date was recalculated under Act 150.4
In essence, James claims his sentence w as incorrectly calculated in February 2025. He seeks an
order from the Court directing that he be entitled to good time under Act 1099, which would result
in him being eligible for an earlier release date. James also seeks compensatory damages.5

On December 22, 2025, the undersigned issued a report and recommendation
recommending that James’s federal civil rights claims against defendants Carolyn Thomas and
Mrs. Ellington be dismissed pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1915, § 1915(e), § 1915A, and 42 U.S.C. §
1997e as frivolous and otherwise for failure to state a claim for which relief can be granted.6
Alternatively, the Court recommended that the claims against defendants in their official capacities
be dismissed without prejudice on the basis of Eleventh Amendment immunity and that the claims
be dismissed as Heck barred.7
On December 31, 2025, the Court received James’s untitled motion in which he sought
leave to amend his complaint.8 In that filing, James clarified that he is suing the defendants in both
their official and individual capacities  and seeks monetary damages. 9 On January 7, 2026, the
District Judge granted the motion and referred the matter to the undersigned for screening and a
report and recommendation to address James’s claims against defendants in their individual and
official capacities.10 For the reasons below and those previously explained in the original Report
and Recommendation, James’s claims against defendants in their official and individual capacities
should be dismissed as frivolous, failing to state a claim on which relief may be granted , seeking
monetary damages against defendants who are immune from such relief, and/or Heck barred.
Initially, to the extent that James challenges the duration of his imprisonment and seeks a
speedier release, as previously explained in the original Report and Recommendation,11 such relief
cannot be granted in a civil rights action brought pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983. A § 1983 civil
rights proceeding simply is not the appropriate means of pursuing release from incarceration .

For the same reasons and based upon the same caselaw citations in the original Report and
Recommendation,13 the Court finds that James is not entitled  to monetary damages against the
defendants in their official capacities  as they are state employees who (1) are not considered
“persons” subject to suit under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and (2) are entitled to Eleventh Amendment
immunity.
Finally, James’s individual -capacity claims against defendants are barred by Heck v.
Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477 (1994). Claims which are barred by Heck are legally frivolous. Kingery
v. Hale, 73 F. App’x 755 (5th Cir. 2003); Hamilton v. Lyons, 74 F.3d 99, 102 (5th Cir. 1996).Were
this Court to determine that James i s entitled to a declaratory judgment and monetary damages,
this would necessarily imply the invalidity of James’s sentence as calculated. Because James has

RECOMMENDATION
For all the reasons set out in the original and supplemental Reports and Recommendations,
it is again RECOMMENDED that Charley James’s federal civil rights claims against defendants
Carolyn Thomas and Mrs. Ellington be DISMISSED pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1915, § 1915(e), §
1915A, and 42 U.S.C. § 1997e as frivolous and otherwise for failure to state a claim for which relief
can be granted.
ALTERNATIVELY, it is RECOMMENDED that the claims against defendants Thomas
and Ellington in their individual capacities be DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE to their being
asserted until the Heck conditions are met.
ALTERNATIVELY, it is RECOMMENDED that the claims against defendants Thomas
and Ellington in their official capacities  be DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE on the basis
of Eleventh Amendment immunity.14
