I. BACKGROUND
 Plaintiff First Guaranty Bank (“First Guaranty”) filed a Petition for Declaratory Judgment,
Breach of Contract, Indemnity, Conversion and Other Relief in state court on April 29, 2026,
challenging Defendant First Bank & Trust Company’s (“First Bank”) actions in relation to a
Participation Agreement and Deposit Control Agreement between these two commercial banks
following the bankruptcy filing of the participation agreement’s underlying obligor, Carnaby FA,
LLC.  ECF No. 1–2.  After Defendant First Bank removed the case to this Court, it filed a motion
to dismiss on July 6, 2026.  ECF Nos. 1 & 10.

Although the Fifth Circuit has not addressed whether a party seeking to supplement after
the deadline for filing amending pleadings must satisfy Rule 16’s good cause standard,2 one judge
in this district has required the movant to establish good cause under Rule 16 before addressing
the requirements of Rule 15(a) or Rule 15(d).3  Rule 16’s “good cause standard requires the party
seeking relief to show that the deadlines cannot reasonably be met despite the diligence of the
party needing the extension.”4  The Fifth Circuit has established a four-part test to aid the court in
determining whether a movant has established good cause : (1) movant’s “ explanation for the
failure to timely move for leave to amend, (2) the importance of the amendment, (3) the potential
prejudice the other party would suffer if the amendment was allowed, and (4) the availability of a
continuance to cure that prejudice.”5
While Rule 15(a) and Rule 15(d) motions are distinct, “the Fifth Circuit has indicated that
the same factors that are considered under a Rule 15(a) analysis control a Rule 15(d) analysis. ”6
Notably, however, the text of Rule 15(a) provides that leave to amend should be freely granted,

but the text of Rule 15(d) does not .  Rather, it simply makes clear that the court may permit a
supplemental complaint “on just terms.”7  “Generally, leave to supplement should not be granted
where a plaintiff attempts to present new and different causes of action ” or alleges “subsequent
events that are not germane to the claims in the live pleadings.”8  Under Rule 15(a)(2), the “court
should freely give leave [to amend] when justice so requires.” 9  The five relevant considerations
for examination by the court in determining whether to grant leave to amend a complaint are: (1)
undue delay, (2) bad faith or dilatory motive, (3) repeated failure to cure deficiencies by previous
amendments, (4) undue prejudice to the opposing party, and (5) futility of the amendment.10  Given
Rule 15(a)’s “bias in favor of granting leave’” absent a “substantial reason,” the court’s discretion
“is not broad enough to permit denial.”11

In order to address the futility objection, this Court would necessarily address the issues
raised in the motion to dismiss now pending before Judge Vance.  The substantive issues raised
by Defendant’s Opposition are best addressed in the context of resolving the pending motion to
dismiss rather than in the procedural context of leave to file a supplemental pleading. 12  Further,
allowing amendment at this early stage gives Plaintiff the opportunity to “plead its best case”
before asking the Court to decide the substantive issue within the confines of the motions to
dismiss.13  This is “the preferred course” of action, even if doing so “renders moot the motion to
dismiss, rather than granting the motion to dismiss and rendering moot the motion for leave” as it
conserves both judicial and party resources to permit amendment and allows the ruling on the
motion to dismiss determine the facial validity of the claim.14
