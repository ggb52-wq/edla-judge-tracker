LAW AND ANALYSIS
 Under Rule 11(b)(1) of the Federal Rules of  Civil Procedure, an attorney’s signature
on a pleading certifies that to the best of the attorney’s knowledge, information, and belief,
the pleading “is not being presented for any im proper purpose, such as to harass, cause
unnecessary delay, or needlessly increase the cost of litigation[.]” Rule 11(c)(1) of the
Federal Rules of Civil Procedure empowers  a court, after notice and reasonable
opportunity to respond, to “impose an appropriate sanction on any attorney, law firm, or
party that violated [Rule 11(b)] or is responsible for the violation.”
 Rule 11(c)(2) provides a “safe harbor” prov ision, which allows a party that may be
in violation of Rule 11(b)(1) a twenty-one day period to cure its alleged violation before
the filing of any motion for sanctions.9 Rule 11(c)(2) provides:
Motion for Sanctions. A motion for sanctions must be made separately from
any other motion and must describe the specific conduct that allegedly
violates Rule 11(b). The motion must be served under Rule 5, but it must not
be filed or be presented to the court if the challenged paper, claim, defense,
contention, or denial is withdrawn or appropriately corrected within 21 days
after service or within another time the court sets. If warranted, the court
