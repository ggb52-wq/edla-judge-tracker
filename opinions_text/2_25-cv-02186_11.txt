LAW AND ANALYSIS
I. The Limitation of Liability Act
Congress enacted the Limitation of Liability Act of 1851 , 46 U.S.C. § 30501 et seq. ( the
“Act”), “to encourage ship -building and to induce capitalists to invest money in this branch of
industry.” Lewis v. Lewis & Clark Marine, Inc. , 531 U.S. 438, 446 (2001) (quoting Norwich &
N.Y Transp. Co. v. Wright , 80 U.S. 104, 121 (1872)). Under the Act, shipowners may bring an
action in federal court and limit liability to the value of the vessel and pending freight if the incident
occurred without the owner’s privity or knowledge. In re N&W Marine Towing, L.L.C., 31 F.4th

5

968, 970 (5th Cir. 2022)  (first citing 46 U.S.C. § 30511(a) ; then citing 46 U.S.C. § 30523; also
citing Magnolia Marine Transp. Co. v. Laplace Towing Corp., 964 F.2d 1571, 1575 (5th Cir.
1992) (summarizing Limitation Act); Odeco Oil & Gas Co., Drilling Div. v. Bonnette , 74 F.3d
671, 674 (5th Cir. 1996) (same)).
When a shipowner files a limitation action, the federal court stays related proceedings,
requires all claimants to assert their claims in the limitation proceeding, and preserves the owner’s
right to seek limitation  of liability in a federal forum. Magnolia Marine , 964 F.2d at 1575. The
court’s primary concern is protecting the owner’s right to limitation and ensuring that liability does
not exceed the limitation fund. Id. The Act defines “owner” to include certain charterers who man,
supply, and navigate a vessel at their own expense. 46 U.S.C. § 30501(2). Federal courts have
exclusive jurisdiction over actions brought under the Act. See, e.g., Texaco, Inc. v. Williams , 47
F.3d 765 (5th Cir. 1995).
II. The Savings to Suitors Clause
 The Limitation Act is tempered by the saving to suitors clause. Odeco Oil, 74 F.3d at 674
(citing 28 U.S.C. § 1333(1) (giving federal district courts exclusive jurisdiction over “[a]ny civil
case of admiralty or maritime jurisdiction,” but “saving to suitors in all cases all other remedies to
which they are otherwise entitled”)); N&W Marine Towing, 31 F.4th at  971 (citing In re Tetra
Applied Techs. L P, 362 F.3d 338, 340 (5th Cir. 2004) (noting although the Act grants shipowners
the exclusive right to seek limitation of liability in federal court, claimants generally retain a choice
of forum and remedies under  the saving to suitors clause )). The resulting tension between these
competing rights is “a recurring and inherent conflict.” Id.
The Fifth Circuit has
recognized “two instances in which a district court must allow a state
court action to proceed”:

(1) w
hen the total amount of the claims does not exceed the
shipowner’s declared value of the vessel and its freight, and
(2) when all claimants  stipulate that the federal court has
exclusive jurisdiction over the limitation proceeding, and
that the claimants will not seek to enforce a damage award
greater than the value of the ship and its freight until the
shipowner’s right to limitation has been determined by the
federal court.
Id. (quoting Odeco Oil, 74 F.3d at 674). Thus, when sufficient stipulations protect a shipowner’s
rights under the Act, claimants may proceed in state court. Tetra Applied Techs., 362 F.3d at 341
(citing Lewis, 531 U.S. at 454) (finding where a district court “satisfies itself that a vessel owner’s
right to seek limitation will be protected, the decision to dissolve the injunction is well within
the court’s discretion”).
The Fifth Circuit has additionally held that “a single claimant’s choice of forum is a
sufficient interest to warrant the dissolution of an injunction if the claimant files stipulations that
adequately protect the shipowner’s rights under the Act.” Inland Dredging v. Sanchez , 468 F.3d
864, 867 (5th Cir. 2006). In that circumstance, the rights of both the shipowner and claimant  are
protected. Id.
III. Adequacy of Claimant’s Stipulations
The Supreme Court has held that a shipowner’s limitation rights are “adequately protected
by stipulations that allow[] the federal proceeding to go forward after a determination on the merits
by a state court and with ultimate recovery limited, at a maximum, to the total value of the fund.”
Tetra Applied Techs., 362 F.3d at 343 (citing Lewis 531 U.S. at 453–54).
At a minimum, a claimant must stipulate that: (1) the admiralty court has exclusive
jurisdiction over all limitation issues; and (2) no judgment will be enforced in excess of the
limitation fund until the limitation proceeding is resolved. Texaco, 47 F.3d at 767.

all limitation issues. Claimant may therefore renew his motion to lift the stay, provided he  files
amended stipulations consistent with this ruling no later than November 30, 2026.
