D. Claims Under § 1983
Section 1983 creates a damages remedy for th e violation of federal constitutional or
statutory rights under color of state law:
Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom,
or usage, of any State . . . subjects, or causes to be subjected, any . . . person
within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or
immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party
injured in an action at law . . . .30

“The purpose of § 1983 is to deter state actors fro m using their badge of authority to deprive
individuals of federally guaranteed rights and to  provide relief to victims if such deterrence
fails.”31
Generally, a plaintiff must satisfy three elements to establish § 1983 liability:

(1) deprivation of a right secured by the U.S. Constitution or federal law;
(2) that occurred under color of state law; and
(3) was caused by a state actor.32

Because § 1983 merely provides a remedy for desi gnated rights, rather than creating any
substantive rights, “an underlying constitutional or statutory violation is a predicate to liability.”33
This requires the plaintiff to identify both the constitutional violation and the responsible person

minimis injuries only.”131  Thus, the “only one inquiry requir ed” to determine whether an officer
employed excessive force in violation of the Fourth Amendment is whether plaintiff has suffered
“some injury . . .  resulting from an officer’s unreasonably excessive force.”132
 Although “relatively insignifi cant injuries and purely psyc hological injuries” can be
cognizable depending on the circumstances,133 a plaintiff must show that he suffered at least some
injury.134  But Plaintiff does not allege any injury wh atsoever.  Indeed, Plaintiff does not allege
that Deputy Mayon pushed, shoved or  touched him at all.  Rather , he alleges only that Deputy
Mayon threatened to tase him, after which he went in to the cold water and was not given dry
clothes for over an hour.135  These allegations fail to amount to excessive force in violation of the
Fourth Amendment.136  This claim should be dismissed with prejudice for failure to state a claim.
