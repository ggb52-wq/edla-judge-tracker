ORDER AND REASONS
Before the Court is Plaintiffs  Erica Dandry Constanza and Monica Dandry Hallner’s
(collectively, “Plaintiffs”) Motion to Quash Trial Subpoenas.1 On September 29, 2026, a copy of
trial subpoenas, issued by Defendant Huntington Ingalls Incorporated (“Avondale”) to Plaintiffs,
was delivered to the office of Plaintiffs’ counsel.2 The subpoenas commanded Plaintiffs to appear
at the November 2, 2026 trial scheduled for this case to provide testimony, and produce all
settlement agreements and claim documents submitted to any settlement/bankruptcy trusts which
were executed by Plaintiffs in relation to this matter. 3 Plaintiffs move the Court to quash the trial
subpoenas.4 Avondale opposes the motion. 5 Having considered the motion, the memoranda in
support and opposition, the record, and the applicable law, the Court grants the motion.

 Turning to the procedural deficiencies in the instant subpoena, Plaintiffs assert  that their
counsel was served with a copy of the subpoena s, rather than service being perfected on
Plaintiffs.27 Avondale does not attempt to rebut this claim in its response.28 Rule 45(b)(1) lays out
the requirements for serving a subpoena as follows:
Any person who is at least 18 years old and not a party may serve a subpoena.
Serving a subpoena requires delivering a copy to the named person and, if the
subpoena requires that person's attendance, tendering the fees for 1 day's attendance
and the mileage allowed by law. Fees and mileage need not be tendered when the
subpoena issues on behalf of the United States or any of its officers or agencies.29

The Fifth Circuit has h eld that a “plaintiff ’s position is well taken[,]” when that plaintiff asserts
that service of a subpoena duces tecum served upon “plaintiff’s counsel, as opposed to the plaintiff
himself, renders such service a nullity.” 30 Therefore, the Court grants the motion, because
Avondale does not offer any reason why service upon Plaintiffs’ counsel should be effective here,
and the intended purpose of Avondale’s subpoena, compelling Plaintiffs to produce all settlement
documents to the Court in camera, can be achieved without the subpoena.
