Marine, LLC’s Motion to Dismiss for Failure to State a Claim (Doc. 39). For

BACKGROUND
 On June 3, 2025, Glenn Seals died in an accident on board the M/V
Polaris, owned by GulfMark Americas, Inc. (“Gulfmark”), while working as a
Jones Act seaman for Tidewater Marine, LLC (“Tidewater”). Shortly after,
Plaintiff Mysti Seals, the decedent’s wife, was appointed as the personal
representative of the decedent’s estate by a probate court in Alabama. Mysti
Seals then brought claims in this Court for Jones Act negligence and
unseaworthiness against Gulfmark and Tidewater arising out of her husband’s
death (“the Wrongful Death Action”). Subsequently, Gulfmark and Tidewater
filed a Complaint for Exoneration from or Limitation of Liability as owner and
owner pro hac vice of the M/V Polaris (“the Limitation Action”). The Court
entered a standard limitation injunction prohibiting prosecution of any actions

LEGAL STANDARD
To survive a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss, a plaintiff must plead
enough facts “to state a claim for relief that is plausible on its face.”6 A claim is
“plausible on its face” when the pleaded facts allow the court to “draw the
reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.” 7
A court must accept the complaint’s factual allegations as true and must “draw
all reasonable inferences in the plaintiff’s favor.”8 The court need not, however,
accept as true legal conclusions couched as factual allegations. 9 To be legally
sufficient, a complaint must establish more than a “sheer possibility ” that the
plaintiff’s claims are true.10 If it is apparent from the face of the complaint that
an insurmountable bar to relief exists and the plaintiff is not entitled to relief,

LAW AND ANALYSIS
 Petitioners correctly point out the Death on the High Seas Act
(“DOHSA”) requires that an action for wrongful death that occurs “on the high
seas beyond 3 nautical miles from the shore of the United States” be brought
by “the personal representative of the decedent.” 13 In addition, the Jones Act
authorizes a cause of action for the wrongful death of a seaman to be brought
by “the personal representative of such seaman.”14 Accordingly, “beneficiaries,
including the decedent’s children, are not real parties in interest and may not
independently prosecute such a claim.”15 “The Supreme Court has held that a
‘personal representative’ is the court-approved executor or administrator of the
decedent’s estate.”16 It therefore cannot be seriously contested that Mysti Seals
is the proper party to bring the DOHSA and Jones Act wrongful death and
survival claims on behalf of the decedent Glenn Seals.
That said, “this limitation does not extinguish a beneficiary’s underlying
interest; rather, it channels prosecution of the claim through a single

improperly included in a claim against Petitioners and do not transform
Decedent’s Children into real parties in interest to bring a wrongful death
claim against Petitioners. If Decedent’s Children contend that the personal
representative does not adequately represent their interests in the Wrongful
Death Action, they should instead file a motion to intervene in that action.
Because no such motion has been filed, the Court declines to address whether
Decedent’s Children have adequately alleged a conflict of interest.

CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, the Motion is GRANTED. The Answer and
Claims filed by Glenn Seals Jr. and G.S., a minor represented by Marzella
Wesley, against Petitioners GulfMark Americas, Inc. and Tidewater Marine,
LLC are DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE. This dismissal is without
prejudice to the rights of Glenn Seals Jr. or G.S. to seek intervention under
Rule 24.
