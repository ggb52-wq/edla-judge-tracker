RDER AND REASONS
Before the Court is a Motion to Modify Scheduling Order filed by the Government.  R.
Doc. 33.  The Government asks the Court to reopen the parties’ dispositive motion deadline
because the Government recently learned of information it argues is dipositive of the claims of
Plaintiff, Emily Williams-Toennies, who opposes this motion.  R. Doc. 35.  A scheduling order
“may be modified only for good cause and with the judge’s consent.”  FED. R. CIV. P. 16(b)
(4).  In determining whether the movant has met its burden, the court considers:  (1) the
movant’s explanation for failing to meet the deadline; (2) the importance of the requested relief;
(3) the potential prejudice in granting the relief sought; and (4) the availability of a continuance to
cure such prejudice.  S&W Enters., LLC v. SouthTrust Bank of Ala., NA, 315 F.3d 533, 536 (5th
Cir. 2003) (internal citation omitted).  Disedare v. Brumfield, No. 22-CV-2680, 2024 WL 1514195
(E.D. La. Apr. 8, 2024) (applying this standard to a motion to reopen the dispositive motion
deadline).
Despite Plaintiff’s insistence to the contrary, the Court finds the Government has met this
burden.  The information forming the basis for the Government’s proposed motion was
disclosed for the first time in the parties’ April 24, 2024 pretrial order.  Although Plaintiff appears
to contest whether this information is dispositive of the case, the Court finds the motion will, at a
minimum, assist the Court, as the trier of fact in this case, to narrow the issues in this matter.
