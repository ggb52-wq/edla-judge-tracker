I. BACKGROUND

On August 22, 2023, Plaintiff visited Marrero Healthcare Center to inquire about Mr.
Sherrod becoming a resident at the facility. R. Doc. 133 -4 at 22–26, 33. Plaintiff spoke with two
facility representatives, including Nexion Health at Marrero employee Brandy Bush. R. Doc. 133-
4 at 24–25. Plaintiff avers that Ms. Bush told her that the facility was “very well staffed.” R. Doc.
133 at 15. Plaintiff maintains this representation was false at the time it was made.

II. PRESENT MOTION

Nexion Management moves for summary judgment of the art. 1953 fraud claim against it.
R. Doc. 113. Nexion Management argues that privity of contract is required to sustain an art. 1953
claim, and because it is not a party to the admission agreement with Plaintiff, the claim against it
should be dismissed. R. Doc. 133-1 at 1.
Plaintiff’s response is two -fold. First, Plaintiff maintains that privity of contract is not
required for a party to be held liable for fraud under art. 1953. R. Doc. 137 at 2. Second, assuming
privity is a requirement, Plaintiff maintains  that record evidence creates a genuine dispute as to
whether a principal-agent relationship existed between Defendants binding Nexion Management
to the admission agreement. Id. at 14.

III. LEGAL STANDARD
Summary judgment is proper when “there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and
the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a). The court must view
the evidence in the light most favorable to the nonmovant. Coleman v. Hous. Indep. Sch. Dist .,
113 F.3d 528, 533 (5th Cir. 1997). Initially, the movant bears the burden of presenting the basis
for the motion; that is, the absence of a genuine issue as to any material fact or facts. Celotex Corp.
v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 323 (1986). The burden then shifts to the nonmovant to come forward
with specific material  facts showing there is a genuine dispute for trial. Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(c);
Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp ., 475 U.S. 574, 586 –87 (1986). A fact is
“material” if its resolution in favor of one party may affect the outcome of the case. Saketkoo v.
Adm’rs of Tulane Educ. Fund, 31 F.4th 990, 997 (5th Cir. 2022). “A dispute about a material fact
is ‘genuine’ if the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving
party.” Bodenheimer v. PPG Indus., Inc., 5 F.3d 955, 956 (5th Cir. 1993).
IV. DISCUSSION
Nexion Management maintains that it cannot be held liable  under art. 1953 because it is
not a  party to Mr. Sherrod ’s admission agreement . The Court agrees with D efendant’s initial
premise that privity is required to recover under art. 1953. This conclusion is grounded in La. Civ.
Code Art. 1958, the statutory mechanism for recovering damages for art. 1953 claims:

The party against whom rescission is granted because of fraud is liable for damages and
attorney fees.

This article, by its text , authorizes damages only against parties in contract : the “party
against whom rescission is granted.” The defrauded party may recover against a third party who
has committed fraud, but their action lies in tort through art. 2315, not contract. See art. 1958

cmt. c (“If fraud is committed by third person, that person is also liable for damages . C.C. Art.
2315.”).  Thus, absent a contractual relationship between Nexion Management and Plain tiff,
recovery against Nexion Management for art. 1953 fraud is impermissible.
This privity requirement may be satisfied, however,  through a mandatary relationship
between Nexion Management and Nexion Health at Marrero. Under Louisiana law, “[a] mandate
is a contract by which a person, the principal, confers authority on another person, the mandatary,
to transact one or more affairs for the principal.” La. C iv. Code art. 2989. “Under this provision a
principal, whether disclosed or undisclosed, is bound to perform the contracts that the mandatary,
acting within the limits of his authority, made with third persons. A third person who contracts
with the mandatary has a cause of action directly against the principal, whether disclosed or
undisclosed.” La. Civ. Code art. 3020, cmt. b.; see also Resolution Tr. Corp. v. Gasper-Virgillio,
27 F.3d 178, 180 (5th Cir. 1994)  (“The Louisiana Supreme Court held in  Woodlawn Park Ltd. v.
Doster Constr. Co., 623 So.2d 645, 648 (La. 1993), that if the agent enters into a contract for the
benefit of an undisclosed principal, the principal may be held liable under the contract.”).  The
defining characteristic of a m andatary relationship “is the special relationship of confidence or
trust imposed by one in another who undertakes to act primarily for the benefit of the principal in
a particular endeavor.” Scheffler v. Adams & Reese, LLP, 2006-1774, p. 7 (La. 2/22/07), 950 So.2d
641, 648.
The record creates a genuine dispute as to whether Nexion Health at Marrero was Nexion
Management’s agent.1 Evidence indicates that Nexion Management was involved in the
admissions process, directing how Nexion Health at Marrero conducted it. Nexion Management’s
corporate representative testified that Nexion Management wrote the Nursing Facility Admission
