ORDER AND REASONS
On September 12, 2025, Plaintiff Summer Sears (“Plaintiff”) filed a complaint1
for violation of civil rights (42 U.S.C. §  1983), wrongful death, and related state law
claims against several defendants, including Dr. Charles E. Eckert (“Eckert”). Eckert
filed a motion2 to dismiss for failure to state a claim. Plaintiff, who is proceeding pro
se, did not file an opposition to th is motion. On February 20, 2026, Plaintiff moved 3
for leave to file a first amended complaint. No defendants filed an opposition, and this
Court granted4 Plaintiff’s motion . On March 16, 2026, Plaintiff  filed her first
amended complaint,5 which no longer names Eckert as a defendant.
Eckert then filed a motion6 to dismiss because he is no longer a party to the
lawsuit. Eckert brings this motion “ out of an abundance of  caution to the extent
Plaintiff contends that any claims remain pending or are otherwise being  asserted

against him.”7 Alternatively, Eckert contends that any remaining claims against him

Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15, “[a]n amended complaint supersedes
the original complaint and renders it of no legal effect unless the amended complaint
specifically refers to and adopts or incorporates by reference the earlier pleading.”
Stewart v. City of Hou. Police Dep’t, 372 Fed. App’x 475, 478 (5th Cir. 2010) (quoting
King v. Dogan, 31 F.3d 344, 346 (5th Cir. 1994)).
Here, Plaintiff’s amended complaint9 does not refer to, adopt, or incorporate
her original c omplaint. Additionally, the a mended complaint omits Eckert as a
defendant entirely.
Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that Eckert’s motion10 to dismiss is DENIED AS MOOT.
Based on Plaintiff’s amended complaint, 11 Dr. Charles E. Eckert is dismissed from
the lawsuit, and no claims remain pending against him.
