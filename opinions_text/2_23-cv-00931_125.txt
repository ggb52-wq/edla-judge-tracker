II.  Standard of Review
    Under Texas law, a prevailing party may recover reasonable attorney ’s fees for a valid
claim based on an oral or written contract. T EX. CIV. PRAC. & REM. CODE ANN. § 38.001(b). To
recover attorney’s fees under this chapter, the claimant must establish that: (1) it was represented
by an attorney; (2) it presented the claim to the opposing party or a duly authorized agent; and (3)
payment for the just amount owed was not tendered within thirty days after presentment. TEX. CIV.
PRAC. & REM. CODE ANN. § 38.002. The statute also provides that usual and customary attorney
fees are presumed reasonable, that presumption is rebuttable. TEX. CIV. PRAC. & REM. CODE ANN.
§ 38.003.
     Fee-shifting under Chapter 38 is designed to compensate a prevailing party for losses
incurred through the litigation process —not to provide a windfall or to enhance counsel’s
economic position. Rohrmoos Venture v. UTSW DVA Healthcare, LLP, 578 S.W.3d 469, 487 (Tex.
2019). Accordingly, only those fees that are both reasonable and necessary for legal representation
may be recovered, and the amount agreed to between a client and its attorney does not, standing
alone, establish that the requested fee is reasonable or necessary. Id. at 488. The burden remains
on the fee claimant to prove both elements. Id. at 489.

3

Both elements are questions of fact to be determined by the fact finder and act as limits on the
amount of fees that a prevailing party can shift to the non-prevailing party. See Transcon. Ins. Co.
v. Crump, 330 S.W.3d 211, 231 (Tex. 2010) (observing that generally the reasonableness of
particular fees presents a fact question that the fact finder must decide, as does necessity); see also
Bocquet v. Herring, 972 S.W.2d 19, 21 (Tex. 1998) (explaining that reasonableness is a question
of fact for the jury, and that “[t]he second limitation, that fees must be necessary, is likewise a fact
question” (citing Gen. Motors Corp. v. Bloyed, 916 S.W.2d 949, 961 (Tex. 1996))).
This calculation produces an objective figure that approximates the fee that the attorney would
have received had he or she properly billed a paying client by the hour in a similar case. Id. When
supported by sufficient evidence, there is a presumption that the base lodestar calculation reflects
the reasonable and necessary attorney’s fees that can be shifted to the non-prevailing party. El
Apple I, Ltd. v. Olivas, 70 S.W.3d 757, 760 (Tex. 2012).
III. Analysis
1. Entitlement to A Fee Award
The first issue is whether the Plaintiffs are a prevailing party to be entitled to a fee award.
Enforceable judgments on the merits confer prevailing-party status.  See Buckhannon Bd. and Care
Home, Inc. v. W. Va. Dept. of Health, 532 U.S. 598, 121 S. Ct. 1835 (2001).  The Court therefore
finds that the Plaintiffs are a prevailing party because this Court granted judgment in favor of
BlueTeam Roofing, LLC and Sendero Restoration Services, LLC in this breach of contract
litigation.
2. Reasonable Attorneys Fees
 Sendero contends that it is entitled to recover attorneys’ fees for 453.95 billed hours, which
totals $149,535.00. R. Doc. 114. Sendero contends that although the judgment was entered in favor

of both Plaintiffs, the contract claim belonged to it alone. Sendero contends that it prevailed on its
breach of contract claim and recovered damages entitling it to recover reasonable attorneys’ fees
under Chapter 38.
The Defendants contend that Sendero’s billing reflects a consistent pattern of partner -
associate duplication which results in the inflation of the fee request. R. Doc. 116. The Defendants
further contend that Sendero’s counsel engaged in block billing that obscured the reasonableness
of the time claim.  Id.  Additionally, the Defendants contend that Sendero’s counsel is seeking fees
for administrative tasks and therefore the fees sought are not reasonable and should be adjusted

The Supreme Court has indicated that the “lodestar” calculation is the “most useful starting
point” for determining the award of attorney’s fees. Hensley v. Eckerhart,  461 U.S. 424, 433
(1983).  The lodestar equals “the number of hours reasonably expended on the litigation multiplied
by a reasonable hourly rate.” Id. The lodestar is presumed to yield a reasonable fee. La. Power &
Light Co. v. Kellstrom, 50 F.3d 319, 324 (5th Cir. 1995).
After determining the lodestar, the Court must then consider the applicability and weight
of the twelve factors set forth in Johnson v. Ga. Highway Express, Inc., 488 F.2d 714, 717-19 (5th
Cir. 1974). The Court can make upward or downward adjustments to the lodestar figure if the
Johnson factors warrant such modifications.  See Watkins v. Fordice, 7 F.3d 453 (5th Cir. 1993).
However, the lodestar should be modified only in exceptional cases. Id.
After the calculation of the lodestar, the burden then shifts to the party opposing the fee to
contest the reasonableness of the hourly rate requested or the reasonableness of the hours expended
“by affidavit or brief with sufficient specificity to give fee applicants notice” of the objections.
Rode v. Dellarciprete, 892 F.2d 1177, 1183 (3d Cir. 1990).

A. Reasonable Hourly Rate
Sendero seeks to recover fees for its attorneys John Philip Graf, A rmistead Long, Samuel
E. Masur, Elizabeth McGovern, C Ponds, and B. Dupree. R. Doc. 114- 4. In support of the
application, Sendero submitted the affidavit of Mr. Graf only. Id. However, the affidavit
acknowledges that it is seeking to recover the work performed by not only Graf ($275.00-$400.00
per hour ), but also Long ($395.00- $475.00), Masur ($ 495.00-$525.00), McGovern ($ 215.00-
$250.00), Ponds ($100.00), Dupree  ($260.00).  The application is devoid of any information
regarding the background training and experience of any of the lawyers. It provides minimal
information regarding Mr. Graf, namely that he is licensed in Louisiana, is trial counsel and a
member of Gordon, Arata, Montgomery, Barnett, McCollam, Duplantis and Eagan, LLC.
The Defendants primarily challenge the sufficiency of the information and whether the
Court can make a meaningful evaluation of the fee application. R. Doc. 116 at 3.  According to the
opposition, Defendants contend that the fee request fails to reflect the disciplined billing judgment
required under law.  Id.  Further, the Defendants contend that rather than limiting their request to
hours reasonably expended on necessary litigation tasks, Sendero’s attorneys’  billing records
reveal systemic inefficiencies and non -compensable charges that materially inflate the requested
award. Id.
Attorney's fees must be calculated at the “prevailing market rates in the relevant community
for similar services by attorneys of reasonably comparable skills, experience, and reputation.”
Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 895, 104 S. Ct. 1541 (1984). The applicant bears the burden of
producing satisfactory evidence that the requested rate is aligned with prevailing market rates. See
NAACP v. City of Evergreen, 812 F.2d 1332, 1338 (11th Cir. 1987). Satisfactory evidence of the

6

reasonableness of the rate necessarily includes an affidavit of the attorney performing the work
and information of rates actually billed and paid in similar lawsuits. Blum , 465 U.S. at 896 n. 11.
However, mere testimony that a given fee is reasonable is not satisfactory evidence of a market
rate. See Hensley, 461 U.S. at 439 n. 15.
Rates may be determined through direct or opinion evidence as to what local attorneys
charge under similar circumstances. The weight to be given to the opinion evidence is affected by
the detail contained in the testimony on matters such as similarity of skill, reputation, experience,
similarity of case and client, and breadth of the sample of which the expert has knowledge. Norman
v. Housing Auth. of Montgomery, 836 F.2d 1292 (11th Cir. 1988); see also  White v. Imperial
Adjustment Corp., No. 99-03804, 2005 WL 1578810, at *8 (E.D. La. Jun. 28, 2005) (recognizing
that attorneys customarily charge their highest rates only for trial work, and lower rates should be
charged for routine work requiring less extraordinary skill and experience).
Evidence of reasonableness of the proposed hourly rate must include an affidavit of the
attorney performing the work in addition to information about rates actually billed and paid in
similar lawsuits. Blum, 465 U.S. at 896 n.11; Hensley, 461 U.S. at 433  (noting that “[w]here the
documentation of hours is inadequate, the district court may reduce the award accordingly”); s ee
also Jordan v. Dep't of Justice, 691 F.2d 514, 518 (D.C. Cir.1982) (“holding that complete denial
of fees should be reserved for only extreme situations, such as when the petitioner offers no
affidavits or timesheets, or when the application is filed in bad faith”) cited in Baker v. D.C. Pub.
Schs., 815 F. Supp. 2d 102, 109 (D.D.C. 2011).
The fee application in this case does not provide the C ourt with satisfactory evidence by
affidavit of the attorneys performing the work and information of the reasonableness of their rates
billed and paid in similar lawsuits. The only affidavit submitted is by Mr. Graf and he identifies as

The duty to exercise billing judgment “refers to the usual practice of law firms in writing

reduce the hours awarded by a percentage intended to substitute for the exercise of billing

2.  Duplicate entries
The Defendants contend that Sendero’s fee request should be reduced or excluded because
of inefficient Partner-Associate overstaffing.  R. Doc. 116.  The Defendants cites to five dates that
contained alleged overbilling, namely: March 8, 2023, May 18, 2023, July 10- 12, 2023, March-
April 2024 and November-December 2024. Id.
When a party chooses two attorneys to represent it in a lawsuit, the opposing party is not
required to pay for duplicative work incurred by the attorneys. Riley v. City of Jackson , 99 F.3d
757, 760 (5th Cir. 1996).  “If more than one attorney is involved, the possibility of duplication of
effort along with the proper utilization of time should be scrutinized. The time of two or three
lawyers in a courtroom or conference when one would do, may obviously be discounted.”  Walker,
99 F.3d at 768.
Since the Court has excluded time for Long whose entries were duplicative during the
proposed time periods, no further reduction for duplicative time is necessary.

4. Clerical and Administrative Tasks
Now the Defendants contend that Sendero  spent time on clerical or administrative tasks
that are not compensable at attorney rates because such tasks do not constitute legal work.  R. Doc.
118. The Defendants also contend that Texas  courts require specific evidence showing the
qualifications of the staff member performing the work, the nature of the work, their rate, and the
time expended.  Id.    The Defendants contend that the Plaintiffs’ billing records include five entries
showing precisely that this type of work is not compensable.  Id.
It is well-established that “clerical entries . . . such as filing court documents, calendaring
events, processing billing invoices, and requesting copies of documents” are “‘not recoverable in
an award of attorneys' fees.’” Wyndham Props. II, Ltd. V. Buca Teas Rests., LP, 2023 WL 2392090,
at *5 (quoting Black v. SettlePou, P.C., No. 3:10-cv-1418-K, 2014 WL 3534991, at *6 (N.D. Tex.
July 17, 2014); citing Tovar v. Sw. Bell Tel., L.P., No. 3:20-cv-1455-B, 2022 WL 2306926 (N.D.
Tex. Jun. 27, 2022)); see also Vela v. City of Hous., 276 F.3d 659, 681 (5th Cir. 2001) (“Paralegal
work can only be recovered as attorney's fees if the work is legal rather than clerical.” (citing Allen
v. U.S. Steel Corp., 665 F.2d 689, 697 (5th Cir. 1982))); Tovar , 2022 WL 2306926, at *5 (“The
court should consider whether the work performed was legal work in the strict sense or was merely
clerical work that happened to be performed by a lawyer.” (cleaned up; quoting Abrams v. Baylor
Coll. of Med., 805 F.2d 528, 536 (5th Cir. 1986)).

C.   Nontaxable Expenses

of mileage and lodging actually incurred for travel between Lafayette and New Orleans for the
summary judgment hearing, the pretrial and settlement conference.  It also contains an expense for

Rule 54(d)(2) provides, in pertinent part  that c laims for attorneys' fees and related
nontaxable expenses shall be made by motion unless the substantive law governing the action
provides for the recovery of such fees as an element of damages to be proved at trial.   United
Industries, Inc. v. Simon–Hartley, Ltd., 91 F.3d 762, 766 (5th Cir.1996).  Taxable costs, those set
out in 28 U.S.C. § 1920, and nontaxable expenses. Vanderbilt Mortg. & Fin., Inc. v. Flores, Civil
Action No. C–09–312, 2011 WL 2160928, at *7 (S. D. Tex. May 27, 2011).
Under Rule 54(d)(2), related nontaxable expenses are to be considered in conjunction with
attorney's fees and awarded in the same manner.
