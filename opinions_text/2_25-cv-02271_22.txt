I.  Introduction
A.  Factual Summary
This case was filed by the State and the United States  on behalf of the United States
Environmental Protection Agency (“EPA”) and through the State  of Louisiana through the
Louisiana Department of Environmental Quality (L.D.E.Q.)  alleging Clean Water Act violations.
R. Doc. 1. On August 22, 2025, an explosion and fire occurred at Defendant’s lubricant oil
manufacturing facility, and it engulfed above-ground storage tanks and other areas of the Facility
that stored oil and chemical products. Id. The fire burned for approximately two weeks until it was
fully extinguished. Id. As a result of this catastrophic event, oil and other pollutants flowed from
the Facility into adjacent water bodies and the Tangipahoa River. Id.
Once in the river, the pollutants  allegedly traveled approximately 47 miles downstream.
EPA and L.D.E.Q., along with other agencies, responded to the incident to reduce risks to public

II.  Standard of Review
Under Rule 24(a), the Court must permit anyone to intervene on timely motion if they have
an unconditional right to intervene by a federal statute or if they claim an interest relating to the
property or transaction at issue and the movant is so situated that disposing of the action woul d
impair or impede the movant's ability to protect its interest, unless existing parties adequately
Case 2:25-cv-02271-CJB-KWR     Document 22     Filed 09/14/26     Page 3 of 11

represent that interest. FED. R. CIV. PRO. 24(a)(2). The movant in a motion to intervene  bears the
burden of establishing their right to intervene, but federal jurisprudence provides that Rule 24 is to
be liberally construed with any doubt resolved in favor of the intervenor. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.
v. Tex. Alcoholic Beverage, Comm’n, 834 F.3d 562 (5th Cir. 2016).
Where the movant does not have an unconditional right to intervene under a federal statute,
the movant must satisfy a four -part test to intervene as of right: (1) the motion to intervene must
be timely, (2) the movant must have an interest relating to the property or transaction which is the
subject of the action, (3) the movant must be so situated that the disposition of the action may, as
a practical matter, impair or impede their ability to protect that interest, and (4) the movant’s interest
must be inadequately represented by the existing parties to the suit. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc., 834 F.3d
at 565.
Additionally, Fifth Circuit jurisprudence has established four factors that the Court must
consider when evaluating the timeliness of a motion to intervene:
“(1) the length of time during which the would- be intervenor actually knew or reasonably
should have known of its interest in the case before it petitioned for leave to intervene; (2)
the extent of the prejudice that the existing parties to the litigation may suffer as a result of
the would-be intervenor's failure to apply for intervention as soon as it knew or reasonably
should have known of its interest in the case; (3) the extent of the prejudice that the  would
be intervenor may suffer if intervention is denied; and (4) the existence of unusual
circumstances militating either for or against a determination that the application is timely.”

Sierra Club v. Espy, 18 F.3d 1202, 1205 (5th Cir. 1994).

B.  Louisiana Scenic Rivers Act, La. R.S. 56:1847 claim

LEAN and NSRW also seek to assert a claim pursuant to Louisiana Scenic Rivers Act, La.
R.S. 56:1847 and 56:1849. R. D oc. 11-3. LEAN and NSRW seek to allege that the Tangipahoa
River is a designated Scenic R iver from the Mississippi state line to Interstate 12. Id.  LEAN and
NSRW seek to allege that SSI was not permitted to engage in activities that result in ecological
degradation without a permit from the LDWF and that SSI had no such permit to discharge.
 SSI contends LEAN and NSRW seek to intervene to expand the s cope of the litigation to
include the Louisiana Scenic Rivers Act, La. R.S. 56:1847 and 56: 1849 alleging that SSI was
required to obtain a permit from the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries. R. D oc. 12.
SSI points out that this claim arises under a different Louisiana statute which  is enforced by a
different state agency, LDWF, and invokes a wholly distinct regulatory regime.
Rule 24(a) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provides that on timely motion, the court
must permit anyone to intervene who (1) is given an unconditional right to intervene  by a federal
statute; or (2) as matter of right if it: (1) timely files an application, (2) has an interest in the subject
matter of the action, (3) shows that the disposition of the action may impair or impede the protection
of that interest, and (4) shows that the existing parties to the action will not adequately represent
that interest. Heaton v. Monogram Credit Card Bank of Ga., 297 F.3d 416, 422 (5th Cir. 2002)
(quoting United States v. Franklin Parish Sch. Bd., 47 F.3d 755, 756 (5th Cir. 1995)). The applicant
bears the burden of establishing an entitlement to intervene and must satisfy all of the above
requirements. Edwards, 78 F.3d at 999.

The burden of establishing inadequate representation is on the applicant for intervention.
See Hopwood v. Texas, 21 F.3d 603, 605 (5th Cir.1994); Espy, 18 F.3d at 1207. The Supreme Court
of the United States has decided that “[this] requirement . . . is satisfied if the applicant shows that
representation of his interest ‘may be’ inadequate; and the burden of making that showing should
be treated as minimal.” Trbovich, 404 U.S. at 538 n.10.
Although the applicant's burden of showing inadequate representation is minimal, “it cannot
be treated as so minimal as to write the requirement completely out of the rule.” Cajun Elec. Power
Coop. v. Gulf States Utilities, Inc., 940 F.2d 117, 120 (5th Cir. 1991) (quoting Bush v. Viterna, 740
F.2d 350, 355 (5th Cir.1984)). Accordingly, our jurisprudence has created two presumptions of
adequate representation. First, when the putative representative is a governmental body or officer
charged by law with representing the interests of the abs entee, a presumption of adequate
representation arises whether the would -be intervenor is a citizen or subdivision of the
governmental entity. Texas v. U.S., 805 F.3d 653 (5th Cir. 2015).
 To overcome this presumption, the applicant must show “that its interest is in fact different
from that of the [governmental entity] and that the interest will not be represented by [it].”
Hopwood, 21 F.3d at 605. This presumption, and the heightened showing required to overcome it,
is restricted, however, to those suits involving matters of sovereign interest. Id.  The State of
Louisiana through the Louisiana Department of Environmental Quality in the present action is
appearing as a sovereign and therefore the presumption of adequate representation arises.
The second presumption of adequate representation arises when the would- be intervenor
has the same ultimate objective as a party to the lawsuit. In such cases, the applicant for intervention
must show adversity of interest, collusion, or nonfeasance on the  part of the existing party to
overcome the presumption. Franklin Parish Sch. Bd., 47 F.3d at  757 (citing Kneeland v. Nat’l
Collegiate Athletic Ass’n, 806 F.2d 1285, 1288 (5th Cir. 1987)).

Factors (1) and (2) are not contested in this matter. The proposed intervention seeks a
judgment  against SSI and to award the Louisiana Department of Environmental Quality its unpaid
response costs and civil penalties not exceeding $32,500 for each day of violation.  Further, LEAN
and NSRW seek as an additional a ward for the intentional discharge or  disposal, a penalty of not
more than $1,000,000 pursuant to La. R.S. 30:2025(E). R. Doc. 11- 3. Further, the proposed
intervention seeks an award of their costs. Id. Additionally, they seek an order requiring SSI to take
appropriate action to prevent spills from its facility into waters of the United States in compliance
with Facility Response Plan and SPCC regulations and the Clean Water Act. See id. ¶E, p. 7.
The proposed intervention seeks to allege that SSI continued to discharge contaminated
water to the Tangipahoa River in violation of the STED -G permit, LAG420176. Id.  at p. 5.
Intervenors have gathered months of photographic and video evidence showing persistent oil
sheens, discolored water, sludge accumulation, and noxious odors along the discharge pathway. Id.
These observations were made during a period of extremely dry conditions, with less than one inch
of rainfall during February 2026 recorded at Hammond Airport, indicating that the petroleum
sheens are not the result of stormwater runoff nor mobilization of historic contamination. Id.
Defendant is discharging untreated or inadequately treated water in violation of its permits. Id.
The proposed intervenors also seek to allege that Defendant has obtained four “containment
ponds” in which it stores contaminated water from its clean -up operations and storm water. Id.
Defendant discharges directly from these ponds into the ditches that take the water to the
Tangipahoa River. Id. This is not a permitted outfall in either of Defendant’s permits and is therefore
prohibited and in violation of the law. Id.
The proposed complaint also seeks to allege that pursuant to La. R.S. § 56:1847(4), the
Tangipahoa River is a designated Scenic River from the Mississippi state line to Interstate 12.  Id.
The Scenic Rivers Act prohibits anyone from conducting activities with the potential for significant

ecological degradation unless they have first obtained a permit from the Louisiana Department of
Wildlife and Fisheries (LDWF). Id. According to the proposed intervention, the intervenors seek
to allege that SSI has not applied for nor obtained a permit to discharge into the  Tangipahoa from
the LDWF, in violation of La. R.S. § 56:1849.
Although this statutory provision is not identified in the complaint, the S tate of Louisiana
seeks to hold SSI to account for the results of their unauthorized discharge, specifically that the
waters of the state were not free from floating debris, scum, oil, or other matter that formed
nuisances and negatively impacted the aesthetics of the waters of the state. R. D oc. 1. The State
alleges that SSI’s unauthorized discharge injured, was toxic to, or produced demonstrated adverse
physiological responses in animals, fish, shellfish, wildlife, or plants  in violation of other state
statutes. Id. The State also alleges that the discharge forced the closure of a portion of the
Tangipahoa River to boating on or about August 24, 2025, and for some time thereafter. Id. The
State alleges that b oating, which is a designated secondary contact usage of this water body, was
hindered by the presence of free or floating oil. Id.
While the statutory reference is different, the claim sought by the S tate is to address the
illegal discharge of oil by SSI. There is no evidence by either LEAN or NSRW that the state
representation is or would be inadequate nor is there evidence that their interests would not be
protected. Therefore, the Court finds that LEAN and NSRW have failed to establish either
entitlement to intervene pursuant to Fed. Rule Civ. Pro. 24(a) or 24(b).
