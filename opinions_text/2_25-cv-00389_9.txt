REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

On February 25, 2025, plaintiff Orlando Lamar Williams filed this pro se civil
rights complaint pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983.1
As Williams’s complaint had deficiencies that required curing before it could
survive the screening process, on May 7, 2025, he was ordered to file an amended and
superseding complaint by June 9, 2025. 2 On June 27, 2025, when Williams did not
respond, the Court issued an order for Williams to show cause by July 17, 2025, why
his complaint should not be dismissed for failure to prosecute by his failure  comply
with the May 7, 2025 order and file an amended  complaint.3 The Court warned
Williams that “FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH THIS ORDER WILL RESULT IN
A RECOMMENDATION THAT THIS CASE BE DISMISSED. ”4 The envelope
mailed to  Williams at his prison address of record has not been returned as
undeliverable.

To date, Williams h as not responded to the order  or submitted an amended
complaint. Williams’s failure to comply with court orders clearly reflects a failure on
his own part to prosecute.
In the light of the foregoing, it is appropriate to dismiss Williams’s complaint
for want of prosecution. The authority of a federal trial court to dismiss a plaintiff’s
action because of failure to prosecute is clear. Link v. Wabash R.R., 370 U.S. 626
(1962); McCullough v. Lynaugh, 835 F.2d 1126 (5th Cir. 1988). The Federal Rules
of Civil Procedure specifically provide that a court may, in its discretion, dismiss a
plaintiff’s action for failure to prosecute or for failure to comply with the Federal
Rules of Civil Procedure or any order of the court. Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(b). The Court’s
power to dismiss for want of prosecution should be used sparingly, although it may
be exercised sua sponte whenever necessary to achieve the orderly and expeditious
disposition of cases. Ramsay v. Bailey, 531 F.2d 706, 707 (5th Cir. 1976).
Because Williams is proceeding pro se, the Court need consider only his
conduct in determining whether dismissal is proper under Rule 41(b). As noted,
Williams was twice instructed to file an amended complaint  to correct the
deficiencies in his original complaint . To date, he has not done so.  For these
reasons, the Court is unable to advance Williams ’s claims on the docket.
Accordingly, his complaint should be dismissed for failure to prosecute.

RECOMMENDATION

It is therefore RECOMMENDED that plaintiff’s complaint be DISMISSED
WITHOUT PREJUDICE for failure to prosecute pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(b).
A party’s failure to file written objections to the proposed findings,
conclusions, and recommendation in a magistrate judge’s report and
recommendation within fourteen (14) days after being served with a copy shall bar
that party, except upon grounds of plain error, from attacking  on appeal the
unobjected-to proposed factual findings and legal conclusions accepted by the
district court, provided that the  party has been served with notice  that such
consequences will result from a failure to object. 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1); Douglass
v. United Services Auto. Ass’n, 79 F.3d 1415, 1430 (5th Cir. 1996) (en banc).
