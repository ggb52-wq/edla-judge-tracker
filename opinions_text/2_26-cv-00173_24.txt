I. Law & Analysis
Ordinarily, an amended complaint supersedes the original complaint and
renders the earlier pleading inoperative unless the amended complaint specifically
refers to and adopts or incorporates the earlier pleading by reference.7 Shirah’s
amended complaint fails to do this. Instead, Shirah only provides a brief discussion
of the two incidents underlying his claims. He fails to state any claim for relief or to
refer to his prior claims and allegations. 8 Accordingly, Shirah fails to state a claim
upon which relief may be granted.
Nevertheless, because Shirah proceeds pro se and in forma pauperis, the Court
will liberally construe his amended complaint  as supplementing rather than
supplanting his original complaint.9 The Court therefore considers whether Shirah’s
additional allegations cure the deficiencies identified by the Court. 10 Even with this
favorable construction, they do not.

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2), a court “shall” dismiss a case sua sponte at any
time if the Court determines that the action is frivolous or fails to state a claim on
which relief may be granted.11 “A claim is frivolous where it lacks an arguable basis
either in law or in fact. ”12 In addition, a complaint fails to state a claim upon which
relief may be granted when it does not contain “sufficient factual matter, accepted as
true, to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.” 13 “A claim has facial
plausibility when the plaintiff pleads factual content that allows the court to draw
the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.”14
Shirah’s amended complaint has two components. He first adds factual
allegations relating to his October 11, 2025. arrest. He alleges that he lacked the
intent to permanently deprive the owner of the property because he believed the
device was a dead cell phone, took it home to charge it, and intended to return it to
its owner.15 Those allegations do not cure the previously identified deficiency because
Shirah’s claim remains barred by Heck v. Humphrey .16 Shirah was convicted of
misdemeanor theft, and he acknowledges that conviction has not been overturned. 17
Because Shirah’s challenge to the probable cause supporting his arrest would
necessarily call the validity of that conviction into question, the claim is not
cognizable under Heck.18

Shirah alternatively asks the Court to stay his claims pending favorable
termination of his state criminal proceedings pursuant to Wallace v. Kato.19 A stay is
unwarranted. In Wallace, the Supreme Court explained a district court may stay a
§ 1983 action when a plaintiff files suit before the related criminal proceeding has
resulted in a conviction.20 Where the plaintiff is ultimately convicted and the §  1983
action would impugn that conviction, however, “ Heck will require dismissal.”21 That
situation applies here, as Shirah’s conviction remains  solidly in place. 22 The Fifth
Circuit has rejected staying a Heck -barred action while the plaintiff pursues post -
conviction relief,  and, indeed, Heck itself involved a § 1983 action filed while the
plaintiff’s direct appeal remained pending.  23 Because Shirah’s conviction has not
been overturned, dismissal—not a stay—is appropriate.

Nor does Shirah’s claim that he lacked the intent to permanently deprive the
owner plausibly establish an absence of probable cause. Probable cause exists when,
under the totality of circumstances, the facts known to the officer warrant a
reasonable belief that the suspect had committed or was committing an offense. 27
Here, Shirah fails to allege facts plausibly showing that Mayon lacked probable cause
to arrest him.28 Instead, he relies on his own assertion that he intended eventually to
return the property and that he lacked the specific intent to permanently deprive the
owner of the property.29 This assertion bears on Shirah’s ultimate culpability, not
whether Mayon knew or had reason to know of Shirah’s alleged innocent intent.
Therefore, it does not plausibly allege that the arrest lacked probable cause.30
Accordingly, Shirah’s amended allegations concerning October 11, 2025, do not
cure the deficiencies previously identified. His claims remain barred by Heck and,
independently, they fail to plausibly allege a malicious prosecution claim.

II. Conclusion
For the foregoing reasons, Shirah’s amended complaint fails to cure the
deficiencies previously identified and, even liberally construed as a supplemental
complaint, fails to state a claim upon which relief may be granted.
Accordingly, Shirah’s request to stay his claims arising from the October 11,
2025, incident is DENIED. His claims arising from that incident are DISMISSED
WITH PREJUDICE to being asserted again until the Heck conditions are met.40
Shirah’s false-arrest claim against Mayon from the January 18, 2025, incident
is DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE for failure to state a claim under 28 U.S.C. §
1915(e)(2). To the extent Shirah attempts to reassert his previously dismissed
excessive-force claim against Mayon arising from that incident, that claim remains
DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE. Shirah’s bystander-liability and single-decision-
to-rehire claims against Falcon are likewise DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE
pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2) for failure to state a claim. To the extent Shirah
