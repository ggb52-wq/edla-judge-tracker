FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATION

 Plaintiff Antoine Edwards filed a pro se civil complaint suit pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983
challenging the conditions of his confinement. On April 10, 2024, I denied Edwards’s application
to proceed in forma pauperis due to the three strikes provision of 28 U.S.C. § 1915(g). ECF No.
7. Edwards then filed a motion for reconsideration , which was denied. ECF No. 9. Edwards
appealed the denial to the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals. ECF Nos. 11, 13. While the appeal was
pending, Edwards filed a motion for legal redress and a motion for judgment on the merits and
pleadings. ECF Nos. 23, 24.
On October 11, 2024, the United States District Judge issued an order staying the matter
pending resolution of the appeal. ECF No. 25. The Court noted the case would be restored to the
docket upon the motion of any party following the appellate court’s resolution of the appeals filed.
Id. The Fifth Circuit dismissed Edwards’s appeal for want of jurisdiction and denied his motion to
proceed in forma pauperis  as moot. ECF No. 26 at 2. The Fifth Circuit noted the order denying
Edwards’s in forma pauperis motion “is not appealable as a final judgment because it did not end
the litigation on the merits” as a magistrate judge’s order “is not a final, appealable order over
which this court has jurisdiction.” Id. After the appellate court’s resolution of the appeal, Edward’s
filed a motion to remove the stay. ECF No. 27. The District Judge issued an order removing the
stay and referring the matter to the undersigned Magistrate Judge for final disposition. ECF No.
29.

I. FACTUAL BACKGROUND
Edwards is a prisoner housed in the Louisiana State Penitentiary in Angola, Louisiana.
ECF No. 1, at 8.  He filed a civil rights complaint against Senior United States District Judge Carl
J. Barbier of the Eastern District of Louisiana (“EDLA”), United St ates Magistrate Judge Jani s
van Meerveld of the EDLA, then-Chief Fifth Circuit Court Judge Priscilla Richman (Owen), Fifth
Circuit Judge Jennifer W. Elrod, and Fifth Circuit Deputy Clerk Melissa Shanklin .  Id., ¶4-7, at
3-4.  Under a broad reading of his c omplaint, Edwards alleges that the defendants have engaged
in misconduct by interfering with his efforts to overturn his state court conviction by denying relief
in his federal habeas corpus proceedings in the EDLA and via the denial of relief in the judicial
complaint process at the Fifth Circuit.
Edwards is a prolific filer 1  who, while incarcerated, has filed three prior civil rights
complaints or appeals in the federal courts that were dismissed as frivolous and/or for failure to
state a claim for which relief could be granted before he sought to proceed as a pauper in this case.2
II. LAW AND ANALYSIS
The passage of the Prison Litigation Reform Act (“PLRA”) was effected, in part, “to deter
frivolous prisoner litigation in courts ‘by making all prisoners seeking to bring lawsuits or appeals
feel the deterrent effect created by liability for filing fees.’”3 The goals of 28 U.S.C. § 1915 were
modified by the PLRA to include curbing abuse of the privilege to proceed without prepayment of
fees.  “Because in forma pauperis status is a privilege, it follows that the privilege may be revoked

when the goals of section 1915 are not being furthered.”4  For example, § 1915(g) provides that a
prisoner shall not be allowed to bring a civil action as a pauper pursuant to § 1915 if he has, on
three or more prior occasions, while incarcerated or detained in any facility, brought an action or
appeal in a court of the United States that was dismissed as frivolous, malicious, or for failure to
state a claim for which relief can be granted, unless the prisoner is under imminent danger of
serious physical injury.
Because Edwards has accumulated three “strikes” under the PLRA and §  1915(g), he is
prohibited from proceeding as a pauper in this civil action unless he fits within the “imminent
danger” exception of § 1915(g).  To meet the imminent danger requirement, the threat must be
“real and proximate” and based on fact, not conjecture.5  Allegations of past harm do not suffice.
Instead, the harm must be imminent or occurring at the time that the complaint is filed. 6  The
exception in the statute refers to “a genuine emergency” in which “time is pressing.”7  Further, an
inmate who claims the benefit of this exception must also show that the alleged danger faced rises
to the level of exposure to a “serious physical injury.” 8  The mere possibility of serious injury at
some indefinite point in the future does not constitute the type of emergency envisioned in the

Edwards complains about his continued detention as a result of a conviction from which
he has been unable to gain relief in both state and federal post-conviction proceedings.  Edwards
has not alleged nor does his complaint demonstrate that he is in the type of imminent danger of
serious physical injury at the level required by the PLRA.  As his claims do not involve and do
not allege physical harm or is imminent danger  at the time of filing,  Edwards is prohibited from
proceeding as a pauper.
The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals denied Edwards’s appeal to proceed as a pauper , and
he did not submit the $405.00 filing fee to the clerk of court’s office. Thus, Edwards’s § 1983
claims are not entitled to proceed  and should be dismissed without prejudice .  Even if Edwards
were allowed to proceed as a pauper or paid the filing fee, he is not entitled to relief under § 1983
as all of the named defendants are immune from suit. Edwards filed suit against Senior United
States District Judge Carl J. Barbier , United States Magistrate Judge Jani s van Meerveld, former
Chief Fifth Circuit Court Judge Priscilla Richman (Owen), Fifth Circuit Judge Jennifer W. Elrod,
and Fifth Circuit Deputy Clerk Melissa Shanklin. ECF No. 1, ¶4-7, at 3-4. Judges enjoy absolute
judicial immunity from suits for damages for all judicial acts except those undertaken with a “clear
absence of all jurisdiction.” 11 Similarly, absolute immunity extends to non -judicial officers who

III. RECOMMENDATION
For the foregoing reasons,
It is RECOMMENDED that plaintiff Antoine Edwards’s 42 U.S.C. § 1983 claims be
DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE as barred by the three -strikes provision of 28 U.S.C.
§ 1915(g) without payment of the $405.00 filing fee to the clerk of court’s office  and for seeking
relief against immune defendants.
It is FURTHER RECOMMENDED that Edwards’s Motion for Legal Redress (ECF No.
23) and Motion for Judgment on the Merit and Pleading (ECF No. 24)  be DENIED .
