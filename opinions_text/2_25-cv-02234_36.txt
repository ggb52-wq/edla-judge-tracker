Plaintiff Timothy Volter (“Plaintiff” or “Volter”) is a former employee of Defendant James
Marine Hahnville, LLC (“James Marine”). R. Doc. 1. Plaintiff alleges that he was employed as a

Transportation”). Id. Plaintiff contends that the NICK BUFORD underwent repairs at James
Marine’s facility. Id.

II. Standard of Review
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 35 governs physical and mental examinations of a party
during discovery. According to FED. R. CIV. P. 35(a)(1), the court “may order a party whose mental
or physical condition . . . is in controversy to submit to a physical or mental examination by a
Case 2:25-cv-02234-EEF-KWR     Document 36     Filed 11/06/26     Page 4 of 10
5

suitably licensed or certified examiner.” The court may order a party to submit for a physical
examination only upon motion demonstrating good cause. FED. R. CIV. P. 35(a)(2)(A). If an order
is issued, the court must give notice to all parties and the person to be examined, and the order
“must specify the time, place, manner, conditions, and scope of the examination, as well as the
person or persons who will perform it.” FED. R. CIV. P. 35(a)(2)(B).
Rule 35 “should be liberally construed in favor of discovery.” Crumedy v. XYZ Ins. Co. ,
No. 22-4570, 2023 WL 6293867, at *2 (E.D. La. Sept. 27, 2023) (citation omitted). It is within the
district court’s sound discretion whether to order an independent medical examination. Glaze v.
Bud's Boat Rental, Inc. , No. 93 -1334, 1993 WL 441890, at *1 (E.D. La. Oct. 21,
1993) (citing Coca–Cola Bottling v. Torres, 255 F.2d 149 (5th Cir. 1958)), aff'd, 68 F.3d 472 (5th
Cir. 1995).
The Court uses a two-part test to determine whether a Rule 35(a) motion will be granted.
First, the moving party must show that the physical or mental state of the party to be examined is
in controversy. Schlagenhauf v. Holder , 379 U.S. 104, 106 (1964). A “plaintiff in a negligence
action who asserts mental or physical injury places that mental or physical injury clearly in
controversy and provides the defendant with good cause for an examination to determine the
existence and extent of such asserted injury.” Id. at 119.
Second, the moving party must demonstrate good cause. “Good cause” requires a showing
of specific facts that demonstrate the need for the information sought and an inability to obtain it
elsewhere. Id. at 118. Rule 35 does not limit the number of examinations so long as “good cause”
is shown for each exam. Sadler v. Acker, 263 F.R.D. 333, 336 (M.D. La. Nov. 2, 2009) (as cited
in Campbell v. Helmerich & Payne, Inc., 2025 WL 1891681, at *4 (M.D. La. June 20, 2025)); see

FED. R. CIV. P. 35. Good cause generally exists in every case where the plaintiff is claiming

III. Analysis
Defendants contend that there is good cause to order an IME of the Plaintiff because this
is the first Rule 35 IME that Defendants have requested in the instant litigation. Defendants
contend that the two physical examinations conducted were ordered by a non-party, AEU, whose
interests are adverse to Defendants’ in this litigation because AEU has claimed a lien over
Plaintiff’s recovery. Additionally, Defendants find the previous examinations insufficient because
they were conducted for workers’ compensation purposes, not for litigation. Therefore, Defendants
contend that the examining physicians, Drs. Lurie and Harrison, did not consider all information
relevant to defending against Plaintiff’s claims in this litigation, including Plaintiff’s past injuries
and pain medication use, as well as Dr. Haddad’s new recommendation for total knee replacement
surgery.
Plaintiffs conversely characterize the previous two workers’ compensation physical
examinations as having been requested by Defendants. Plaintiffs characterize Defendants’ current
Rule 35 request as the potential third examination in this litigation. Plaintiff therefore contends
that Defendants must make a heightened showing of good cause to be entitled to an additional
examination. Plaintiff further argues that Defendants cannot ma ke such a showing, and that
Defendants should be required to use information which already exists from the previous two
examinations.
First, it is undisputed and clear to the Court that the conditions of Plaintiff’s knee and
shoulder are “in controversy” because his complaint centers on those injuries and Defendants
dispute the causation of such injuries. Thus, Plaintiff’s physical condition is in controversy.

As to the second element of good cause, the Court reiterates that Rule 35 does not explicitly
limit the number of examinations a party may be required to undergo. Jackson v. Entergy
Operations, Inc., No. 97-943, 1998 WL 28272, at *2 (E.D. La. Jan. 26, 1998) (citation omitted).
Where a party requests authorization for more than one IME, each request “must turn on its own
facts, and the number of authorized examinations depends solely upon the circumstances
underlying the request.” Crumedy, 2023 WL 6293867, at *3 (citing Jackson, 1998 WL 28272, at
*2 (citation omitted)). “When the moving party has already obtained an IME, courts require a
stronger showing of necessity, and the party seeking the examination always bears the burden of
showing good cause to order additional examinations.” Id. (citing Case v. JFP Offshore, Inc., No.
95-3239, 1996 WL 210720, at *1 (E.D. La. Apr. 29, 1996)) (emphasis added).
Here, according to the record, Defendants have not obtained an IME in the instant
litigation.1 Plaintiff has undergone two physical examinations , but they were conducted at the
request of James Marine’s insurer, AEU, for purposes of workers’ compensation. No evidence
suggests that either Defendants James Marine or James Transportation had any input into AEU’s
choice of examiner or the scope of the examination. 2 Thus, the Court finds no reason to believe
that Defendants have already obtained two IMEs in the instant litigation.
Further, this Court and other courts in the Fifth Circuit “consistently reject the notion that
a workers’ compensation carrier’s pre -suit examination precludes an IME by the defendant in a
later suit.” Id. at *5 n.32.3 Here, one worker’s compensation examination was conducted pre -suit

Finally, although the ability to obtain information from other sources “ is pertinent to the
inquiry [of good cause], a plaintiff may not avoid a Rule 35 examination simply on the grounds
that other sources of information, such as medical reports and statements by plaintiff’s treating
physicians, are available.” Crumedy, 2023 WL 6293867, at *3 (citing Ornelas v. Southern Tire -

avoid a Rule 35 IME by arguing that Defendants can instead rely on records from his previous
examinations, depositions, and their own expert witnesses. The Court does not find this argument
persuasive given that Defendants have not requested a single Rule 35 IME thus far in this litigation
and because the previous two physical examinations were conducted for purposes of workers’
compensation and therefore do not provide Defendants with enough information to build their
defense against Plaintiff’s claims here.
When considering all the above, the Court finds that the Defendants have stated good cause
for a Rule 35 IME by an orthopedic surgeon of their own choosing.
