ORDER AND REASONS

Pending before me is  the motion of defendant REC Marine Logistics, LLC  (“REC
Marine”) to compel discovery.  ECF No. 28.  Plaintiff Robert N. Jones filed an untimely opposition
memorandum.  ECF No. 29.  REC Marine filed a reply memorandum.  ECF No. 31.  No party
requested oral argument in accordance with Local Rule 78.1, and the Court agrees that oral
argument is unnecessary.   Having considered the record, the  submissions and arguments of
counsel, and the applicable law, defendant’s motion to compel discovery is GRANTED for the
reasons stated below.
I. BACKGROUND
Plaintiff Robert N. Jones filed suit against REC Marine (and others) for personal injuries
sustained aboard the M/V Gol Warrior, asserting claims of Jones Act negligence, general maritime
negligence, unseaworthiness, and maintenance and cure.  ECF No. 1 at 2-5, ¶¶ 8–23.  Jones seeks
$10 million dollars in exemplary and actual damages for pecuniary losses, mental anguish, pain,
disfigurement, and medical expenses, along with interest, costs, and all other relief to which he
may be entitled.  Id. at 8–9.  The Court issued a scheduling order setting trial for December 14,
2026, with a discovery deadline of October 2, 2026.  ECF No. 18, at 2, 4.
REC Marine issued discovery on June 17, 2026, and later filed this motion to compel.  ECF
Nos. 28-1, at 2; 28- 3, at 1.  It contends that Jones improperly refers to a mass of documents in

A. Scope of Discovery
Rule 26 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure lays  out the broad scope of discovery.

Information need not be admissible into evidence to be discoverable.1  Rather, information merely
needs to be proportional and relevant to any claim or defense. 2  “Mutual knowledge of all the
relevant facts gathered by both parties is essential to proper litigation.  To that end, either party
may compel the other to disgorge whatever facts he has in his possession.”3
Parties must respond to interrogatories and requests for production within 30 days of
service, absent court order or stipulation.4  “Each interrogatory must, to the extent it is not objected
to, be answered separately and fully in writing under oath.” 5  A nd all objections must be stated
with specificity. 6  “Discovery by interrogatory requires candor in responding. . . . The candor
required is a candid statement of the information sought or of the fact that objection is made to
furnishing the information.”7  Although a party responding to interrogatories is not required to
make an extensive investigation in responding to an interrogatory, it must review all sources of
responsive information reasonably available and provide the relevant facts.8
For each request for production, the respondent must state its objection with specificity or
produce copies of the documents or electronically stored  information.9  A party responding to
requests for production must produce responsive documents within that party’s possession,
custody or control.10  A party has “control” over documents or materials that it has the legal right
to obtain even though it has no copy and even if the documents are owned or possessed by a

B. Rule 37 Sanctions
Rule 37 requires the imposition of reasonable expenses , including attorney’s fees,
when: (1) a motion to compel is granted  or (2) the requested disclosure or discovery is produced
after the motion was filed. 14 There are only three exceptions to the imposition of expenses. First,
“the movant filed the motion before attempting in good faith to obtain the disclosure or discovery
without court action.”15 Second, “the opposing party’s nondisclosure, response, or objection was
substantially justified.”16 Or, third, “other circumstances make an award of expenses unjust.”17
The purpose of discovery sanctions are to secure compliance with the rules of discovery,
deter others from violating them, and punish those who do violate them. 18  The presence of good
faith (or lack thereof) is relevant to the sanctions analysis.19  While a finding of bad faith or willful

III. ANALYSIS
Initially, Jones’ opposition does not assert any specific objections to REC Marine’s
discovery requests.  Instead, he argued that the motion should be denied as moot because he
supplemented (or will supplement) his answers to fulfill his obligations.  But the late delivery of
discovery responses after the filing of the Motion the Compel does not necessarily render the
instant motion moot ,21 as Rule 37(a)(5)(A) explicitly addresses the post -filing delivery of
discovery responses.
Further, as of the date of its reply, REC Marine reports that Jones still has not fulfilled his
discovery obligations. For interrogatories 2 and 15 and requests for production 17 and 22, Jones
promised to supplement his answer, but failed to do so.  And for interrogatories 10, 12–13, and 16,
Jones failed to provide a full response. The  untimely and piecemeal supplementations waste the
parties’ and the C ourt’s time and resources.  Jones ’s fundamental failure to comply with basic
discovery obligations and requirements under the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, including the
still delinquent responses, demonstrates that he has no substantial justification for the delay nor
are there other circumstances that make the award unjust.  REC Marine’s request for fees and costs

III. CONCLUSION
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS ORDERED that REC Marine’s motion to compel is GRANTED.  Plaintiff must
provide supplemental written responses and produce any responsive documents within 21 days of
today’s date.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that REC Marine ’s request for reasonable expenses,
including attorney’s fees, is GRANTED.  The parties are directed to confer as to a reasonable
amount of fees and costs  for the preparation and filing of this motion.  If the parties agree, then
plaintiff Robert N. Jones  shall pay that amount within 30 days  of today’s date.  If the parties do
not agree, then REC Marine must, no later than October 6, 2026, file a m otion for fees and costs
pursuant to FED. R. CIV. P. 37(a)(5), which must attach supporting documentation for the requested
amount ( e.g., (a) an affidavit attesting to the attorney’s education, background, skills and
experience; (b) sufficient evidence of rates charged in similar cases by other local attorneys with
similar experience, skill, and reputation; and (c) a verified, contemporaneous report reflecting the
date, time involved, and nature of the services performed, as required by Local Rule 54.2).
