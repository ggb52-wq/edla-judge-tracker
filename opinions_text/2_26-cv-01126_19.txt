II. Legal Standard & Analysis
Federal courts have broad discretion when deciding whether to set aside an
entry of default,  as  defaults  are  extreme  sanctions  and  courts generally  prefer to
resolve disputes on the merits. 12 Rule 55(c) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure
provides that a court “may set aside an entry of default for good cause, and it may
set aside a default judgment under Rule 60(b).” 13 To determine whether good cause
has been shown by a party, a court should consider, nonexclusively, (1) whether
the default was willful; (2) whether setting it aside would prejudice the adversary;
and (3) whether a meritorious defense is presented.14
