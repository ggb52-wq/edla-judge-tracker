STANDARD OF REVIEW
 A district court has jurisdiction to hear appeals from final judgments of bankruptcy
courts.27 Rule 8002 of the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure requires that
bankruptcy appeals to district courts shall be taken in the same manner as appeals from
district courts to courts of appeals.28 “Under 28 U.S.C. § 1334(b), a bankruptcy court has
jurisdiction over ‘all civil proceedings related to bankruptcy cases. A proceeding relates to
a bankruptcy case if the outcome of that proceeding could conceivably have any effect on
the debtor's estate.’”29
 “The standard of review for a bankruptcy appeal is the same standard used by an
appellate court reviewing a district court proceeding. ”30 For conclusions of law, the

 Review of a bankruptcy court’s finding of a mixed question of law and fact is more
complicated.33 It is clear that courts determining ownership of immovable property under
Louisiana state law apply both factual and legal analyses. As a result, a determination of
the ownership of immovable property must be treated as a mixed question of fact and
law.34 La. C.C.P. article 3654 further demonstrates the mixed nature of ownership
determinations.35 Under this article, when the issue of ownership of immovable property
or of a real right therein is presented in an action for a declaratory judgment, or in a
concursus, expropriation, or similar proceeding, the court must render judgment in favor
of the party who proves better title to the immovable property or real right therein.  To
make this determination, th e court must weigh factual evidence of possession and title
documents against legal standards —including whether possession was in good faith,
whether just title existed, and whether the requisite prescriptive period was met —before
rendering a legal judgment on ownership.

alike” and the standard of review for a mixed question depends on “whether answering it

Acquisitive prescription requires possession of ten years, good faith, just title, and a thing
susceptible of acquisition by prescription. 86 As cited by the bankruptcy court, the

establish possession under Louisiana law. Without possession, the Coupels cannot prevail
on any claim under Article 3475 for ownership of the Disputed Property by acquisitive
prescription. The bankruptcy court did not err in its findings of law.

or inadequate, the trial was unfair, or prejudicial error was committed. 89 A motion for a
new trial must “clearly establish either a manifest error of law or fact or must present
newly discovered evidence. These motions cannot be used to raise arguments which
could, and should, have been made before the judgment issued.”90 Moreover, “[a] motion
for a new trial pursuant to Rule 59 is an extraordinary remedy that should be used
sparingly.”91 “A motion for a new trial is left to the sound discretion of the trial court.”92
 In their motion for a new trial, the Co upels did not present newly -discovered
evidence, but instead provided older pleadings relevant to their litigation of the Disputed
Property.93 To prevail, then, the Coupels must clearly establish a manifest error of law or
fact. In their motion  for a new trial , the Coupel s argue that the bankruptcy court made
such an error, first, when it stated that the Coupels could not have “reasonably believed”
they owned a road connected to the Disputed Property and, second, when the bankruptcy
court adopted the Taracena Survey.94
 Neither contention fits the criteria for granting a new trial motion , and the Court
finds the bankruptcy court did not abuse its discretion in denying the new trial motion.
