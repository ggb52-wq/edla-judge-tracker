ORDER AND REASONS
 Before this Court is a Report and Recommendation from the Magistrate Judge1
on a Motion to Dismiss for Failure to State a Claim by defendants.2 Plaintiff Gregory
Jynes opposed the motion,3 a nd he  filed an Objection 4 to the Report and
Recommendation. After de novo review of the parties’ memoranda, the record, the
Report and Recommendation, and applicable law, the Court hereby adopts the Report
and Recommendation and overrules Jynes’s objections in part.
I. Legal Standard

According to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 72 , a district court judge “must
determine de novo any part of the magistrate judge’ s disposition that has been
properly objected to .”5 The district judge “may accept, reject, or modify the
recommended disposition; receive further evidence; or return the matter to the
magistrate judge with instructions.”6

II. Legal Analysis
First, t he Magistrate Judge recommended that the d efendants’ motion to
dismiss be denied without prejudice to defendants’ ability to reassert the exhaustion
claim in a properly supported motion for summary judgment. 7 No party objected to
this recommendation. As a matter of judicial efficiency and because of this lack of
objection, the Court adopts sections III.A and III.B of the Report and
Recommendation.8
Second, in connection with the Court’s frivolousness review, the M agistrate
Judge recommended two separate dismissals: (1) the dismissal with prejudice of
Jynes’s civil rights claims for  compensatory relief relating to his First Amendment
right to free exercise of religion  against Jones, Giovingo, Williams, Stevenson, and
Ahmad, individually, and Sheriff Hutson, officially ; and (2) the dismissal with
prejudice of Jynes’s claims for declaratory and injunctive relief relating to his civil
rights claim for failure to protect  on January 30, 2025, against Giovingo, Duncan,
Brown, Lewis, and Ahmad, individually. Jynes has no written objection to the latter
dismissal—his civil rights claims for declaratory and injunctive relief . Th e Court
adopts the Magistrate Judge’s reasoning in regard to that issue.9
Jynes does object to the dismissal of his First Amendment claims for
compensatory and nominal or punitive damages. He raises two arguments in support
of his claims for compensatory damages in relation to the alleged violation of his First

The Report and Recommendation correctly concluded that the Prison
Litigation Reform Act , 42 U.S.C. § 1997 e(e), precludes monetary relief for  First
Amendment claims whe re, as here,  there is a lack of physical injury. 13 The Court
finds Jynes’s arguments in response unpersuasive. The Fifth Circuit  has squarely
held that the P LRA bars recovery of compensatory damages by prisoners alleging a
constitutional violation  absent physical injury , including First Amendment
violations.14 Jynes does not allege that he suffered any physical injury as a result of
defendants preventing him from practicing his religion. Jynes’ s assertion that the
physical injury requirement is satisfied by the alleged harm suffered by his failure-
to-protect claim is too attenuated of a conclusion. Indeed, Jynes cites no case law in
which the alleged physical harm was analog ously attenuated to a First Amendment
claim. That he later allegedly suffered physical injury at the hands of fellow inmates
when he was moved to a different housing section “at least part[ly]” to minimize his
