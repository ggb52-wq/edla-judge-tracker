II.  Standard of Law
The good cause requirements of Rule 16(b) govern a party’s motion for leave to
amend or supplement after the deadline set forth in the scheduling order.  Rule 16(b)
provides that a scheduling order “may be modified only for good cause and with the
judge’s consent.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(b)(4). “The good cause standard requires the ‘party
seeking relief to show that the deadlines cannot reasonably be met despite the
diligence of the party needing the extension.’” S&W Enterprises, L.L.C. v. SouthTrust
Bank of Alabama, NA, 315 F.3d 533, 535 (5th Cir. 2003) (citation omitted).

The Fifth Circuit has established a four -part test to aid the trial court in
exercising its discretion when determining whether a movant has established good
cause. See Lampkin v. UBS Fin. Servs., Inc., 925 F.3d 727, 741 (5th Cir. 2019). The
trial court considers: “ (1) the explanation for the failure to timely move for leave to
amend, (2) the importance of the amendment, (3) the potential prejudice the other
party would suffer if the a mendment was allowed, and (4) the availability of a
continuance to cure that prejudice.” Id.  (citing S&W Enters., 315 F.3d at 536). Not
only must a party establish good cause under Rule 16(b), but it also must satisfy the
additional requirements of Rule 15(a) or Rule 15(d).
Rule 15(a) applies to amended pleadings —i.e., when the facts underlying the
new or modified claim occurred before the filing of the original pleading. 6A  Charles
A. Wright & Arthur R. Miller, Fed. Prac. & Proc. § 1504 (3d ed. 2024).  Under Rule
15(a), a party may amend its pleading once as a matter of course within 21 days or
service or, “if the pleading is one to which a responsive pleading is required, 21 days
after service of a responsive pleading or 21 days after service of a motion under Rule
12(b), (e), or (f), whichever is earlier.” A party may amend its pleading only upon the
opposing party’s written consent or the court’s leave if th e deadline to amend has
passed. Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a)(2). “The  court should freely give leave when justice so
requires.” Id.
Rule 15(d) applies to supplemental pleadings—i.e., when the facts underlying
the new or modified claim occurred after the filing of the original pleading. Wright et
al., supra, § 1504. Under Rule 15(d), “the court may, on just terms, permit a party to

serve a supplemental pleading setting out any transaction, occurrence, or event that
happened after the date of the pleading to be supplemented .” Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(d).
“[T]he discretion exercised in deciding whether to grant leave” is similar with respect
to amended and supplemental pleadings. L ewis v. Knutson, 699 F.2d 230, 239 (5th
Cir. 1983).  But the Fifth Circuit has observed that “[w] hile the text of Rule 15(a)
provides that leave should be freely granted, the text of Rule 15(d) does not similarly
provide.” Burns v. Exxon Corp., 158 F.3d 336, 343 (5th Cir. 1998).  Stated otherwise,
“Rule 15(d) is clear that the court may permit a supplemental pleading setting forth
changed circumstances[,]” which provides the court with even greater discretion than
exists for amended pleadings. Id. (emphasis original). A mislabeling of amended and
supplemental pleadings, however, does not prevent the court from analyzing each
according to its true nature under Rule 15. Wright et al., supra, § 1504.
III.  A nalysis
The deadline to amend any pleadings passed on April 12, 2024. 10 Rule 16(b)
therefore governs the motion and requires a showing of good cause.  A party
demonstrates good cause when it can show that, after a diligent attempt to meet the
deadline, such deadline cannot be reasonably met. S&W Enters., 315 F.3d at 533. The
Fifth Circuit’s four- factor test weighs in favor of allowing J.M. to amend or
supplement his complaint. Lampkin, 925 F.3d at 741.
First, the proposed new alleged facts either arose or were discovered after the
April 2024 deadline. Thus, J.M.’s delay is not unreasonable under the circumstances.

Second, the proposed claims are significant because they allow J.M. and his parents
to raise claims that may entitle them to relief that was previously unavailable. Third,
the Louisiana High School Athletic Association has filed no opposition and identified
no prejudice that will result from granting leave to file a supplemental and amended
pleading. Fourth, any prejudice may be cured by a continuance if the District Court
believes that is necessary. Thus, J.M. satisfies the Rule 16(b) standard.
Because J.M.’s proposed pleading implicates events that occurred both before
and after the original complaint was filed, both the Rule 15(d) and Rule 15 (e)
standards apply. Under Rule 15(d), t he Court has discretion to allow supplemental
pleadings regarding any developments that occurred after the filing of the original
complaint. For reasons that overlap with those described above,  J.M. has
demonstrated good cause to allow supplementation of the original complaint. And for
similar reasons, the liberal Rule 15(a) standard supports the proposed amendment.
