II. ANALYSIS
A. Rule 12(b)(6) Standard
A Rule 12(b)(6) motion challenges the sufficiency of a plaintiff’s allegations.
A complaint, or any part of it, may be dismissed for failure to state a claim upon
which relief may be granted if plaintiff fails to make factual allegations entitling him
to relief.  Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007); Cuvillier v.
Taylor, 503 F.3d 397, 401 (5th Cir. 2007).  “A motion to dismiss may be granted on
a statute of limitations defense where it is evident from the pleadings that the action
is time-barred, and the pleadings fail to raise some basis for tolling.”  Taylor v. Bailey
Tool & Manufacturing Co., 744 F.3d 944, 946 (5th Cir. 2014) (citation omitted).

A copyright infringement claim must be filed within three years of the claim’s
accrual.  Edwards v. Take Fo’ Records, Inc., No. 19-cv-12130, 2020 WL 3832606,
at *15 (E.D. La. July 8, 2020) (citing 17 U.S.C. § 507(b)) (“No civil action shall be

after the claim accrued.”).  Claims for copyright infringement accrue upon actual or
constructive discovery of the infringement.   Id.  (citing Graper v. Mid -Continent
Cas. Co., 756 F.3d 388, 393 (5th Cir. 2014)  (other citations omitted).  “Under the
Copyright Act, ‘[r]ecordation of a document in the Copyright Office gives all
persons constructive notice of the facts stated in the recorded documents.’”   Id.

(5th Cir. 1995))).  The recorded document must contain the information necessary
to put the other party on notice.  Id.  (citations omitted).

Defendants present three U.S. Copyright Office certificates of registration.16
For a Rule 12(b)(6) motion, district courts may consider documents “integral to the

408, 409 (5th Cir. 2013) (citing Tellabs, Inc. v. Makor Issues & Rights, Ltd., 551 U.S.

2483, 2005 WL 6225822, at *2 (E.D. La. June 13, 2005) (“[C] opyright certificates
… are the type of evidence that a court may judicially notice….”) (citations omitted).
The court will consider the certificates.
Brun contends he is claiming ownership, not a copyright, so filing the
certificates did not trigger the three -year delay for filing suit.  Because the Fifth
Circuit’s analysis in Jordan v. Sony BMG Music Entertainment Inc., 354 Fed. App’x.
942 (5th Cir. 2009), is persuasive, the distinction is immaterial.
In Jordan, a dispute arose between a music composer and a distributor over
whether the composer was entitled to royalties regarding sound recordings he
executed for three songs.  Id., 354 Fed. App’x. at 944.  Following the distribution of
the songs, the distributor registered the sound recordings with the U.S. Copyright
Office, listing itself as sole author.  Id.  The composer sued, alleging co-ownership
in the three sound recordings.  Id. at 944–95.  The district court found the claims

C. Accounting Claim
“The authors of a joint work are co-owners of copyright in the work, and each
owner owes a duty of accounting to the other. ”  Jordan, 354 Fed.  App’x. at 94 5
(citing 17 U.S.C. § 201(a); Goodman v. Lee, 78 F.3d 1007, 1012 (5th Cir.1996)).  To
enforce an accounting claim, a  plaintiff must be an  owner of the work , as it  is
derivative to the ownership claim.  See Jordan, 637 F.Supp.2d at 460 (citing cases).
Because Brun’s ownership claims fail, his accounting claims must fail.   The
accounting claims, except for the studio recording of Concrete (Fish Out of Water),
are dismissed.
D. Conversion Claim
Brun’s conversion claim  also presumes h e is an owner.  Because the
ownership claim s are time-barred and fail , the conversion claim s must fail .
Additionally, as pled, Brun’s conversion claims are preempted.  See Mapp v. UMG
Recordings, Inc., 310 F.Supp.3d 743, 753–54 (M.D. La. 2018) (“Here, Plaintiff does
not allege that Defendant converted Plaintiff’s physical personal property for its own
use or benefit, but merely that Defendant has distributed Plaintiff ’s intellectual
property without paying Plaintiff a fair share.  Again, these claims are preempted by
the Copyright Act.”).
