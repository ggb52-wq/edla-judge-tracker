II. Legal Standard
Unless otherwise limited by court order, Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1) defines the
scope of discovery: “Parties may obtain discovery regarding any nonprivileged matter
that is relevant to any party’s claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the
case.” A court assesses the proportionality by considering (1) the importance of the
issues at stake in the litigation; (2) the amount in controversy; (3) the parties’
resources; (4) the importance of the proposed discovery in resolving the issues; and
(5) whether the burden or expense of the proposed discovery outweighs its likely
benefit. Id. Information within this scope of discovery need not be admissible in
evidence to be discoverable. Id. “The moving party bears the burden to establish that
the materials requested are within the scope of permissible discovery, after which the
burden shifts to the party resisting discovery to show why the discovery is irrelevant
or otherwise should not be p ermitted.” Cannon v. CSX Transp., Inc. , 2021 WL
5358758, at *1 (E.D. La. Apr. 16, 2021) (citations omitted).
Objections to a discovery request  must be stated with specificity and identify
whether any materials a re being withheld based on  that objection. Fed. R. Civ. P.
33(b)(4); Fed. R. Civ. P. 34(b)(2)(C); Rodriguez-Meza v. Venegas, 2020 WL 14100945,
at *3 (W.D. Tex. Nov. 30, 2020).  General boilerplate discovery objections are
insufficient. McLeod, Alexander, Powel & Apffel, P.C. v. Quarles, 894 F.2d 1482, 1485-
86 (5th Cir. 1990).
Under Rule 26(c)(1), courts may restrict discovery “for good cause ... to protect
a party or person from annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden or

expense.” The burden  rests on the party objecting to the discovery request to
demonstrate good cause exists to restrict discovery . See Cazorla v. Koch Foods of
Mississippi, LLC, 838 F.3d 540, 549 (5th Cir. 2016) (citing cases).  “Discovery rules
are accorded broad and liberal treatment to effect their purpose of adequately
informing litigants in civil trials. ” Herbert v. Lando , 441 U.S. 153, 177 (1979).
Discovery rules, though, are subject to the “injunction of Rule 1 that they be construed
to secure the just, speedy, and inexpensive determination of every action.” Id.
(quoting F ed. R. C iv. P. 1). To that end, “the requirement of Rule 26(b)(1) that
material sought be ‘relevant’ should be firmly applied.” Id.
 Pursuant to Rule 9(b),  a fraud  claimant “must state with particularity the
circumstances constituting fraud or mistake.”  Indeed, “Rule 9(b) requires the who,
what, when, where, and how to be laid out.”  Benchmark Elecs., Inc. v. J.M. Huber
Corp., 343 F.3d 719, 724 (5th Cir. 2003). This is of particular import when analyzing
responses to contention interrogatories. “Failure to provide a rule-compliant response
to a contention interrogatory hinders the discovering party’s ability to conduct follow-
up discovery and to formulate a trial strategy.” V5 Techs. V. Switch, Ltd., 334 F.R.D.
297, 305 (D. Nev. 2019) ; see also Lawman v. City & City of San Francisco , 159 F.
Supp. 3d 1130, 1140—41 (N.D. Cal. 2016) (excluding theory from summary judgment
motion practice because it was not properly identified in response to
contention interrogatory).

III. Analysis
A. Unverified Responses and External References
Pursuant to Rule 33(b)(3),  parties must  answer each interrogatory , to the
extent not objected, “ separately and fully in writing under oath. ” Verifications are
“critical because ‘interrogatories serve not only as a discovery device but as a means
of producing admissible evidence.’” Whale Capital, L.P. v. Ridgeway, 2024 WL 838505
at *5 (E.D. La. Feb. 28, 2024), reconsideration denied, 2024 WL 894866 (E.D. La. Mar.
1, 2024) (internal citations omitted). Apollo’s responses do not contain the requisite
verification.10  In its o pposition, Apollo fails to address or otherwise resolve this
issue.11 Apollo must provide a verification for all interrogatory responses.12
Bohr argues that Apollo’s responses impermissibly reference external
documents such as the  second amended complaint.13 Bohr is correct. While Rule 33
permits references to produced business records, it does so where the records are
identified “in sufficient detail to enable the interrogating party to locate and identify
them as readily as the responding party could.” See also Whale Capital, L.P. , 2024
WL 838505 at *5 (ordering supplemental responses where answers referenced a
“second amended complaint” and “documents being produced”).
