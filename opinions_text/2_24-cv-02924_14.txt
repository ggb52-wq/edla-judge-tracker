REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

Pro se plaintiff Charles Edward Taylor filed this civil action against Leslie Roy Jackson
for allegedly asserting fraudulent child support claims against him, against Hearing Officer Felicia
Higgins for  allegedly denying him due process by ordering him to pay child support without
conducting a DNA test to establish paternity, and against Child Support Enforcement Department
Representative GeJoan Garrett for allegedly failing to follow protocol. Because Plaintiff’s federal
claims have prescribed  and there is no basis for this Court to exercise jurisdiction , it is
RECOMMENDED that, pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B), Mr. Taylor’s federal law claims
be DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE as time-barred, and Mr. Taylor’s state law claim for fraud be
DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction.
Background
 Mr. Taylor filed this lawsuit on December 16, 2024. Rec. Doc. 1. In his Complaint,  Mr.
Taylor contends that his Constitutional rights were violated throughout a child enforcement action
against him that began on or about September 25, 2003. Specifically,  Mr. Taylor alleges that
Defendant Leslie Joy Jackson brought forth fraudulent child  support claims by forging Mr.
Taylor’s signature on certain child support documents. He also argues that he was entitled to a
DNA paternity test before he was subject to child support enforcement actions like the ordering of
child support payments , and, because he was denied a DNA test in late 2013 or early 2014, the

Law and Analysis
There exists no absolute right to proceed in forma pauperis in federal civil matters; instead,
it is a privilege extended to those unable to pay filing fees when it is apparent that the claims do
not lack merit on their face. See Startii v. United States, 415 F.2d 1115, 1116 (5th Cir. 1969). 28
U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B) thus provides for summary dismissal sua sponte , should the Court
determine that a case is frivolous.  Section 1915(e)(2)(B) provides in pertinent part as follows:
(2) Notwithstanding any filing fee, or any portion thereof, that may have been paid,
the court shall dismiss the case at any time if the court determines that –
*   *   *
  (B) the action or appeal –
  (i) is frivolous or malicious;
  (ii) fails to state a claim on which relief may be granted; or
(iii) seeks monetary relief against a defendant who is immune from
such relief.

28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(i) -(iii) (emphasis added).  A claim is “frivolous” where it lacks an
arguable basis either in law or in fact.  Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319, 325 (1989). Furthermore,
federal courts are courts of limi ted jurisdiction. “Subject matter jurisdiction may not be waived,
and the district court ‘shall dismiss the action’ whenever ‘it appears by suggestion of the parties
or otherwise that the court lacks jurisdiction of the subject matter.’” Avitts v. Amoco Pr od. Co.,
53 F.3d 690, 693 (5th Cir. 1995) (quoting FED. R. CIV. P. 12(h)(3)).
 “Where it is clear from the face of a complaint filed in forma pauperis  that the claims
asserted are barred by the applicable statute of limitations, those claims are properly dismissed
pursuant to § 1915(d) .” Riley v. Office of Alcohol & Tobacco Control , No. 18 -6984, 2019 WL
175301, at *1  (E.D. La. Jan. 11, 2019) (quoting Gartrell v. Gaylor , 981 F.2d 254, 256 (5th Cir.
1993)). “[T]he statute of limitations for a Section 1983 action is a state ’s general or residual
personal injury statute of limitations. ” Brown v. Pouncy, 93 F.4th 331, 334 (5th Cir. 2024), cert.

denied, 145 S. Ct. 170 (2024). In Louisiana, that period (known as a “prescriptive period”) is two
years. LA. CIV. CODE art. 3493.1.2
The documentation submitted by Mr. Taylor shows that ongoing child support payments
ended in 2009 when the child reached major status. The documents indicate that Mr. Taylor sought
and was denied a paternity test with regard to his child support payments in late 2013 or early 2014
and that he was ordered to pay child support arrears of about $22,000 at a rate of $105 per month
in January 2014. These orders were entered over ten years ago, well beyond the prescriptive period.
Thus, Mr. Taylor’s claims brought pursuant to § 1983 that arise out of these events are out of time.
Mr. Taylor does not disp ute this in his response to the Court’s Order to Show Cause.  Therefore,
based on the allegations in the Complaint, Taylor’s federal claims have prescribed.
Further, because Mr. Taylor does not dispute tha t each party in this proceeding is a
Louisiana citizen, Mr. Taylor’s state law fraud claim does not provide a basis for federal
jurisdiction, as there is no diversity of citizenship under 28 U.S.C. § 1332.
Accordingly,
IT IS RECOMMENDED that, pursuant to  28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B ), Mr. Taylor’s
federal law claims be DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE as time-barred, and Mr. Taylor’s state law
claim for fraud be DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction.
