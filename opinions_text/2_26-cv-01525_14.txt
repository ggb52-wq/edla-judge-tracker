ORDER AND REASONS

Before the Court is the Motion for Interlocutory Sale of the M/V MISS DORIS (R. Doc.
13) filed by Plaintiff, HS Group, LLC. The Court has reviewed the motion, the record, and the
applicable law. For the reasons that follow, the motion is denied without prejudice.
I. BACKGROUND
On July 11, 2026, Plaintiff filed the Verified Complaint in this action, stating that the M/V
MISS DORIS has been stored at property owned by HS Group, LLC since November 1, 2023, and
that HS Group, LLC has provided necessaries for the M/V MISS  DORIS, including storage,
docking, oversight and movement of the M/V MISS DORIS totaling $99,277.50, as of March 30,
2025 and accruing at the rate of $115.83 per day.1 In order to secure payment for the services of
HS Group, LLC for the M/V MISS DORIS, HS Group, LLC filed a Notice of Claim of Lien with
the United States Coast Guard National Vessel Documentation Center, which was recorded against
the vessel on April 9, 2025. 2 As of the filing of the Verified Complaint, the amount owed to
Plaintiff for the necessaries provided for the M/V MISS DORIS remained unpaid and was
continuing to accrue.3 Plaintiff thus filed this action for enforcement of the maritime lien and the
Notice of Claim of Lien against the M/V MISS DORIS. A Warrant of Arrest was issued against

II. LAW AND ANALYSIS
Rule E(9)(a)(i) of the Supplemental Rules for Admiralty or Maritime Claims  provides, in
pertinent part, as follows:
On application of a party, . . . the court may order all or part of the property sold—
with the sales proceeds, or as much of them as will satisfy the judgment, paid into
court to await further orders of the court—if:
(A) the attached or arrested property is perishable, or liable to deterioration,
decay, or injury by being detained in custody pending the action;
(B) the expense of keeping the property is excessive or disproportionate; or
(C) there is an unreasonable delay in securing release of the property.

 Satisfaction of any one of the three criteria is sufficient to justify an interlocutory sale. 8
Plaintiff’s memorandum in support of its motion argues all three of the criteria are satisfied in this
case. On the record currently before the Court, the Court cannot find that any of the criteria are
satisfied. A Court’s factual findings must be based on evidence in the record, and here Plaintiff
has failed to provide any evidence to support its arguments as to the criteria in subsections (A) and
(B). The record also does not support the finding Plaintiff seeks under subsection (C).
With respect to the criterion in subsection (A), Plaintiff’s memorandum in support of its
motion states that the M/V MISS DORIS is deteriorating in a number of respects and that the
vessel is susceptible to decay and deterioration by sitting idle in the water. 9 But statements by
counsel in a memorandum are argument, not evidence . And, Plaintiff has not provided any
evidence upon which the Court could make the finding Plaintiff seeks. 10 The same is true for
Plaintiff’s argument that the expense of holding the vessel under arrest is excessive  under
subsection (B) . Plaintiff’s memorandum states that Plaintiff has been paying the substitute
custodian $300.00 per day to maintain and preserve the vessel,11 but Plaintiff has not provided any
evidence to support its claim that it has been paying this expense. Moreover, Plaintiff cites to
Stewart & Stevenson Services, Inc. v. M/V Chris Way MacMillan, 890 F. Supp. 552, 564 (N.D.
Miss. 1995), in support of its contention that $300.00 per day to maintain custody of the vessel is
excessive.12 But in that  case, the court found the daily expense of $150.00 justified the
interlocutory sale of the vessel because the expense of maintaining the property was “clearly

The distinction between July 13, 2026, and August 11, 2026, is relevant for purposes of
determining whether the owners of the M/V MISS DORIS have unreasonably delayed securing
the release of the vessel. If the vessel was arrested on July 13, 2026, as Plaintiff contends, then the
delay in securing the release of the vessel has been  longer than 4 months, which is generally
considered unreasonable.20 But if the vessel was not arrested until August 11, 2026, as the record
currently indicates, then there has not yet been a 4 -month delay. Because there is no evidentiary
basis to support a finding that the arrest occurred on July 13, 2026, the Court uses August 11, 2026,
the earliest date on which the record reflects service of the warrant, as the date of the arrest .
Additionally, the Court applies the general 4-month rule because Plaintiff has not shown that any
circumstances exist that would warrant finding an unreasonable delay based on some lesser period
of time.21 Under these facts, Plaintiff has not shown  that there has been an unreasonable delay in
securing the release of the M/V MISS DORIS, and thus there is no basis to order the interlocutory
sale of the vessel at this time.
III. CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons,
IT IS ORDERED that Plaintiff HS Group, LLC’s Motion for Interlocutory Sale of the
M/V MISS DORIS (R. Doc. 13) is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE.
