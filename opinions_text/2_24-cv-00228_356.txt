Before the Court is Plaintiff’s Motion for Judicial Notice, filed by the plaintiff,
Kathleen Moller (“Moller”).1  Defendant, JOpen, LLC (“JOpen”), opposes the Motion,2
and Plaintiff has filed a Reply. 3  After careful consideration of the parties’
memoranda, the record, and the applicable law, the Court GRANTS IN PART and
DENIES IN PART Plaintiff’s Motion for Judicial Notice.

II. LEGAL STANDARD
Federal Rule of Evidence 201(b) provides that a “court may judicially notice a
fact that is not subject to reasonable dispute because it: (1) is generally known within
the trial court ’s territorial jurisdiction; or (2) can be accurately and readily
determined from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned.”18  “Under
Rule 201(c), a court “may take judicial notice on its own[]” or “must take judicial notice
if a party requests it and the court is supplied with the necessary information.” 19
“Rule 201 authorizes the court to take notice only of adjudicative facts , not legal
determinations.”20  “Adjudicative facts are simply the facts of the particular case,
whereas, legislative facts . . . are those which have relevance to legal reasoning and
the lawmaking process, whether in the formulation of a legal principle or ruling by a

judge or court or in the enactment of a legislative body .”21  Ultimately, pursuant to

if a party requests it and the court is supplied with the necessary information.”22
“Judicial notice may be taken of matters of public record.” 23  “When
taking judicial notice, however, the courts generally are limited to recognizing the
existence of a document, rather than taking as true potentially disputed factual
statements or findings contained within that document, unless the facts themselves
satisfy the Rule 201(c) criteria.”24  Fundamentally, “judicial notice is discretionary by
the district court, unless it is requested by a party and the court is supplied with the
necessary information.”25
