II. LAW & ANALYSIS
A. Westbank’s motion to stay
“There is no general federal constitutional, statutory, or common law rule barring the
simultaneous prosecution of separate civil and criminal actions … against the same defendant
involving the same transactions.”  SEC v. First. Fin. Grp. of Tex., Inc., 659 F.2d 660, 666 (5th Cir.
1981); see also Hillhouse v. IPFS Corp., 2025 WL 1884847, at *4 (E.D. La. July 8, 2025).  District
courts generally have wide discretion to stay proceedings before it “in the interests of justice.”  In
re Ramu Corp., 903 F.2d 312, 318 (5th Cir. 1990).  But “‘[p]roper use of this authority calls for
the exercise of judgment, which must weigh competing interests and maintain an even balance.’”
Breaud v. Cantrell, 2024 WL 4503252, at *5 (E.D. La. Oct. 16, 2024) (quoting Wedgeworth v.
Fibreboard Corp., 706 F.2d 541, 545 (5th Cir. 1983)) ; see also I n re Ramu, 903 F.2d at 318
(explaining that discretionary stays should not be of indefinite duration).  In deciding whether
to stay a civil action pending resolution of a criminal case, courts in the Fifth Circuit generally
examine: (1) the extent of the overlap between the criminal and civil cases; (2) the status of the
criminal case, including whether the defendant has been indicted; (3) the plaintiff’s private
interests in proceeding expeditiously, weighed against the prejudice to the plaintiff created by a
delay; (4) the private interests of and burden on the defendants; (5) the interest of the court; and
(6) the public interest.  Breaud, 2024 WL 4503252, at *6.

The Court will first analyze the six stay factors.  The first factor is the extent to which the
issues in the civil and criminal cases overlap, and it generally carries the most weight in a court’s
determination of whether to grant a stay.  See Hillhouse , 2025 WL 1884847, at *4; Walker, 2015
WL 5873392, at *6.  In this case, there is a great deal of overlap between the issues in the cases
because both cases largely depend on the resolution of the Cottrell brothers’ fault ( including
potential criminal liability) for Johnson’s death.  Dennis’s argument that there is minimal overlap
between her case against Westbank and the criminal case against the Cottrell brothers fails because
one of Westbank’s defenses to Dennis’s civil suit is that the Cottrell brothers committed an
intentional tort which could absolve it of civil liability, but which could prove the Cottrell brothers’
fault for Johnson’s death.  The overlap of the claims is not negated merely because Dennis’s claims
against Westbank also involve some of Westbank’s conduct before and after the incident (such as
its hiring and training practices and its rendering of aid to Johnson after the incident) that does not
necessarily overlap with the stabbing.  Accordingly, this factor weighs in favor of granting a stay.

The second factor requires consideration of the status of the criminal case.  Where a party
has been indicted for the conduct underlying a civil case, a stay of the civil case is especially
appropriate because the accused is more likely to make incriminating statements.45  See Hillhouse,
2025 WL 1884847, at * 5; Walker, 2015 WL 5873392, at *7.  The Cottrell brothers have been
indicted for Johnson’s murder and are awaiting trial, so their participation in civil discovery poses
the most risk to them at the current stage in their criminal proceedings.  Thus, the Court finds that
the second factor weighs in favor of granting a stay.
The third factor is the plaintiff’s interest in expediency.  Courts generally require a greater
showing of prejudice than a delay to plaintiff’s right to pursue a claim.  See D’Angelo v. Pintado,
2009 WL 4642009, at *2 (E.D. La. Dec. 2, 2009); Hillhouse, 2025 WL 1884847, at *6.  The facts
of this case took place onboard a fishing vessel, and Dennis asserts that “[s]everal crewmembers
have already returned to different states or custodial circumstances,” and that a substantial delay
could seriously risk the unavailability of witnesses.46  The Court finds that, given the sometimes
transient nature of fishing vessel crews, a stay could result in prejudice to Dennis, especially
considering Dennis’s representation that discovery has been productive thus far in certain respects
that do not implicate the Cottrell brothers’ Fifth Amendment rights.  Further, the Court recognizes
that a stay might be prejudicial to the financial stability of Johnson’s young son.  As a result, the
third factor weighs against granting a stay.
The fourth factor is the private interests of the defendant in securing the stay and the burden
the defendant might face if the civil proceeding were not stayed.  In this case, the Court finds this

Considering all six factors above, the Court finds that Westbank would be prejudiced if the

of the proceedings.  Accordingly, the Court will issue a stay of the proceedings for a limited

In this case, bifurcation is not warranted.  Dennis submits that bifurcation “addresses
Westbank’s stated concerns [of prejudice resulting from the Cottrell brothers’ invocation of their
Fifth Amendment rights during civil discovery] without requiring [her] and her minor child to wait
for the resolution of criminal proceedings and any later proceedings before obtaining a civil
adjudication against Westbank.”64  But Dennis’s position fails to appreciate the overlap of the
Cottrell brothers’ criminal proceeding and the civil claims she asserts against Westbank.  As this
Court has previously explained, Westbank is unable to obtain facts vital to its defense of Dennis’s
Jones Act and unseaworthiness claims without the testimony of the Cottrell brothers who are
currently awaiting trial and who have a Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination.  In other
words, the Cottrell brothers are crucial witnesses in regard to the resolution of  Dennis’s claims.
Westbank does not merely speculate that the ir testimony could inure to its benefit ; instead, it
provides the statements of two of the brothers which together  create a serious factual dispute
central to its defense, making David Cottrell’s testimony even more critical. 65  Accordingly,
without the meaningful participation of the Cottrell brothers  (especially David) in the civil trial
(including the Phase I Dennis proposes) , Westbank would be  unduly prejudiced not only in its
pursuit of its third-party claims, but in its defense against Dennis’s claims.  Bifurcation of Dennis’s

III. CONCLUSION
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS ORDERED that Westbank’s motion to stay (R. Doc. 64) is GRANTED IN PART,
and this action is STAYED  until further notice , except that the parties may pursue any and all
discovery not directed to the Cottrell brothers or that would implicate the Fifth Amendment rights
of any of the Cottrell brothers.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that the parties file a status report, on or before November 1,
2026, and the first day of each third month thereafter until the stay is lifted, specifically addressing
the status of the Cottrell brothers’ criminal proceedings.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that Dennis’s motion to bifurcate (R. Doc. 65) is DENIED.
