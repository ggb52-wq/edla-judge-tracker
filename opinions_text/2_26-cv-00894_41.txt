III. LAW & ANALYSIS
“[F]ederal law governs the enfo rceability of forum-selection clauses in this circuit.”
Barnett v. DynCorp Int’l, L.L.C., 831 F.3d 296, 301 (5th Cir. 2016).  A motion to transfer under
28 U.S.C. § 1404(a) is the proper mechanism to enforce a valid forum-selection clause providing
for venue in a different federal district.  Atl. Marine Constr. Co. v. U .S. Dist. Ct. for the W. Dist.
of Tex., 571 U.S. 49, 59 (2013). Section 1404(a) provide s that “[f]or the conve nience of parties
and witnesses, in the interest of justice, a distri ct court may transfer any civil action to any other
district or division where it might  have been brought or to any di strict or divisi on to which all
parties have consented.”  28 U.S.C. § 1404(a).  Thus, a case, as a whole, cannot be transferred “if
at the time suit was originally brought the transf eree district would have lacked jurisdiction over
the defendant or if venue ther e would have been improper.”  Liaw Su Teng v. Skaarup Shipping
Corp., 743 F.2d 1140, 1148 (5th Cir. 1984), overruled on other grounds by In re Air Crash
Disaster Near New Orleans, La. , 821 F.2d 1147 (5th Cir. 1987).  “If, however, suit might have
been brought against one or more defendants in the court to which transfer is sought, the claims
against those defendants may be severed and transferred while th e claims against the remaining
defendant, for whom transfer would not be proper, are retained in the original court.”  Id.
“For cases where all parties signed a forum selection contract, the analysis is easy: except
in a truly exceptional case, the contract controls.”  In re Rolls Royce Corp., 775 F.3d 671, 679 (5th
Cir. 2014); see also Atl. Marine, 571 U.S. at 59-60 (recognizing that under § 1404(a), “a forum-
selection clause [is] ‘given c ontrolling weight in all but the most exceptional cases.’” (quoting
Stewart Org., Inc. v. Ricoh Corp. , 487 U.S. 22, 33 (1988))).  Howe ver, the analysis is more
complicated when the lawsuit includes parties who have not signed the forum-selection clause, as
in this case.  In Rolls Royce, “[t]he Fifth Circuit explained that there are essentially three options:

(1) transfer the entire case; (2 ) sever and transfer only the pa rties bound by the forum-selection
clause; or (3) maintain the entire lawsuit in this district notwithstanding the forum-selection
clause.”  Royal Smit Transformers BV v. HC Bea-Luna M/V , 2017 WL 819243, at *3 (E.D. La.
Mar. 2, 2017) (discussing Rolls Royce, 775 F.3d at 679).
Under Rolls Royce, the severance-and-transfer inquiry in situations where some but not all
parties have entered into the forum-selection clause proceeds as follows:
First, pursuant to Atlantic Marine, the private factors of the parties who have signed
a forum agreement must, as matter of law, cut in favor of severance and transfer to
the contracted for forum.  Second, the district court must consider the private factors
of the parties who have not signed a forum selection agreement as it would under a
Rule 21 severance and section 1404 transfer analysis.  Finally, it must ask whether
this preliminary weighing is outweighed by the judicial economy considerations of
having all claims determined in a single lawsuit.  In so  determining, the district
court should consider whether there are procedural mechanisms that can reduce the
costs of severance, such as common pr e-trial procedures, video depositions,
stipulations, etc.

Rolls Royce, 775 F.3d at 681 (emphasis in original); see also Def. Distributed v. Bruck , 30 F.4th
414 (5th Cir. 2022) (applying the Rolls Royce inquiry to hold that severance and transfer was not
supported by the relevant factors, especially wher e severance would split intertwined claims that
the plaintiff had chosen to bring together agains t multiple defendants).  The Fifth Circuit further
explained that “[t]his is necessarily a fact-s ensitive analysis, and while we agree that Atlantic
Marine informs the analysis, we ca nnot conclude that it categor ically requires severance-and-
transfer in all situations.”  Id.
A. Private Interest Factors of Nonsignatory Defendants
1. Rule 21 private interest factors
Rule 21 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure permits a court to exercise its discretion to
sever any claim against any party.  To determine whether a claim should be severed, a court may
consider: (1) whether the claims involved arose out of the same transaction or occurrence; (2)

8

whether the claims present common questions of la w or fact; (3) whether settlement or judicial
economy would be promoted; (4) whether prejudi ce would be averted by severance; and (5)
whether different witnesses and documentary proof are required fo r separate claims.  See Rolls
Royce, 775 F.3d at 680 n.6 (noting that  courts have considered th ese factors when analyzing
severance).
Here, the Rule 21 factors weigh against severance.  All of Plaintiffs’ claims arise out of the
same transaction or occurrence, present common issues of law and fact, and involve the same
witnesses and documentary proof.  Plaintiffs’ claims arise out one transaction – namely, their
purchase of an adaptive vehicle which they say has failed to perform as expected.  They allege that
Superior sold them the vehicle and also acted as  the installer, repairer , and warranty channel for
the component parts manufactured by Honda, Braun, and EMC.  Superior is alleged to have been
responsible for integrat ing Braun’s and EMC’s component parts into the Honda van.  The facts
regarding why the integrated vehicle allegedly fa iled to perform as expected are intertwined, not
separable.  Moreover, Plaintif fs allege redhibition and MMWA claims against all defendants,
including Superior.  It is immaterial that th ere are additional warranty claims against the
manufacturer defendants and breach-of-contract and negligence claims alleged against Superior,
because ascertaining why the vehicle did not wo rk properly, as alleged, will involve the same
witnesses and documents, regardless of the theories of lability.   In addition, settlement and judicial
economy would be promoted by having the claims against all defendants tried in the same forum.
Finally, the prejudice prong is neutral.  Thus, on balance, the Rule 21 factors disfavor severance.
2. Section 1404(a) private interest factors
The private interest factors that are considered in the § 1404(a) analysis are: (1) the relative
ease of access to sources of proof; (2) the availability of compulsory process to secure the

attendance of witnesses; (3) the cost of attendance for willing witnesses; and (4) all other practical
problems that make trial of a case easy, expediti ous, and inexpensive.  In re Volkswagen of Am.,
Inc., 545 F.3d 304, 315 (5th Cir.  2008).  Plaintiffs, as the part ies seeking to avoid the forum-
selection clause, bear the burden of estab lishing that transfer is unwarranted.  Atl. Marine, 571
U.S. at 63.
Like the Rule 21 factors, the § 1404(a) privat e interest factors di sfavor severance and
transfer.  The vehicle was sold, modified, repaired, and used in  Louisiana.  The bulk of the
documents and witnesses are thus in this forum,  making compulsory process available to secure
the attendance of unwilling witnesses and lower the cost for willing witnesses.  Further, keeping
all of the parties in one forum would make the trial easier, more expeditious, and less expensive.
B. Judicial Economy § 1404(a) Public Interest Factors
The public interest factors that are consider ed in the § 1404(a) an alysis are: (1) the
administrative difficulties flowing from court congestion; (2) the local interest in having localized
interests decided at home; (3) the familiarity of the forum with the law that will govern the case;
and (4) the avoidance of unnecessary problems of conflict of laws or in the application of foreign
law.  In re Volkswagen, 545 F.3d at 315.
The § 1404(a) public interest fact ors also disfavor transfer.  The Court is unaware of its
congestion relative to that of the proposed transferee court; however, having one case in federal
court, instead of two, would certainly militate against severance and transfer.  Next, this is a
Louisiana-centered controversy.  Plaintiffs are Louisiana citizens.  They purchased the vehicle
from Superior in Louisiana, and it was serviced and repaired within the forum.  Plaintiffs brought
several claims arising under Louisiana law a nd, obviously, a Louisiana federal court would be

more familiar with that law than would one sitting in Kentucky.  Finally, the Court is unaware at
this time of any problems of conflict of laws or in the application of foreign law.

In sum, although the private interest factors of Superior (as a signatory to the forum-
selection clause) must, as a matter of law, be held to cut in favor of severance and transfer to the
contracted-for forum, the Cour t finds that the private interest factors of the manufacturer
defendants (i.e., the nonsignatories to the forum-selection clause) and the public interest factors
as a whole weigh against it.  Thus, on balance, the Court holds that Superior’s motion to sever
and transfer should be denied.
IV. CONCLUSION
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS ORDERED that Superior’s motion to sever and transfer (R. Doc. 25) is DENIED.
