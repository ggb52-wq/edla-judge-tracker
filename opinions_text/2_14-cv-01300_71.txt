ORDER AND REASONS
 Before the Court is Plaintiff Hancock Bank’s (“Plaintiff”) Motion to Revive Judgment.1 In
the motion, Plaintiff seeks to revive a judgment2 entered by this Court on January 27, 2017, against
the Succession of Melvin Chris Oller, in the total amount of $118,585.04, consisting of a principal
amount of $75,000, attorney’s fees in the amount of $37,294.20, costs in the amount of $6,290.84,
together with judicial interest accruing from October 31, 2016 until paid. The motion was set for
submission October 28, 2026. Pursuant to Local Rule 7.5, any response to the motion was to be
filed on or before October 20, 2026.3 To date, there has been no response filed to the motion. This
Court has the authority to grant an unopposed motion, although it is not required to do so.4 Having
considered the motion, the memorandum in support, the record, and the applicable law, the Court
grants the motion.

judgment was rendered.”13 A money judgment “is prescribed by the lapse of ten years from its
signing if no appeal has been taken, or, if an appeal has been taken, it is prescribed by lapse of ten
years from the time the judgment becomes final.”14 “The court may order the judgment debtor to

 On January 27, 2017, the Court entered judgment in favor of Plaintiff and against the
Succession of Melvin Chris Oller , awarding a total of $118,585.04, consisting of $75,000 in
principal, $37,294.20 in attorney’s fees, and $6,290.84 in costs , together with judicial interest
accruing from October 31, 2016 until paid. Plaintiff filed the instant Motion to Revive Judgment
on October 7, 2026. Plaintiff represents that, as of September 29, 2026, the total amount
outstanding under the judgment was $126,443.40, inclusive of $7,858.36 in accrued judicial
interest, with interest continuing to accrue until paid. Because the motion was filed before the
expiration of the ten-year prescriptive period, the request for revival is timely. Moreover, Plaintiff
seeks revival of the judgment in the same C ourt and proceeding in which the original judgment
was rendered, as required by Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure article 2031(A). Plaintiff also
submitted a declaration of Richard Buntin, the Vice President of Hancock Whitney Bank, who
declared that the judgment remains outstanding. 16 Thus, Plaintiff has satisfied the procedural
requirements of revival under Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure article 2031(A). As such, the
Court finds that revival of the January 27, 2017 judgment is warranted.
 Accordingly,
