I. BACKGROUND
 This case concerns a claim on an uninsur ed/underinsured motorist (“UIM”) policy
following an automobile accident.4  Hinton, a citizen of Texas, ma intained an UIM policy issued
by State Farm with a $50,000 policy limit. 5  On July 5, 2025, Hint on was injured when an
underinsured motorist hit her vehicle in New Orleans, Louisiana.6  Hinton’s injuries exhausted the
$15,000 limit of the tortfeasor’s automobile insurance policy. 7  As a result, Hinton submitted a
claim to State Farm on her UIM policy.8  Hinton asserts that State Farm refused to tender adequate
payment.9

III. LAW & ANALYSIS
A. Remand Standard
A defendant may remove from state court to th e proper United States district court “any
civil action brought in a State court of which the district courts of the United States have original
jurisdiction.”  28 U.S.C. § 1441(a).  “A federal district court has subject matter jurisdiction over a
state claim when the amount in controversy is met and there is complete diversity of citizenship
between the parties.”  Mumfrey v. CVS Pharmacy, Inc., 719 F.3d 392, 397 (5th Cir. 2013) (citing
28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)).  Because federal courts have only limited jurisdiction, the removal statute is
strictly construed, and any doubts or ambiguities are resolved ag ainst removal and in favor of
remand.  Vantage Drilling Co. v. Hsin-Chi Su , 741 F.3d 535, 537 (5th Cir. 2014); Manguno v.
Prudential Prop. & Cas. Ins. Co., 276 F.3d 720, 723 (5th Cir. 2002).  The party seeking removal
has the burden of establishi ng “that federal jurisdiction exists and that removal was
proper.”  Manguno, 276 F.3d at 723.
B. Amount in Controversy
Federal courts have original jurisdiction over matters involving state-law claims when the
parties are completely diverse, meaning no plaintiff is a citizen of the same state as any defendant,
and the amount in controversy ex ceeds $75,000.  28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(1); SXSW, L.L.C. v. Fed.
Ins. Co., 83 F.4th 405, 407 (5th Cir. 2023).  “The amount in controversy is ‘not proof of the amount
the plaintiff will recover’ but ‘an estimate of the amount that will be put at issue in the course of
the litigation.’”  Durbois v. Deutsche Bank Nat’l Tr. Co. as Tr. of Holders of AAMES Mortg. Inv.
Tr. 20054 Mortg. Backed Notes , 37 F.4th 1053, 1057 (5th Cir. 2022) (quoting McPhail v. Deere
& Co., 529 F.3d 947, 956 (10th Cir. 2008)).  It “is meas ured by the direct pecuniary value of the
right the plaintiff seeks to enforce or protect; st ated somewhat differently, it is the value of the

“[T]he party invoking federal diversity jurisdiction … bears the bur den of establishing the amount
in controversy by a preponderance of the evidence.”  Allstate Fire & Cas. Ins. Co. v. Love , 71
F.4th 348, 351 (5th Cir. 2023).  The jurisdictional facts at the time of removal control.  Gebbia v.
Wal-Mart Stores, Inc., 233 F.3d 880, 883 (5th Cir. 2000).  And “if it is facially apparent from the
petition that the amount in controversy exceed s $75,000 at the time of removal,” a post-removal
stipulation to the contrary does not “deprive the district court of jurisdiction.”  Id.
Here, it is facially apparent from the petiti on, coupled with the pr e-suit demand, that the
amount in controversy at the time of removal exceeded $75,000.  In her petition, Hinton alleged
that she “sustained severe and painful personal injuries, including but not limited to bodily injuries,
pain and suffering, mental angu ish, inconvenience, medical expenses , loss of enjoyment of life,
lost wages and earning capacity, and other damages,” and she a sserted a claim for statutory
penalties and attorney’s fees under Louisiana law.28  Those penalties, when added to the $50,000
policy limit, demonstrate that more than $75,000 was in controversy at the time of removal on the
face of the petition alone.  Moreover, Hinton’s pre-suit demand sought $335,000 in damages for
her injuries including medical expenses, pain and suffering, mental anguish, loss of enjoyment of
life, and disability.29  The demand did not even include amounts for the claimed statutory penalties
or her lost wages and earning capacity.30  The pre-suit demand is relevant evidence that the amount
in controversy exceeded $75,000 at the time of removal.  See Medine v. Lane-Valente Indus., Inc.,
