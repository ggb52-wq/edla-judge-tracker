II. LEGAL STANDARD
The duty to disclose ongoing medical records is governed by Federal Rule of
Civil Procedure 26(e)(1).  The Rule provides that parties must supplement discovery
responses “in a timely manner if the party learns that in some material respect the
disclosure or response is incomplete or incorrect, and if the additional or corrective
information has not otherwise been made known to the other parties during the
discovery process or in writing.”13  Documents that have not been disclosed in
accordance with Rule 26 are not permitted for  use as evidence at trial unless the
failure to disclose was substantially justified or harmless.14  Courts look at the
following factors to determine whether a violation of R ule 26 is harmless, “(1) the

B. Motion in limine to exclude exhibits 19 through 22 from evidence
Two weeks before trial, the Plaintiffs produced exhibits 19 through 22 to the

excluded because they alter the landscape of the case with respect to the purported
future medical treatment of Mr. Rodriguez .28  Defendants argue that the lack of
information regarding the physical therapy and resulting injection recommendation

Under Rule 26(e)(1) parties must supplement discovery responses in a timely
manner upon learning that they are incomplete or incorrect. 35  That duty is only
triggered when the additional or corrective information has not otherwise been made
known to the other parties during the discovery process. 36  Regarding the physical
therapy billing information, it is not contested that Defendants had access to a note
by Mr. Rodriguez’s treating neurologist recommending physical therapy and stating
the Mr. Rodriguez should follow up with the neurologist after  the therapy was
completed.37  Plaintiffs further contend that Defendants  also had access to billing
statements from the physical therapist for Plaintiff’s earlier treatment.38  While the
Defendants should have been aware that the Mr. Rodriguez was in physical therapy,
the Defendants still lacked important information related to the physical therapy,
such as how many sessions of therapy the Plaintiff was expected to attend prior to
his follow up visit and the cost of the sessions.  Because the physical therapy billing
information contained additional  information not  previously disclosed to the
Defendants, the Court finds the Plaintiffs had a duty to supplement discovery with
the physical therapy billing information.

In Texas A&M Research Foundation v. Magna Transportation, Inc.  the Fifth
Circuit found that any prejudice that Defendant faced because of the Plaintiff ’s
failure to disclose  a six page affidavit accompanied by  329 pages of documents was
cured by the Defendant having one month to examine and respon d to the contested

but the combine d exhibits total only eight  pages.53 The Court finds that two  weeks
was enough time for the Defendant to examine and respond to th is evidence.  The
Court finds that the Defendant s w ere not prejudiced by the Plaintiffs ’ failure to
disclose and, therefore, the failure to disclose was harmless .  According ly, the
Defendants’ motion to exclude exhibits 19 through 22 is DENIED.

Medical Expenses is MOOT.  The Defendants’ Motion to Exclude Exhibits 19 through
22 is DENIED.
