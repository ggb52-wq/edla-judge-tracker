LAW AND ANALYSIS
I. Legal Standard
Federal Courts are courts of limited jurisdiction, possessing only the authority granted by
the United States Constitution and conferred by the United States Congress. Howery v. Allstate
Ins. Co., 243 F.3d 912, 916 (5th Cir. 2001). Courts should determine whether subject-matter
jurisdiction is present before addressing other issues. Ramming v. United States, 281 F.3d 158, 161
Case 2:26-cv-02036-GGG-JVM     Document 16     Filed 11/16/26     Page 2 of 8
3

(5th Cir. 2001) ( citation omitted ). Because a defendant’s invocation of the removal statute
“deprives a state court of a case properly before it and thereby implicates important federalism
concerns[,]” id., federal courts must construe § 1441 strictly and “scrupulously confine their own
jurisdiction to the precise limits which the statute has defined.” Gregoire v. Enter. Marine Servs.,
LLC, 38 F. Supp. 3d 749, 764 (E.D. La. 2014) (quoting Romero v. Int ’l Terminal Operating Co.,
358 U.S. 354, 380 (1959)).
Under 28 U.S.C. § 1446, a  defendant may remove a civil action filed in state court if the
plaintiff could have brought the action in federal court from the outset. The removing party bears
the burden of showing that federal jurisdiction exists. Allen v. R & H Oil & Gas Co., 63 F.3d 1326,
1335 (5th Cir. 1995). A federal court must resolve any ambiguities regarding whether removal
jurisdiction is proper against federal jurisdiction and in favor of the party seeking remand. See,
e.g., Manguno v. Prudential Prop. and Cas. Ins. Co., 276 F.3d 720, 723 (5th Cir. 2002); Gutierrez
v. Flores, 543 F.3d 248, 251 (5th Cir. 2008) (citation omitted)  (“[A]ny doubt about the propriety
of removal must be resolved in favor of remand.”). Remand is proper at any time the Court lacks
subject-matter jurisdiction. 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c).
In making a jurisdictional assessment, a federal court is not limited to the pleadings; it may
look to any record evidence, and may receive affidavits, deposition testimony, and/or live
testimony concerning the facts underlying the citizenship of the parties. Coury v. Prot , 85 F.3d
244, 249 (5th Cir. 1996); Jones v. Landry, 387 F.2d 102 (5th Cir. 1967). “The jurisdictional facts
that support removal must be judged at the time of removal.” Gebbia v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc., 233
F.3d 880, 883 (5th Cir. 2000).
Under 28 U.S.C. § 1446(b), a defendant must generally remove within 30 days of receiving
a removable pleading. See Boudreaux v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., No. 21- CV-2874, 2022

WL 2352333, at *2 (W.D. La. June 7, 2022), report and recommendation adopted, No. 21-CV-
2874, 2022 WL 2347162 (W.D. La. June 28, 2022)  (citing 28 U.S.C. § 1446(b)(3) ) (“When the
initial pleadings do not provide grounds for removal, defendants may remove the action ‘ within
30 days after receipt ... of an amended pleading, motion, or other paper from which it may first be
ascertained that the case is one which is or has become removable.’”). However, when removal is
based on diversity jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1332, the “action may not be removed more than
one year from its commencement unless the district court finds that the plaintiff acted in bad faith
in order to prevent removal.” Id. (citing 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1)).
Here, AXA seeks removal based on diversity jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1332.
Diversity jurisdiction exists in civil cases where the parties are citizens of different states and the
amount in controversy exceeds $75,000 excluding costs and interest. 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(1). The
“burden of proving that complete diversity exists rests upon the party who seeks to invoke the
court’s diversity jurisdiction.” G etty Oil Corp., a Div. of Texaco v. Ins. Co. of N. Am., 841 F.2d
1254, 1259 (5th Cir. 1988) (citation omitted).
II. Timeliness of Removal
The question before this Court is whether AXA timely removed the case. AXA contends
its removal was timely because it removed within 30 days of Jackson’s August 13, 2026, motion
seeking penalties, attorneys’ fees, and costs. AXA argues that this motion was the first filing from
which removability could be ascertained. R. Doc. 4, pp. 8–9.
Jackson argues removal is barred by the one -year limitation of 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1)
because the state court action commenced on August 6, 2020.
A. Jackson’s Motion Did Not Commence a New Action
AXA attempts to avoid the plain language of 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1) by asserting  that

Jackson commenced a “new action” against it on August 13, 2026, when Jackson filed her motion
seeking penalties, attorneys’ fees, and costs. R. Doc. 12, p. 6.
AXA relies on Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure art. 421, which defines an “action” as
“a demand for the enforcement of a legal right” that is “commenced by the filing of a pleading
presenting the demand to a court of competent jurisdiction.” R. Doc. 12, p. 8. That argument
overlooks Louisiana authority recognizing that multiple judicial demands may arise within the
same action. Mehta v. Baton Rouge Oil co., Inc., 1999-1773 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/22/00), 768 So. 2d
243, 245 (emphasis added) ( “[T]he original judicial demand [may not] be the only demand filed
in the proceeding. There may be many … other demands which emanate from the original demand
in the same action. Such demands … must be classed as judicial demands, even though they are
not the first demand or the original action .”); see also Boudreaux , 2022 WL 2352333, at *4
(internal quotations and citations omitted)  (“[A]n Action … may conclude with more than one
demand.”).
Mehta makes clear that an “action” may encompass many “demands” and distinguishe s
those other demands from the “first demand or the original action.” Mehta, 768 So. 2d 245.
AXA’s reliance on Braud v. Transp. Serv. Co. of Illinois , 445 F.3d 801 (5th Cir. 2006)  is
also misplaced. Braud involved the Class Action Fairness Act (“CAFA”), which expressly
exempts qualifying class actions from the one-year limitation of 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1). Id. at 806
(“[U]nder CAFA a new defendant may remove regardless of whether it was added more than one
year after the original complaint was filed in state court.”). Id. at 806; see also 28 U.S.C.A. § 1453
(emphasis added) (“ A class action may be removed to a district court of the United States in
accordance with section 1446 (except that the 1 -year limitation under section 1446(c)(1) shall
not apply)[.]”).

In Mendoza v. Old Republic Ins. Co., No. CV 18-7031, 2018 WL 5292365, at *3 (E.D. La.
Oct. 25, 2018) the Court noted CAFA’s exception to the one -year limitation does not apply to a
non-CAFA case. Id. at *3 (quotation omitted) (citing Loyola Univ. New Orleans v. MAPP Constr.
Inc., No. 10- 934, 2010 WL 11538698, at *3 (E.D. La. May 6, 2010) ). The Court held that
“[b]ecause this lawsuit commenced when it was filed in state court in 2011, the instant removal is
untimely. The fact that the AIG Defendants were added seven years later does not alter the Court’s
analysis of ‘ commencement of the action. ’” The Court further noted “[i]n cases not initially
removable, the one year limitation applies even if new defendants are added or new claims are
asserted.” Id. (citations omitted).
“In the Fifth Circuit, the ‘ default rule under Louisiana law is that, absent special
circumstances, a suit is commenced only at the time the original petition is filed  in a court of
competent jurisdiction.’” Boudreaux, 2022 WL 2352333, at *4 (emphasis added) (citing Admiral
Ins. Co. v. Abshire , 574 F.3d 267, 273 (5th Cir. 2009) ).  “The exception to the ‘default rule’ …
occurs when a new pleading adds a new defendant and the allegations as to that defendant are
sufficiently distinct from those in the original pleading that they constitute a wholly new demand
as to that new defendant.” Id. (emphasis added) (citing Abshire, 574 F.3d at 273–74). “Any such
precedent must be construed narrowly because ‘Congress made clear that the one-year limitation
is intended to prevent defendants from removing state court actions after substantial litigation has
progressed in state court.’” Id. (citing Holleman v. Golden Nugget Lake Charles, LLC , No. 2:19-
00477, 2019 WL 5855770, at *4 (W.D. La. Nov. 6, 2019)).

Watts and Core. R. Doc. 6- 1, p. 2. Following settlement, AXA did not timely remit payment,

prompting Jackson to seek statutory penalties, attorneys’ fees, and costs under La. R.S. § 22:1892.
AXA asserts that Jackson needs to amend her complaint or file a new lawsuit to seek
recovery of penalties, attorneys’ fees, and costs and that she cannot seek such penalties through a
motion. R. Doc. 12, p. 5. Defendant, however, cites no law in support of its assertion. The Court,
however, has found numerous examples where statutory penalties were sought via motion. See
Bridges v. Chubb Indem. Ins. Co., 24- 593, pp. 6– 7 (La. App. 5 Cir. 7/2/25), 420 So. 3d 71, 78
(holding a “ hearing on the penalty motion”); Katie Realty, Ltd. v. Louisiana Citizens Prop. Ins.
Corp., 2012-0588, p. 3 (La. 10/16/12), 100 So. 3d 324, 327 (appealing statutory penalties awarded
based on plaintiff’s motion to enforce settlement and assess penalties); Chateau Living Ctr., L.L.C.
v. Hanover Ins. Co., No. CIV.A 06-3211, 2008 WL 4432368, at *1 (E.D. La. Sept. 25, 2008) (filing
motion in existing litigation to enforce settlement and seeking statutory penalties); Grant -Walker
v. Gen. Ins. Co. of Am., 2018-0094, p. 2 (La. App. 1 Cir. 7/3/19) (same); Culotta v. Acadiana Pest
Control, Inc., 2025- 522, p. 1 (La. App. 3 Cir. 3/4/26), 431 So. 3d 677, reh’g denied (May 13,
2026), writ denied, 2026-00765 (La. 9/22/26), 439 So. 3d 109 (same).
AXA wants this Court to consider Jackson’s motion a new action so  that its removal is
timely. This Court has previously found that seeking penalties for bad faith failure to pay an
insurance claim, in violation of La. R.S. § 22:1892 is derivative of the plaintiff’s main claim.
Johnson v. Am. Sec. Ins. Co., 650 F. Supp. 3d 483, 487–88 (E.D. La. 2023); see also Kosak v.
Louisiana Farm Bureau Cas. Ins. Co., 2020- 0222, p. 11 (La . App. 1 Cir. 12/10/20) , 316 So. 3d
522, 530–31 (citation omitted) (“[T]o recover under … La. R.S. 22:1892, a plaintiff must first have
a valid, underlying, substantive claim upon which insurance coverage is based…. The penalties
authorized by these statutes do not stand alone; they do not provide a cause of action against an
insurer absent a valid, underlying insurance claim.”).

Jackson’s request for penalties arises directly from AXA’s handling of the settlement of
the underlying negligence action. Thus, Jackson’s motion for penalties, attorneys’ fees, and costs
remains a part of that same action and does not constitute a new proceeding.
C. AXA’s Removal was Untimely
The lawsuit commenced in August of 2020. Although AXA was added as a defendant in
2025 and complete diversity did not exist until June 29, 2026, when Watts was dismissed, the one-
year limitation of 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1) had long since lapsed. AXA does not contend that
Jackson joined Watts as a defendant in the case in bad faith to prevent  removal. To the contrary,
Watts was a key defendant because he was the driver of the vehicle that collided with Jackson’s
vehicle.
Because AXA removed the case well over one year after the action commenced, AXA’s
removal is untimely under 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1).
CONCLUSION
Jackson’s claim for penalties , attorneys’ fees, and costs  under La. R.S. § 22:1892 is
derivative of the underlying negligence action and does not constitute a separate action for removal
purposes. Thus, because the suit commenced in August 2020, and AXA removed it more than one
year later, AXA has not met its burden to establish removal was timely.
Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that the motion to remand (R. Doc. 6) is GRANTED, and this case is
hereby remanded to the Civil District Court for Orleans Parish.
