II. LEGAL STANDARD
Summary judgment is appropriate under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56
“if the movant shows that there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the
movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”45  A dispute is “genuine” if it is
“real and substantial, as opposed to merely formal, pretended, or a sham.”46  Further,
a fact is “material” if it “might affect the outcome of the suit under the governing
law.”47  When assessing whether a genuine dispute regarding any material fact exists,
the Court considers “all of the evidence in the record but refrain[s] from making
credibility determinations or weighing the evidence.”48  While all reasonable
inferences must be drawn in favor of the nonmoving party, a party cannot defeat
summary judgment with conclusory allegations, unsubstantiated assertions, or “only

a scintilla of evidence.”49  Instead, summary judgment is appropriate if a reasonable

If the dispositive issue is one on which the moving party will bear the burden
of proof at trial, the moving party “must come forward with evidence which would
entitle it to a directed verdict if the evidence went uncontroverted at trial.” 51  The
non-moving party can then defeat summary judgment by either submitting evidence
sufficient to demonstrate the existence of a genuine dispute of material fact or by
“showing that the moving party’s evidence is so sheer that it may not persuade the
reasonable fact-finder to return a verdict in favor of the moving party.”52  If, however,
the nonmoving party will bear the burden of proof at trial on the dispositive issue,
the moving party may satisfy its burden by merely pointing out that the evidence in
the record is insufficient with respect to an essential element of the n onmoving
party’s claim.53  The burden then shifts to the nonmoving party who must go beyond
the pleadings and, “by her own affidavits, or by the ‘depositions, answers to
interrogatories, and admissions on file,’ designate ‘specific facts showing that there

1. Plaintiffs’ Claim against Bank and Mangipano under the Fair
Housing Act (FHA)
Plaintiffs assert a claim under the Fair Housing Act against the Bank. 55 The
Bank now moves for summary judgment on that claim, arguing that Plaintiffs’ claim
is prescribed due to the two-year statute of limitations period for claims arising under
that statute.56 Plaintiffs respond that “ the challenged housing credit structure was
not meaningfully disclosed and that  discovery, concealment, diligence, and notice
cannot be resolved by drawing Defendants’ preferred inference from the disputed

The Fair Housing Act (“FHA”) makes it unlawful “[t]o discriminate against any
person in the terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling, or in the
provision of services or facilities in connection therewith, because of race, color,
religion, sex, familial status, or national origin.”58  It likewise provides that “[i]t shall
be unlawful for any person or other entity whose business includes engaging in
residential real estate- related transactions to discriminate against any person in
making available such a transaction, or in the terms o r conditions of such a
transaction, because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national
origin.59
