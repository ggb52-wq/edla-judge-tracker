III. DISCUSSION
 Plaintiffs request the Court amend the Scheduling Order to reopen discovery for the limited
purpose of investigating the recent allegations concerning Burmaster’s past conduct. Fed. R. Civ.
P. 16(b)(4) provides that “[a scheduling order] may be modified only for good cause and with the
judge's consent.” “ The good cause standard requires the party seeking relief to show that the
deadlines [could not] reasonably be met despite the diligence of the party needing the extension.”
Leza v. City of Laredo, 496 F. App'x  375, 376 (5th Cir. 2012). In the Fifth Circuit, four factors
guide this determination: (1) “the importance of the amendment ;” (2) “the explanation for the
failure to complete discovery on time;” (3) “the potential prejudice in allowing the amendmen t;”
and (4) “the availability of a continuance to cure such prejudice.”  Id. As a general matter, courts
hesitate in reopening discovery on the eve of trial when the movant fails to provide a satisfactory
explanation for the delay. See, e.g., Turnage v. Gen. Elec. Co., 953 F.2d 206, 208 (5th Cir. 1992)
(affirming the district court ’s denial of a discovery request on the eve of trial that would have
produced relevant evidence because counsel waited two years before attempting to investigate the
issue); Leza, 496 F. App’x at 377 –78 (affirming the district court’s decision not  to reopen
discovery when the movant made no showing they were denied the opportunity to conduct the
requested discovery during the discovery period).
 Plaintiffs maintain that three re cent revelations warrant reopening discovery: (1)
Burmaster’s alleged battery upon a Kenner police officer and demotion; (2) the City’s purportedly
incomplete discovery response; and (3) Burden’s allegations in the sealed p ortion of Most ’s
declaration. After evaluating each of these developments through the governing framework, the
Court concludes that none provide good cause for reopening discovery.
A. Burmaster’s Battery Upon a Police Officer and Demotion

 Plaintiffs recently learned through Burden of Burmaster’s alleged battery upon a Kenner
police officer and demotion by NOPD . Plaintiffs ask for limited discovery to investigate these
revelations. The Court finds that good cause does not support Plaintiffs’ request.
 First, Plaintiffs have not made a strong showing that reopening discovery to investigate the
battery and demotion is important for their case. Two considerations guide the Court’s assessment
of this first factor. First, there is tenuous evidence that the alleged battery upon a Kenner police
officer and demotion occurred. Burmaster did not confirm th ese events, and Plaintiffs offer no
police report or court records indicating they took place. Plaintiffs rely entirely on the recollection
of a neighbor from two decades ago. It is unclear whether the neighbor witnessed the battery, and
his knowledge of Burmaster’s alleged demotion is based entirely on what he supposedly heard
from someone else. The limitations on hearsay do not restrict what evidence the Court can consider
in resolving this motion. But hearsay is still relevant to the weight and persuasive ness of the
evidence Plaintiffs present. A word-of-mouth account of a two-decade-old event is a shaky basis
for reopening discovery.
 Second, the relevance of the information sought is  only moderate. The domestic violence
incident occurred nearly two decades before the shooting at the center of this case. The incident
did not result in charges and did not occur while Burmaster was on duty. R. Doc. 313 at 7. Plaintiffs
have information of “at least thirty” reported incidents involving an excessive use of force filed
against Burmaster. See R. Doc. 122 at 4. These considerations suggest that while the information
sought may be helpful to some extent, it is not critical to Plaintiffs’ case.
 Next, Plaintiffs have failed to offer a satisfactory explanation for why the requested
discovery could not have taken place in the two -year period between Burmaster’s March 2023
deposition—when they first learned of t he domestic violence  incident—and the May 2025

discovery cutoff. The record presented suggests that Plaintiffs had avenues available to investigate
the incident during the discovery period  that they did not pursue. First and foremost, Plaintiffs’
counsel could have pressed Burmaster for additional details during his deposition.  Plaintiffs take
the position that the City’s knowledge of Burmaster’s history of violence is relevant to their Monell
claims. Counsel learned in deposition that Burmaster was arrested for simple battery of his spouse,
but neglected to ask whether the NOPD was aware of the incident.  R. Doc. 313 at 7. The portion
of the deposition transcript submitted to the Court shows Burmaster answered each question posed
to him candidly. Id. There is no indication that Burmaster would have withheld information about
his battery upon a Kenner officer and demotion, assuming these events actually occurred. It was
the responsibility of Plaintiffs’ counsel to elicit information, not Burmaster to voluntarily offer it.
Beyond the deposition, it appears that Plaintiffs’ counsel did not engage in any targeted efforts to
learn more about the incident. There is no record of Plaintiffs submitting public records requests,
pulling the arrest record , or asking representatives for the City or NOPD in deposition about the
police department’s knowledge of the arrest. If Plaintiffs engaged in these efforts, they have not
made it known to the Court. Consequently, the Court is left with the impression that Plaintiffs ’
counsel simply neglected to investigate what they claim is a relevant incident.
 Regarding the third factor —prejudice resulting from amen dment—the City has not
identified any prejudice that would result from reopening discovery. Accordingly, the Court finds
this factor weighs in favor of reopening discovery.
 Finally, concerning continuance, the Fifth Circuit has cautioned against granting a
continuance for missed pretrial deadlines when it would add to the opposing party’s expense and
“not deter future dilatory behavior.” Garza v. Allstate Tex. Lloyd's Co., 284 F. App'x 110, 113 (5th
Cir. 2008). Trial is less than two months away. Reopening discovery would almost certainly result
in a continuance of this case. Additional discovery and a continuance is likely to increase the cost

of litigation, particularly if it results in a discovery dispute involving the adequacy of the City’s
discovery response. And allowing Plaintiffs to conduct additional discovery would not discourage
what appears to be dilatory behavior on their part.
 In weighing these factors, the Court finds that Plaintiffs have not demonstrated good cause
for reopening discovery on the basis of the newly discovered information concerning the battery
and demotion . The Court recognizes t hat this information might have limited relevance for
Plaintiffs’ case and there is no demonstrated prejudice which would result to the City from limited
discovery. However, the Court finds it is inappropriate to impose on the City the additional expense
of a continuance and further discovery  when the evidence these events even occurred is tenuous
and Plaintiffs’ explanation for their delayed investigation is insufficient.
B. Challenge to the City’s Discovery Production

 Next, Plaintiffs accuse the City of intentionally withholding information about the arrest
when producing Burmaster’s personnel file. R. Doc. 310-1 at 4. Specifically, Plaintiffs emphasize
that the personnel file made no mention of Burmaster’s alleged assault on a police officer and
demotion. Id. As aforementioned, the only evidence these events occurred is Burden’s statement
to Plaintiffs’ counsel. This evidence is questionable for the reasons identified earlier. The City, in
its response memorandum, is adamant that it did not withhold any documentation when producing
the personnel file.  R. Doc. 313 at 4.  In the Court’s view, Burden’s statement does not provide
compelling grounds to believe the City committed discovery misconduct. The Court therefore will
not reopen discovery on this basis.
C. Sealed Allegations Raised by Burden
 Finally, t he Court  does not find that allegations made in the sealed p ortion of Most ’s
declaration su fficient to reopen discovery . The accusations concern conduct occurring twenty
years ago, and Plaintiffs have not explained its relevance to their Monell claims. If the allegations

For the foregoing reasons;
