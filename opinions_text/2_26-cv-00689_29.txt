ORDER AND REASONS

Pending before me is Plaintiff Olivia Howard’s Motion for Leave to File Opposition [to
Defendant’s Motion to Compel] Out of Time.  ECF No. 28.  Having considered the record, the
submissions, and the applicable law, Plaintiff’s Motion is DENIED for the reasons stated herein.
I. BACKGROUND
Plaintiff filed this employment discrimination suit alleging that Defendant discriminated
against her on the basis of pregnancy, in violation of Title VII and the Americans With Disabilities
Act.  ECF No. 21; see also ECF No. 1.  Plaintiff seeks lost wages and general damages, including
emotional distress damages.  ECF No. 21, at 2.  Defendant filed a Motion to Compel on September
1, 2026, and scheduled same for hearing on September 16, 2026.  ECF Nos. 25 & 25-2.
Plaintiff filed a Motion for Leave to File Opposition [to Defendant’s Motion to Compel]
Out of Time at 11:08 p.m. on Tuesday, September 15, 2026, for a matter scheduled for submission
at 10:00 am on Wednesday, September 16, 2026.  See ECF Nos. 28-6; 25-2.  The Court’s signed
Order granting the motion as unopposed was entered at 11:50 a.m. on Wednesday, September 16,
2026.  ECF No. 27.
II. ANALYSIS
This is not a situation where Plaintiff seeks to file a memorandum a day after the deadline.
Plaintiff seeks leave to file an opposition over seven days later and less than 12 hours before the

scheduled submission.  The Court has ruled on the matter.  Plaintiff’s filing is simply far too late
to be considered in response to Defendant’s motion.
Even if the Court were to consider Plaintiff’s untimely opposition, it would not change the
ruling on the motion to compel.  Plaintiff’s primary dispute lies with the scope of the medical
release.  Plaintiff seeks to limit the release of medical records to records related to the “(1) the
pregnancy relevant to this case; (2) pregnancy -related symptoms, treatment, prenatal care,
delivery, and postpartum care; (3) work restrictions . . . ; and (4) menta l-health treatment related
to postpartum depression  and emotional distress claimed in this litigation.”   ECF No. 25- 7.  As
Defendant argued in its supporting memorandum, it is entitled to explore whether Plaintiff’s “other
physical conditions of or injuries to Plaintiff are the actual or intervening cause(s) of her alleged
emotional distress.”  ECF No. 25-3, at 6.
Defendants are entitled to explore whether any other conditions or injuries could have
contributed to Plaintiff’s emotional distress and/or ability or inability to work.  Given that other
medical conditions may be relevant to the defenses in this case, and in light of the date limitation
of four years and the requests directed to specific providers, Defendant’s proposed HIPPA
authorizations are appropriate.
III. CONCLUSION
Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to File Opposition Out of Time is
DENIED.
