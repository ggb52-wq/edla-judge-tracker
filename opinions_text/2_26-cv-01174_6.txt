REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

 This 42 U.S.C. § 1983 proceeding was filed in forma pauperis  by pro se p laintiff and
pretrial detainee, Javal Anthony Green, against Defendant, Ochsner Medical Center, as the
employer of an unidentified emergency room doctor.1  Plaintiff, an inmate at the Terrebonne
Parish Criminal Justice Complex , complains that following his arrest on April 17, 2026, an
unknown emergency room doctor at Ochsner Medical Center refused to provide him medical
treatment for his breathing difficulties and dislocated bone in his shoulder  on two separate
occasions.  He alleges  that the refusal was based on plaintiff  having criminal charges for
obscenity.  He claims that the refusal to provide medical treatment violated hospital protocol.
He requests punitive damages.2
 A proceeding brought in forma pauperis  may be dismissed as frivolous under
§ 1915(e)(2)(B)(i), if the claim alleged therein has no arguable basis in law or fact, Booker v.
Koonce, 2 F.3d 114 (5th Cir. 1993), or  if it fails to state a claim upon which relief can be

 To state a cognizable claim under § 1983, a plaintiff must allege that the defendant, a
person acting under color of state law and in accordance with an established state procedure,
deprived him of the rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution or law of
the U nited States.   Flagg Bros., Inc. v. Brooks , 436 U.S. 149, 156 (1978).  Ochsner is not
considered to be a state actor for purposes of § 1983 liability.  See Wheat v. Mass, 994 F.2d
273, 275-76 (5th Cir. 1993) (a hospital does not become a state actor simply due to receipt
of public funds and being subject to state regulation); Lott v. Lopinto, Civ. Action No. 19 -
14010, 2021 WL 11550132, at *2 (E.D. La. Jan. 29, 2021) (citations omitted) (“neither
Walmart nor its employees are “considered to be acting under color of state law for purposes
of § 1983 liability.”).  A private actor may be held liable under § 1983 if the actor engaged in
“a conspiracy with state actors to violate [the plaintiff's] constitutional rights.”  Tebo v. Tebo,
550 F.3d 492, 496 (5th Cir. 2008); see Priester v. Lowndes County, 354 F.3d 414, 420 (5th Cir.
2004) (private company not liable under § 1983 unless plaintiff alleges and proves an
agreement between private and public defendants to commit an illegal act resulting in a
deprivation of the plaintiff's constitutional rights).   Here, Green has not alleged that Ochsner
or the unidentified emergency room physician3 were acting under color of state law, and his
