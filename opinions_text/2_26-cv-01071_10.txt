ORDER AND REASONS

Before the Court is a Report and Recommendation from the Magistrate Judge
recommending that Plaintiff Londell Harper’s claims be dismissed without prejudice
following mandatory screening under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1915(e)(2)(B) and 1915A.1 Harper
filed an objection.2 After de novo review of the Report and Recommendation, the
objection, the record, and applicable law, the Court adopts the  Report and
Recommendation as the opinion of the Court and overrules Harper’s objection.
Harper, who is currently incarcerated,  filed his case pro se and in forma
pauperis, alleging claims pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983 against Warden Travis Day,
Carolyn Thomas, Kristin Herbert, Lemanda Magee, Jennifer Ellington, and the
Department of Corrections.3 H is claims center on the Department’s calculation of
good time credit and time served in relation to his sentence. He seeks compensatory
and punitive damages. 4 The Magistrate Judge  recommended that (1) his claims
against defendants in their individual capacities be dismissed without prejudice to
his right to pursue habeas relief; (2) his claims against the Department of Corrections

Harper objects to the Report and Recommendation on the grounds that Day,
Thomas, Herbert, Ellington, Magee, and the Department of Corrections are not
immune.6 He fails to provide any legal support or argument in support of this
position. As the Magistrate Judge discussed, the Department of Corrections is
immune from suit under the doctrine of sovereign immunity because it is a state
agency and the State of Louisiana has not waived its sovereign immunity.7 As for the
individual defendants, these are state employees sued in their official capacities for
monetary damages. As such, and as the Report and Recommendation discusses, they
are not considered persons subject to suit under § 1983.8 Instead, a suit against a
state official in her or her official capacity is, in substance, a suit against the official’s
office and thus not a “person” against whom a § 1983 claim for money damages may
be asserted.9
Harper further objects on the ground that the applicable regulation and
defendants’ application of law relating to earned credits are arbitrary and capricious,

Travis W. Day, Carolyn Thomas, Kristin Herbert, Lemanda Magee, and Jennifer

IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that Harper’s § 1983 claims against defendants
