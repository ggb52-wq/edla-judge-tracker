trial court is free to reconsider and reverse its decision for any reason it deems

sufficient, even in the absence of new evidence or an intervening change in or

granted.” 45 The power to reconsider an interlocutory order “must be exercised

burdens and delays.” 46 Plaintiffs give no new facts, authority, or argument

warranting reconsideration of the Order. At its core, Plaintiffs’ Motion rest on the

“A genuine issue of material fact exists ‘if the evidence is such that a reasonable jury

could return a verdict for the non-moving party.’” 48 “Summary judgment is

appropriate if the non-movant ‘fails to make a showing sufficient to establish the

existence of an element essential to that party's case.’” 49 The Court is not required to

resolve all facts in favor of the non-movant at summary judgment. Speculation by the

Plaintiffs regarding execution of the documents is not sufficient factual grounds to

base a denial of summary judgment, nor is it grounds for reconsideration. 50
