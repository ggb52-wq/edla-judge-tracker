ORDER & REASONS
Before the Court is a Motion to Review Magistrate Judge’s Order filed by Plaintiff Kathy
Sherrod (“Sherrod”). R. Doc. 115. Defendants Nexion Health at Marrero, inc., d/b/a Marrero
Healthcare Center (“Marrero”) and Nexion Health Management Inc. (“Nexion”) (collectively,
“Defendants”) oppose the motion. R. Doc. 131.
The factual background of this case has been summarized in prior court orders. See R.
Docs. 15, 89. Relevant here, on July 17, 2026, Sherrod served a request for production on Nexion
attached to a Rule 30(b)(6) notice of deposition. Nexion responded to the request on July 31, 2026.
On August 17, 2026, Sherrod filed a motion to compel challenging  the sufficiency of  Nexion’s
response to her request for production.  R. Doc. 78. Sherrod maintained that the documents sought
were relevant and Nexon’s response was rife with improper, boilerplate objections . R. Doc. 78-2
at 5–6.
Sherrod’s motion to compel was set for submission on September 1, 2026. Id. Sherrod
moved to expedite consideration of her motion in light of the August 31, 2026, discovery deadline.
R. Doc. 81 . On August 31, 2026, the magistrate judge issued an order and reasons denying
Sherrod’s motion to compel. R. Doc. 98 . In her order, the magistrate judge concluded that
Sherrod’s motion was facially untimely because it was set for submission after the discovery

deadline.1 The magistrate judge further awarded Nexion reasonable expenses, including attorney’s

Sherrod moves for review of the magistrate judge’s  decision.  R. Doc. 115 . Sherrod

Nexion opposes the motion on the grounds that Sherrod’s motion to compel was untimely and an
award of attorney’s fees was therefore appropriate. R. Doc. 131 at 4–6, 9.

the Court ordered Sherrod to state in writing the documents she still sought and had yet to receive.
Sherrod complied with the Court’s order and sent a letter later that day to Nexion and the Court
requesting Nexion produce the following documentation:
1. The budget created by Nexion Health Management, Inc. for Marrero Healthcare Center
encapsulating the time period of March 1, 2023 through December 22, 2023; and

on Marrero Healthcare Center between March 1, 2023 and December 22, 2023.

Nexion responded by stating it would produce the documentation requested in Sherrod’s

Because Sherrod and Nexion have reached an agreement on the requested documentation,
the issues with respect to the documentation sought in Sherrod’s motion to compel are moot.
On the issue of expenses and fees, the magistrate judge’s awarded Nexion expenses and
attorney’s fees having determined that Sherrod’s motion to compel was untimely. The Court
reviews the magistrate judge’s decision under a clearly erroneous” standard of review, Fed. R. Civ.
P. 72(a), overturning it only if the court “is left with the definite and firm conviction that a mistake
has been committed.” Anderson v. Bessemer City, N.C., 470 U.S. 564, 565 (1985).
 Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(a)(5)(B) provides that, upon denying a motion to compel , a court must
“require the movant, the attorney filing the motion, or both to pay the party or deponent who
opposed the motion its reasonable expenses incurred in opposing the motion, including attorney’s
fees.” The court should not, however, award expenses and fees “if the motion was substantially
justified or other circumstances make an award of expenses unjust.” Id.
Fed. R. Civ. P.  16(b) directs courts to  issue scheduling orders limiting the time for
completing discovery. Applying Rule 16(b), courts routinely hold that “all disputes relative to
discovery . . . must be filed and resolved prior to the discovery deadline.” Parkcrest Builders, LLC
v. Hous. Auth. of New Orleans , No. CV 15 -1533, 2017 WL 4156499, at *2 (E.D. La. Sept. 19,
2017).
In the Court ’s view, Sherrod’s motion to compel was not untimely. While the motion to
compel was dated for submission one day after the close of discovery, Sherrod appropriately
moved for expedited submission and  consideration. R. Doc. 81 . Sherrod’s motion  was filed,
briefed, and resolved prior to the close of discovery. Because the motion was resolved within the
discovery period, a fee award on the basis of Sherrod’s supposed delay would be unjust.
