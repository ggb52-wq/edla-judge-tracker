REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

 Plaintiff, Don Lewis, a former state prisoner who is proceeding in this case pro se, filed
this federal civil action pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983.
By order dated July 3, 2025, the Court ordered Lewis to show cause why his case should
not be dismissed for the failure to timely serve the defendants.1 The Court warned Lewis that if he
failed to comply with the order to show cause , the Court would recommend that his claims be
dismissed for failure to prosecute. 2 That order was not returned as undeliverable. To date, Lewis
has not responded to the order to show cause.
In light of the foregoing, it is appropriate to dismiss Lewis’s complaint for want of
prosecution. The authority of a federal trial court to dismiss a plaintiff’s action because of failure
to prosecute is clear.  Link v. Wabash R.R., 370 U.S. 626 (1962); McCullough v. Lynaugh,
835 F.2d 1126 (5th Cir. 1988). The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure specifically provide that a
court may, in its discretion, dismiss a plaintiff’s action for failure to prosecute or for failure to
comply with the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure or any order of the court.  Fed. R. Civ. P.
41(b). The Court’s power to dismiss for want of prosecution should be used sparingly, although
it may be exercised sua sponte whenever necessary to achieve the orderly and expeditious
disposition of cases. Ramsay v. Bailey, 531 F.2d 706, 707 (5th Cir. 1976).

Because Lewis is proceeding pro se , the Court need consider only his conduct in
determining whether dismissal is proper under Rule 41(b).  As noted, Lewis has failed to respond
to the Court’s order to show cause why this case should not be dismissed for failure to timely serve
the defendants.
For these reasons, the Court is unable to advance Lewis’s claims on the docket.
Accordingly, his complaint should be dismissed for failure to prosecute.
RECOMMENDATION

It is therefore RECOMMENDED that plaintiff’s complaint be DISMISSED
WITHOUT PREJUDICE for failure to prosecute.
