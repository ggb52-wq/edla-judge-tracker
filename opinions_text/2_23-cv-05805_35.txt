I. BACKGROUND
Defendant issued discovery on September 4, 2025, in this Hurricane Ida case.  ECF No.
32, at 1.  Now a year later, despite efforts to ob tain same, Plaintiff has still not provided written
responses to same.  Id.  Further, Plaintiff has not provided availability for her deposition despite
Defendant’s repeated requests for same.  Id.
Defendant filed this Motion to Compel on August 31, 2026, and set it for submission on
September 16, 2026.  ECF Nos. 32 & 32-7.  The discovery deadline lapsed on September 3, 2026,
and a jury trial is set for December 14, 2026.  ECF No. 31.

Courts generally look to the deadline for co mpletion of discovery in considering whether

a discovery motion has been timely filed.1  “It is axiomatic that to complete discovery means that
all disputes relative to discovery . . . must be filed and resolved prior to the discovery deadline.”2
Indeed, the Fifth Circuit has confirmed the district court’s discretion to deny as untimely a motion
filed after the discovery deadline.3
When a party asks the court to  address an untimely motion to compel on the merits, the
movant must establish good cause under Rule 16(b) to extend the discovery deadline.4  Good cause
requires a showing that the relevant scheduling order deadline cannot reasonably be met “despite
the diligence of the part y needing the extension.”5  In determining whether a party has provided
good cause to seek discovery beyon d the deadline set in the Scheduling Order, courts examine

several factors: “(1) the explanation for the untimely conduct; (2) the importance of the requested
untimely action; (3) potential prejudice in allowing the untimely conduct; and (4) the availability

about the discovery, (3) whether the discovery deadline has been extended, (4) the explanation for

of the case,” (7) “any prejudice to the party fr om whom late discovery  was sought,” and (8)
“disruption of the court’s schedule.”7  The Fifth Circuit reviews the trial court’s decision to grant
or deny a motion to modify a discovery schedule for abuse of discretion.8
The governing Scheduling Order in this case required that all discovery must be completed
by September 3, 2026.  ECF No. 31.  The motion to  compel was not filed until August 31, 2026.
ECF No. 32.  Defendant did not seek expedited h earing or otherwise attempt to have the matter
resolved before the Septembe r 3, 2026, discovery deadline.  Nor does movant provide any
explanation whatsoever for the almost one-year delay between filing this motion and the October
4, 2025, deadline for Plaintiff to respond.  The motion is untimely.
Should the district judge issue a new scheduling order that re-opens discovery, Defendant
may re-file this motion to compel as it is enti tled to receive discovery responses and conduct
Plaintiff’s deposition.
