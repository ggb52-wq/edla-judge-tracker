REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION
 Pro se Plaintiff Lee Mohamed Bey  filed the above- captioned matter in this Court on
December 18, 2024 in which he sues Defendant Kimya M. Holmes .  (Rec. doc. 1).  While
largely incomprehensible and devoid of alleged facts, Plaintiff’s complaint tersely alleges that
Defendant committed “Constitutional blasphemy” and coerced a plea of guilty from him.  (Id.
at 4).
 Given the dearth of information in the Complaint, the Court ordered Plaintiff to show
cause by Thursday, January 23, 2025 as to why this case should not be summarily dismissed
for lack of subject -matter jurisdiction, for failure to state a claim on which relief may be
granted under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(i -iii), and/or on the ground of absolute judicial
immunity.  Plaintiff has responded to this Court’s show cause or der.  (Rec. doc. 4).  His
response does not cure several of the deficiencies in his Complaint noted by this Court.
 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B) provides for summary dismissal sua sponte , should the
Court determine that a case is frivolous.   Section 1915(e)(2)(B) provides in pertinent part
as follows:
(2) Notwithstanding any filing fee, or any portion thereof, that may have been
paid, the court shall dismiss the case at any time if the court determines that

28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(i)-(iii) (emphasis added).   In plain language, Section 1915 requires
dismissal if the Court is satisfied that the case fails to state a claim on which relief may be
granted.  See Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319, 325 (1989) (noting that a claim is "frivolous"
where it lacks an arguable basis either in law or in fact).
 The Court has permitted P laintiff to proceed in forma pauperis  in the instant
proceeding under the provisions of 28 U.S.C. § 1915(a).   However, summons has not issued
in order to allow the Court to review P laintiff's Complaint to determine whether it satisfies
the requirements of the federal  in forma pauperis statute.   On its face, Plaintiff's Complaint
fails to meet the requirements of the statute.  There exists no absolute right to proceed in
forma pauperis in federal civil matters; instead, it is a privilege extended to those unable to
pay filing fees when it is apparent that the claims do not lack merit on their face.  See Startii v.
United States, 415 F.2d 1115, 1116 (5th Cir. 1969).
 While it appears that Plaintiff’s response to the show cause order may state a claim
under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and the Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Co nstitution
given his allegedly coerced confession (thereby rendering this Court’s concerns over subject-
matter jurisdiction moot) , a cursory review of Plaintiff’s Complaint and his attendant
response demonstrates that his claims are frivolous, and Plaintiff has failed to  state a cause
of action against Defendant.
Courts must liberally construe pleadings filed by pro se litigants.  Erickson v. Pardus ,
551 U.S. 89, 94 (2007).  But even under the most liberal construction, Plaintiff's allegations
fail to describe a claim against Defendant:

short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to

Here, even broadly construing the Complaint as written, it appears that Plaintiff has failed to
comply with the most rudimentary pleading requirements, much less state a claim on which
relief may be granted.   Plaintiff alleges only that Defendant committed “Constitutional
blasphemy” – whatever that entails – and may have coerced a guilty plea from him .  (Rec.
doc. 1 at 4 ).  There is no other information in the Complaint or in his response to the show
cause order that would apprise Defendant of the factual circumstances underpinning
Plaintiff’s claims against her.1  This wholesale lack of information fails to meet the standards
set by the Supreme Court and the Fifth Circuit and comprise mere frivolous claims meant to
harass Defendant.
 Moreover, Defendant is a state-court judge on the Orleans Parish Criminal District
Court.  Defendant assumed her judicial seat in 2021, and the dates listed in Plaintiff’s
Complaint – 2023 and 2024 – occurred after she assumed her judicial seat.  It is well settled
that judges are protected by absolute immunity for actions taken in their judicial capacity.
See In re Foust, 310 F.3d 849, 855 (5th Cir. 2002) (citing Mays v. Sudderth, 97 F.3d 107, 109-
13 (5th Cir.  1996)).  Essential policy considerations support this grant of absolute judicial
immunity.  A judge's role in the judicial system requires that she enjoy “freedom to
determine the law unfettered by the threat of collateral attacks” against h er personally.

Mays, 97 F.3d at 111.  “The independence of the judiciary must not b e sacrificed one
microscopic portion of a millimeter, lest the fears of [legal]  intrusions cow the judge from
[her] duty.”  McAlester v. Brown , 469 F.2d 1280, 1283 (5th Cir.  1972).  Absolute immunity
applies to judicial acts within a judge's jurisdiction in suits brought under federal and state
law.  Mays, 97 F.3d at 111.  Moreover, a judge does not lose immunity even if she was in error,
acted maliciously, or acted in excess of her authority.  Id.  Rather, she loses immunity only if
her act was not a “judicial act” or was “performed in the clear absence of jurisdiction.”   Id.
Plaintiff has not alleged  that any act performed by Defendant  was not a “judicial act” or
“performed in the clear absence of jurisdiction.”   Plaintiff ’s Complaint and r esponse to the
show cause order detail the steps taken during Plaintiff’s plea in state court, none of which
appears out of the ordinary for state criminal proceedings.  Plaintiff provides no factual detail
for his frivolous allegation that Defendant coerced any confession from him.  Accordingly,
 IT IS RECOMMENDED that Plaintiff’s Complaint be DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE
for failure to state a claim on which relief may be granted under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(i-
iii) and on the ground of absolute judicial immunity.
