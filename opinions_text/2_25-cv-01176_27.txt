 ORDER AND REASONS
Before the Court is Defendant the Board of Supervisor’s of Louisiana State University and
Agricultural and Mechanical College’s (“Defendant”) Motion to Dismiss for Lack of Prosecution.1
On January 10, 2025, pro se  Plaintiff Jeffrey J. Belmont filed a Complaint against Defendant
asserting claims under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Americans with Disabilities
Act (“ADA”), the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (“GINA”), and Louisiana state
law.2
On January 15, 2026, Defendant filed a Motion to Dismiss.3 Plaintiff did not file a response
to the motion. On August 31, 2026, the Court granted the motion in part and denied the motion in
part.4 Specifically, the Court dismissed Plaintiff’s claims brought under Title I of the ADA, Title
V of the ADA, GINA, and Louisiana state law. 5 As to Plaintiff’s remaining claims, the Court
granted Plaintiff leave to amend the Complaint to cure the deficiencies identified  in the Court’s

Plaintiff did not file an amended complaint within the time prescribed by the Court.
Defendant now seeks dismissal of Plaintiff’s remaining claims based on his failure to prosecute

Rule 41(b) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure allows a court to dismiss an action or
claim upon the motion of a defendant or its own motion if “the plaintiff fails to prosecute or to
comply with [the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure] or a court order.”8 Pro se litigants are not
exempt from complying with same.9 Dismissal only appropriate where, considering the history of
the case,10 there is “(1) a clear a record of delay or contumacious conduct by the plaintiff, and (2)
when lesser sanctions would not serve the best interests of justice.”11
 Although Plaintiff is proceeding pro se, his status does not exclude his obligation to comply
with procedural rules and orders of the Court. 12 Plaintiff has failed to comply with the Court’s
August 31, 2026 Order directing him to amend the Complaint within thirty days. Moreover,
Plaintiff last participated in this litigation on October 14, 2025, and has not filed anything in the
record since that date. Thus, more than one year has elapsed without any effort by Plaintiff to
