A. Legal Standards
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure  65 governs requests for injunctive relief ,
including Plaintiffs’ request for a temporary restraining order. See FED . R.  CIV . P.
65(a)–(b); see also Parker v. Ryan , 960 F.2d 543, 545 (5th Cir. 1992) (“[T]he
requirements of [R]ule 65 apply to all injunctions.”) (internal citation omitted).
1. The Notice Requirement
Rule 65(a)(1) states that “[n]o preliminary injunction shall be issued without
notice to the adverse party.” This notice requirement “has constitutional as well as
procedural dimensions; it implies ‘a hearing in which the defendant is given a fair
opportunity to oppose the application and to prepare for such opposition.’” Parker,
960 F.2d at  544 n.1 (citations omitted).  A temporary restraining order  may be
granted ex parte (without prior notice) under limited circumstances:
(1) Issuing Without Notice. The court may issue a temporary
restraining order without written or oral notice to the adverse party or
its attorney only if:
(A) specific facts in an affidavit or a verified complaint clearly
show that immediate and irreparable injury, loss, or damage will result
to the movant before the adverse party can be heard in opposition; and
(B) the movant's attorney certifies in writing any efforts made to
give notice and the reasons why it should not be required.

FED . R.  CIV . P.  65(b)(1).

2. The Four Elements

“Temporary restraining orders are ‘extraordinary relief and rarely issued.’”
Greer’s Ranch Café v. Guzman , 540 F. Supp. 3d 638, 644 (N.D. Tex. 2021) (quoting
Albright v. City of New Orleans , 46 F. Supp. 2d 523, 532 (E.D. La. 1999)).  As the
parties seeking a temporary restraining order, Plaintiffs must “establish the same

8

four elements for obtaining a preliminary injunction.” Greer’s Ranch Café , 540 F.
Supp. 3d at 645 (internal citation and quotation marks omitted). To show entitlement
to this extraordinary remedy, t hat means Plaintiffs must show “(1) a substantial
likelihood of success on the merits, (2) a substantial threat of irreparable injury if the
[temporary restraining order] is not issued, (3) that the threatened injury if the
[temporary restraining order] is  denied outweighs any harm that will result if the
[temporary restraining order] is granted, and (4) that the grant of a[] [temporary
restraining order] will not disserve the public interest.” Mock v. Garland , 75 F.4th
563, 577 (5th Cir. 2023) (internal citation and quotation marks omitted).  “Failure to
establish any of these elements results in the denial of the motion” for a temporary
restraining order. Sacal-Micha v. Longoria, 449 F. Supp. 3d 656, 662 (S.D. Tex. 2020)
(citing Guy Carpenter & Co. v. Provenzale , 334 F.3d 459, 464 (5th Cir. 2003)).
Ultimately, as the parties seeking a temporary restraining order, Plaintiffs must
“unequivocally show the need for its issuance.” Id. (citing Valley v. Rapides Par. Sch.
Bd., 118 F.3d 1047, 1050 (5th Cir. 1997)).
This is so because such preliminary relief is “an extraordinary remedy” that a
court should not grant unless the applicant “has clearly carried the burden of
persuasion” on all four requirements. Bluefield Water Ass'n Inc. v. City of Starkville,
577 F.3d 250, 253 (5th Cir. 2009) (internal quotation marks omitted). Granting such
relief “‘is to be treated as the exception rather than the rule.’” House the Homeless v.
Widnall, 94 F.3d 176, 180 (5th Cir. 1996) (quoting Miss. Power & Light Co. v. United
Gas Pipe Line Co., 760 F.2d 618, 621 (5th Cir. 1985)).

The temporary restraining order Plaintiffs seek “is simply a highly accelerated
and temporary form of preliminary injunctive relief [.]” Greer’s Ranch Café , 540 F.

temporary restraining orders are “designed to preserve the status quo until there is
an opportunity to hold a hearing on the application for a preliminary injunction[.]”

B. Plaintiffs Fail to Demonstrate Entitlement to a TRO or Preliminary
Injunction.

Applying those principles here, Plaintiffs’ motion must be denied without
prejudice because they fail to show  that the y complied with the requisite notice
provisions, including that immediate relief is necessary and warranted  to prevent
irreparable injury, and further because they fail to show clear entitlement to such
extraordinary relief.
1. Plaintiffs failed to comply with Rule 65(b)(1).
As a threshold matter, Plaintiffs fail to comply with Rule 65(b)(1)’s basic but
fundamental requirements concerning issuing TROs without notice. There is nothing
in the record indicating that Plaintiffs’ attorney has made any efforts to give notice
to Defendants, nor are reasons provided as to why such notice should not be required.
Specifically, there is no indication in the record that the original or amended
complaints were ever served on any defendant . What is more, there is no indication

that counsel for Plaintiffs have attempted to serve or notify Defendants concerning
their request for extraordinary relief —not even a certificate of service accompan ies
Plaintiffs’ filings. Absent written certification of efforts made to provide notice (or
reasons why it should not be required), the Court may not issue a temporary
restraining order. See FED . R.  CIV . P.  65(b)(1).
Even if Plaintiffs had certified what efforts had been made to notify
Defendants, Rule 65(b)(1) authorizes issuance of a temporary restraining order
without notice only where specific sworn facts “clearly show that immediate and
irreparable injury, loss, or damage will result to the movant before the adverse party
can be heard in opposition[.]”  Id. Plaintiffs have failed to satisfy this essential
prerequisite. Though Plaintiffs indicate concern regarding H&E’s ability to access its
data in ServiceTitan , for example, Jessica Beckham’s sworn assertions fail to
implicate the immediacy or irreparable injury  requirements. Ms. Beckham’s
assertions that certain changes to accounting or payroll functions of H&E’s Intuit
account “would interfere” with business operations and that the transition from
Quickbooks to Enterprise Suite “caused [her] concern about further changes affecting
H&E’s ability to operate” are generalized concerns that do not implicate the
immediacy or irreparable injury requirements. Ms. Beckham’s assertions and
concerns regarding H&E’s data being rendered inaccessible by ServiceTitan by the
end of this month come close r in relative terms  to meeting the immediacy
requirement but fall short of satisfying it, considering that is over three weeks away.

Likewise, Ms. Beckham’s  assertions of harm stop short of meeting
“irreparability” insofar as she fails to indicate that H&E’s data will be irretrievable

III . CONCLUSION
Plaintiffs fail to satisfy Rule 65(b)(1)(A) or (B) when both are essential
prerequisites to issuing a temporary restraining order of the sort requested here, that
is, without notice. Furthermore, on this briefing and record, Plaintiffs fail to provide
support for entitlement to “immediate protection” of their banking and accounting
systems and fail to indicate that their access to their business records on the
ServiceTitan platform cannot be restored through resort to less extraordinary
measures, or at least upon notice to Defendants and following an opportunity to be
heard. Among other shortcomings, Plaintiffs fail to offer concrete evidence clearly
demonstrating the requisite injury. Rule 65 and the case law applying it demands
more. Plaintiffs have failed to carry their burden to unequivocally show the need for
this Court to issue a TRO to restrain Defendants from taking (or compel them to take)
certain actions before the Court could hold a hearing on a properly supported
application for preliminary injunctive relief.  Having failed to clearly support their
application for preliminary injunctive relief, they will have an opportunity to re-urge
a motion that complies with the rules and case law. Accordingly,
