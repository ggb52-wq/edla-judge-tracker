ORDER AND REASONS
Before the Court in this civil action and adversary proceeding are four motions
to transfer venue.1 The Chapter 7 trustee of seven jointly administered debtor estates
filed this proceeding in this Court, where the business he says the defendants looted
was organized and run. The consolidated bankruptcy case is pending in the Southern
District of Texas. Twenty-five of the twen ty-six named defendants ask the Court to
send the proceeding there, invoking 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a), 28 U.S.C. § 1412, or both.
The Fifth Circuit has not decided which of those statutes governs the transfer
of a proceeding related to a bankruptcy case , and district courts within the circuit
have reached contrary results. The Court need not take a side, because the movants
have not carried their burden under either. The Southern District of Texas is a
district in which this proceeding might have been brought, but that establishes only
that transfer is permissible. It does not establish that transfer is warranted.
Accordingly, for these reasons and those that follow, the Court DENIES  all
four motions.

II. ANALYSIS
A. Legal Standards.
Section 1404(a) provides that “[f]or the convenience of parties and witnesses,
in the interest of justice, a district cour t may transfer any civil action to any other
district or division where it might have been brought or to any district or division to
which all parties have consented.” 28 U.S. C. § 1404(a). The party seeking transfer
“must show good cause.” In re Volkswagen of Am., Inc. , 545 F.3d 304, 315 (5th Cir.
2008) (Volkswagen II) (en banc) (quoting Humble Oil & Refining Co. v. Bell Marine
Serv., Inc., 321 F.2d 53, 56 (5th Cir. 1963)). To do  so, it “must satisfy the statutory
requirements and clearly demonstrate that a transfer is ‘[f]or the convenience of
parties and witnesses, in the interest of justice.’” Id. (quoting 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a)).
That burden “reflects the appropriate defe rence to which the plaintiff’s choice of
venue is entitled.” Id. “Thus, when the transferee venue is not clearly more convenient

“[W]hether a § 1404(a) venue transfer is  for the convenience of parties and
witnesses and in the interest of justice” tu rns on four “private” interest factors and
four “public” interest factors. Id. “The private interest factors are: ‘(1) the relative
ease of access to sources of proof; (2) the availability of compulsory process to secure
the attendance of witnesses; (3) the cost of attendance for willing witnesses; and (4)
all other practical problems that make trial of a case easy, expeditious and
inexpensive.’” Id. (quoting In re Volkswagen AG , 371 F.3d 201, 203 (5th Cir. 2004)
(Volkswagen I)). “The public interest factors are: ‘(1) the administrative difficulties
flowing from court congestion; (2) the loca l interest in having localized interests
decided at home; (3) the familiarity of th e forum with the law that will govern the
case; and (4) the avoidance of unnecessary pr oblems of conflict of laws [or in] the
application of foreign law.’” Id. (quoting Volkswagen I, 371 F.3d at 203).
The factors “are not necessarily exhaustive or exclusive,” and “none . . . can be
said to be of dispositive weight.” Id. (quoting Action Indus., Inc. v. U.S. Fid. & Guar.
Corp., 358 F.3d 337, 340 (5th Cir. 2004)). District courts may not conduct “a raw
counting of the factors in each side, weighi ng each the same and deciding transfer
only on the resulting ‘score[.]’” In re Radmax, Ltd. , 720 F.3d 285, 290 n.8 (5th Cir.
2013). Even so, “the standard is not met by  showing one forum is more likely than
not to be more convenient, but instea d the party must adduce evidence and
arguments that clearly establish good caus e for transfer based on convenience and

justice.” Def. Distrib. v. Bruck , 30 F.4th 414, 433 (5th  Cir. 2022) (citing Volkswagen
II, 545 F.3d at 314–15). And “[w]here there is  no demonstration by the movant, let
alone a clear one, the court cannot weigh a factor against the non-movant and in favor
of transfer.” Id. at 434.
Section 1412 provides that “[a] district court may transfer a case or proceeding
under title 11 to a district court for another district, in the interest of justice or for
the convenience of the parties.” 28 U.S.C. § 1412. It differs from  § 1404(a) in three
respects. First, § 1412 does not require that the transferee district be one where the
action “might have been brought.” Cf. 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). Second, § 1412 is
disjunctive, so a movant need only establish that transfer is either “in the interest of
justice” or “for the convenience of the parties.”  Marquette Transp. Co. v. Trinity
Marine Prods., Inc., No. 06-826, 2006 WL 2349461, at *3 (E.D. La. Aug. 11, 2006); see
also In re Directory Distrib. Assocs., Inc., 566 B.R. 869, 878 (B ankr. S.D. Tex. 2017).
And third, the movant’s burden is a “preponderance of the evidence” rather than a
clear demonstration. Marquette Transp. Co., 2006 WL 2349461, at *3 (quoting In re
Commonwealth Oil Ref. Co., Inc., 596 F.2d 1239, 1241 (5th Cir. 1979)).
“Regardless of whether transfer is sought pursuant to Section 1412 or Section
1404, the principal considerations are the convenience of the parties and the interests
of justice.” Union Oil Co. of California v. Shaffer, 563 B.R. 191, 204–05 (E.D. La. 2016)
(citation omitted). Courts in this circuit assess the interest of justice under § 1412 by
reference to six factors:
(a) Efficiency and economics of esta te administration; (b) Presumption
in favor of the “home court”; (c) Judicial economy and efficiency; (d)

No. 05-3461, 2006 WL 213951, at *1 (S.D. Tex.  Jan. 25, 2006)). Convenience of the

ease of access to necessary proof; (c) conv enience of witnesses; (d) availability of

witnesses.” Directory Distrib. Assocs. , 566 B.R. at 878 (quoting Campbell, 2015 WL
3657627, at *3).
The Court’s analysis proceeds as follows. The Court first concludes that it may
resolve all four motions under both statutes without deciding which governs. It then
determines that this proceeding might have been brought in the Southern District of
Texas, which satisfies the threshold that  § 1404(a) imposes and § 1412 does not. It
next finds that the movants have not clea rly demonstrated under § 1404(a) that the
transferee district is more convenient. It finally concludes that the movants have not
shown by a preponderance under § 1412 that transfer would serve either the interest
of justice or the convenience of the parties.

E. The Movants Have Not Shown by a Preponderance Under 28 U.S.C.

1. The Interest of Justice Does not Support Transfer.
The efficiency and economics of estate administration weigh against
transfer. This factor is “the most important consideration” in the § 1412 transfer
analysis, Commonwealth Oil, 596 F.2d at 1247, and measur es what transfer would
cost or save the estate. See Directory Distrib. Assocs., 566 B.R. at 880; In re Think3,
Inc., 529 B.R. 147, 209 (Bankr. W.D. Tex. 2015). Fo r example, it favored transfer in
Directory Distributing, where the debtor itself moved and would “save precious estate
funds litigating the Texas Proceeding in its hometown, thereby maximizing the
distribution to creditors.” 566 B.R. at 879–80. It cut against transfer in Think3, where
the estate’s successor opposed transfer and the “cost to Plaintiff Trust . . . would be
higher if th[e] adversary proceeding were  transferred,” which “will likely reduce the
recovery (if any) to creditors.” 529 B.R. at 209. In each, the party speaking for the
estate supplied evidence of what the estate would spend.
The movants offer nothing to support that the Southern District of Texas would
be a more cost-efficient forum for the esta te. They do not identify an expense the
estate would avoid in Houston, a professional whose rates or travel would change, or
any sum the estates would save. What they offer instead is a conclusory assertion
that “adjudicating the Adversary Proceeding in the same federal district as the
