REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION
 Pro se plaintiff Simon Amunga Nasio  filed this lawsuit  alleging securities fraud against
FBS Markets Inc., an international brokerage firm.  However, based on the facts alleged in the
Complaint, this dispute has no connection to the Eastern District of Louisiana and the defendant
has no contacts with this jurisdiction. Accordingly, it is RECOMMENDED that this lawsuit be
DISMISSED for improper venue and for lack of personal jurisdiction.
Background
Mr. Nasio filed this lawsuit in September 2025. Mr. Nasio alleges that he is an individual
investor who transacted with defendant FBS Markets Inc. through its trading platforms. He alleges
that FBS fraudulently uses “U.S. securities and related financial instruments to conduct
manipulative trading operations in violation of federal securities laws, the Commodity Exchange
Act, and related statutes.” Rec. Doc 9, at 2. He admits that FBS bars U.S. residents and purportedly
operates outside the jurisdiction of the United States. He complains that FBS nonetheless relies on
U.S. securities, U.S. market data, and U.S. -based liquidity providers to conduct its business. Mr.
Nasio alleges that FBS’s pricing deviates materially from actual U.S. market benchmarks. And he
alleges that FBS profits by “skimming, stop -hunting, and using off-market spreads derived from
concealed U.S. liquidity provider data while denying Plaintiff and similarly situated traders
accurate access to U.S. securities pricing.” He claims that FBS h as violated Section 10(b) of the

 The  general venue statute at 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) provides:
A civil action may be brought in -- (1) a judicial district in which any defendant
resides, if all defendants are residents of the State in which the district is located;
(2) a judicial district in which a substantial part of the events or omissions giving
rise to the claim occurred, or a substantial part of property that is the subject of the
action is situated; or (3) if there is no district in which an action may otherwise be
brought as provided in this section, any judicial district in which any defenda nt is
subject to the court’s personal jurisdiction with respect to such action.

When a case has been filed in the wrong venue, the court must “dismiss, or if it be in the interest
of justice, transfer such case to any district or division in which it could have been brought.”  28
U.S.C. § 1406(a). A court may address venue sua sponte or on motion. Caldwell v. Palmetto State
Sav. Bank of S.C., 811 F.2d 916, 919 (5th Cir. 1987); see Collins v. Evans, No. 25-CV-0079, 2025
WL 3698410, at *4 (W.D. La. Dec. 3, 2025), report and recommendation adopted, No. 3:25-CV-
00079, 2025 WL 3698405 (W.D. La. Dec. 19, 2025).
According to the Complaint, defendant FBS is a foreign entity that purports to operate
outside of the jurisdiction of the United States by banning U.S. residents from using its services.
There is no basis to conclude that FBS resides in this district as provided in subsection (1).
Mr. Nasio does not allege where the incidents giving rise to his purported claims occurred
to establish venue pursuant to subsection (2). But there is no reason to believe they occurred in the
Eastern District of Louisiana. Even if the events alleged occurred where Mr. Nasio resides, it
appears that Mr. Nasio does not reside in Louisiana. Although he has provided an address in
Louisiana, the address provided is a building at Nichols State University. Mr. Nasio is the plaintiff
in a suit he filed in the Central District of California in July 2025. Nasio v. University of the People,
No. 25-cv-7183 (C.D. Cal. July 26, 2025). There he provided a homeless shelter in Pasadena,
California as his address. He is also the plaintiff in a lawsuit filed in the Western District of Texas
in March 2025. Nasio v. United States Department of Defense , No. 4:25-cv-14 (W.D. Tex. Mar.

2. Personal Jurisdiction
A judgment entered without personal jurisdiction is void. Sys. Pipe & Supply, Inc. v. M/V
VIKTOR KURNATOVSKIY, 242 F.3d 322, 323 (5th Cir. 2001). A court may consider whether
it has personal jurisdiction over the defendant, as long as the court allows the “plaintiff a reasonable
opportunity to present any available evidence supporting the court's jurisdiction.” Id.  at 325.
“A federal court may exercise personal jurisdiction over a non -resident defendant where,
(1) the long-arm statute of the forum state confers personal jurisdiction over that defendant, and
the exercise of such jurisdiction comports with due process under the United States Constitution.”
In re Chinese Manufactured Drywall Prods. Liab. Litig., 767 F. Supp. 2d 649, 656 (E.D. La. 2011).
“The limits of the Louisiana long -arm statute are coextensive with constitutional due process
limits.” Jackson v. Tanfoglio Giuseppe, S.R.L., 615 F.3d 579, 584 (5th Cir. 2010)
Due process in the personal jurisdiction context requires that, (1) the defendant has
purposefully availed itself of the benefits and protections of the forum state by
establishing minimum contacts with the forum state, and (2) the exercise of
jurisdiction over the defendant does not offend traditional notions of fair play and
substantial justice.

Chinese Drywall , 767 F. Supp. 2d at 656. Courts may exercise general jurisdiction when the
defendant “‘has continuous and systematic general business contacts' with the forum state,” and
the court may exercise specific jurisdiction “‘in a suit arising out of or related t o the defendant's
contacts with the forum.’” Luv N' care, Ltd. v. Insta-Mix, Inc., 438 F.3d 465, 469 (5th Cir. 2006)
(quoting Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia, S.A. v. Hall, 466 U.S. 408, 415 n. 8, 416 (1984).
As noted above with regard to venue, Mr. Nasio  has alleged no contacts of FBS with
Louisiana or this district. There is no basis on which to find this Court has personal jurisdiction
over FBS.
Conclusion and Recommendation
 On the complaint as written, this is not the proper venue for Mr. Nasio’s claims and this
Court lacks personal jurisdiction over FBS. Accordingly,
 IT IS RECOMMENDED  that this lawsuit be DISMISSED without prejudice for
improper venue and for lack of personal jurisdiction.
