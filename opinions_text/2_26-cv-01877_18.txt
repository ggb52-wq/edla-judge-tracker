LEGAL STANDARD
Generally, a defendant may remove a civil state court action to federal
court if the federal court has original jurisdiction over the action.1 The burden
is on the removing party to show “that federal jurisdiction exists and that
removal was proper.” 2 When determining whether federal jurisdiction exists,
courts consider “the claims in the state court petition as they existed at the
time of removal.”3

LAW AND ANALYSIS
 Defendant removed this matter to this Court on the basis of diversity
jurisdiction. Diversity jurisdiction requires complete diversity of citizenship
and an amount in controversy that exceeds $75,000. 4 It is undisputed that
Plaintiff is a citizen of Texas, Defendant is a citizen of Louisiana and Alaska,
and the amount in controversy is $426,522.04. Accordingly, Plaintiff does not
dispute that diversity jurisdiction is present. Rather, Plaintiff argues that the
forum defendant rule prohibits removal of this matter.
Under 28 U.S.C. § 1441(b)(2), or what is sometimes called the “forum-
defendant rule,” “[a] civil action otherwise removable solely on the basis of
[diversity jurisdiction] may not be removed if any of the parties in interest

 The Louisiana Binding Arbitration Act provides that:
Notice of a motion to vacate, modify, or correct an award shall be
served upon the adverse party or his attorney within three months
after the award is filed or delivered, as prescribed by law for service
of a motion in an action. For the purposes of the motion any judge,
who might issue an order to stay the proceedings in an action
brought in the same court may issue an order, to be served with
the notice of motion, staying the proceedings of the adverse party
to enforce the award.8
In reliance on this provision, Plaintiff argues that service of its Motion to
Vacate Arbitration Award was properly done in the mann er of service of a
motion in compliance with Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure Article 1313,
which applies to “every pleading subsequent to the original petition.” Article
1313 provides for service by electronic mail, stating:
A. Except as otherwise provided by law, every pleading subsequent
to the original petition, and every pleading which under an express
provision of law may be served as provided in this Article, may be
served either by the sheriff or by: . . . (4) Transmitting a copy by
electronic means to counsel of record, or if there is no counsel of
record, to the adverse party, at the number or addresses expressly
designated in a pleading or other writing for receipt of electronic
service.
Plaintiff argues that it therefore properly effected service of its Motion by
emailing the filing to Defendant’s arbitration counsel.
 Defendant, on the other hand, argues that because Plaintiff’s
Petition/Motion to Vacate Arbitration Award was the original filing in the state

does not authorize service by email.
 This Court, however, need not resolve the issue of whether service of
Plaintiff’s filing should have been completed under Article 1313 or 1314
because Plaintiff’s email to Defendant’s arbitration counsel was not proper
service under either article. 9 Article 1313 allows for electronic service “to
counsel of record, or if there is no counsel of record, to the adverse party, at the
number or addresses expressly designated in a pleading or other writing for
receipt of electronic service.” Here, there was not yet counsel of record in the
state court matter and no email address had been expressly designated in a
pleading for receipt of electronic service. Plaintiff’s email to Defendant’s
arbitration counsel therefore did  not satisfy the requirements for electronic
service under Article 1313, and Article 1314 does not allow for electronic
service. Accordingly, Defendant had not been formally serviced prior to
removal, and its removal was therefore not procedurally defective.

CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, the Motion is DENIED.
