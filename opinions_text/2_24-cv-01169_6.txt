Law and Analysis
There exists no absolute right to proceed in forma pauperis in federal civil matters; instead,
it is a privilege extended to those unable to pay filing fees when it is apparent that the claims do
not lack merit on their face. See Startii v. United States, 415 F.2d 1115, 1116 (5th Cir.1969); see
also Adepegba v. Hammons, 103 F.3d 383, 387 (5th Cir. 1996) (noting that the revocation of the
privilege of proceeding in forma pauperis is not new).
 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B) provides for summary dismissal sua sponte, should the Court
determine that a case is frivolous.  Section 1915(e)(2)(B) provides in pertinent part as follows:

28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(i) -(iii) (emphasis added).  “[A] complaint fails to state a claim upon
which relief may be granted when it does not contain ‘sufficient factual matter, accepted as true,
to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.’” Rogers v. Boatright, 709 F.3d 403, 407 (5th
Cir. 2013) (quoting Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009)). A complaint is frivolous “if it
lacks an arguable basis in law or fact.” Reeves v. Collins, 27 F.3d 174, 176 (5th Cir. 1994).
 Furthermore, federal courts are courts of limited jurisdiction.  “Subject matter jurisdiction
may not be waived, and the district court ‘shall dismiss the action’ whenever ‘it appears by
suggestion of the parties or otherwise that the court lacks jurisdiction of the subject matter.’” Avitts
v. Amoco Prod. Co., 53 F.3d 690, 693 (5th Cir.1995) (quoting Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(h)(3)).
 Plaintiff names eight defendants:  judges, court staff, attorneys , and a police captain . Yet
his complaint contains not a single factual allegation implicating any one of them. His statement
of claim appears to seek relief from Bill Hood Auto Group and Ford, who are not named as
defendants. He has not offered any clarity as to the facts unde rlying his claims in his response to
the Court’s show cause order. As such, the Court must find that plaintiff has failed to state a claim
upon which relief could be granted as to the named defendants.
 Even if plaintiff had intended to name Bill Hood Aut o Group and Ford  as the sole
defendants in this action, it appears this Court would lack subject matter jurisdiction over any
claims he has against them. Plaintiff invokes this  Court’s federal-question jurisdiction under 28
U.S.C. § 1331. He purports to assert claims under RICO. “In order to state a claim under 18 U.S.C.

§ 1962, a plaintiff must allege: 1) the conduct; 2) of an enterprise; 3) through a pattern; 4) of
racketeering activity.” Elliott v. Foufas, 867 F.2d 877, 880 (5th Cir. 1989). As to Bill Hood Auto
Group and Ford, plaintiff has not alleged any enterprise or pattern of racketeering activity. It does
not appear that he has any basis for a RICO claim. 1 Nor does there appear to be any other federal
statute providing him a cause of action.
 This Court also has jurisdiction to consider state law claims where the complaint satisfies
the requirements of 28 U.S.C. § 1332 . However, plaintiff here has not alleged diversity
jurisdiction. In order to exercise diversity jurisdiction over state law claims under § 1332, the
citizenship of all plaintiffs must be different from the citizenship of all defendants and the amount
in controversy must be over $75,000. 28 U.S.C. § 1332; see Getty Oil Corp., Div. of Texaco, Inc.
v. Ins. Co. of North Am. , 841 F.2d 1254, 1258-59 (5th Cir. 1988) . To the extent that Bill Hood
Auto Group is a Louisiana corporation or a limited liability company with Louisiana members,
diversity jurisdiction would be destroyed. Plaintiff has offered no basis to conclude that Bill Hood
Auto Group has non -Louisiana citizenship. Furthermore, the damages sought by plaintiff do not
appear to reach the required threshold of $75,000. There is no basis for this Court to exercise
diversity jurisdiction over any claims against Bill Hood Auto Group and Ford even if these entities
had been named as defendants.
Accordingly,
IT IS RECOMMENDED that this lawsuit be DISMISSED for failure to state a claim as
to the named defendants and for lac k of subject-matter jurisdiction as to any claims against Bill
Hood Auto Group and Ford.
