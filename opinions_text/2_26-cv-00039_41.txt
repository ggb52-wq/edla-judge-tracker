ORDER AND REASONS
Before the Court is pro se Plaintiff Bobby Rand Golden, Jr.’s Motion to Reopen
Case (Rec. Doc. 39). For the following reasons, the Court construes Plaintiff’s motion
as a motion for post-judgment relief under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b) and
DENIES the motion.

I. Background
On August 20, 2026, Magistrate Judge Eva J. Dossier entered a Report and
Recommendations (“Report”) as to the above-captioned action, recommending that
Plaintiff’s § 1983 claims against Sergeant Ford, “Jefferson Parish Correctional Center
Medical Personnel,” and “Jefferson Parish Correctional Center Security
Administration”, as well as his § 1983 claims of unlawful arrest, excessive force during
search/arrest, and unlawful seizure of vehicle against Detective Charles Carollo,
Detective Carl Marshall, Detective Patrick Evans, Sergeant Josh Bermudez, Derek
Green, Keith Branchcomb, Blake Holliefield, and Sergeant Julio Alvarado, be dismissed
with prejudice as they are frivolous and/or fail to state a claim on which relief may be
granted. (R. Doc. 31 at 28). The Report further recommended that Plaintiff’s § 1983
claims of food tampering, foul language, and deliberate indifference to serious medical
needs, and his request that defendants be criminally prosecuted be dismissed with
prejudice as they are frivolous and/or fail to state a claim on which relief may be
granted. (Id.) The Report also recommended that Plaintiff’s request for habeas relief be

II. Legal Standard and Analysis
 Plaintiff’s motion does not reference any legal authority that would entitle him to
the relief he seeks. Because Plaintiff is seeking to reopen the case after it was dismissed,
the Court construes Plaintiff’s motion as seeking relief under Federal Rule of Civil
Procedure Rule 60(b), which allows the Court to relieve a party from a final judgment or
order under certain circumstances. Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure Rule 60(b)(1),
relief is available if the movant can show “mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable
neglect.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(1).  Additionally, under the catch-all provision in Rule
60(b)(6), the Court may relieve a party from a final order for “any other reason that
justifies relief,” but this clause is reserved for exceptional circumstances. See Fed. R. Civ.

P. 60(b)(6); D’Aquin v. HANO, No. CV 25-1165, 2025 WL 3281768, at *1 (E.D. La. Nov.
25, 2025).
 The Fifth Circuit has instructed the district courts to give pro se papers “a liberal
construction.” Brown v. Sudduth, 675 F.3d 472, 477 (5th Cir. 2012). However, even pro
se litigants must comply with procedural rules, and ignorance of the law is not a basis
for Rule 60(b)(1) relief. Vafaiyan v. City of Wichita Falls, 398 Fed.Appx. 989, 990 (5th
Cir. 2010) (cleaned up). “The burden of establishing at least one of the Rule 60(b)
requirements is on [Plaintiff] as the movant.” Bahsoon v. Wells Fargo Bank, NA, No.
3:12-cv-2017-D, 2013 WL 1831786, at *1 (N.D. Tex. May 1, 2013) (internal citations
omitted). Implicit in the fact that Rule 60(b)(1) affords extraordinary relief is the
requirement that the movant make a sufficient showing of unusual or unique
circumstances justifying such relief. D’Aquin, 2025 WL 3281768, at *1 (cleaned up).
 Construing Plaintiff’s motion liberally in light of his pro se status, the Court finds
that Plaintiff has not shown that any of the Rule 60(b) factors are present. Plaintiff’s pro
se status does not relieve him from complying with relevant procedural rules,
substantive law, or the court’s orders. Further, even construing the instant motion as
Plaintiff’s objections to the magistrate’s Report, the “objections” consist entirely of
similar iterations of what appear to be the same facts from the original filings,
previously reviewed and considered by the Court in adopting the magistrate’s Report in
its entirety. It is not until the last paragraph of his motion that Plaintiff appears to
address his failure to provide timely objections to the Report and Recommendations,
alleging that after being released from Northlake Behavioral Health System, he “was not
set another Court date,” and that he “file[d] for a motion of extension of time while
being incarcerated at [N]orthlake.” (R. Doc. 39 at 17). Notably, notice of the Court’s
order granting Plaintiff’s motion for extension of time to reply/file objections to the
Report was mailed to Plaintiff and was not returned as undeliverable. Regardless of his

Accordingly;
IT IS ORDERED that the Motion to Reopen Case (Rec. Doc. 39), filed by
Plaintiff, Bobby Rand Golden, Jr., is DENIED.
