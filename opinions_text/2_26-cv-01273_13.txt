II. Standard of Review
If service is not timely waived, a defendant must serve an answer within 21 days after being
served with the summons and complaint. FED. R. CIV. P. 12(a)(1)(A).
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 55(a) provides that “[w]hen a party against whom a
judgment for affirmative relief is sought has failed to plead or otherwise defend, and that failure is
shown by affidavit or otherwise, the clerk must enter the party’s default.” FED. R. CIV. P. 55(a).
However, according to Rule 55(c), the court may set aside an entry of default for “good
cause.” FED. R. CIV. P. 55(c). The party challenging the entry of default bears the burden of proving
good cause. Sindhi v. Raina, 905 F.3d 327, 332 (5th Cir. 2018) (citation omitted). Whether to set
aside an entry of default “lies within the sound discretion of the district court.” U.S. v. One Parcel
of Real Prop., 763 F.2d 181, 183 (5th Cir. 1985).
On a motion to set aside an entry of default, the district court should consider “whether the
default was willful, whether setting it aside would prejudice the adversary, and whether a
meritorious defense is presented.”  Id.; In re Chinese Manufactured Drywall Prods. Liab. Litig. ,
742 F.3d 576, 594 (5th Cir. 2014) . Additionally, the court may consider other factors such as
“whether the party acted expeditiously to correct the default.” Walsh v. Eco Roof and Solar, Inc.,
No. 24-2153, 2026 WL 672750, at *3 (E.D. La. Mar. 10, 2026).
“[T]he standard for setting aside a default decree is less rigorous than setting aside a
judgment for excusable neglect.” One Parcel, 763 F.2d at 183. “The Fifth Circuit has adopted a
policy in favor of resolving cases on their merits and against the use of default judgments.”
Hernandez v. Cajun Bourbon, LLC, No. 25-2015, 2026 WL 709681, at *2 (E.D. La. Mar. 13, 2026)
(quoting Roger v. Hartford Life & Acc. Ins. Co., 167 F.3d 933, 936 (5th Cir. 1999)).

III. Analysis
Five Happiness argues that good cause exists to set aside the clerk’s entry of default. Five
Happiness argues that its failure to respond was not willful , that it has a meritorious defense to
Plaintiff’s claims, and that Plaintiff would not be prejudiced if the entry of default were to be set
aside. Five Happiness specifically contends service of process was effectuated improperly because
the summons referred to Defendant as “Five Happiness, Inc.” rather than “Five Happiness, LLC.”
Plaintiff counters these arguments by contending that Five Happiness has not alleged facts
specific enough to satisfy its burden of proving good cause. Plaintiff urges the Court to order Five
Happiness to specify the facts which led to its failure to timely respond.
In response, Five Happiness reasserts its arguments and specifically contends that the entry
of default should be set aside because it was not properly served with Plaintiff’s Complaint. R.
Doc. 11 at 1. In support of this argument, Five Happiness provides a copy of its registration as a
limited liability company with the Louisiana Secretary of State . R. Doc. 11 -1. According to the
records provided by Five Happiness, the business changed its registration from a corporation to a
limited liability company in 2009. Id. The alleged records also provide the name and address of
Defendant’s agent for service of process, Xikai Yao (“Yao”). Id.
The two factors which are determinative and have particular significance in a court’s
determination of whether to set aside an entry of default are (1) whether the default was willful,
and (2) whether the defendant presents “a meritorious defense sufficien t to support a finding on
the merits for the defaulting party.” Walsh, 2026 WL 672750, at *3 (citing In re OCA, Inc. , 551
F.3d 359, 370 (5th Cir. 2008) (citation omitted)).
Relevant here, if service of process is not proper, “the court lacks jurisdiction over a
defendant and an entry of default granted under such conditions is void.” Conwill v. Greenberg

A. Willfulness
“A willful default is an ‘intentional failure’ to respond to litigation.”  Walsh, 2026 WL
672750, at *4 (citing In re OCA , 551 F.3d at 370 n.32). If the court finds that a defendant
intentionally failed to file responsive pleadings, the inquiry ends and the entry of default will not
be set aside. Id. (quoting Wooten v. McDonald Transit Assocs., Inc. , 788 F.3d 490, 500 (5th Cir.
2015). In all other instances, the court must examine whether there has been “excusable neglect.”
Young v. Telerecovery Corp., No. 19-11731, 2020 WL 6685008, at *2 (citing CJC Holdings, Inc.
v. Wright & Lato, Inc., 979 F.2d 60, 64 (5th Cir. 1992)).
Five Happiness contends that its failure to respond was not willful, but instead was due to
its own mistake, inadvertence, and excusable neglect. Five Happiness contends that it thought it
had more time to file responsive pleadings . In support of this argument, Five Happiness alleges
that it was served process on August 31, 2026. R. Doc. 8-2 at 1. However, according to the docket
for the instant action, Five Happiness , Inc., not Five Happiness, LLC,  was allegedly served with
process on August 27, 2026 . If the service was accurate then the deadline to file responsive
pleadings would have been September 17, 2026.
Five Happiness contends that it was not properly served with process, and this Court has
previously determined that defective or unresolved issues with service of process render a
defendant’s default not willful. See Damond v. Experian Info. Sols., Inc., No. 25-1570, 2025 WL
3980883, at *7 (E.D. La. Dec. 12, 2025); J&J Sports Prods., Inc. v. Bundee’s, Inc., No. 18-3480,
2019 WL 5102239, at *2 (E.D. La. Oct. 11, 2019); Badeaux v. Grand Isle Marina Constr., LLC ,

No. 17-4984, 2019 WL 1755523, at *3 (E.D. La. Apr. 18, 2019); Vega v. Autumnwood Homes,
Inc., No. 16-751, 2016 WL 6649215, at *2-3 (E.D. La. Nov. 9, 2016). For reasons not asserted by
the Defendant, the Court finds that service of process was not accurate.
According to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(h)(1), an unincorporated association, such
as a limited liability company, must be served either: (A) “in the manner prescribed by Rule 4(e)(1)
for serving an individual” (i.e., following the applicable state law for service, which in Louisiana
includes personally delivering copies of the summons and complaint to the individual, leaving the
copies at the individual's dwelling or usual place of abode with a person of suitable age  and
discretion who resides there, or delivering the copies to an agent authorized by appointment, law,
or mandate to receive service of process); or (B) “by delivering copies of the summons and
complaint to an officer, a managing or general agent, or any other agent authorized by appointment
or by law. . . .” FED. R. CIV. P. 4(h)(1).
Five Happiness contends that the entry of default should be set aside  because service was
not effectuated on the correct entity, Five Happiness, LLC, but instead on  its previous identity,
Five Happiness, Inc.  However, this Court has held that the misnomer of a corporation —or
unincorporated association, as is the case here—in a summons which does not injure any party is
generally immaterial. See Young, 2020 WL 6685008, at *3 (citing Grandey v. Pacific Indem. Co.,
217 F.2d 27, 29 (5th Cir. 1954)). Five Happiness does not contend that it was injured by Plaintiff’s
misnaming on the summons. Therefore, the Court finds that Plaintiff’s misnaming of the
Defendant does not invalidate service of process.  However, Defendant’s belief that it had a good
defense in insufficient service of process favors the conclusion that its default was not willful. See
Hunt Valley Restoration, LLC, 2023 WL 3159401, at *3 (finding that a defendant’s failure to act

was not willful because although defendant was mistaken, it  believed it had a good defense in
insufficient service of process).
The parties’ arguments aside, the Court independently finds that service of process on Five
Happiness, LLC, was ineffective. As previously mentioned, this Court lacks jurisdiction over a
defendant that has not properly been served, and an entry of default against such a defendant is not
enforceable. See Conwill, 2010 WL 2773239, at *3 (citation omitted). Further, “[w]hen service of
process is challenged, the serving party bears the burden of proving its validity or good cause for
failure to effect timely service.” Sys. Signs Supplies v. U.S. Dep’t of Justice, 903 F.2d 1011, 1013
(5th Cir. 1990).
According to the business filings maintained by the Louisiana Secretary of State, Five
Happiness, LLC, is domiciled at 3605 S. Carrollton Avenue, New Orleans, Louisiana 70118. R.
Doc. 11-1. Five Happiness’s officers include Xikai Yao (Manager), Danqiu Lu (Member), and
Yanrong Wang (Member). Id. The registered agent for service of process for Five Happiness, LLC,
Xikai Yao, is located at 2830 Magazine Street, New Orleans, Louisiana 70115. Id. According to
FED. R. CIV. P. 4(h), service of process may be properly rendered on Five Happiness, LLC, by
delivering the summons and complaint to its registered agent, Yao, at 2830 Magazine Street.
Also located at 2830 Magazine Street in New Orleans, Louisiana, is Super Clawdom, LLC
d/b/a Sake Cafe Uptown .1 The only officer listed for the business is Yanrong Wang (Manager).
The registered agent for service of process for Super Clawdom, LLC, is Yanrong Wang (“Wang”),
who is located at 2830 Magazine Street, New Orleans, Louisiana 70115.

Here, service was attempted on Five Happiness, LLC, at the proper address for its
registered agent, 2830 Magazine Street, New Orleans, LA 70115. R. Doc. 3. However , the proof
of service does not indicate the identity of the individual , if any,  served with summons and the
complaint on behalf of Five Happiness. R. Doc. 3 at 2. Without proof of the identity of the
individual served, there is no proof that service was properly rendered upon Five Happiness, LLC’s
registered agent, Yao, and not some other individual at the same location, such as Wang. Therefore,
this Court finds that Plaintiff did not effectively render service upon Five Happiness, LLC.
Because Defendant was not properly served, the clerk’s entry of default is void, and further,
Five Happiness, LLC’s  answer was never due.  Conwill, 2010 WL 2773239, at *3 (citation
omitted). Thus, Defendant Five Happiness, LLC, was never required to file responsive pleadings
and only did so to protect its own interests. The Court finds this is good cause to set aside the
clerk’s entry of default.
IV. Conclusion
The Court having found good cause to set aside the clerk’s entry of default against Five
Happiness pursuant to FED. R. CIV. P. 55(c),
IT IS ORDERED that the Defendant's Motion to Set Aside Entry of Default  (R. Doc.
