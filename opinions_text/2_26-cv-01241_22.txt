LEGAL STANDARD
To survive a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss, the plaintiff must plead sufficient
facts to “state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.” Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S.
662, 678 (2009) (quoting Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 570 (2007)). A claim
is facially plausible when the plaintiff pleads facts th at allow the court to “draw the
reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.” Id. The
factual allegations in the complaint “must be enough to ra ise a right to relief above
the speculative level.” Twombly, 550 U.S. at 555. “[D]etailed factual allegations” are
not required, but the pleading must present “more than an unadorned, the -
defendant-unlawfully-harmed-me accusation.” Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 678. The court must
accept all well-pleaded facts as true and must draw all reasonable inferences in favor
of the plaintiff. Lormand v. US Unwired, Inc. , 565 F.3d 228, 232 (5th Cir. 2009).
However, “conclusory allegations or legal conclusions masquerad ing as factual
conclusions will not suffice to prevent a motion to dismiss.” Beavers v. Metro. Life Ins.
Co., 566 F.3d 436, 439 (5th Cir. 2009) (citation omitted).
DISCUSSION
 In the instant case, the Court finds that  C–T Homes provides insufficient
information in its  initial complaint to identify what specific allegations Andries
Mortgage must answer for. The allegations made by C –T Homes do not properly
differentiate between the various defendants. The practice of grouping together

multiple defendants raises concern s under the  pleading requirements identified in
Rule 8 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 8. Under Rule 8, a
complaint must contain a statement which is “sufficient to ‘give the defendant fair
notice of what the [plaintiff’s] claim is and the grounds upon which it rests.’” Shepherd
ex rel. Est. of Shepherd v. City of Shreveport, 920 F.3d 278, 287 (5th Cir. 2019) (citing
Twombly, 55 0 U.S. at 555) (alteration in original ). To reach this standard, “a
complaint must do more than name laws that may have been violated by the
defendant; it must also allege facts regarding what conduct violated those laws.”
Anderson v. U.S. Dep’t of Hous. & Urban Dev., 554 F.3d 525, 528 (5th Cir. 2008). The
grouped-together nature of the complaint at issue creates a situation where Andries
Mortgage is not aware of its specific conduct which led to alleged violations of the law.
See id. (holding that claims against defendants who were allegedly at the scene of use
of excessive force did not sufficiently put defendants on notice as to which actions
violated the law). As such, the complaint has failed to satisfy the pleading
requirements of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure.
 C–T Homes accuses Andries Mortgage of being a participant in an alleged
scheme that fraudulently used its Cretin Townsend Homes trade name to enrich the
defendants. However, Andries Mortgage’s conduct is largely combined with the
actions of Andries Builders throughout the complaint. See Rec. Doc. 1, at 5 –17.
Occasionally, the conduct of multiple defendants “may be lumped together if the
plaintiff’s allegations elsewhere designate the nature of the defendants’ relationship
to a particular scheme and identify the defendants’ role.” Bhatia v. Dischino, No. 3:09-

the cretintownsend.com domain, service@aabuilders.com, and does not explain how
Andries Mortgage’s involvement created a claim of false representation. (Rec. Doc. 1,
at 5, 9) . Every other allegation of wrongful conduct refers to the conduct of the
“Defendants.” Based on the allegations in the complaint, this Court is unable to “draw
the reasonable inferences[s] that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.”
Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 678. As such, the Court concludes that C–T Homes’ allegations fail
to meet the requisite pleading requirements as they pertain to Andries Mortgage.
C–T Homes has requested an opportunity to amend the original complaint to
rectify any deficiencies identified by this Court. The decision to allow leave to amend
is within the discretion of a trial court and should be “freely given when justice so
requires.” Dunn v. Koehring Co., 546 F.2d 1193, 1198 (5th Cir. 1977) (quoting Fed. R.
Civ. P. 15). As mentioned previously, C –T Homes failed to explain the specific role
the mortgage company played in the alleged scheme beyond simply providing
mortgages to Andries Builders customers and failed to identify any other specific acts,
omissions, or representations connecting Andries Mortgage to the supposed scheme.
