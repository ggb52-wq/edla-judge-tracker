 REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

Pro se plaintiff, Norris Joseph Foret,  a former inmate at Terrebonne Parish Criminal
Justice Complex, filed this civil complaint pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983, challenging conditions
of his arrest and confinement.  Upon filing, the Clerk’s Office notified him that his complaint
was deficient because it was not accompanied by the re quired filing fee or an application to
proceed in forma pauperis .  The record indicates that the Clerk mailed this notice, along
with the proper form, to Foret at the personal address he provided on his complaint and gave
him 21 days to comply.  ECF No. 2.  The envelope mailed to Foret was not returned as
undeliverable.  He failed to respond to that deficiency notice.
On October 29, 2024, the undersigned issued an order directing Foret to pay the filing
fee or submit a pauper application .  ECF No. 3.  He was given ample additional time  to
respond with the necessary information and he was warned that if he failed to comply with
the order, the undersigned would recommend dismissal of his civil complaint .  The
envelope was not returned as undeliverable.  To date, he has failed to comply with  the
Court’s order.

prosecute is clear.  The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure specifically provide that a court
may, in its discretion, dismiss a plaintiff's action for failure to prosecute  or for failure to
comply with the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure or any order of the court.  Fed. R. Civ. P.
41(b); McCullough v. Lynaugh, 835 F.2d 1126, 1127 (5th Cir. 1988) .  The Court's power to
dismiss for want of prosecution should be used sparingly, although it may be exercised sua
sponte when necessary to achieve the orderly and expeditious disposition of cases.  See
Link v. Wabash R.R. Co., 370 U.S. 626, 630–31, 82 S.Ct. 1386, 1388–89, 8 L.Ed.2d 734 (1962);
McCullough, 835 F.2d at 1127; Ramsay v. Bailey, 531 F.2d 706, 707 (5th Cir. 1976).
A pro se litigant is not exempt from compliance with relevant rules of procedural and
substantive law.  Grandpre v. Normand, Civ. Action No. 16-1541, 2018 WL 691650 (E.D. La.
Feb. 2, 2018) (citing Birl v. Estelle , 660 F.2d 592, 593 (5th Cir. 1981) ).  Foret has ignored
the show cause order directing him to remedy the deficiency and pay the required filing fee
or submit a completed pauper application.  His refusal to comply impedes the Court’s
ability to advance the case.  In light of the foregoing, it is appropriate  to dismiss plaintiff's
complaint.
RECOMMENDATION
For the foregoing reasons, it is RECOMMENDED that plaintiff’s complaint be
DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE for failure to  prosecute or  comply with this Court’s
order.
