REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION
 Plaintiff AC Haynes (“Haynes”) filed this action on June 19, 2024, seeking damages for
alleged racial discrimination, harassment, and retaliation under Title VII, Louisiana’s anti-
discrimination law, and 42 U.S.C. § 1983. R. Doc. 1 at  1. The matter was referred to the
undersigned United States Magistrate Judge pursuant to Title 28 U. S.C. ' 636(b) to address non-
dispositive pretrial matters and for the submission of Proposed Findings and Recommendations.
I. Introduction
A. Factual Background
Haynes is a sixty -six-year-old African American citizen of the United States, who was
allegedly discharged from his employment as a carpenter by Defendant Renovation Pro’s LLC
(“Renovation Pros”) in January 2023. R. Doc. 1 at 3- 4. Haynes alleges that Defendant Jeremy
Seaux (“Seaux”) is the general manager for Renovation Pro, and the father -in-law of Defendant
Jordan Hollier (“Hollier”), the owner of Renovation Pros. Id. at 2.
Haynes alleges that Seaux used racial slurs in his presence on two occasions in January
2023, and that Hollier did nothing about it. R. Doc. 1 at 3-4. Haynes alleges that on the day of the
second incident, he told Seaux that he did not appreciate his language. Id. Haynes alleges that after
he completed work that day, he received a call from Hollier informing him that he was fired for

In response, Haynes filed a Motion for Appointment of Process server, in which he alleged

II. Standard of Review
Rule 41(b) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure gives the court discretion to dismiss a
plaintiff's claim for failure to prosecute, for  failure to comply with the Federal Rules of Civil
Procedure, or failure to comply any order of the court.  FED. R. CIV. P. 41(b). When “determining
whether to dismiss a claim, courts have traditionally considered the extent to which the plaintiff,
rather than his counsel, has been responsible for any delay or failure to comply with a rule or
order.” Malone v. Boeing Company, No. 21-211, 2021 WL 4505437, at *1 (E.D. La. Aug. 20,
2021) (Roby, M.J.) (citations omitted).
Rule 4 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provides that the service of the summons
and a copy of the complaint must be made upon each defendant within ninety days of the filing of
the complaint. FED. R. CIV. P. 4(m). Under Rule 4, “if service is not made, the court must dismiss
the action without prejudice or order service by a specific time.” Dantzler v. United States
Department of Justice, No. 22-2211, 2023 WL 1427244, at *1 (E.D. La. Jan. 10, 2023) (Currault,
M.J.). Malone, No. 21-211 at *1. However, the Court should only extend the time for service if
the plaintiff shows good cause for their failure to serve. Harrell v. Edwards, No. 05-4002, 2006

III. Analysis
In this case, the record contains no indication that Defendants Hollier, Seaux, and
Renovation Pros were ever served. In addition, the Court has not received an explanation of the
cause or nature of Haynes’  delay. Harrell, No. 05- 4002 at *2 (recommending dismissal of the
plaintiff’s claims pursuant to Rule 4(m) and Rule 41(b), since their response to the Court’s show
cause order did not provide good cause for their failure to serve).
Instead, Haynes has simply asserted that regular service was not effective, without
providing any further information on his efforts to serve the Defendants since the Complaint was
filed on June 19, 2024. R. Doc. 18 at 1. The Court unambiguously informed Plaintiff that failure
to comply with the Court’s Show Cause orders could result in dismissal without further notice. R.
Doc. 9 at 1. R. Doc. 16 at 2. Therefore, dismiss al of Haynes’ claims against the Defendants is
proper under Rule 41(b) and Rule 4(m) for his failure to serve summons.
IV. Conclusion
Accordingly,
IT IS RECOMMENDED that Plaintiff AC Haynes’s claims against Defendants Jordan
Hollier, Jeremy Seaux, and Renovation Pro’s, LLC, be DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE
pursuant to FED. R. CIV. P. 41(b) and FED. R. CIV. P. 4(m).
