REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION
Plaintiff, Rico Newman, a Louisiana s tate prisoner, filed this pro se and in
forma pauperis civil action pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983. 1 Newman sued Our Lady
Angels Corporation.
 In his complaint, Newman claims that, after he sustained a shoulder injury in
June or July 2024, he was taken to Our Lady Angels hospital for shoulder surgery. 2
He claims that, during the surgery, medical personnel implanted electronic medical
devices in various places in his body that allow signals to be sent to his body through
the Secu rus/JPAY system .3 He also complains of the denial of medical care at
Rayburn Correctional Center  by Dr. Martinez and Dr. Augustine. 4 For the reasons
explained below, it is recommended that the action be dismissed with prejudice.

A screening of Newman’s complaint confirms that it is malicious. Newman
candidly admits that he filed a previous lawsuit based on the same facts alleged in
this case. 5 In that earlier civil lawsuit, he raised identical claims related to the
implanting of foreign devices and the denial of medical care, although, in addition to
suing Our Lady Angels Corporation, he sued a host of other defendants, including Dr.

Martinez and Dr. Augustine.6 That lawsuit was recently dismissed with prejudice as

It is proper to dismiss an action on the basis that it is malicious and frivolous
if it duplicates claims raised by the same plaintiff in previous or pending litigation.
See Roberson v. Breen, 444 F. App’x 841, 842 (5th Cir. 2011) (citing Pittman v. Moore,
980 F.2d 994, 994- 95 (5th Cir. 1993)). “When declaring that a successive in forma
pauperis suit is ‘malicious’ the court should insure that the plaintiff obtains one bite
at the litigation apple – but not more.” Pittman, 980 F.2d at 995.
Accordingly, because they are redundant, Newman’s federal civil rights claims
in this lawsuit are subject to dismissal as malicious.
RECOMMENDATION
IT IS THEREFORE RECOMMENDED  that Newman’s federal civil rights
claims be DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE as duplicative and malicious.
