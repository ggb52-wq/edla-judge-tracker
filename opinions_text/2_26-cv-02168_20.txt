Cir. 1987) (per curiam). “The district court has broad discretion in deciding whether
to order a transfer. ” Id. If a case has been filed in a proper venue but transfer is
appropriate, the court must transfer, and not dismiss, the case pursuant to 28 U.S.C.
§ 1404. See In re Volkswagen of Am., Inc., 545 F.3d 304, 309 (5th Cir. 2008) (en banc).
A district court may transfer a case pursuant to 28 U.S.C. §  1404 if two conditions
are satisfied. First, the case can only be transferred to a venue in which it “might
have been brought.” In re Planned Parenthood Fed’n of Am., Inc. , 52 F.4th 625, 630
(5th Cir. 2022) (quoting 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a)). Pursuant to the general venue statute,35

28 U.S.C. § 1391(b), “[a] civil action may be brought in . . . a judicial district in which

Second, the destination venue must be “clearly more convenient than the venue
chosen by the plaintiff.” Planned Parenthood , 52 F.4th at 629 (quoting Volkswagen ,
545 F.3d at 315). There must be good cause for a transfer. See Volkswagen, 545 F.3d
at 315. “ This ‘good cause ’ burden reflects the appropriate deference to which the
plaintiff’s choice of venue is entitled. ” Id. “When . . . the transferee venue is clearly
more convenient, however, [there is] good cause and the district court should
therefore grant the transfer.” Id.
In the Fifth Circuit, courts weigh the factors from Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330
U.S. 501 (1947), to determine if a venue is clearly more convenient. Planned
Parenthood, 52 F.4th at 630. These factors consist of four “private interest factors”
and four “public interest factors:”
The private interest factors are: (1) the relative ease of access to sources
of proof; (2) the availability of compulsory process to secure the
attendance of witnesses; (3) the cost of attendance for willing witnesses;
and (4) all other practical problems that make trial of a case easy,
expeditious and inexpensive.
The public interest factors are: (1) the administrative difficulties flowing
from court congestion; (2) the local interest in having localized interests
decided at home; (3) the familiarity of the forum with the law that will
govern the case; and (4) the avoidance of unnecessary problems of
conflict of laws or in the application of foreign law.
Id. (alterations omitted) (quoting Volkswagen, 545 F.3d at 315).
The factors “are not necessarily exhaustive or exclusive,” and none “can be said
to be of dispositive weight.” Id. (quoting Volkswagen, 545 F.3d at 315). Transfer is

only justified if “the marginal gain in convenience will be significant ” and “those
marginal gains will actually materialize in the transferee venue.” In re Clarke, 94
F.4th 502, 508 (5th Cir. 2024) (emphasis in original).
III. ANALYSIS
This case should be transferred to the Western District, because it is a “clearly
more convenient ” forum for this dispute.  See Volkswagen, 545 F.3d at 315. As a n
initial matter, this lawsuit might have been filed  in the Western District . Pursuant
to 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2), LSUA, the site of the central dispute regarding plaintiff’s
removal from her registered nurse to Bachelor of Science in Nursing program,36 is
located in the Western District.37 She also alleges that other key events took place in
the Western District, such as her February 2023 arrest in Monroe, Louisiana that led
to the LSBN’s investigation into her nursing license .38 To be sure, plaintiff alleges
that some events occurred in the United States District Court for the Middle District
of Louisiana (“Middle District”), such as her disputes with the Livingston Parish
School Board39 and her late father’s mesothelioma litigation. 40 She also claims that

The second factor, “ the availability of compulsory process to secure the

has effectuated service on all the defendants, it is unknown who will be a nonparty
witness, and whether those witnesses w ill be willing to testify . “The availability of
compulsory process receives less weight when it has not been alleged or shown that
any witness would be unwilling to testify.” TikTok, 85 F.4th at 360 (quoting Planned
Parenthood, 52 F.4th at 630–31). As such, this factor is neutral.

The third factor, “the cost of attendance for willing witnesses,” is also neutral.
The Fifth Circuit “assesses convenience as a function of distance.” Clarke, 94 F.4th at
514. If the two venues are more than one hundred miles apart, “the factor of
inconvenience borne by witnesses positively and linearly scales with the additional
distance they must travel.” Id. All the divisions of the Western District are located
more than one hundred miles from the courthouse in the Eastern District. However,
as with the second factor, the Court does not have information with respect to who
will be a witness, the distance they will travel, or the cost for those witnesses to attend
a proceeding in the Western District. Th us, this factor does not bear on the Court’s
analysis.
