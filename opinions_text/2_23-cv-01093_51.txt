1986).14 Louisiana law provides that  “a compromise is a contract whereby the
parties, through concessions  made by one or more of them, settle a dispute or an
uncertainty concerning an obligation or other legal relationship.”  La. Civ. Code art.
3071. Article 3072 of the Louisiana Civil Code instructs: “[ A] compromise shall be
made in writing or recited in open  court, in which case the recitation shall be
susceptible of being transcribed from the record of the proceedings.”  Article 3071
has an implied signature requirement; the compromise must be signed by the
parties or their agents. Lavan v. Nowell, 708 So. 2d 1052 n.3 (La. 1998).
 As explained above, the  parties exchanged a series of emails revising the
Agreement.15 The Agreement circulated on July 26 was consistent with the email
correspondence in the record. That is, neither side had introduced significant new or
different terms. Yet while defense counsel said that they “believe[d] it [the attached
Agreement] is ready for signatures,” their client ultimately refused to sign it.  Thus,
while it appears there was a clear meeting of the minds, the question is whether the
email correspondence fulfilled art. 3071’s signature requirement.
“Emails can qualify as the signed writings needed to form contracts.”  Preston
Law Firm, L.L.C. v. Mariner Health Care Mgmt. Co., 622 F.3d 384, 391 (5th Cir.
2010) (citing La. Rev. Stat. § 9:2607).  For example, an enforceable settlement may
arise when one party emails a list of material terms and the other party responds
that they have “agreed to” those terms.  See id. Similarly, statements such as “we
have a deal,” may demonstrate acceptance of a settlement propos al. Klebanoff v.
