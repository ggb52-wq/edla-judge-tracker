I. Mandatory Screening Provisions
“There is no absolute right to be allowed to proceed in forma pauperis in civil
matters; rather it is a privilege extended to those unable to pay filing fees when the
action is not frivolous or malicious.” Startti v. United States, 415 F.2d 1115, 1116 (5th
Cir. 1969). 28 U.S.C. 1915(e)(2) thus directs, in relevant part, that courts “ shall
dismiss [an in forma pauperis plaintiff’s] case at any time ” if the plaintiff’s
complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief may be granted or is determined to
be frivolous.
In addition, because Collins is  incarcerated, he is subject to the screening
provisions of 28 U.S.C. § 1915A. That statute mandates that federal courts “review,
before docketing, if feasible or, in any event, as soon as practicable after docketing, a
complaint in a civil action in which a prisoner seeks redress from a governmental
entity or officer or employee of a governmental entity.” 28 U.S.C. § 1915A(a).
Regarding such lawsuits, federal law similarly requires:
On review, the court shall identify cognizable claims or dismiss the
complaint, or any portion of the complaint, if the complaint—
(1) is frivolous, malicious, or fails to state a claim upon which relief
may be granted; or
(2) seeks monetary relief from a defendant who is immune from such
relief.

28 U.S.C. § 1915A(b).

A complaint is frivolous “if it lacks an arguable basis in law or fact.” Berry v.
Brady, 192 F.3d 504, 507 (5th Cir. 1999) (citation omitted). This standard, “when
applied to a complaint, embraces not only the inarguable legal conclusion, but also
the fanciful factual allegation.” Neitzke v. Williams , 490 U.S. 319, 325 (1989). A
“complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief may be granted when it does not
contain ‘sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to state a claim to relief that is
plausible on its face.’” Rogers v. Boatright, 709 F.3d 403, 407 (5th Cir. 2013) (quoting
Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009)).
II. Analysis
 Collins filed this action under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. In pertinent part, that statute
provides:
Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation,
custom, or usage, of any State or Territory or the District of Columbia,
subjects, or causes to be subjected, any citizen of the United States or
other person within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any
rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws,
shall be liable to the party injured in an action at law, suit in equity, or
other proper proceeding for redress[.]

42 U.S.C. § 1983. Accordingly, “[t]o state a claim under § 1983, a plaintiff must allege
the violation of a right secured by the Constitution and laws of the United States, and
must show that the alleged deprivation was committed by a person acting under color
of state law.” West v. Atkins, 487 U.S. 42, 48 (1988) (citation omitted).  Any alleged
constitutional violation must be intentional. See Cnty. of Sacramento v. Lewis, 523
U.S. 833, 849 (1998) (“[L]iability for negligently inflicted harm is categorically
beneath the threshold of constitutional due process.”).

A. Jerry Larpenter Criminal Justice Complex
First, Collins named Jerry Larpenter Criminal Justice Complex , formerly
known as the Terrebonne Parish Criminal Justice Complex, as a defendant. However,
“[t]he Terrebonne Parish Criminal Justice Complex simply is not a proper defendant
under any circumstances. A jail is merely a building, not a ‘person’ subject to suit
under 42 U.S.C. § 1983.” Coleman v. Terrebonne Parish Criminal Justice Complex ,
No. 13-4325, 2013 WL 6004051, at *5 (E.D. La. Nov. 13, 2013) (quotation marks and
brackets omitted); accord Authement v. Terrebonne Parish Sheriff’s Office, No. 09 -
5837, 2009 WL 4782368, at *4 (E.D. La. Dec. 3, 2009); Bland v. Terrebonne Parish
Criminal Justice Complex, No. 09 -4407, 2009 WL 3486449, at *3 (E.D. La. Oct. 23,
2009). Accordingly, Collins’s claims against Jerry Larpenter Criminal Justice
Complex should be dismissed.
B. Terrebonne Parish Consolidated Government
Collins also named the Terrebonne Parish Consolidated Government.
Terrebonne Parish Consolidated Government is a governmental entity. See
Fitch v. Terrebonne Parish Consol. Government, No. 06-3940, 2007 WL 275901, at *2
(E.D. La. Jan. 26, 2007). A governmental entity is not vicariously liable for its
employees’ actions. Hinojosa v. Livingston, 807 F.3d 657, 668 (5th Cir. 2015) (“[T]he
Supreme Court held that claims against local governments premised on a theory of

respondeat superior liability are not cognizable under § 1983.”); Snyder v. Trepagnier,
142 F.3d 791, 795 (5th Cir. 1998) (“[T]he [Supreme] Court held that a local
government may not be held liable under respondeat superior for constitutional torts
committed by a municipal employee.”); Victoria W. v. Larpenter, 369 F.3d 475, 482
(5th Cir. 2004). Rather, a governmental entity is liable under § 1983 when a
constitutional tort is caused through the execution of a policy or custom of the
municipality. Monell v. Dep’t of Soc. Servs. of New York, et al. , 436 U.S. 658, 691
(1978). “To establish municipal liability under § 1983, a plaintiff must show that (1)
an official policy (2) promulgated by the municipal policymaker (3) was the moving
force behind the violation of a constitutional right.” Peterson v. City of Fort Worth ,
588 F.3d 838, 847 (5th Cir. 2009).
Here, Collins fails to allege any of the elements of municipal liability. He does
not allege that anyone violated his constitutional rights pursuant to a Terrebonne
Parish Consolidated Government policy or custom of which a policymaker had actual
or constructive knowledge.
 Further, even had Collins alleged a valid Monell  claim against Terrebonne
Parish Consolidated Government, it is not the proper defendant. First, t he parish
governing authority plays no role in the day-to-day operation of the parish jail; rather,
its role is limited to the funding of the jail. See Arce v. Louisiana, 226 F.Supp.3d 643,
649 (E.D. La. 2016); Fairley v. Stadler, 294 F. App’x 805, 812 (5th Cir. 2009); Galo v.
Blanco, No. Civ.A. 06-4290, 2006 WL 2860851, at *2 (E.D. La. Oct. 4, 2006); see also
La. Rev. Stat. §§ 15:702 (“The governing authority of each parish shall be responsible

for the physical maintenance of all parish jails and prisons.”) and 33:4715 (“The police
jury of each parish shall provide ... a good and sufficient jail....”). The parish sheriff is
responsible for operating jail and providing any needed maintenance. Courteaux v.
Larpenter, No. 19-13173, 2020 WL 2771755, at *6 (E.D. La. Apr. 27, 2020), adopted ,
2020 WL 2769877 (E.D. La. May 28, 2020). Here, Collins does not allege that the
conditions at the jail are a result of the inadequate funding by the parish government.
Therefore, Terrebonne Parish Consolidated Government is not the proper defendant
with respect to any claim challenging those conditions. Id.  at *7 (citing Ates v.
Terrebonne Parish, No. 13 -5732, 2013 WL 6858455, at *3 (E.D. La. Dec. 30, 2013)
(“[T]he proper defendant for Monell claims concerning the conditions of confinement
at [a parish jail] is the ... Parish Sheriff in his official capacity, not the parish

Further, with regard to the medical care provider, the Parish government’s
“sole responsibility shall be ... its contractual obligations with [the] health care
provider,” and not how the medical care is administered. La. Stat. Ann. § 15:703(D);
accord Belcher v. Lopinto, 492 F. Supp. 3d 636, 649 (E.D. La. 2020) (Under Louisiana
law, a parish government is not policymaker for the prison and had no duty to monitor
or verify the performance of medical services it contracted with for the jail).  Thus, it
is not a proper defendant with respect to any claim relating to the specific medical
treatment provided.
 Accordingly, Collins’s claims against Terrebonne Parish Consolidated

C. Deputy Rayshawn Brown
Collins claims that Deputy Brown, the “Deputy of Clean Up Department,” was
aware of the water intrusion that ultimately caused Collins to slip and fall. 10 He
claims that Deputy Brown failed to follow “proper procedure” and place warning signs
in the area to prevent the incident.11
Initially, Deputy Brown may not be held responsible under a theory of
respondeat superior under § 1983, based on a claim that prison personnel under his
supervision caused his injury. Eason v. Thaler, 73 F.3d 1322, 1327 (5th Cir. 1996);
accord Field v. Corr. Corp. of Am. Inc., 364 F. App’x 927, 929 (5th Cir. 2010). A
supervisory official may be held liable for his subordinates’ actions under § 1983 only
if the official implemented an unconstitutional policy that causally resulted in the
injury. See Monell, 436 U.S. at 691–95; Thompson v. Johnson, 348 F. App’x 919, 921
(5th Cir. 2009) (citing Mouille v. City of Live Oak, 977 F.2d 924, 929 (5th Cir. 1992)).
Further, Collins does not allege that Brown is a policymaker; nor does he allege that
an official policy resulted in his injury. Thus, the official capacity claim against Brown
must be dismissed.
To the extent Collins sues Brown in his individual capacity , Collins fails to
state a constitutional violation for the following reasons.
Collins cannot succeed on his claim that Brown failed to place signs warning
of the wet floor in accordance with jail procedure.12 A plaintiff is not constitutionally

E. Nurse Guidry and Nurse Erica
Collins admits that Nurse Guidry and Nurse Erica responded to the incident,
and that, after was brought to the medical unit, they gave him pain medication, and
had him transported to the hospital where he received additional medical care.17 He,
however, faults Guidry and Erica for allowing two inmates and a jail guard to lift him
from the floor, carry him downstairs, and place him in a wheelchair.18 It also appears
that he finds fault with their failure to order that he be placed in a bottom bunk  on
the first floor upon his return from the hospital.19
In order to state a § 1983 claim for denial of adequate medical treatment, a
prisoner must allege the officials acted with deliberate indifference to serious medical
needs. Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97, 105– 06 (1976); Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294,
303, (1991). Deliberate indifference is an extremely high standard to meet . Domino
v. Texas Dept. of Criminal Justice, 239 F.3d 752, 756 (5th Cir. 2001);  Johnson v.
Treen, 759 F.2d 1236, 1238 (5th Cir. 1985) . It requi res facts that demonstrate that
defendants “refused to treat [plaintiff], ignored his complaints, intentionally treated
him incorrectly, or engaged in any similar conduct that would clearly evince a wanton
disregard for any serious medical needs.” Brewster v. Dretke, 587 F.3d 764, 770 (5th
Cir. 2009).
An inmate is not guaranteed optimal medical care.  See Gobert v. Caldwell, 463
F.3d 339, at 349 (5th Cir. 2006) (“[D]eliberate indifference exists wholly independent

Terrebonne Parish Sheriff’s Office and the “Medical Department” as additional

A Louisiana parish sheriff’s office is not a legal entity capable of being sued.
Cozzo v. Tangipahoa Parish Council –President Government, 279 F.3d 273, 283 (5th

jail or its administrative departments are not entities that can be sued under Section

(E.D. La. June 9, 2008) (citations omitted) ; see Henderson v. Mid States Servs., Inc. ,

Medical Department is a legal entity amenable to suit ....”).20 Instead, a § 1983 action

state law claims against these defendants and that those claims be dismissed without
prejudice.
I. Leave to Amend
“Ordinarily, a pro se litigant should be offered an opportunity to amend his
complaint before it is dismissed.” Wiggins v. Louisiana St. Univ.-Health Care Services
Division, 710 F. App ’x 625, 627 (5th Cir. 2017) (internal quotation marks omitted).
Leave to amend is not required, however,  when an amendment would be futile, i.e. ,
“an amended complaint would still ‘fail to survive a Rule 12(b)(6) motion.’”  See Stem
v. Gomez, 813 F.3d 205, 215–16 (5th Cir. 2016) (citations omitted).
Under the circumstances, it does not appear that Collins w ill be able to state
any cognizable federal claims consistent with the facts alleged. For that reason, leave
to amend, should Collins seek it, should be denied.
RECOMMENDATION

It is therefore RECOMMENDED Collins’ § 1983 claims against defendants be
DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE as frivolous and/or for failure to state a claim on
which relief may be granted.
IT IS FURTHER RECOMMENDED the Court decline to exercise
supplemental jurisdiction over any state law claims under 28 U.S.C. § 1367(c) and
that such claims against these defendants be DISMISSED WITHOUT
PREJUDICE.
