ORDER AND REASONS
 Before the Court is a Motion to Dismiss Defendants Trevor J. Valdez and
the United States Coast Guard (Doc. 12) filed by Trevor J. Valdez, United
States Coast Guard, and the United States. For the following reasons, the
Motion is GRANTED.

BACKGROUND
This matter arises out of a vehicle accident between Plaintiff Claiborne
Roberson and Defendant Trevor Valdez on April 10, 2025. Plaintiff brings
negligence claims under the Federal Tort Claims Act (“FTCA”) against Valdez,
the United States Coast Guard (“Coast Guard”) , and the United States.
Plaintiff alleges Valdez negligently operated his vehicle owned by the Coast
Guard while in the course and scope of his employment and that the United
States and the Coast Guard negligently supervised, trained, or hired Valdez,
causing personal injury damages.

Defendants move to dismiss Plaintiff’s claims against Valdez and the
Coast Guard pursuant to Rule 12(b)(1) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure
and 28 U.S.C. §§ 2679(a) and (d)(1), arguing lack of subject matter jurisdiction.
Plaintiff has not filed a response to this motion; thus, the Court will consider
it unopposed.

LEGAL STANDARD
A Rule 12(b)(1) motion challenges the subject matter jurisdiction of a
federal district court. “A case is properly dismissed for lack of subject matter
jurisdiction when the court lacks the statutory or constitutional power to
adjudicate the case.”1 In ruling on a Rule 12(b)(1) motion to dismiss, the court
may rely on (1) the complaint alone, presuming the allegations to be true,
(2) the complaint supplemented by undisputed facts, or (3) the complaint
supplemented by undisputed facts and by the court’s resolution of disputed
facts.2  The proponent of federal court jurisdiction—in this case, Plaintiff—
bears the burden of establishing subject matter jurisdiction.3

LAW AND ANALYSIS
Defendants assert that the United States is the sole proper defendant
under the FTCA, and, therefore, this Court must dismiss Plaintiff’s claims
against Defendants Trevor Valdez and the Coast Guard  for lack of subject
matter jurisdiction. The Court agrees.
The FTCA, as amended by the Federal Employees Liability Reform and
Tort Compensation Act of 1988, provides that a suit against the United States
is the exclusive remedy for persons with claims for damages resulting from
