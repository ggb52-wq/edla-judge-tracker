ORDER & REASONS
Before the Court is a Motion to Appoint Counsel filed by Plaintiff Charles Patterson. R.
Doc. 7. Patterson, proceeding pro se, filed the above-captioned action against two supervisors at
his place of employment, Southeastern Louisiana Veterans Healthcare System, R. Doc. 1 at 2 –3.
Patterson alleges that Defendants failed to promote him and retaliated against him due to his prior
complaints. Id. at 4 –6. Patterson’s Complaint states that he is a veteran and a person with a
disability, but does not clarify which federal law he brings this case under. See id. at 3.
There is no absolute right to court-appointed counsel in civil cases. Hudson v. Univ. of Tex.
Med. Branch, 441 F. App'x 291, 293 (5th Cir. 2011) . Rather, a pro se plaintiff must demonstrate
that the appointment of counsel is appropriate under the circumstances.  Id. The governing test
depends on the claims asserted. If the action is brought under Title VII,  courts analyze a request
for counsel by considering the following factors : “(1) the merits of the plaintiff's claims of
discrimination; (2) the efforts taken by the plaintiff to obtain counsel; and (3) the plaintiff's
financial ability to retain counsel. ” Gonzalez v. Carlin, 907 F.2d 573, 580 (5th Cir. 1990) . If the
plaintiff raises a different type of employment discrimination claim, they must demonstrate that
“exceptional circumstances” warrant the appointment of counsel. See Bravo v. Kendall, No. 5-22-
CV-01186-JKP-RBF, 2022 WL 23043275, at *1 (W.D. Tex. Dec. 8, 2022) ; Kim v. Bible Study
Fellowship, No. 5-22-CV-01142-FB-RBF, 2022 WL 23045416, at *1 (W.D. Tex. Dec. 13, 2022).

In evaluating whether such exceptional circumstances exist, courts consider the following: “(1) the
type and complexity of the case; (2) whether the litigant is capable of adequately presenting and
investigating his case; and  (3) whether the evidence  will consist in  large part of conflicting
testimony so as to require skill in the presentation of evidence and in cross-examination of
witnesses.” Ulmer v. Chancellor, 691 F.2d 209, 213 (5th Cir. 1982) (citations omitted)
At present,  the Court  is unable  to determine  whether  the appointment  of counsel  is
appropriate. Patterson’s Complaint is silent as to the claims asserted, and his motion provides no
justification for the appointment of counsel. The Court will allow Patterson to refile his motion. In
his refiled motion, Patterson should clarify the following information: (1) what federal law or laws
does Patterson believe Defendants violated; (2) what did Defendants do that violated federal law;
(3) what efforts has Patterson made to retain an attorney; (4) what is Patterson’s highest level of
education; (5) any  other information explaining  Patterson’s need for court-appointed counsel.
Accordingly;
IT IS ORDERED that the Motion, R. Doc. 7, is DENIED, without prejudice to Patterson’s
right to refile the motion in accordance with the instructions above.
