Supplemental and A mended Complaint  (R. Doc. 13) . Defendant, the  State of

visiting a public welcome center owned and operated by the DOTD, the restroom door

forth in the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design.1 Ellis alleges that, as a result
of this noncompliance, the restroom door rapidly closed as Ellis attempted to exit the

When, as here, the motion to amend a pleading is filed beyond the scheduling
order’s permitted date, the movant must first establish good cause under Federal
Rule of Civil Procedure 16(b)(4). See e.g., S&W Enters., L.L.C. v. SouthTrust Bank of
Alabama, NA, 315 F.3d 533, 535- 56 (5th Cir. 2003). Only upon a showing of good
cause will the “more liberal standard of Rule 15(a) apply.” Id. ; Jackson v. Gray, 345
F.R.D. 597, 607 (E.D. La. Jan. 26, 2024) (quoting Butler v. Taser Intern., Inc. , 535 F.
App’x 371, 372 (5th Cir. 2013)). Courts consider “(1) the explanation for the failure to
timely move for leave to amend; (2) the importance of the amendment; (3) potential
prejudice in allowing the amendment; and (4) the availability of a continuance to cure
such prejudice.” Jackson, 345 F.R.D. at 607 (citing Fahim v. Marriott Hotel Servs.,
Inc., 551 F.3d 344, 348 (5th Cir. 2008)); S&W Enters., L.L.C., 315 F.3d at 536.

All four factors weigh in favor of allowing amendment. Ellis learned of the non-
preservation of the door via DOTD’s interrogatory responses o n August 28, 2026. 7
Ellis alleges that the door closer’s operational status is integral to her case , and she
seeks to add a n alternative and  independent claim under Louisiana law relative to
the alleged spoliation .8 Likewise, the third and fourth factors weigh in favor of
amendment. The proposed pleading adds no new defendants, and a continuance may
cure any other potential prejudice.  Accordingly, good cause  exists to extend the
amendment deadline. The Court thus turns to Rule 15’s more permissive standard.
Under Rule 15(a)(2), if more than 21 days have passed after service of a
responsive pleading, then the party may amend its pleading only with the opposing
party’s written consent or the court’s leave. The court “should freely give leave when
justice so requires.”9 The language of Rule 15(a)(2) “evinces a bias in favor of granting
leave to amend.”  Chitimacha Tribe of Louisiana v. Harry L.L. Co.,  690 F.2d 1157,
1163 (5th Cir. 1982);  see also Mayeaux v. Louisiana Health Serv. & Indem. Co. , 376
F.3d 420, 425 (5th Cir. 2004). The Fifth Circuit has instructed that the “district court
must possess a ‘substantial reason’ to deny a request for leave to amend.”  Smith v.
EMC Corp., 393 F.3d 590, 595 (5th Cir. 2004). Although leave to amend is to be freely

given under Rule 15(a)(2) , “that generous standard is tempered by the necessary
power of a district court to manage a case.” Yumilicious Franchise, LLC v. Barrie, 819
F.3d 170, 177 (5th Cir. 2016).  Relevant factors to consider include “undue delay, bad
faith or dilatory motive on the part of the movant, repeated failures to cure
deficiencies by amendments previously allowed, undue prejudice to the opposing
party by virtue of allowance of the amendment, and futility of the amendment.”
Schiller v. Physicians Res. Grp. Inc. , 342 F.3d 563, 566 (5th Cir. 2003).  “Absent a
‘substantial reason’ … ‘the discretion of the district court is not broad enough to
permit denial.’” Id.  (quoting Martin's Herend Imports, Inc. v. Diamond & Gem
Trading United States of Am. Co., 195 F.3d 765, 770 (5th Cir. 1999)).
Each of the Rule 15 factors weighs in favor of amendment. The parties are in
the early stages of litigation. Trial is nearly eight months away, the proposed pleading
does not add any new parties, and DOTD does not object to the proposed amendment.
Nor is there any indication that amendment would be unduly prejudicial  or futile.
Ellis discovered the basis for her amendment  on August 28, 2026, and originally
sought leave to amend on October 7, 2026. The undersigned finds no undue delay.
For the reasons set forth above,
IT IS ORDERED that the Motion (R. Doc. 13) is GRANTED. Ellis’s proposed
Second Amended Complaint (R. Doc. 13-8) shall be filed in the record.
