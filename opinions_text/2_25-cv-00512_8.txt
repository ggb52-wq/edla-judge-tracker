REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

 This 42 U.S.C. § 1983 proceeding was filed in forma pauperis  by pro se plaintiff, Kent
Taylor, an inmate at the Dixon Correctional Institute in Jackson, Louisiana.  The complaint
was originally filed in the United States District Court for the Middle  District of Louisiana.
The case was subsequently transferred to this Court because the incident complained of took
place while Taylor was incarcerated at the Nelson Coleman Correctional Center in St. Charles
Parish.1
Taylor filed this lawsuit against defendants, Nelson Coleman Correctional Center,
Sheriff Greg Champagne, and the Medical Department.  In his statement of claim , he alleges
that his “medical condition was denied and look over and over again, medical malpractice
was performed at clock work, crys for help was a constant accura nce, he was denied to see
the neurologist for 3 months, and the nursing staff did not give him the proper meds or try
to help him in any way.” 2  He alleges that being locked in a small cell 23 hours a day with a

 A proceeding brought in forma pauperis  may be dismissed as frivolous under §
1915(e)(2)(B)(i) if the claim alleged therein has no arguable basis in law or fact, Booker v.
Koonce, 2 F.3d 114 (5 th Cir. 1993), or if it fails to state a claim upon which relief can be
granted.  28 U.S.C. §  1915(e)(2)(B)(ii); see also  28 U.S.C. §1915A, 42 U.S.C. §  1997e(c).
Liberally construing the complaint, it is the recommendation of the undersigned Magistrate
Judge that this matter be dismissed with prejudice as frivolous and for failing to state a claim
upon which relief can be granted.
 Taylor names the prison, Nelson Coleman Correctional Center, as a defendant in this
case.  However, the jail is not a proper defendant.  It is well -settled that “[t]he [prison] is
merely a building, not a ‘person’ subject to suit under 42 U.S.C. § 1983.”  Mitchell v. Jefferson
Parish Correctional Center, Civ. Action No. 13 -4963, 2013 WL 6002770, at *3 (E.D. La. Nov.
12, 2013); accord Culbertson v. J.P.S.O. , Civ. Action No. 16 -15958, 2017 WL 5133209, at *3

12-1767, 2012 WL 3026808, at *2 (E.D. La. July 12, 2012), adopted, 2012 WL 3027945 (E.D.
La. July 24, 2012) ); Porter v. St. Bernard Parish Jail, Civ. Action No. 15 -5716, 2015 WL
10323883, at *1 n. 1 (E.D. La. Dec. 21, 2015), adopted, 2016 WL 687655 (E.D. La. Feb. 19,

 Next, Taylor names Sheriff Greg Champagne as a defendant.  He does not allege that
Sheriff Champagne was personally involved in the incident.   He appears to name the Sheriff
simply for his purported accountability for acts or omissions that occur at the prison.   He
indicates in his complaint that Sheriff Greg Champagne is employed as Sheriff at Nelson
Coleman Correctional Center.5  In any event, he has failed to state a non- frivolous claim
against the Sheriff in either his official or individual capacity.
 Sheriff Champagne cannot be held liable pursuant to § 1983 under a theory of
respondeat superior or vicarious liability simply because an employee or a subordinate at the
jail allegedly violated the plaintiff’s constitutional rights.  See Alton v. Texas A&M University ,
168 F.3d 196, 200 (5th Cir. 1999).   While a supervisory official may bear responsibility for
such actions by a subordinate if the named official, as a final policymaker, issued an order or
implemented an unconstitutional policy or custom, that proximately caused the damage, no
such allegation was made here.   Monell v. Dept. of Soc. Servs. of City of New York, 436 U.S. 658,
694 (1978); Spiller v. City of Texas City, Pol. Dep’t , 130 F.3d 162, 167 (5th Cir. 1997).  Taylor
has not identified any policy implemented by the Sheriff directed at his medical condition or
treatment that resulted in injury.
 Taylor has also failed to state a claim against the Sheriff in his individual capacity.
“Plaintiffs suing governmental officials in their individual capacities… must allege specific
conduct giving rise to a constitutional violation.  This standard requires more than
conclusory assertions:  The plaintiff must allege specific facts giving rise to a constitutional

 Finally, Taylor names the “Medical Department” as a defendant.  However, neither the
medical department nor its staff as a group are suable entities under § 1983.  “[A] prison or
jail or its administrative departments are not entities that can be sued under Section 1983
because they are not juridical entities under state law capable of being sued and/or because
they are not persons for purposes of suit under Section 1983  as the statute and case law
define that term .”  Douglas v. Gusman , 567 F. Supp. 2d 877, 892 (E.D. La. June 9, 2008)
(citations omitted).6  A § 1983 action must be filed against an actual identified person, not a
department or general staff group at the jail.  See Carter v. Strain , Civ. Action No. 09 -3401,
2009 WL 2390808, at *3 (E.D. La. July 31, 2009) (citing August v. Gusman, Civ. Action No. 06-
