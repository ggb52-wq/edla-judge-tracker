ORDER AND REASONS

Pending before me is Plaintiff’s Motion for Reconsideration of September 9, 2026 Order,
to Correct the Record, and to Consider the Corrected Privilege Log Under Rule 54(b).  ECF No.
90.
In connection with the original filings, Plaintiff sought production of certain witness
statements not identified by date, which Defendant’s privilege log incorrectly identified as having
been taken on August 29, 2026, rather than August 29, 2025.  ECF No. 84-2.  Defendant’s
subsequent filing corrected the date error and noted the statements were taken on August 29, 2025,
not 2026.  ECF No. 88, at 3. 1  Plaintiff correctly note s that my September 9, 2026 Order and
Reasons erroneously carried over the initial mistaken date reference.   For that reason, correction
of the Order and Reasons is appropriate.
However, contrary to Plaintiff’s assertion in this motion, the Court did not rely on the
incorrect date to support the conclusion that the statements qualify as work product.  Thus, the date
error contained in the Order and Reasons does not change the analysis.  Indeed, the mistaken date
has no impact on the analysis of whether the statements taken by Defendant’s litigation counsel
qualify as work product.  As Defendant explained in its opposition, t he witness statements were

not taken by a party in the ordinary course of business.  Rather, the statements were taken by
litigation counsel ( attorney Joseph Devall ) as part of the investigation of the incident in
anticipation of litigation.  ECF Nos. 84, at 4–5; 84-2.  As such, the statements constitute ordinary
work product (rather than an attorney’s mental processes work product).  Plaintiff does not now
suggest that Defendant’s assertion is inaccurate.  Thus, Defendant carried its burden to establish
work product.
Having established that the statements constitute work product, the decisive issue then
became whether Plaintiff had established substantial need for the work product.  As the Court
explained in the paragraph immediately following the paragraph containing the incorrect date, the
fact that these witnesses also provided statements to the Plaquemines Par ish Sheriff’s Office ,
which were delivered to Plaintiff, and Plaintiff  had not yet deposed the witnesses to determine
whether they recall the event or lacked sufficient memory to answer relevant questions, she failed
to carry the burden of establishing substantial need for the statements.  And, as the Court already
indicated:  “Should the statements given to the Plaquemines Parish Sheriff’s Office be insufficient
and the witnesses are unable to answer questions when deposed, an in camera inspection may
be justified.  At this point, however, an in camera inspection is not required as Plaintiff has
not established substantial need for the discovery of ordinary work product materials. ”
The Court’s prior basis for denying the motion to compel remains undisturbed:  Until
Plaintiff deposes the witnesses to d etermine whether or not they recall the relevant events,
Plaintiff has not established substantial need necessary to overcome work product.  Thus, an
in camera inspection is not warranted at this time.  Should Plaintiff develop facts sufficient
to establish substantial need for the statements, she may re-urge her motion and provide the
statements made to police for comparison purposes.

Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS ORDERED that the Motion for Reconsideration of September 9, 2026 Order, to
Correct the Record, and to Consider the Corrected Privilege Log Under Rule 54(b) (ECF No. 90)
is GRANTED IN PART AND DENIED IN PART .  A corrected Order and Reasons will be
entered to remove the incorrect date included in the original filings.  Production of the statements
at this time, however, is improper as Plaintiff has not demonstrated substantial need given that
Plaintiff has the statements given to the police and she has not yet deposed the witnesses to
determine whether they lack knowledge of the relevant events.
