II. LEGAL STANDARDS
The two-step procedure for the entry of a default judgment is governed by
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 55. First, “the plaintiff must petition the clerk for an
entry of default, which is simply ‘a notation of the party’s default on the clerk’s record
of the case.’” Hillman v. S. Insulators, LLC, No. 23-659, 2023 WL 8113621, at *1 (E.D.
La. Nov. 22, 2023) (Africk, J.) (quoting Trahan v. PLC Fin., Inc., No. 18-859, 2018 WL
10758657, at *1 (E.D. La. Mar. 29, 2018) (Barbier, J.) ). This requires the plaintiff to
“show ‘by affidavit or otherwise’ that the defendant ‘has failed to plead or otherwise

defend.’” Id. (quoting Fed. R. Civ. P. 55(a)). Once the plaintiff has done so, “an entry
of default may be made by the clerk without any action being taken by the court .”
10A Wright & Miller’s Federal Practice & Procedure § 2682 (4th ed. 2026).
After the entry of default, the plaintiff may apply for a default judgment. N.Y.
Life Ins. Co. v. Brown, 84 F.3d 137, 141 (5th Cir. 1996). “If the plaintiff’s claim is for
a sum certain and the defendant has not made an appearance in court, the clerk may
enter a default judgment. ” Hillman, 2023 WL 8113621, at *2 (citing Fed. R. Civ. P.
55(b)(1)). Otherwise, the plaintiff must apply to the court. Fed. R. Civ. P. 55(b)(2).  A
plaintiff “is not entitled to a default judgment as a matter of right, even where the
defendant is technically in default.” Lewis v. Lynn, 236 F.3d 766, 767 (5th Cir. 2001)
(per curiam) (quoting Ganther v. Ingle, 75 F.3d 207, 212 (5th Cir. 1996) (per curiam)).
A default judgment  is “a drastic remedy, not favored by the Federal Rules and
resorted to by courts only in extreme situations.” Id.  (quoting Sun Bank of Ocala v.
Pelican Homestead & Sav. Ass’n, 874 F.2d 274, 276 (5th Cir. 1989)).
“The role of a district court in adjudicating a motion for default judgment is
limited.” Escalante v. Lidge, 34 F.4th 486, 492 (5th Cir. 2022) . However, “a district
court has the duty to assure that it has the power to enter a valid default judgment.”
Sys. Pipe & Supply, Inc. v. M/V Viktor Kurnatovskiy , 242 F.3d 322, 324 (5th Cir.
2001). Before entering a default judgment against a non -appearing party, the Court
must “look into its jurisdiction both over the subject matter and the parties.”  Id.
(quoting Williams v. Life Sav. & Loan, 802 F.2d 1200 (10th Cir. 1986) (per curiam)).

This includes determining whether the plaintiff properly served the defaulting party.
Espinoza v. Humphries, 44 F.4th 275, 276 (5th Cir. 2022).
“When a case involves multiple defendants, courts may not grant default
judgment against one defendant if doing so would conflict with the position taken by
another defendant.” Escalante, 34 F.4th at 495. Otherwise, a court risks entering a
future judgment on the merits that is inconsistent with the  default judgment , a
situation described by the Supreme Court  as “unseemly and absurd. ” Id. (quoting
Frow v. De La Vega,  82 U.S.  (15 Wall.) 552, 554 (1872) ). When faced with this
possibility, courts should refrain from entering a default judgment “until the matter
has been adjudicated with regard to all defendants, or all defendants have defaulted.”

2. Service of Process
While the Court has subject matter jurisdiction over this action, it does not
have person al jurisdiction over the Succession because it has not been properly
served. “Before a federal court may exercise personal jurisdiction over a defendant,
the procedural requirement of service of summons must be satisfied.” Hazim v. Schiel
& Denv. Book Publishers, 647 F. App ’x 455, 460 (5th Cir. 2016) (quoting Omni Cap.
Int’l, Ltd. v. Rudolf Wolff & Co., 484 U.S. 97, 104 (1987)). The Federal Rules of Civil
Procedure provide that, subject to exceptions not relevant here, a plaintiff must serve
an individual in a judicial district of the United States by either:
(1) following state law for serving a summons in an action brought in
courts of general jurisdiction in the state where the district court is
located or where service is made; or
(2) doing any of the following:
(A) delivering a copy of the summons and of the complaint to the
individual personally;

The Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure provides that a plaintiff may serve an
individual by personal or domiciliary service. La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 1231. “Personal

the person to be served with a person of suitable age and discretion residing in the
domiciliary establishment.” Id. art. 1234.  For out -of-state defendants , Louisiana’s
long-arm statue permits a plaintiff’s attorney to  perform service by “ registered or
certified mail” or “commercial courier.” La. Rev. Stat. § 13:3204.
