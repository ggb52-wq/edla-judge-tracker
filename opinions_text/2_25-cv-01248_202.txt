ORDER & REASONS
Before the Court is a Motion to Set Recoverable Damages filed by Defendants Nexion
Health at Marrero, Inc., d/b/a Marrero Healthcare Center (“Nexion Health at Marrero”) and Nexion
Health Management Inc. (“Nexion  Management”) (collectively, “Defendants”). R. Doc . 176.
Plaintiff Kathy Sherrod opposes the motion. R. Doc . 184 . Defendants  replied. R. Doc. 188.
Considering the record, briefing, and applicable law, the Court rules as follows:
I. BACKGROUND
 Plaintiff Kathy Sherrod is the surviving spouse of Sheldon Sherrod, a former resident of
Marrero Healthcare Center. R. Doc. 1-1 at 1. Marrero Healthcare Center is a nursing home owned
and operated by Nexion Health at Marrero and managed by Nexion Management. Id.
 In August 2023, Mr. Sherrod was experiencing difficulties with breathing and balance, and
was no longer able to get out of bed, use the restroom, or bath e independently. R. Doc. 133-4 at
18. Plaintiff was unable to care for Mr. Sherrod at home in his condition. Id. On August 22, Plaintiff
visited Marrero Healthcare Center to inquire about her husband becoming a resident at the facility.
Id. at 22–26. Plaintiff asked facility representatives about the care provided to residents, including
residents’ meal schedules, the frequency  with which staff members bathed the residents, and
whether staff members assisted residents in going outside. Id. at 31 –32. Plaintiff testified in

 As part of the registration process, Marrero Healthcare Center presented Plaintiff with a

action against Defendants on a fraud claim pursuant to La. Civ. Code art. 1953. R. Doc. 15. Plaintiff
alleges that Defendants intentionally misrepresented and concealed their violation of statutory and
regulatory requirements governing the minimum staff necessary to adequately operate a nursing
home. Plaintiff represents that had she known Marre ro Healthcare Center was understaffed, she
would never have signed the admission agreement and Mr. Sherrod would not have resided at the
facility. Plaintiff seeks damages and attorney fees under La. Civ. Code art. 1958. Doc. 1-1 at 11.

III. DISCUSSION
La. Civ. Code art. 1998 governs the recovery of nonpecuniary losses in contract cases,
including fraud claims arising under art. 1953.2 Pursuant to art. 1998, “[d]amages for nonpecuniary
loss may be recovered when the contract, because of its nature, is intended to gratify a
nonpecuniary interest and, because of the circumstances surrounding the formation or the non -
performance of the contract, the obligor knew, or should  have known, that his failure to perform
would cause that kind of loss.” Art. 1998. Unpacked, this provision permits the recovery of
nonpecuniaries where two conditions are satisfied: (1) the plaintiff “intended to gratify a

Revision Comment (c) to art. 1998 defines a nonpecuniary interest as “one intended to
satisfy an interest of a spiritual order, such as a contract to create a work of art, or a contract to
conduct scientific research, or a contract involving matters of sen timental value.” The first
requirement—that the plaintiff “intended to gratify a nonpecuniary interest”—may be satisfied in
“hybrid, or mixed, situation[s]” where a plaintiff enters a contract “to gratify both pecuniary and
nonpecuniary interests.” Young v. Ford Motor Co., 595 So. 2d 1123, 1133 (La. 1992). The plaintiff
need only show that a nonpecuniary interest “constitute[d] . . . . one of the significant interests that
[they] intended to gratify” by entering the agreement. Id. “[T]he nature of the contract (including
the facts and circumstances attending its formation)” dictate whether the gratification of a
nonpecuniary interest constitutes a significant interest. Id. at 1124.
Plaintiff directs the Court to two cases permitting the recovery of nonpecuniary damages
under art. 1998 that she contends are analogous to the present matter. First, in Smith v. University
Animal Clinic, Inc ., the Louisiana Third Circuit Court of Appeal allowed the recovery of
nonpecuniary losses in an action involving the breach of a contract to board a house pet. 2009 -
745, p. 1, 3 (La. App. 3 Cir. 2/10/10), 30 So.  3d 1154, 1155, 1157, writ denied, 2010-0566 (La.
5/28/10), 36 So. 3d 247. In the court’s view, “[t]he contract to board [] went beyond simply paying
someone to feed a cat,” and clearly “involved matters of a sentimental value,” satisfying Revision
Comment (c)’s definition of a nonpecuniary interest. Id. at 1157. The boarding service had
advertised itself as providing “tender loving care” to the boarded pets to keep them “happy and
active.” Id. at 1157 –58. The intrinsic nature of the business coupled with the defendant’s
representations was sufficient to find that the contract was made for the gratification of a

nonpecuniary interest and the defendant knew or should have known that its failure to perform

Second, Free v. Franklin Guest Home concerned a suit brought against a nursing home on
behalf of a resident for breach of contract and negligence. 463 So. 2d 865, 873 (La. Ct. App. 1985),
writ denied sub nom. Free v. Franklin Guest Home , 467 So.2d 535 (La.1985) . Free was decided
under art. 1998’s predecessor, La. Civ. Code art. 1934, which similarly permitted the recovery of
nonpecuniary damages where the object of a contract was the gratification of a nonpecuniary
interest.3 There, the Louisiana Second Circuit Court of Appeal allowed the recovery of
nonpecuniary losses for the breach of the nursing home agreement, reasoning “[t]he contract was
not one simply for room, or board, or linens and beddings, but was one for [the resident’s] complete
care and well -being.” Id. at 874. The court further emphasized that, “[i]n a document entitled
‘Patient’s Rights,’ the home promised to treat [the resident] with ‘consideration, respect, and full
recognition of his dignity and individuality.’” Id.
The Court finds Smith and Free instructive, and consistent with their reasoning, concludes
that Plaintiff may recover nonpecuniary damages in the instant action. 4 Like Smith’ s boarding
service and Free’ s nursing home , the nature of the contract involved matters of a sentimental

and dignified way. When Plaintiff visited Marrero Healthcare Center, she inquired about multiple
facets of the residents’ lives, from meal schedules, to the frequency of assisted bathing, to whether
mobility-impaired residents were given assistance in going outside.  According to Plaintiff, staff
