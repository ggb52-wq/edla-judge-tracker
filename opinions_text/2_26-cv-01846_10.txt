ORDER AND REASONS

Plaintiff, Kathryn S. Pierce (“Pierce”), filed a Motion for Leave to File Amended
Complaint (R. Doc. 4). Defendants Jody Catalano (“Catalano”) and Rooms to Go
Louisiana Corp. (“RTG”) oppose (R. Doc. 6 ). Having considered the submissions of
counsel, the record, and the applicable law, the Court GRANTS the motion.
On December 30, 2025, Pierce filed a lawsuit against Defendants in the 24 th
Judicial District in Jefferson Parish, Louisiana, alleging she suffered injuries from a
fall at RTG’s storehouse.1 RTG filed a notice of removal on August 21, 2026, based on
diversity jurisdiction and what it alleges is the improper joinder of  nondiverse
defendant, Catalano, “solely to destroy diversity jurisdiction.”2 Pierce moved to
remand3 and filed the instant  motion to a mend, seeking to “clarify and specify the
allegations against non-diverse Defendant, Jody Catalano,” the on-duty manager the
day of Plaintiff’s alleged fall.4

Under Rule 15(a)(2), if more than 21 days after service of a responsive pleading
have passed, a party may amend its pleading only with the opposing party ’s written
consent or the court ’s leave. “ The court should freely give leave when justice so
requires.”5 Rule 15(a)(2) “evinces a bias in favor of granting leave to amend.”
Chitimacha Tribe of L a. v. Harry L.L. Co. , 690 F.2d 1157, 1163 (5th Cir. 1982); see
also Mayeaux v. La. Health Serv. & Indem. Co., 376 F.3d 420, 425 (5th Cir. 2004).
Indeed, the “district court must possess a ‘substantial reason’ to deny a request for
leave to amend.”  Smith v. EMC Corp. , 393 F.3d 590, 595 (5th Cir. 2004).  “That
generous standard is tempered by the necessary power of a district court to manage
a case.” Yumilicious  Franchise, L.L.C. v. Barrie, 819 F.3d 170, 177 (5th Cir. 2016)
(internal citations omitted).  Relevant factors to consider include  “undue delay, bad
faith or dilatory motive on the part of the movant, repeated failures to cure
deficiencies by amendments previously allowed, undue prejudice to the opposing
party by virtue of allowance of the amendment, and futility of the amendment.”
Schiller v. Physicians Res. Grp., Inc., 342 F.3d 563, 566 (5th Cir. 2003).
Defendants’ sole argument against amendment is that the proposed
amendment is futile —specifically, that it offers only conclusory allegations against
Catalano, which in turn necessitates a finding that the proposed amendment “fails to
state a viable claim of personal liability against [Catalano] .”6 Futility is an issue

“better addressed in the context of a Rule 12(b)(6) motion rather than a Rule 15(a)
motion for leave to amend.” Manship v. Throne, 2025 WL 1591444,  at *2 (E.D. La .
June 4, 2025) (citing Tesla, Inc. v. La.  Auto. Dealers Ass’n, 2023 WL 9059650, at *7
(E.D. La. Jan. 11, 2023)). Granting leave to amend is “the best exercise of discretion”
as it provides the opportunity for “ plaintiff’s best case” to be tested under the Rule
12(b)(6) framework. Tesla, Inc., 2023 WL 9059650, at *7 (“[I]t is not appropriate for
this Court to address that pending [Rule 12(b)(6)] matter in ruling on the proposed
amended complaint”).
Each of the relevant factors weighs in favor of amendment. The parties are in
the early stages of litigation , no trial date has been set, and the proposed pleading
does not add any new parties.  Further, there is no indication that amendment would
be unduly prejudicial.
For the reasons set forth above,
IT IS ORDERED that  the Motion (R. Doc. 4 ) is GRANTED. Plaintiff’s
proposed Amended Complaint (R. Doc. 4-3) shall be filed in the record.
