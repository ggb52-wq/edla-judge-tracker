REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

Petitioner, Zachary Le bourgeois, was a state pretrial detainee  confined at the
Jefferson Parish Correctional Center when he filed his petition seeking federal habeas corpus
relief pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 2241.  In that petition, he claimed that his constitutional rights
were violated by the denial of his pretrial motion to suppress illegally seized evidence, and
he asked the Court to intervene in his state -court criminal proceedings.  The matter was
referred to the undersigned magistrate judge for proposed findings and recommendations
for disposition.1 The record suffices to dispose of the matter without an evidentiary hearing.
For the following reasons, it is recommended that the petition be DISMISSED AS MOOT.

Analysis
In limited instances, a state pretrial detainee may raise constitutional challenges in a
habeas petition brought pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 2241 .  See  28 U.S.C. 2241(c); Dickerson v.
Louisiana, 816 F.2d at 226, 229 (acknowledging the “distinction drawn by the Supreme Court
in Braden and reiterated in appeals circuit  cases like Brown  and Atkins 28  between a
defendant “disrupting the orderly functioning of a state's judicial processes as opposed to
enforcing his right to have the state bring him promptly to trial” and noting that “f ederal
habeas relief should not be used as a ‘pre-trial motion forum for state prisoners.’ ”).  Here,
Lebourgeois cites no controlling law for the proposition that federal intervention and habeas
relief is available or proper when challenging a ruling denying a motion to suppress evidence
in an ongoing state -court criminal prosecution.  Nevertheless,  the Court need not resolve
that issue, because an overriding concern exists and warrants dismissal of the petition.

The resolution of Lebourgeois’ criminal case has mooted the instant petition.  See
U.S. Parole Comm'n v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388, 395–96 (1980) (explaining that a case becomes
moot “when the issues presented are no longer ‘live’ or the parties lack a legally cognizable
interest in the outcome”); United States v. Sanchez -Gomez, 584 U.S. 381, 391– 94 (2018)
(holding that claims challenging use of physical restraints during pretrial proceedings were
moot when petitioners were no longer in pretrial custody upon entering guilty pleas); Clark
v. Payne, 341 F. App'x 355, 356 (10th Cir. 2009) (“Because Mr. Clark entered a plea of guilty
and was subsequently sentenced, his request for dismissal of his pending prosecution under
§ 2241 has been mooted.”) ; see also Fassler v. United States , 858 F.2d 1016, 1018 (5th Cir.
1988) (recognizing that habeas petitions challenging legality o f pretrial detention were
rendered moot by conviction); Wilson v. Fleischman, Civ. Action 21-1494, 2023 WL 121987,
at *3 (E.D. La. Jan. 6, 2023) (dismissing as moot § 2241 petition involving pretrial claims over
the revocation of bond because petitioner pleaded guilty and was no longer in pretrial
detention).  Although Lebourgeois filed this § 2241 petition while state criminal charges
were pending against him, he has since entered guilty pleas to those charges.  He is now a
convicted inmate being detained under a state -court judgment of conviction.  Because he
is no longer detained in pretrial custody, he is not eligible for relief under 28 U.S.C. §  2241.
The pretrial suppression issue raised in his habeas petition is moot and may not be resolved
in this federal § 2241 proceeding.29
