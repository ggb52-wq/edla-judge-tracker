When a petitioner  challenges the  revocation of his diminution of sentence  for good

seeking relief in federal court.22
The exhaustion requirement is satisfied only when all grounds urged in a federal petition

Under Louisiana’s C orrections Administrative Remedy Procedure (“CARP”) , an inmate
must utilize the administrative procedure established by the DOC, or any facility in which he is
housed, to resolve any claims related to his confinement, including “time computations, even
though urged as a writ of habeas corpus, or challenges to rules, regulations, policies, or statutes.”26
Upon completion of the administrative process within the DOC, the inmate may seek judicial
review in the 19th Judicial District Court, East Baton Rouge Parish. 27 Thereafter, the inmate is
entitled to appeal the decision to the “appropriate court of appeal.”28 If the appeal is unsuccessful,
Louisiana law allows the inmate to seek supervisory review in the Louisiana Supreme Court.29
The record does not reflect, and Davis has not alleged, that he exhausted administrative
review of his claim under CARP, La. Rev. Stat. § 15:1171 et seq., and the subsequent supervisory

The exhaustion doctrine is subject to limited exceptions.  In a § 2241 action, the exhaustion
requirement may be obviated only when “special circumstances” exist. 30 “Exceptions to the
exhaustion requirement are appropriate where the available administrative remedies either are
unavailable or wholly inappropriate to the relief sought, or where the attempt to exhaust such
remedies would itself be a patently futile cour se of action.” 31 Similarly, in § 2254 actions,
exhaustion may be excused “only if there is no opportunity to obtain redress in state court or if the
corrective process is so clearly deficient as to render futile any effort to obtain relief.”32
In this case, no such circumstances exist. Davis has not provided any basis to conclude that
pursuing his claim through each level of the state courts via the state trial court or the
administrative remedy procedure would be futile. Davis admits that his claim is currently pending
in state court (i.e., Orleans Parish Criminal District Court). Further, Davis has also not pursued his
claim through the appropriate administrative remedy procedure. As such, Davis has not given each
level of the Louisiana courts a fair opportunity to consider the merits of his claim.
For these reasons, Davis’s petition should be dismissed without prejudice for failure to
exhaust available state administrative and judicial remedies.
