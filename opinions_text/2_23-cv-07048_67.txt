broad discretion to grant or to deny a motion for reconsideration. S. Snow Mfg. Co. v.

substantive law.” Austin, 864 F.3d at 336 (quoting Lavespere v. Niagara Mach. & Tool

orders and the resulting burdens and delays .” S. Snow Mfg. Co., 921 F. Supp. 2d at
564–65.
The Court liberally construes Plaintiff’s pro se motion. See Collins v. Dall.
Leadership Found., 77 F.4th 327, 330 (5th Cir. 2023). Liberally construed, Plaintiff’s
motion raises three new arguments. First, the Court made a clear error of law or fact
when it did not require Allstate to provide a disclosure statement under Federal Rule
of Civil Procedure 7.1.25 Second, the Court’s refusal to correct the record is a manifest
injustice to Plaintiff because Allstate’s untimely notice of removal was an abuse of
process not subject to the 30-day timeline.26 Third, the Court’s order denying remand
raises new federalism issues.27 As discussed below,  Plaintiff’s motion for
reconsideration is DENIED .

Plaintiff first claims that the Court made a clear error of law or fact by not
requiring Allstate to file a disclosure statement under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure
7.1.28 Under Rule 7.1, a party must file a disclosure statement when removing an
action to  federal court . FED. R. CIV. P. 7.1(2)(A). The disclosure statement must
“name—and identify the citizenship of—every individual or entity whose citizenship
is attributed to that party .” Id. The purpose of Rule 7.1 is to assist federal courts in
determining whether diversity jurisdiction exists. Genesis Marine, LLC v. Petroleum
Fuel & Terminal Co., No. 26-1050, 2026 WL 1839129, at *3 (E.D. La. June 26, 2026).
To assist litigants, this Court offers a “Rule 7.1 Disclosure Statement Form.”29
Instead of using this form, Allstate filed its own “ Corporate Disclosure Statement
pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure Rule 7.1 .”30 In the statement, Allstate
disclosed that it is a  is a wholly owned subsidiary of The Allstate Corporation, an
Illinois corporation. 31 Moreover, the stock of The Allstate Corporation is publicly
traded, and n o publicly held entity owns 10% or more of the stock. 32 The Court is
satisfied that Allstate’s disclosure statement meets the substantive requirements of
Rule 7.1 and therefore finds no legal error. Requiring Allstate to file another
disclosure form would be unnecessarily duplicative and would not aid this Court in
determining whether diversity jurisdiction exists.

Subject to the 30-Day Timeline Under 28 U.S.C. § 1447

Next, Plaintiff alleges that  she did not waive her ability to seek the relief of
remand because “[t]here is nothing in 28 U.S.C. § 1447 that requires a party to file a
motion to remand  for abuse of process or misconduct within a 30 -day timeline .”37
Plaintiff further asserts that the Court’s failure to correct the record is a “ manifest
injustice to Plaintiff.”38 The Court does not agree.
Under 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c), “[a] motion to remand the case on the basis of a
defect other than lack of subject matter jurisdiction must be made within 30 days
after the filing of the Notice of Removal.” The Fifth Circuit has held this to mean that
any defect in removal that does not go the issue of “whether the case originally could
have been brought in federal district court” is “procedural.” Baris v. Sulpicio Lines,
Inc., 932 F.2d 1540, 1544 (5th Cir. 1991). A plaintiff that fails to object to a procedural
error in the removal within 30 days waives objection to that error. Ragas v. Tenn. Gas
Pipeline Co., 136 F.3d 455, 457–58 (5th Cir. 1998).  As th is Court has previously
explained,39 Plaintiff’s motion does not challenge whether the case originally could
have been brought in federal court as a diversity action ; it is therefore procedural.
Thus, Plaintiff waived her ability to seek the relief of remand  by filing a motion to
correct the record almost two years after Defendant’s notice of removal.40

waived her procedural objections to the notice of removal.
C. Removal of this Action to Federal Court Does Not Conflict with
Principles of Federalism

Finally, Plaintiff raises two federalism issues, neither of which are persuasive.
First, Plaintiff questions whether federal law permit s a federal court to “ disregard”
rulings made by a state court prior to the filing of a notice of removal.42 According to
Plaintiff, “there is nothing in federal law and/or federal court precedents that requires

a plaintiff to begin the litigation process anew after a notice of removal is filed.”43 But
the Court has not asked Plaintiff to “begin the litigation process anew.”  “[W]hen a
case is removed from state court to federal court, the federal court takes the case as
it finds it and treats the state court rulings as its own. ” In re. Ala. & Dunlavy, Ltd.,
983 F.3d 766, 773 (5th Cir. 2020); see also 28 U.S.C. § 1450 (“All injunctions, orders,
and other proceedings had in such action prior to its removal shall remain in full force
and effect until dissolved or modified by the district court.”).
Plaintiff claims that, on November 17, 2023, she filed a motion for entry of
default in state court against Allstate. 44 Five days later, Allstate filed a notice of
removal.45 According to Plaintiff, she then attempted to file a motion for default
judgment and a hearing request in state court. 46 But once Allstate filed its valid
notice of removal,47 the state court no longer had jurisdiction over the action. See Wolf
v. Deutsche Bank Nat’l Tr. Co. ex rel. Am. Home Mortg. Inv. Tr. 2007-1, 745 F. App’x
205, 208 (5th Cir. 2018)  (“[O]nce [defendant] removed the case to federal court, no
state court retained jurisdiction  over the [action]; there were no concurrent
proceedings.”). Therefore, what remains from state court is Plaintiff’s pending motion
for entry of default. But filing a notice of default without any action taken by the state
court does not amount to an injunction, order, or other proceeding that would have
force and effect in federal court. See 28 U.S.C. § 1450.

Second, Plaintiff claims that this Court wrongfully declared Louisiana Code of
Civil Procedure Article 42 unconstitutional. 48 The Court did not. Instead, t he Court
explained49 that state law venue rules do not override federal statutes that confer
subject matter jurisdiction to federal district courts to hear certain cases and
controversies. See Randall v. Arabian Am. Oil Co., 778 F.2d 1146, 1150 (5th Cir. 1985)
(“Only the Constitution and the laws of the United States can dictate what cases or
controversies our federal courts may hear.”); 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(1) (“The district
courts shall have original jurisdiction of all civ il actions where the matter in
controversy exceeds the sum or value of $75,000, exclusive of interest and costs, and
is between [] citizens of different States.”).
Louisiana Code of Civil Procedure Article 42 provides the general venue rules
for litigation in Louisiana state courts. LA. CODE CIV. PROC. art. 42. Plaintiff correctly
identifies that, under the code, the 40th  Judicial District Court for the Parish of St.
John the Baptist was a proper venue to bring her action in state court.50 But being a
proper venue does not mean it is the only  available venue. That is, the same action
can be properly brought  in multiple courts  because statutory rules provide several
alternative venues for filing a lawsuit . Here, Plaintiff’s action is proper in both
Louisiana state court and federal district court under the court’s diversity
jurisdiction. See 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(1) . And the Louisiana Code  of Civil Procedure
does not—and cannot—strip jurisdiction conferred by Congress to the federal courts.

See Chi. & N.W.R. Co. v. Whitton, 80 U.S. 270, 271 (1871) (“Whenever a general rule
as to property or personal rights  . . . is established by State legislation, its
enforcement by a Federal court in a case between proper parties is a matter of course,

principle does not undermine federalism; rather, it is a n extension of its dual -
sovereignty structure. Despite Plaintiff’s assertions to the contrary,51 this Court does
not dispute that Allstate agreed to abide by Louisiana law by registering to conduct
business in the state. However, this does not prevent Allstate from exercising its right
under U.S. law to remove the action to federal court. See 28 U.S.C. § 1441(a).
For these reasons, the Court declines to reconsider its decision on the grounds
of federalism.
