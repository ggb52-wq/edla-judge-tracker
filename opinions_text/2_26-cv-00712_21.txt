REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

On April 3, 2026, Plaintiff Curtis Lavson Mitchell sought to file a petition for a writ of habeas
corpus pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 2254 challenging his 2026 state court conviction.  ECF Nos. 1, 7.  On
August 3, 2026, petitioner filed a Letter to the Court, titled an “Affidavit of Truth,” in which he states
that he is a natural living man that is indigenous to America and, therefore, the state trial court lacks
subject matter jurisdiction over the criminal matters for which he was convicted. ECF No. 20 at 2.
Mitchell requests his immediate release from Jefferson Parish Correctional Center.  Id. at 4.  Broadly
construing the letter, Mitchell appears to request immediate injunctive relief (i.e., release from
custody) pending resolution of his pending habeas proceeding.
I. APPLICABLE LAW
A. The Court’s Authority to Release Pending Determination of Habeas Proceeding
Despite the lack of specific statutory authorization, federal district courts have inherent power
and jurisdiction to release prisoners, including state prisoners, on bail pending determination of a
federal habeas corpus petition. 1  In order to show entitlement to release pending determination of a
habeas petition, a state prisoner must show he has (1) raised substantial constitutional claims upon
which he has a high probability of success, and (2) extraordinary and exceptional circumstances exist
which make the grant of bail necessary to make the habeas remedy effective. 2 Extraordinary

B. Injunctive Relief
Further, a request for immediate release pending a ruling on a habeas petition is essentially a
request for immediate injunctive relief that mirrors the relief requested in the habeas.4  To warrant the
extraordinary remedy of injunctive relief, the movant must show: (1) a substantial likelihood his cause
will succeed on the merits, (2) a substantial threat of irreparable injury if the injunction is not granted,
(3) the threatened injury outweighs the threatened harm the injunction may do to the opposing party,
and (4) granting the injunction will not disserve the public interest. 5   Preliminary relief should not
“conclusively resolve legal disputes[,]” and thus a temporary restraining order is an inappropriate
vehicle for granting ultimate relief.6  A court cannot render ultimate relief sought in a habeas petition—
releasing a detainee—in the form of a preliminary injunction or temporary restraining order.7
