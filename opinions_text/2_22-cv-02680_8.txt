FED. R. CIV. P. 4(m) requires that a defendant be served within 90 days of the filing of the
complaint, and if service is not made, the court must dismiss the action without prejudice or order
service by a specific time.  For a defendant not served in state court, Rule 4(m)’s “90-day deadline
. . . commences at the time of removal.”1  The jurisprudence has come to expect strict compliance
with the service rules within the 90-day period prescribed by Rule 4(m) and its predecessor. 2  I n
addition, FED. R. CIV. P. 41(b) provides that a court may in its discretion dismiss any action based
on the failure of the plaintiff to prosecute or comply with any order of the court.3
Disedare filed his state cour t petition on October 21, 2021, and more than 90 days have
elapsed since the Defendants remove the case to this Court on August 16, 2022.  To date, there
has been no service on defendant Adams as required under Rule 4(m), nor has Disedare responded
to my order or provided any information or excuse for the failure to serve and prosecute this case
as to defendant Adams.  Disedare has provided no basis for the Court to c onsider an alternative
to dismissal of his claims against Adams.  Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the court’s orders and
rules also has created delay in the court’s ability to efficiently manage the case.  Thus, dismissal
without prejudice of Disedare’s claims against defendant Sgt. Miley Adams is proper under both
Rule 4(m) and Rule 41(b) for his failure to re spond to my order, serve summons, and prosecute
this case.
