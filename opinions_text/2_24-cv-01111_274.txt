Inc., 869 So. 2d 930, 932 (La. App. 2004).  The evidence may be direct or circumstantial.  Williams v. Boeing Co., 23
F.4th 507, 512 (5th Cir. 2022).  When there are multiple causes of injury, “a defendant’s conduct is a cause in fact if
it is a substantial factor generating plaintiff’s harm.”  Adams v. Owens-Corning Fiberglas Corp., 923 So. 2d 118, 122
(La. App. 2005) (citing Vodanovich, 869 So. 2d at 932).  Because there is a medically demonstrated causal relationship
between asbestos exposure and mesothelioma, “every non-trivial exposure to asbestos contributes to and constitutes
a cause of mesothelioma.”  McAskill v. Am. Marine Holding Co., 9 So. 3d 264, 268 (La. App. 2009) (observing that
the substantial-factor “burden can be met by simply showing that [the plaintiff] was actively working with asbestos-
containing materials”). To defeat a motion for summary judgment regarding his asbestos exposure, a plaintiff “need
only show that a reasonable jury could conclude that it is more likely than not that [he] inhaled defendant’s asbestos
fibers, even if there were only ‘slight exposures.’”  Williams, 23 F.4th at 512 (citing Held v. Avondale Indus., Inc. ,
672 So. 2d 1106, 1109 (La. App. 1996)).  At his deposition,  Thibodeaux testified that one of his job duties was to
assemble “kits” of helicopter components for deployed helicopters and he thought HH-52 helicopters might have had
brakes made by Reddaway.  R. Doc. 239-1 at 2, 4 (citing R. Doc. 239-5).  At that time, he was reading from discovery
responses.  Id. (citing R. Doc. 239-5).  However, without the aid of the discovery responses, he later testified that he
did not remember the manufacturer of the brakes.  Id. at 2-3 (citing R. Doc. 239-6).  Thibodeaux did not testify that
he was ever exposed to asbestos from any product manufactured, sold, or distributed by Reddaway.  Id. at 3.  And
importantly, regardless of the manufacturer, Thibodeaux testif ied that the brakes from the kits he oversaw were not
removed from the packaging in his presence and he was not present fo r the actual work on the brakes.  Id. at 3-4.
Plaintiffs have submitted no summary-judgment type evidence to refute these facts.  Accordingly, Reddaway’s motion
must be granted.
