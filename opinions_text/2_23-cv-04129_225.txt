LAW AND ANALYSIS
I. The Court in its discretion will award deposition costs.
 This Court will grant Plaintiff’s $24,03 9 in deposition costs over Defendant’s
objection. The prevailing party bears the burden to demonstrate the necessity of costs and
reasonableness of the amount it seeks to recover.35 When a prevailing party is entitled to
costs, the scope of that award is controlled by  28 U.S.C. § 1920. The statute instructs in
relevant part that: “A judge or clerk of any court of the United States may tax as costs the
following: . . . (2) Fees for printed or elec tronically recorded transcripts necessarily
obtained for use in the case.”36 In determining whether deposition costs are taxable under
28 U.S.C. § 1920, courts look as to whet her the deposition or copy was “reasonably
expected to be used for trial or trial preparation.” 37 Furthermore, “whether a deposition
or copy was necessarily obtained for use in the case is a factual determination within the
district court’s discretion,” and there is “great latitude in this determination.”38
 In the instant case, Plaintiff argues that deposition transcripts of all deponents and
applicable video recordings were necessary to prepare trial strategy and to plan for the
possibility that certain witnesses could not be subpoenaed or would not be able to attend

II. The Court for similar reasons will award trial technician costs.
 This Court also will grant, over Defendant’s objection, Plaintiff’s $11,841.19 costs
incurred in retaining a trial technician’s services. The prevailing party bears the burden
of demonstrating the necessity of costs and reasonableness of the amount it seeks to
recover.44 When a prevailing party is entitled to costs, the scope of that award is controlled
by 28 U.S.C. § 1920. The statute instructs in relevant part that: “A judge or clerk of any
court of the United States may tax as costs the following: . . . (4) Fees for exemplification
and the costs of making copies of any materials where the copies are necessarily obtained
for use in the case.”45
 The Court’s broad discretion in taxing costs is limited by the provisions of 28 U.S.C.
§ 1920. 46 District courts in the Fifth Circuit routinely have granted costs for trial
technician’s services under 28 U.S.C. § 1920(4).47 In Contogouris v. WestPac Res., LLC ,
the district court granted costs incurred by hi ring a trial technician “who prepared all
exhibits for use at the trial by enabling those exhibits to be instantly enlarged and
presented to jurors on their individual mo nitors and through the Court’s projection
screen.”48 Examining the services rendered by the trial technician in this case, the Court
notes that the trial technician also perfor med a similar, if not the same, function of
carefully preparing exhibits for trial and helpin g with the projection of such exhibits at
trial itself.49 While Defendant argues the Court in Contogoris made a specific finding that

Plaintiff has demonstrated that the hiring of a trial technician was  “anticipated, useful
and necessary,” because it allowed for the seam less presentation of evidence to the jury
by displaying exhibits. Thus, Plaintiff can recover “fees for exemplification” under 28
U.S.C. § 1920(4).
