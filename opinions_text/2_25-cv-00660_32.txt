REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

Plaintiff Bradley Joseph Lawrence Savoy’s Motion for Appointment of Counsel,
Litigation Support, Postage Relief, Discovery Production, Extension of Time to
Respond, and Notice Regarding Medical Privacy and SSI Status (R. Doc. 18) was
referred to the undersigned for a finding and recommendation relative to whether
this matter should be dismissed as frivolous under 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1)(B) and Local
Rule 73.1 A finding and recommendation relative to whether his Complaint should be
dismissed as frivolous is to follow and, if his Complaint survives, Plaintiff may file a
new motion for appointment of counsel  at the appropriate time . For the following
reasons, the undersigned recommends that the Motion  for Appointment of Counsel
be denied.
Plaintiff filed this pro se and in forma pauperis  complaint pursuant to 42
U.S.C. § 1983  against the New Orleans Police Department , City of New Orleans,
Tulane University Hospital, Tulane Police Department, Acadian Ambulance,
University Medical Center, and Ochsner Hospital. 2 Plaintiff’s allegations arise from

A plaintiff in a civil rights case has no right to the automatic appointment of
counsel. Ulmer v. Chancellor, 691 F.2d 209, 212 (5th Cir. 1982). A district court, in
its discretion, may appoint counsel to represent a plaintiff in a § 1983 proceeding “if
doing so would advance the proper administration of justice.” Id. at 213. Thus, the
court should only appoint counsel for an indigent plaintiff in a civil rights case if the
case presents exceptional circumstances. Norton v. E.U. Dimazana, 122 F.3d 286, 293
(5th Cir. 1997). In making this determination, the undersigned has considered all of
the following factors: (a) the type and complexity of the case; (b) whether the plaintiff
is capable of presenting his  case adequately; (c) whether he is in a position to
investigate his case adequately; and (d) whether the  evidence will consist in large
part of conflicting testimony so as to require skill in the presentation of evidence and
in cross-examination. Parker v. Carpenter, 978 F.2d 190, 193 (5th Cir. 1992); Ulmer,
691 F.2d at 213.
Plaintiff has not identified any extraordinary circumstances.  Excessive force
and involuntary medical treatment claims, like those in this case, are not considered
complex and require no difficult investigation to determine the alleged actions taken

by Defendants. See, e.g., Lewis v. Brengesty , 385 F. App’x 395, 397 (5th Cir. 2010)
(affirming denial of counsel in § 1983 action alleging excessive force); see also , Kiser
v. Dearing, 442 F. App’x 132, 135 (5th Cir. 2011) (“[T]he legal contours of excessive
force claims are well -established and not particularly complex.”).  Plaintiff’s case
likely will involve mostly factual questions and possible conflicting testimony, as is
the nature of litigation. However, such conflicts are not indicative of unusual or
exceptional circumstances requiring assistance of counsel. See James v. Gonzalez, 348
F. App’x 957, 958 (5th Cir. 2009) (“conflicting testimony” are among the “common
elements in civil rights cases” and does not necessarily “rise to the level of exceptional
circumstances.”).Plaintiff’s claims , while serious as alleged,  are not legally
complicated either and thus not complex is circumstance or presentation.
Although it is difficult to determine at this early stage of the proceeding either
that discovery will be required or that the case is susceptible to summary disposition,
the Court notes that Plaintiff has not stated that he is incapable of adequately
investigating or presenting his case, but rather that he needs legal assistance due to
the sheer number of cases circulating before the federal court. While Plaintiff may
not be trained in the law, he has demonstrated the ability to express himself through
the filing of the complaint and motions on his own behalf, and more than adequately
understand the facts of his case without assistance of counsel. Plaintiff also has not
shown whether any efforts to secure counsel have been made. Even assuming that
Plaintiff has exhausted efforts to retain counsel, this Court is not convinced that

Plaintiff’s situation justifies the special benefit of having counsel appointed to
represent him at this time.
Thus, although Plaintiff is indigent, and any litigant could potentially benefit
from assistance with conducting discovery and engaging in cross -examination
assuming this matter proceeds to trial, his case is not an exceptional one under the
foregoing factors and presents no circumstances that would require appointment of
counsel. Akasike v.  Fitzpatrick, 26 F.3d 510, 512 (5th Cir. 1994) (counsel is only
appointed under exceptional circumstances in a civil rights case); see also Wendell v.
Asher, 162 F.3d 88 7 (5th Cir. 1998); Robbins v. Maggio , 750 F.2d 405, 412 (5th Cir.
1985); Ulmer, 691 F.2d at 212-13; Hardwick v. Ault, 517 F.2d 295, 298 (5th Cir. 1975).
The record presents no circumstance that  would warrant appointment of counsel at
this time. Should the case develop such that the judicial  process would benefit from
Plaintiff having counsel, the Court can evaluate any change of  circumstance at that
time. Ulmer, 691 F.2d at 213.
Accordingly,
It is therefore RECOMMENDED that the Plaintiff’s Motion for Appointment
of Counsel (R. Doc. 18) be DENIED.
