I. Background
In March 2020 Congress passed and President Trump signed into law the
Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Securities (CARES) Act  to provide economic
support for the American people during the COVID -19 pandemic. T he CARES Act
established the Paycheck Protection Program, which provided federally guaranteed,
forgivable loans to help small businesses continue paying their employees.4 This case
arises from one such loan issued to Community Associates, for which the SBA
subsequently found the company in eligible because it was  primarily engaged in the
business of lending.

II. Legal Standards
A. Administrative Procedure Act
Under the Administrative Procedure Act, a reviewing court is to “decide all
relevant questions of law, interpret constitutional and statutory provisions, and
determine the meaning or applicability of the terms of an agency action.”27 The
court is empowered to “hold unlawful and set aside agency action, findings, and
conclusions found to be” arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, contrary to
law, in excess of statutory authority or limitations, or without observance of
procedure required by law.28 Courts are to “exercise their independent judgment in

deciding whether an agency has acted within its statutory authority.”29 So long as
the agency stays within its statutory authority, the court’s role is not to weigh the
evidence for a decision “pro and con,” but to determine “whether the agency has
examine[d] the relevant data and articulate[d] a satisfactory explanation for its
action including a rational connection between the facts found and the choice
made.”30 So long as “the agency’s reasons and policy choices conform to minimal
standards of rationality, then its actions are reasonable and must be upheld,” even
if a court believes the result is less than ideal.31 However, if an agency relied on
improper factors, entirely failed to consider an important aspect of the problem, or
offered explanations contrary to the evidence before the agency, the decision was
arbitrary and capricious.32 In undertaking its review, a court is to consider “the
agency decision based on the record the agency presents to the reviewing court.”33
Here, Defendant filed the administrative record without objection.34
