United States, 866 F.2d 120, 125 (5th Cir. 1989)). For the following reasons, only the

As to the first factor, this case does not involve a newly discovered witness. To
the contrary, it has long been known that Mitchem may be a significant witness
relative to Irvin’s alleged discriminatory selection decisions. Kandan maintains,
however, that there are two new items of potential proof: (1) a particular meeting at
which Mitchem was subject to discriminatory treatment by Irvin , and (2) Mitchem’s
belief that her purportedly panel interview process had been sabotaged by Travis
Nicholson.26 Neither justification is persuasive.
From the lawsuit’s outset, the parties have been aware that Mitchem believed
Irvin discriminated against her with respect to selection decisions.  Even if Kandan
was unaware of the foundation for Mitchem’s belief, she had sufficient information to
prompt a timely discovery deposition. In her original witness list, Kandan timely
disclosed several EEOC employees who may claim discrimination by Irvin—but not
Mitchem.27 Given that Kandan knew Mitchem fit within this same category of
witness, the Court can only assume that Mitchem’s non- inclusion on the earlier
witness list reflected a strategy decision.

The third factor weighs  against allowing amendment —primarily because of

deposed key witnesses as discovery closed.  That delay also encompassed the period
when the parties were engaged in motions practice as to the admissibility of
testimony about Mitchem. Given that the parties were briefing and presenting oral

Further, the nature of Mitchem’s potential testimony would require EEOC to

With respect to the fourth factor, Kandan has not requested a continuance. To
the contrary, both sides appear committed to proceeding with trial on October 2 0,
2025. Thus, this factor also weighs against amendment.
As explained above, the sole factor that supports allowing Kandan to add
Mitchem to the witness list  is the potential significance of her testimony.  Kandan
knew that Mitchem was a potentially significant witness, however, since before filing
this lawsuit.  Even knowing that Mitchem was allegedly subject to parallel
discriminatory treatment by the same supervisor, Kandan opted not to list Mitchem
as a witness nor seek her deposition.  Further, even as the parties  litigated whether
