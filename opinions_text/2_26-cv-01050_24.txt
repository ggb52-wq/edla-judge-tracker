III. Legal Standard
In analyzing the issues presented by the instant motion, the Court is guided by the
fundamental principle that, unlike state courts, federal courts are courts of limited jurisdiction. 88
That is, f ederal courts have the power to exercise jurisdiction only where authorized by the
Constitution or by statute. 89 Federal district courts possess two forms of original subject -matter
jurisdiction: federal question jurisdiction90 and diversity jurisdiction.91
A defendant may remove a state civil court action to federal court provided that the federal
court has original jurisdiction over the matter. 92 The removing party bears the burden of

Diversity jurisdiction exists over any civil matter between citizens of different states, where
the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000. 95 The exercise of diversity jurisdiction requires
“complete diversity” between the parties, meaning that each plaintiff must be diverse from every
defendant.96 When removal is based on diversity of citizenship, the diversity “must exist both at

commencement and removal of the suit, it will not be destroyed by subsequent changes in the
citizenship of the extant parties.”98 On a motion to remand, in particular, the removing party bears
the burden of establishing the citizenship of relevant parties for the purpose of demonstrating
complete diversity.99

determinative.101 A corporation is a citizen of both the state in which it is incorporated and the state

I V.  Analysis
A. Admiralty Jurisdiction as an Alternative Basis for Jurisdiction

Plaintiff asserts that Defendant’s attempt to rely on this Court’s admiralty jurisdiction under
28 U.S.C. § 1333 as an alternative basis of removal is contrary to well-settled law.  Plaintiff notes
that Defendant failed to provide support for this alternative jurisdictional basis other than the mere
assertion that Plaintiff’s Petition alleges a maritime tort.  Defendant has not directly responded to
this argument.
The Court agrees with Plaintiff that Defendant’s jurisdictional allegation under 28 U.S.C.
§ 1333 is contrary to well -established law. “Maritime claims initiated in state court are, by
definition, brought at common law under the saving to suitors clause as  an ‘exception’ to the
original jurisdiction of the federal courts.”111 Therefore, the Court finds that 28 U.S.C. § 1333 alone
does not grant the Court jurisdiction over this matter.

not and cannot adequately establish diversity jurisdiction .112 It is not disputed that the  amount in
controversy requirement is satisfied here. Instead, Plaintiff argues that Defendant, a citizen of
Missouri, “has not and cannot meet its burden of establishing [complete diversity].” 113

Genesis Energy, L.P. and specifically failed to allege the citizenship of  its limited partners. 114
