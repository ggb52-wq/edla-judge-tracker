Plaintiff Tomarcus Porter, a state prisoner, filed this pro se and in forma pauperis civil
action pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983 against Defendants Jonathan Tynes, Aaron Bennett, Lloyd
Spears, Dillion Tynes, and Charles Griffin.   This Court granted Plaintiff pauper statu s (rec.
doc. 4), and the U.S. Marshal subsequently served four of the five Defendants.  1  The four
served Defendants then filed a motion for extension of time within which to answer (rec. doc.
11), which the Clerk of Court corrected.  (Rec. doc. 12).  On June 6, 2024, t his Court granted
the motion for extension of time.   (Rec. doc. 13).  On June 12, 2024, the notice of correction
by the Clerk of Court, which was mailed to Plaintiff at his address of record, was returned by
the United States Postal Service as undeliverable.  (Rec . doc. 15).  Subsequently, two more
orders of this Court have been returned as undeliverable.  (Rec. docs. 17, 18).

 In light of the foregoing, it is appropriate to dismiss P laintiff’s Complaint for want of
prosecution.  The authority of a federal trial court to dismiss a plaintiff’s action because of
failure to prosecute is clear.  Link v. Wabash R.R. Co., 370 U.S. 626, 629 (1962); McCullough v.
Lynaugh, 835 F.2d 1126, 1127 (5th Cir. 1988).  The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure
specifically provide that a court may, in its discretion, dismiss a plaintiff’s action for failure
to prosecute or for failure to comply with the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure or any order
of the court and that such a dismissal is considered to be an adjudication on the merits.  Fed.
R. Civ. P. 41(b).  The Court’s power to dismiss for want of prosecution should be used
sparingly, although it may be exercised sua sponte whenever necessary to achieve the
orderly and expeditious disposition of cases.  Ramsay v. Bailey , 531 F.2d 706, 707 (5th Cir.
1976).

 Because Plaintiff proceed s pro se, the Court need only consider his conduct in
determining whether dismissal is proper under Rule 41(b).  Here, P laintiff has failed to
provide the Court with his current address despite being aware of his obligation to do so,
and mail sent to him at his address of record has been returned as undeliverable.  For these

appropriate.
