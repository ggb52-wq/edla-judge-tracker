ORDER AND REASONS

 Before the Court is the Motion to Quash Trial Subpoena filed by Michelle McGowan (“Ms.
McGowan”).1 Plaintiffs Erica Dandry Constanza and Monica Dandry Hallner (“Plaintiffs”) issued
a trial subpoena (the “Trial Subpoena”) to Ms. McGowan as the corporate representative of EMSL
Analytical, Inc. (“EMSL”) under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 45. 2 Neither EMSL nor Ms.
McGowan is a  party to the above -captioned matter.  A trial for this  matter is set to begin on
November 2, 2026. On October 14, 2026, Ms. McGowan filed the instant motion requesting that
the Court quash the Trial Subpoena issued to Ms. McGowan. Plaintiffs oppose the instant motion.3
Considering the motion, the memoranda in support and opposition, and the applicable law, the
Court grants in part and denies in part  Ms. McGowan’s Motion to Quash Trial Subpoena. The
motion is granted to the extent that it seeks to quash the Trial Subpoena as an improper subpoena
duces tecum. The Trial Subpoena is modified to compel Ms. McGowan’s virtual attendance at
trial. The motion is denied in all other respects.

III. Law and Analysis
Ms. McGowan filed the instant Motion to Quash Trial Subpoena on October 14, 2026. 34
Neither EMSL nor Ms. McGowan is a party to this litigation. In the motion, Ms. McGowan argues
that the subpoena must be quashed because the T rial Subpoena fails to comply with the
geographical limits of Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 45(c). Specifically, Ms. McGowan insists
that she lives and works in the State of New York, which is well beyond Rule 45(c) ’s 100-mile
reach when the place of compliance is set in Louisiana. Plaintiffs oppose the motion, arguing that
Rule 45(c)’s geographical limits are satisfied here because Ms. McGowan’s employment with
EMSL is a national position, requiring her to travel and work at EMSL’s Baton Rouge lab.
However, Ms. McGowan asserts that she has only traveled to Louisiana once for EMSL-related
work, roughly two decades ago.
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 45(d)(3) governs the quashing or modification  of a
subpoena. Rule 45(d)(3)(A) provides, in pertinent part, that, upon timely motion, the Court “must
quash or modify a subpoena that  . . . requires a person to comply beyond the geographical limits
specified in Rule 45(c) . . . [or] subjects a person to undue burden.”

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 45(c) details the geographical limits of trial subpoenas on
both parties and non -parties. R ule 45(c) essentially restricts a court’s authority to compel an
individual to travel long distances to testify at trial. Rule 45(c)(1) provides that “[a] subpoena may
command a [non-party] to attend a trial” that is “ within 100 miles of where the person resides, is
employed, or regularly transacts business  in person.” 35 Rule 45(c)(1) also permits a court to
compel a non-party to attend a trial in “the state where the person resides, is employed, or regularly
transacts business in person,” as long as the subpoenaed party “would not incur substantial
expense.”36
On the other hand, under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 43(a), the Court may permit a
trial witness to testify virtually “[f]or good cause in compelling circumstances and with appropriate
safeguards.” Whether the Court may compel a witness to appear at trial virtually under Rule 43(a),
notwithstanding Rule 45(c)’s traditional geographical limits, remains unsettled as a matter of law.
Recently, the Committee on Rules of Practice and Procedure recognized that “[t]here  have been
disagreements among district courts about whether they have such power.” 37  On May 15, 2025,
the Committee proposed a revision to Rule 45 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, seeking to
clarify that federal courts do in fact have the power to compel remote testimony from witnesses
located beyond Rule 45(c)’s traditional geographical limits.38 The proposed amendment proceeded

The Court’s ruling is permitted by Rule 45, which grants the Court the authority to compel
Ms. McGowan, a non-party to this litigation, to attend a trial in “the state where [she]  resides, is
employed, or regularly transacts business in person,” as long as Ms. McGowan  “would not incur
substantial expense.” Ms. McGowan will not be compelled to attend trial at the United States
District Court, Eastern District of Louisiana, 500 Poydras Street, New Orleans, Louisiana. Instead,
because the Court will permit her virtual attendance, the Court modifies the Trial Subpoena to set
Ms. McGowan’s place of compliance within the state where she resides,  New York. Because the
Trial Subpoena’s place of compliance no longer exceeds the geographical limits of Rule 45(c), the
Court finds that Rule 45(d)(3) does not require the Court to quash the subpoena on this basis.

Finally, the Court turns to whether the Trial Subpoena  is an improper subpoena duces
tecum. Ultimately, service of a Rule 45 subpoena duces tecum that seeks to compel the production
of broad categories of documents after the close of the discovery period is improper. 44 All
discovery deadlines set for this matter have passed. Further, the Trial Subpoena seeks to compel
the production of nineteen broadly -defined categories of documents  in tandem with Ms.
McGowan’s appearance at trial . Therefore, the  Court finds it appropriate to quash the Trial
Subpoena to the extent that it seeks compel Ms. McGowan’s production of the documents listed
in the subpoena.
IV. Conclusion
The Court finds it appropriate to permit Ms. McGowan to attend and testify at trial virtually
under Rule 43(a), thus resetting the Trial Subpoena’s place of compliance within the State of New
York. Therefore, Rule 45(c)’s traditional geographical limits are not exceeded by the Trial
Subpoena. As such, the Court will not quash the Trial Subpoena on the basis of Rule 45(c).
On the other hand, the Court finds that the Trial Subpoena issued to Ms. McGowan is an
improper subpoena duces tecum. As such, pursuant to Rule 45(d)(3), the Court quashes the Trial
Subpoena to the extent that it seeks the production of documents in tandem with an appearance at
trial. Accordingly,
 IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that Ms. McGowan’s Motion to Quash Trial Subpoena 45 is
GRANTED IN PART and DENIED IN PART. The motion is GRANTED to the extent that it
seeks to quash the Trial Subpoena as an improper subpoena duces tecum . The Trial Subpoena is
modified to compel Ms. McGowan’s virtual attendance at trial. The motion is DENIED in all other
