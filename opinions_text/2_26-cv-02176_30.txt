The parties agree that any dispute, controversy, or claim between Employee and GMB,
including without limitation, any dispute arising from Employee’s employment with GMB
or otherwise, shall be resolved exclusively through final and binding arbitration. This
agreement to arbitrate shall apply to all claims and disputes between Employee and GMB
. . . .

III. LEGAL STANDARD
 The Federal Arbitration Act  (“FAA”) expresses a strong policy  in favor  of enforcing
arbitration agreements. Safer v. Nelson Fin. Grp., Inc., 422 F.3d 289, 294 (5th Cir. 2005). Section
2 of the FAA provides, in relevant part, “a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce
to settle by arbitration a controversy thereafter arising out of such contract or transaction . . . shall
be valid, irrevocable, and enforceable. . . .”
 Whether an arbitration clause is enforceable turns on a two-step analysis. “First, the court
must determine whether the parties agreed to arbitrate the dispute.” Will-Drill Res., Inc. v. Samson
Res. Co., 352 F.3d 211, 214 (5th Cir. 2003). Th e first step is subdivided into two considerations:
“(1) whether there is a valid agreement to arbitrate between the parties; and (2) whether the dispute
in question falls within the scope of that arbitration agreement.” Id. The movant carries the burden
of demonstrating a valid arbitration agreement covers the litigation. Halliburton Energy Servs. v.
Ironshore Specialty Ins., 921 F.3d 522, 531 (5th Cir. 2019). Ordinary state-law principles apply to
this analysis. Webb v. Investacorp, Inc., 89 F.3d 252, 258 (5th Cir. 1996). The second step requires
courts to consider whether any federal statute or policy renders the claims nonarbitrable. R.M.
Perez & Assocs., Inc. v. Welch, 960 F.2d 534, 538 (5th Cir. 1992). Where no such statute or policy
exists, a court  should compel arbitration. Mitsubishi Motors Corp. v. Soler Chrysler -Plymouth,
Inc., 473 U.S. 614, 628–29 (1985).

 GMB moves to compel arbitration on the basis that the 2023 Agreement is a lawful
arbitration agreement covering the present dispute.  In its motion, GMB  explains how the four
requirements to contract in Louisiana —capacity, mutual consent, certain object, and a lawful
purpose—are satisfied and attaches a copy of the 2023 Agreement bearing Dr. Hutchins’ signature.
R. Doc. 9-1 at 5–7. Dr. Hutchins acknowledges that he entered into the 2023 Agreement and that
the agreement was valid when executed . R. Doc. 12 at 2.  Dr. Hutchins maintains, however, that
the parties terminated the 2023 Agreement through novation or dissolution.
 Novation under Louisiana law occurs when parties to a contract substitute an existing
obligation for a new one. La. Civ. Code art. 1879. Novation requires the mutual consent of both
parties. Id. There is no evidence that Dr. Hutchins and GMB agreed to substitute the arbitration
provision in the 2023 Agreement with other obligations. Dissolution occurs when both parties, by
mutual consent, agree to terminate the existing obligations. La. Civ. Code art. 1983; see also
Guarantee Sys. Const. & Restoration, Inc. v. Anthony , 97-1877, p. 11 (La. App. 1 Cir. 9/25/98),
728 So. 2d 398, 403, writ denied, 98-2701 (La. 12/18/98), 734 So. 2d 636. Dr. Hutchins has neither
presented evidence nor alleged any facts to explain how the parties dissolved the 2023 Agreement.
With no evidence to suggest the 2023 Agreement was terminated, the Court finds that GMB has
met its burden of establishing the existence of a valid arbitration agreement.
 The final two considerations in the arbitration analysis are readily satisfied. Dr. Hutchin’s
claims concern his employment with GMB and the 2023 Agreement covers “any dispute arising
from [Dr. Hutchins’] employment” with the company. R. Doc. 9 -2 at 3. Dr. Hutchins does not
dispute that his claims fall within the scope of the agreement. See R. Doc. 12. Finally, there are no
statutes or policies barring arbitration in this case.

 The FAA “calls for a . . . speedy disposition of motions or petitions to enforce arbitration

clauses.” Moses H. Cone Mem'l Hosp. v. Mercury Constr. Corp. , 460 U.S. 1, 29, 103 (1983) .  It
was “Congress's clear intent, in the [FAA], to move the parties to an arbitrable dispute out of court
and into arbitration as quickly and easily as possible.” Id. To serve this end, the Fifth Circuit has
instructed that, when considering claims brought under the FAA, federal courts are to “conduct an
expeditious and summary hearing, with only restricted inquiry into factual issues bearing on the
making of the arbitration agreement.” Snap-on Tools Corp. v. Mason, 18 F.3d 1261, 1265 n. 4 (5th
Cir. 1994) (internal quotation marks omitted). Arbitration -related discovery is generally
inappropriate absent a compelling showing that such discovery is required. Kulpa v. OM Fin. Life
Ins., No. 1:07–cv–1136, 2008 WL 351689, at *1 (S.D. Miss. Feb. 6, 2008); Bell v. Koch Foods of
Miss., LLC, 358 F. App'x 498, 501 (5th Cir. 2009); Siefke v. Toyota Motor N. Am., Inc., No. 4:25-
CV-406, 2025 WL 2901531, at *2 (E.D. Tex. Oct. 10, 2025).
 Dr. Hutchins offers no account whatsoever of how he and GMB mutually terminated  the
2023 Agreement through dissolution or novation , information of which Dr. Hutchins would
necessarily have knowledge . Equally, Dr. Hutchins provides no support for his eleventh -hour
theories that GMB waived the 2023 Agreement or that the Agreement was extinguished through
remission.1 In keeping with the federal policy favoring the speedy resolution of motions to compel
arbitration, the  Court finds it inappropriate to open discovery when Dr. Hutchins’ discovery
request is based on nothing more than speculation.
