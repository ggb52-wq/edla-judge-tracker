ORDER AND REASONS
 Before the Court is a Motion for Default Judgment against Defendant Herman Rogge filed
by Plaintiff Great American Assurance Company. R. Doc. 22. The Motion is unopposed, and
Rogge is unquestionably in default. R. Doc. 14. However, a party is not entitled to a default
judgment merely because the defendant is technically in default. Ganther v. Ingle , 75 F.3d 207,
212 (5th Cir. 1996). Rather, “the entry of default judgment is committed to the discretion of the
district judge.” Mason v. Lister , 562 F.2d 343, 345 (5th Cir. 1977). It is well established that
“[d]efault judgments are a drastic remedy, not favored by the Federal Rules and resorted to by
courts only in extreme situations.” Sun Bank of Ocala v. Pelican Homestead & Sav. Ass'n, 874
F.2d 274, 276 (5th Cir. 1989) (footnote omitted).
Moreover, “[w]hen one of multiple defendants has defaulted, judgment should generally
not be entered against the defaulting defendant until the matter has been adjudicated as to all
defendants.” Lohri v. Countrywide Home Loans, Inc., No. 4:12- cv-568, 2013 WL 308893, at *1
(E.D. Tex. Jan. 25, 2013) ; see also Frow v. De La Vega, 15 Wall. 552, 82 U.S. 552, 553–54, 21
L.Ed. 60 (1872) (“[I]f the suit should be decided against the complainants on the merits, the bill
will be dismissed as to all the defendants alike—the defaulter as well as the others. If it be decided
in the complainant's favor, he will then be entitled to a final decree a gainst all. But a final decree
on the merits against the defaulting defendant alone, pending the continuance of the cause, would

be incongruous and illegal.”). This is particularly important when defendants have closely related
defenses because, in such a case, the entry of the default judgment against the defaulted defendant
might prejudice another defendant who has appeared. Lohri , 2013 WL 308893, at *1 (citation
omitted).
Here, to enter default judgment against Rogge would require the Court to make several
merits determinations that would apply equally to Plaintiff ’s claims against non- defaulted
Defendants Terry Weed and Scott Lorenzo Urbina. This would prejudice Weed and Urbina. They
have not defaulted and are entitled to defend against Plaintiff’s claim before a judgment is reached
thereto. Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that Plaintiff’s Motion for Default Judgment, R. Doc. 22, is hereby
DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE  to Plaintiff’s right to reassert its entitlement to default
judgment against Rogge once its claims against Weed and Urbina have been adjudicated.
