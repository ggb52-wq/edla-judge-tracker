that Plaintiff is entitled to a judgment of liability for a constitutional partial taking
under both the United States and Louisiana constitutions.4

II. LEGAL STANDARD
According to the Fifth Circuit, the purpose of a motion in limine is to prohibit
opposing counsel “from mentioning the existence of, alluding to, or offering evidence
on matters so highly prejudicial to the moving party that a timely motion to strike or
an instruction by the court to the jury to disregard the offending matter cannot
overcome its prejudicial influence on the jurors’ minds.” 10  Under Federal Rule of
Evidence 401, evidence is relevant if it has any tendency to make a fact more or less
probable than it would be without the evidence, or if the fact it seeks to prove is of
consequence in determining the action.11

While all relevant evidence is admissible, the Court may exclude relevant
evidence if its probative value is substantially outweighed by danger of “unfair
prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay, wasting time, or
needlessly presenting cumulative evidence.” 12  However, the Fifth Circuit has held
that, “[t]he exclusion of evidence under Rule 403 should occur only sparingly.” 13
Additionally, “ [r]elevant evidence is inherently prejudicial; but it is only unfair
prejudice, substantially ou tweighing probative value, which permits exclusion of
relevant matter under Rule 403.” 14  An order in limine excludes only clearly
inadmissible evidence; therefore evidence should not be excluded before trial unless
it is clearly inadmissible on all potential grounds.”15  Instead, “[i]f the evidence is not
clearly inadmissible on all grounds, it is better for the court to decline to rule in
advance of trial so that it will have the opportunity to resolve issues in context.16

Plaintiff argues the emails  provide evidence of why the property was not
developed for several years. 47  The Court finds that the reasons for why  a property
was not developed could potentially be relevant to the value of the property, which is
at issue in this case .  H owever, the Court finds that the probative value of this
evidence is substantially outweighed by its prejudicial effect.  The Plaintiff is not
prohibited from testifying  to electrical issue s and how such issues may have
prevented development of the property  during trial . The issue before the Court is
admissibility of emails with an entity other than the Defendant.   Since Plaintiff can
testify to the electrical issue he may have encountered with the property , the
probative value of showing the emails to the jury is very minimal.  In comparison,
there is a strong risk of jury confusion  if the email s are admitted because the jury
may conflate the town of Jean Lafitte with the Defendant .  The emails also contain
unduly prejudicial language including the following statement, “the local electrician
that I hired mentioned that in his 15 plus year career in the Lafitte area he has never
experienced what we are experiencing in this process.” 48  In addition to this being
hearsay, there is substantial risk that the jury will read this language and attribute
the town’s actions with the Defendant.  The Court finds that the minimal probative
value of the emails is substantially outweighed by a danger of unfair prejudice,
confusing the issues, and misleading the jury and thus exclusion of the emails under
Fed. R. Evid. 403 is appropriate.

vii. Any Documents Attached to the Expert Report of Baldwin Justice, Fred
Fellner, or Malcolm Guidry

Defendant claims that Plaintiff has failed to identify what documents it seeks
to have admitted from its expert reports, and, therefore, any documents attached to
the expert reports of the Plaintiff should be excluded .49  Plaintiff argues that
photographs and diagrams in the expert reports are clearly admissible and the
experts themselves will be present at the trial to lay foundation and authenticate the
work.50
The Court notes that the parties were required to provide an exhibit list to the
Court on November 6, 2026.51  Both parties are precluded from admitting documents
that were not included on that exhibit list for non-impeachment purposes.  However,
if the Plaintiff included diagrams and documents from their expert reports in the
exhibit list, the Court finds that the Defendant did have notice of the documents, both
through the documents’ inclusion in the Plaintiff’s expert reports, provided to the
Defendant by August 27, 2025, 52 and their inclusion in the exhibit list , provided to
the Court on November 6, 2026. 53  Thus the Court declines to categorically exclude
all documents from the Plaintiff’s expert reports.  In its Motion, the Defendant argues
that some of the attachments to the reports contain speculative information and are
prejudicial. The Court defers ruling on the admissibility of the expert report
attachments until trial.
