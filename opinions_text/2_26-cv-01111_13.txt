B. Section 1983 May Not Be Used for Habeas Relief
Section 1983 and the federal habeas statute are distinct.  Habeas “applies to a specific kind
of plaintiff (a prisoner) seeking a specific and exceedingly powerful injunction (release from
custody) under specific legal standards unique to habeas ” whereas § 1983 is more general and
applies to constitutional and statutory claimants seeking “an array of remedies (including a variety
of injunctions but also money damages) without regard to the common- law and statutory
restrictions on habeas.”25
“Congress has determined that habeas corpus is the appropriate remedy for state prisoners
attacking the validity of the fact or length of their confinement, and that specific determination
must override the general terms of § 1983.”26  The Fifth Circuit has made clear that “a habeas
petition ‘is the proper vehicle to seek release from custody,’ while a civil rights suit . . . is the
‘proper vehicle to attack unconstitutional conditions of confinement and prison procedures.’” 27
Thus, a prisoner cannot challenge the fact or duration of confinement in a § 1983 action.28  But “if
a favorable determination of the prisoner ’s claim would not automatically entitle him to
accelerated release, then the proper vehicle is a civil rights suit.”29

C. Heck Bar
To the extent Ross seeks relief other than dismissal of the charges and/or release (i.e.,
injunctive or monetary relief), Heck v. Humphrey, may preclude his claim.39  In Heck, the Supreme
Court held that a § 1983 claim seeking damages or release from custody is barred if success in the
suit would necessarily imply the invalidity of an outstanding criminal conviction or a plaintiff ’s
present confinement. 40  Heck’s “favorable-termination” requirement 41 thus precludes a § 1983
claim seeking relief that would render a conviction or sentence invalid unless the conviction or
sentence has been reversed, expunged, declared invalid or called into question by issuance of a
writ of habeas corpus.42
A claim for damages bearing that relationship to a conviction or sentence
that has not been so invalidated is not cognizable under § 1983. Thus, when
a state prisoner seeks damages in a § 1983 suit, the district court must
consider whether a judgment in favor of the plaintiff would necessarily
imply the invalidity of his conviction or sentence; if it would, the complaint
must be dismissed unless the plaintiff can demonstrate that the conviction
or sentence has already been invalidated.43

factual basis of the conviction is temporally and conceptually distinct from the claim being
brought.44  The court must determine “ whether success on the claim requires negation of an

D. Grounds for Dismissal
Even if Heck  applies, the Fifth Circuit requires a court to address dismissal of immune
defendants and cull those claims that cannot proceed under § 1983 without regard to any Heck
bar.51
1. Eleventh Amendment Immunity
The Eleventh Amendment provides: “The judicial power of the United States shall not be
construed to extend to any suit in law or in equity, commenced or prosecuted against one of the
United States by Citizens or Subjects of any Foreign State.” 52  This language establishes an
immunity that bars actions in federal court “ against state governments by anyone other than the
federal government or another state ”53 for “retrospective relief in the form of a money judgment
in compensation for past wrongs.”54  Eleventh Amendment immunity extends to bar “suits against
state officials or agencies that are effectively suits against a state,”55 such as official -capacity
claims for damages against state -court judges. 56  “[U]nless the state has waived sovereign
immunity or Congress has expressly abrogated it, the Eleventh Amendment bars the suit.” 57

G. Availability of Amendment
The court should freely grant leave to amend a pleading when justice so requires.101  Unless
it is clear that the plaintiff is unwilling or unable to amend in a manner that will avoid dismissal,
the court errs in not providing a pro se plaintiff with at least one opportunity to cure pleading
deficiencies before dismissing.102  When conducting a frivolousness review, the court may offer a
pro se litigant an opportunity to amend his complaint before it is dismissed if the court finds that
he has not had the opportunity to state his “best case.”103  It is not necessary, however, to afford a
plaintiff an opportunity to amend when any such amendment would be futile.104
