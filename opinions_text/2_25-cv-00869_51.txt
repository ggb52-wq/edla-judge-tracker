III. Legal Standard
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(b)(6) provides that an action may be dismissed for
“failure to state a claim upon which relief can be granted.” 88 A motion to dismiss for failure to
state a claim is “viewed with disfavor and is rarely granted.”89 “To survive a motion to dismiss, a
complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to ‘state a claim to relief that is
plausible on its face.’” 90 “Factual allegations must be enough to raise a right to relief above the
speculative level.”91 A claim is facially plausible when the plaintiff has pleaded facts that allow
the court to “draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct
alleged.”92
On a motion to dismiss, asserted claims are liberally construed in favor of the claimant,
and all facts pleaded are taken as true. 93 However, although required to accept all “well -pleaded
facts” as true, a court is not required to accept legal conclusions as true.94 “While legal conclusions

 The Louisiana Supreme Court has recognized four limited situations where the doctrine
applies to prevent the running of prescription:
1. Where there was some legal cause which prevented the courts or their officers
from taking cognizance of or acting on the plaintiff's action;
