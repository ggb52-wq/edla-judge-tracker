II. APPLICABLE LAW AND ANALYSIS
A. Timeliness of Discovery Motion
As explained above, the discovery deadline expired on September 29, 2026.  Courts
generally look to the deadline for completion of discovery in considering whether a discovery
motion has been timely filed.2  “It is axiomatic that to complete discovery means that all disputes
relative to discovery must be filed and resolved prior to the discovery deadline.” 3  “This means
that a motion to compel discovery must be resolved, not just filed, by the discovery deadline.” 4

B. Scope of Discovery
Courts have broad discretion in discovery matters.10  Rule 26 of the Federal Rules of Civil
Procedure authorizes the parties to
obtain discovery regarding any nonprivileged matter that is relevant to any party’s
claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case, considering the
importance of the issues at stake in the action, the amount in controversy, the
parties’ relative access to relevant information, the parties’ resources, the
importance of the discovery in resolving the issues, and whether the burden or
expense of the proposed discovery outweighs its likely benefit.  Information within
this scope of discovery need not be admissible in evidence to be discoverable.11

At the discovery stage, relevance includes “[a]ny matter that bears on, or that reasonably
could lead to other matter that could bear on, any issue that is or may be in the case.” 12  The
threshold for relevance at the discovery stage is lower than the threshold for relevance of
admissibility of evidence at trial.13  This broader scope is necessary given the nature of litigation,

of a privilege log identifying any document withheld on that basis is improper.18  In any event, the

settlement agreements are not privileged, even if declared confidential by the parties. 21  Indeed,

While both parties characterize this motion as involving efforts to obtain the Gasquet
release, the release at issue does not appear to fall within the definition of a Gasquet  release.  A
“Gasquet” release derives from Gasquet v. Commercial Union Ins. Co.24 and is used “to denote a
type of release in which a plaintiff settles with and releases a defendant insured and its primary
insurer, but reserves his or her right to pursue additional amounts available through the insured’ s
excess insurance policy.” 25  “[ A]fter a Gasquet  release is executed [,] the insured remains in the
lawsuit as a ‘nominal’ defendant while the plaintiff pursues recovery from the excess insurer.”26
In this case, Plaintiff released Defendant Franco and his personal auto insurer Farmers,
reserving all of Plaintiff’s rights against Defendant Franco to the extent necessary to recover from
any other insurer and his employer, Coastline.  ECF No. 186, at 1–2.  As the Honorable Sarah S.
Vance has explained:
Although the release is “adorned with language that appears superficially Gasquet-
like,” the release does not implicate the concerns at issue in Gasquet.  In Gasquet,
the plaintiff settled with the tortfeasor ’s primary liability insurer and continued to
pursue his claim against the tortfeasor ’s excess insurer.  But here, the sole
remaining defendant is RPM Pizza d/b/a Domino’s Pizza, which plaintiff alleges is
[defendant’s] employer.  Louisiana law does not require a plaintiff to reserve rights
against an alleged tortfeasor to bring a claim against the alleged tortfeasor ’s
employer.27

Thus, the release in this case does not appear to be a Gasquet settlement.
