II. APPLICABLE LAW AND ANALYSIS
A. Purported Conflicts of Interest with the Personal Representative
Claims for wrongful death under both the Death on the High Seas Act  and the Jones Act
are brought through a personal representative. 3  Th at “personal representative” is “the court -
approved executor or administrator of the decedent’s estate.” 4  She has a fiduciary duty and must
hold “any recovery in trust for the beneficiaries named in the Act.” 5  If other beneficiaries are
wronged by the personal representative, “they may later bring a cause of action against the personal
representative if necessary.”6
But there is a caveat to the personal representative’s sole control .  If there is a conflict
between different classes of beneficiaries, a beneficiary may intervene in the personal
representative’s lawsuit.7  In other words, “[a] beneficiary who is inadequately represented by the
representative (e.g., where a conflict of interest exists) is not without recourse, but must proceed
by intervention under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 24.”8
In this case, it is undisputed that Mrs. Seals is the personal representative. 9  In light of
Movants’ contention that there is a conflict of interest between them and Mrs. Seals , I must

B. Intervention
To be entitled to intervene as of right  under Rule 24, Movants must demonstrate that
(1) they timely applied for intervention; (2) they have an interest relating to the property or
transaction that is the subject of the case; (3) disposition of the case may practically impair or
impede their ability to protect that interest; and (4) they are  inadequately represented by the
existing parties. 10  “Failure to satisfy one requirement precludes intervention of right ,”11 so the
absence of any one element requires denial of intervention as of right. 12   But Rule 24 is to be
“liberally construed,” with “doubts resolved in favor of the proposed intervenor.”13
Movants bear the burden of establishing their  right to intervene. 14  “[T]he inquiry under
Rule 24 (a)(2) is a flexible one, which focuses on the particular facts and circumstances
surrounding each application,” and “intervention of right must be measured by a practical rather
than technical yardstick.”15  At the intervention stage, the court must analyze the request by
assuming the factual allegations are true.16

3. Disposition of the Case May Practically Impair or Impede Their Ability to
Protect That Interest

While it is true that Movants could bring an action for breach of fiduciary duty after this
lawsuit is resolved, as a practical matter, that remedy is distinct and not as encompassing as
intervention in this proceeding.  A district court calculates pecuniary loss “based upon a reasonable
prediction of how much financial support and personal guidance a particular beneficiary would
have received had the decedent lived.”32  To make this pecuniary loss determination, courts
consider factors including: (1) the number of dependents, (2) the deceased’s estimated income,
(3) the likelihood of a dependent attending college, (4) how much time the deceased would have
spent with the dependent, and (5) how much the deceased contributed to the dependent in the
past.33  Factors 3, 4, and 5, in particular, would benefit from Movants’ active involvement and
evidence.  Relying on Mrs. Seals alone would, as a practical  matter, impair or impede Movants’
ability to protect their interests.  While Mrs. Seals has actively collected evidence on these factors
(and has asked for Movants’ assistance in collecting such evidence which requests have allegedly
been ignored), the practical difference between filtering evidence through the personal
representative versus an independent presentation is clear.  Movants have satisfied the third factor.

In Smith v. Clark Sherwood Oil Field Contractors , the Fifth Circuit held there was a
conflict of interest between the personal representative (the deceased’s brother) who
acknowledged a child with disputed paternity and the widow and the deceased’s four other

income on his lawful family.”38  The widow and the four other children, on the other hand, would

the deceased lived.42
