REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION
Before the Court, on referral from the District Court for preparation of a
recommend disposition,1 is a motion filed by Plaintiff Madeleine Dixon  (R. Doc. 17).
Plaintiff seeks reimbursement of expenses she  incurred to serve her former
employers, Defendants Taqueria LA 20, LLC and Sean Toups (collectively,
“Defendants”) with her complaint2 for unpaid wages following Defendants’ failure to
waive service as requested. See Fed. R. Civ. P.  4(d)(2). She also asks the Court to
require Defendants to pay her reasonable attorney’s fees expended in preparing  the
instant Motion. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(d)(2)(B). Defendants have not responded to the
motion and the deadline has passed.3 Having carefully reviewed Plaintiff’s Motion in
the light of the applicable law and record facts, the undersign ed recommends that
Plaintiff’s unopposed Motion be granted and that Defendants be ordered to reimburse
Plaintiff $200 .00 for service expenses and $825.00 in reasonable attorney’s fees
pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(d)(2).

I. Law and Analysis
 Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(c)(1) requires the plaintiff raising an action
to timely serve each defendant with a summons and a copy of his complaint. Rule
4(d)(1), however,  permits plaintiffs to submit a written request that a named
defendant waive service . Defendants generally have a “ duty to avoid unnecessary
expenses of serving the summons.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(d)(1). Accordingly, provided both
parties are located within the United States, the court “must impose” on a defendant
that fails to sign and return a properly requested waiver of service the  plaintiff’s
“expenses later incurred in making service” and “reasonable expenses, including
attorney’s fees, of any motion required to collect those service expenses” in the
absence of a showing of good cause. Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(d)(2).
Plaintiff has submitted the sworn declaration of her counsel attesting that she
mailed (and, as a courtesy, also emailed) a “Request for Waiver of Service” packet to
each defendant, in compliance with the procedural requirements of Rule 4(d)(1)(a) –
(g).4 After the deadline for Defendants to sign and return the waiver had passed
without response, she hired Algiers Investigation, LLC to execute service. 5 Plaintiff
has included the invoice for that $200.00 charge as an exhibit to her Motion. 6 In the
face of this uncontested ev idence, Defendants’ silence cannot show good cause for
their failure to waive service of process. Rule 4(d)(2) thus requires the Court to order

“[R]easonable hourly rates ‘are to be calculated according to the prevailin g
market rates in the relevant community.’ ” McClain v. Lufkin Indus., Inc. , 649 F.3d
374, 381 (5th Cir. 2011) (quoting Blum v. Stenson, 465 U.S. 886, 895 (1984)). The fee
applicant bears the burden of showing “that the requested rates are in line with those
prevailing in the community for similar services by lawyers of reasonably comparable
skill.” Blum, 465 U.S. at 895 n.11. An attorney's requested hourly rate is prima facie
reasonable, however, if unopposed. Shaw v. Ciox Health, LLC , 2021 WL 928032, at
*2 (E.D. La Mar. 11, 2021)  (citing La. Power & Light Co. v. Kellstrom , 50 F.3d 319,
328 (5th Cir. 1995); Powell v. Comm'r, 891 F.2d 1167, 1173 (5th Cir. 1990)).
In support of her assertion that an hourly rate $275.00 is reasonable, Plaintiff
has submitted the affidavit of an unrelated and experienced attorney attesting based
on his years of practice in New Orleans and Louisiana that this rate  is “well within”
or even “likely lower” than the rates charged by attorneys in this region comparable
experience.9 The affiant also point s to several cases within the Eastern District of
Louisiana in which the court determined comparable or higher hourly rates to be
reasonable in awarding attorney’s fees.10 And Defendants have made no  opposition
to Plaintiff’s requested rate. Plaintiff has thus carried her burden to show that an
award of attorney’s fees at $275.00 per hour is reasonable in this case.

Additionally, in calculating the lodestar “[t]he court should exclude all time
that is excessive, duplicative, or inadequately documented.” Watkins, 7 F.3d at 457.
The fee applicant bears the burden of showing that the time expenditures for which
fees are sought is reasonable.  Hensley v. Eckerhart , 461 U.S. 424, 437  (1983). Fee
applicants are expected to exercise “billing judgment” by making “a good faith effort
to exclude from fee request hours that are excessive, redundant, and otherwise
unnecessary[;]” i.e., hours that were not reasonably expended. Id.  at 343, 347. Here,
Plaintiff’s counsel has attested that she spent three hours in connection with the
instant motion.11 The Court finds no failure to exercise billing judgment or want of
good faith in Plaintiff’s unopposed assertion that three hours were reasonably so
expended.
 Having so determined, calculation of the lodestar results in the precise fee
award Plaintiff seeks: $825.00.  Given the “strong presumption” that the lodestar
amount is reasonable, and in the absence of any opposition, the Court finds no
exceptional circumstance present here justifying modification of the lodestar under
the Johnson factors. Plaintiff’s request for $825.00 in attorney’s fees under Rule
4(d)(2) is reasonable and should be granted.
