FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATION

Plaintiff Duane Anderson, submitted for filing this pro se civil rights complaint pursuant
to 42 U.S.C. § 1983.  ECF No. 1 (Deficient Complaint).  At the time of filing,  Anderson did not
pay the filing fee or submit with the complaint a motion to proceed in forma pauperis.
On June 3, 2025, the clerk of court sent a deficiency notice addressed to plaintiff instructing
him that he was required to either pay the filing fee or provide a separate pauper application. ECF
No. 2.  The clerk of court enclosed a pauper application for  Anderson’s use. The envelope
containing the notice has not been returned as undelivered.  When Anderson failed to respond to
the deficiency notice, I issued an order on July 7, 2025, for Anderson to show cause on or before
August 2, 2025, why his § 1983 complaint should not be dismissed for failure to prosecute. ECF
No. 3. The envelope containing the order has not been returned as undelivered.  Anderson did not
respond to the order, pay the filing fee, or submit a pauper application.
Rule 41(b) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provides that a court may in its
discretion dismiss any action based on the failure of a plaintiff to prosecute or comply with any
order of the court.1  In applying the sanction of dismissal, courts have traditionally considered the
extent to which the plaintiff, rather than his counsel, is responsible for the delay or failure to

comply with the court’s order.2  Because plaintiff is proceeding pro se, it is apparent that this court
must weigh his actions alone in considering dismissal of this action under Rule 41(b).   A pro se
litigant is not exempt from compliance with relevant rules of procedural and substantive law.3  A
pro se litigant who fails to comply with procedural rules has the burden of establishing excusable
neglect, which is a strict standard requiring proof of more than mere ignorance. 4  Anderson has
failed to comply with the court’s rules and orders, which has created delay in the court’s ability to
efficiently manage the case.  His delay is caused by and attributable to Anderson himself, justifying
dismissal of his claims within the complaint for failure to prosecute.5
Anderson has not contacted the court or provided a different address.6  If he is no longer at
the facility, he is still obligated to keep the court advised of any address change.7  The complaint
form used by Anderson to institute this action contains the following declaration: “I understand
that if I am released or transferred, it is my responsibility to keep the Court informed of my
whereabouts and failure to do so may result in this action being dismissed with prejudice.”8  “[T]he
failure of a pro se litigant to notify the district court of an address change may be considered by
the district court as an additional cause for dismissal for failure to prosecute.”9
In a final effort to provide Anderson with an opportunity to show cause why h is claims
should not be dismissed, I issue these Findings and Recommendation to the presiding United States

District Judge.  Anderson is advised that he may object to these Findings and Recommendation

court’s previous orders.  Anderson is further advised that any objection should be in writing and

A party’s failure to file written objections to the proposed findings, conclusions, a nd
recommendation in a magistrate judge’s report and recommendation within fourteen (14) days
after being served with a copy shall bar that party, except upon grounds of plain error, from
attacking on appeal the unobjected-to proposed factual findings and legal conclusions accepted by
the district court, provided that the party has been served with notice that such consequences will
result from a failure to object.10
RECOMMENDATION
It is RECOMMENDED that plaintiff Duane Anderson’s 42 U.S.C. § 1983 c laims be
DISMISSED WITHOUT PREJUDICE for failure to prosecute.  FED. R. CIV. P. 41(b).
