ORDER AND REASONS
Before the Court is John Wesley Patton’s  (“Patton”) Omnibus Motion (ECF No. 8)
requesting various relief in connection with his  petition for a writ of habeas corpus. First, Patton
requests that the state court record be supplemented with transcripts, documents, and tangible
evidence relating to his underlying conviction. ECF No. 8 at 1. Second, Patton requests  that this
Court recuse the Jefferson Parish District Attorney’s Office from this matter. Third, Patton requests
that the Louisiana Department of Public Safety and Corrections cease and desist interference with
his legal mail. Id. at 1-2. Fourth, Patton requests that the Court appoint him counsel in this matter.
Id. at 2. Finally, Patton requests that this Court unseal documents originally sealed in the state trial
court relating to his underlying conviction. For the reasons that follow, Patton is not entitled to the
requested relief.
I. Request to Supplement the State Court Record
Patton’s first request is that the state court record in this matter be supplemented with
transcripts, documents, and tangible evidence in his underlying criminal conviction from the 24th
Judicial District Court for the Parish of Jefferson, Louisiana, in case number 18 -7474. Id. at 2.
Patton states he is worried about the incompleteness of the “entire” record due to portions of the
record that he noticed were missing when filing his direct appeal in the state courts. Id. at 3.
On July 15, 2026, this Court ordered the Respondent to produce a copy of the entire  state
court record in its habeas corpus response order. ECF No. 2. The Respondent currently has until

November 3, 2026, to file a response to Patton’s petition, inclusive of a copy of the state court
record, which this Court will review and use to evaluate Patton’s claims. As such, Patton’s request
for supplementation of the state court record prior to it being filed is denied. Should the
undersigned later find any pertinent or necessary part of the state court record absent to adequately

II. Request to Recuse the Jefferson Parish District Attorney’s Office
Patton’s second request is that this Court recuse the Jefferson Parish District Attorney’s
Office from this matter. Id. at 6. In support of this, Patton states that in December of 2020 he filed
a federal civil action against numerous defendants in Westwego and Jefferson Parish, Louisiana,
that “seemed to bring the wrath” of Jefferson Parish Assistant District Attorney Darren A.
Allemand. Id. at 6-7. Patton alleges that this  resulted in him being framed and then railroaded in
an eight -day criminal trial without any due process or fairness  ultimately resulting in his
underlying conviction. Patton claims this unethical conduct has continued throughout his direct
appeal and post -conviction proceedings. Id. at 8. In light of that , Patton requests that this Court
recuse the Jefferson Parish District Attorney’s Office from this matter and appoint the Louisiana
Attorney General. Id. at 10.
Under Louisiana law, the prosecution of criminal actions in the Louisiana courts is a matter
solely within the discretion of the Louisiana Attorney General and the various District Attorneys.
See La. Code Crim. P. arts. 61 and 62. Patton’s underlying criminal conviction was prosecuted by
the Jefferson Parish District Attorney’s Office, the same office that has enrolled to respond to the
instant federal habeas petition. Patton has not provided any valid reason why the Jefferson Parish
District Attorney’s Office should be recused from this case. A holding by this Court purporting to

recuse the District Attorney’s office from this case on the grounds asserted by Patton would be
tantamount to a holding authorizing recusal of every District Attorney’s office from responding to
a petition for a writ of habeas corpus that prosecuted the underlying criminal conviction of the
petitioner. Thus, the Court denies Patton’s request to recuse the Jefferson Parish District Attorney’s
Office from this matter.
III. Request to Cease and Desist Legal Mail Interference
Patton’s third request is that the Court prohibit the Louisiana Department of Public Safety and
Corrections from violating his constitutional right to access to the courts. Id. at 11. In support of
this, Patton states that he has filed several complaints with the FBI and DOJ against the mailroom
staff at Rayburn Correctional Center for tampering with his legal mail. Patton asserts that a
previously filed writ application to the United States Supreme Court was tampered with as it never
arrived at the Court. Patton also describes an instance where Rayburn staff incorrectly sent a check
intended to the United States Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals to the Lafourche Parish Clerk’s Office,
that JPAY emails have been sent to friends and family in an effort to harass or drive a wedge
between relationships, and that an individual from the Jefferson Parish Sheriff’s Office has been
calling Patton’s friends and family and “spreading discontent” for years. Id. at 12-13.
As of this date, the Court is not aware of any issues in receiving or forwarding Patton’s legal
mail as it pertains to this matter. This includes receipt of Patton’s 226-page federal habeas petition
and the present 174-page omnibus motion. The alleged events of legal mail tampering that Patton
describes occurred outside of these proceedings and involve entities and individuals who are not
parties in this matter. Further, the relief requested by Patton is not available through a federal
habeas action and must be pursued on other grounds. Therefore, Patton’s requested relief as it
pertains to the Louisiana Department of Safety and Corrections is denied.

IV. Request to Appoint Counsel
Patton’s fourth request is that the Court appoint counsel to assist him in this matter. Patton
states his case is very complex, contains forty-three grounds for relief, and that his trial was eight
days long and involved numerous documents and testimony suppressed by the State. Id . at 14.
Patton requests that the Court appoint Graham L. Bosworth to represent him as he was on the
Jefferson Parish Indigent Defender Board.
There is no constitutional right to counsel in habeas corpus actions. Pennsylvania v. Finley,
481 U.S. 551, 555 (1987); Wardlaw v. Cain, 541 F.3d 275, 279 (5th Cir. 2008). Nevertheless, a
habeas petitioner should be appointed counsel when “the interests of justice so require.” Wardlaw,
541 F.3d at 279; Schwander v. Blackburn, 750 F.2d 494, 502 (5th Cir. 1985); see also  18 U.S.C.
§ 3006A(a)(2)(B). Appointed counsel is not required unless there is a need for an evidentiary
hearing. Urias v. Thaler, 455 F. App’x 522, 523 (5th Cir. 2011); United States v. Vasquez, 7 F.3d
81 (5th Cir. 1993)  (if it is determined that an evidentiary hearing is needed in a habeas corpus
action, the appointment of counsel is mandatory). Furthermore, “[i]f the matter can be resolved on
the basis of the record and the pleadings submitted by the parties, the interests of justice do not
require the appointment of counsel.” Jackson v. Warden, W. Monroe City Jail, No. 06-1425, 2006
WL 4041524, at *2 (W.D. La. Oct. 23, 2006)  (citing Boyd v. Groose, 4 F.3d 669 (8th Cir. 1993),
and Smith v. Groose, 998 F.2d 1439, 1442 (8th Cir. 1993)).
Upon initial review of Patton’s pleadings, it appears entirely unlikely that an evidentiary
hearing will be needed to resolve his claims. Contrary to his understanding, the claims Patton
asserted are of a type routinely presented pro se, and he appears capable of presenting his claims
and arguments as evidenced by the filing of his 266-page federal habeas petition.  The information
on which he bases his petition is based on and determinable through review of the state court

records and decisions  and it does not appear that  specialized legal knowledge nor additional
investigative resources would be necessary.
The interests of justice do not warrant appointment of counsel in this case. Should
circumstances change as the case develops or the Court later determines that an evidentiary hearing
is necessary, appointment of counsel may be reconsidered. At this time, however, appointment of
counsel is unwarranted and Patton’s request is denied.
V. Request to Unseal State Trial Court Documents
Patton’s final request is that this Court unseal state trial court records as they pertain to Patton’s
underlying criminal conviction and will be relevant to his federal habeas petition. Id. at 16. Patton
specifically requests that a motion in limine, a letter from the jury, and a question asked by the
jury be unsealed. Id. at 16-17.
As an initial matter, th is Court’s review of the state trial court record is not limited to only
publicly available records. To the extent that Patton seeks an order from this Court to compel the
state trial court to unseal the documents, this Court is without authority to  do so. The law is clear
that federal courts possess no general mandamus powers to direct state courts and their judicial
officers in the performance of their duties where mandamus is the only relief sought. Pursuant to
28 U.S.C. § 1361, “The district courts shall have original jurisdiction of any action in the nature
of mandamus to compel an officer or employee of the United States or any agency thereof to
perform a duty owed to the plaintiff.” By its terms, 28 U.S.C. § 1361 does not apply to state actors,
but only to officers and employees of the United States. As such, this statutory provision also does
not give the Court jurisdiction to issue the requested relief that Patton seeks.  See Paige v.
Bacarisse, 80 F. App’x 299, 300 (5th Cir. 2003) (citing Santee v. Quinlan, 115 F.3d 355, 357 (5th
Cir. 1997) and Moye v. Clerk, DeKalb County Superior Court , 474 2d 1275 (5th Cir. 1973));

Russell v. Knight, 488 F.2d 96, 97 (5th Cir. 1973); Lamar v. 118th Jud. Dist. Ct. of Tex., 440 F.2d

contained in the state court record of his underlying conviction is denied.
 Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that the requested relief outlined in petitioner John Wesley Patton’s
Omnibus Motion (ECF No. 8) is DENIED as set forth above.
