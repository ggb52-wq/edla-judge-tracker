II. Standards of Review for Summary Judgment
The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provid e that “[t]he court shall grant summary
judgment if the movant shows that  there is no genuine dispute as  to any material fact and the
movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” F ED. R. CIV. P. 56(a). The initial burden falls
on the movant, who must show that there is an “absence of a genuine issue as to any material fact.”
Adickes v. S. H. Kress & Co. , 398 U.S. 144, 157 (1970). “The mova nt satisfies this burden by
showing that a reasonable jury could not find for the nonmovant, based on the burdens that would
apply at trial.” Joseph ex rel. Estate of Joseph v. Bartlett, 981 F.3d 319, 329 (5th Cir. 2020).  When
the movant meets this initial burden, “the burden shifts to the nonmoving party to produce evidence

or designate specific facts showi ng the existence of a genuine issu e for trial.  Doubts are to be
resolved in favor of the nonmoving party, and any reasonable inferences are to be drawn in favor

quotation marks omitted).
Therefore, where, as here, a motion for summ ary judgment is filed by a plaintiff, the

III. Discussion
In his motion, Porter relies exclusively on the allegations in his complaint, as reiterated in
his unverified and incorporated st atements of fact, to demonstr ate his entitlement to summary
judgment on the issue of defendants’ liability for his violations of his constitutional rights.  Porter
has not submitted verified statem ents, sworn affidavits, or other competent evidence to establish
the facts alleged in his case.  Regardless, the co urt need not reach the merits of Porter’s motion
because it is premature.
District courts are permitted to dismiss a motion for summary judgment without prejudice
as premature if it is filed before any party is se rved and/or when no party has filed an answer.
Kuperman v. ICF Int’l, No. 08-565, 2008 WL 647557 at *1 (E.D . La. Mar. 5, 2008) (citing 10A
CHARLES ALAN WRIGHT & ARTHUR R. MILLER, FEDERAL PRACTICE AND PROCEDURE, § 2717, at
298-99 (1998)); Watkins v. Monroe, No. 18CV347, 2019 WL 1869864, at *1 (E.D. Tex. Mar. 27,
2019) (“Federal courts are permitted to dismiss a motion for summary judgment without prejudice
if it is filed before any party answers.”), R&R adopted by 2019 WL 1858100, at *1 (E.D. Tex. Apr.
25, 2019); Johnson v. Guiterrez , No. 21-CV-46, 2021 WL 6693938, at  *1 (E.D. Tex. Dec. 16,

2021) (“[t]the record does not de monstrate that any of the Defe ndants have been served with

In this case, Porter filed his motion for su mmary judgment before any defendant filed an
answer or responsive pleading. Thus , the case is still in  its preliminary stages.. The Fifth Circuit
has explained that “[s]ummary judgment assumes some discovery” has been conducted.  Brown v.
Miss. Valley St. Univ., 311 F.3d 328, 333 (5th Cir. 2002); see also FDIC v. Schrader & York, 991
F.2d 216, 220 (5th Cir. 1993) (“Summa ry judgment is appropriate if, after discovery, there is no
genuine dispute over any material  fact.”) (citations omitted); Ala. Farm Bureau Mut. Cas. Co.,
Inc. v. Am. Fid. Life Ins. Co. , 606 F.2d 602, 609 (5th Cir. 1979) (concluding that summary
judgment motions should not “ordinarily be granted before discovery has been completed”).  For
this reason, “courts have denied as premature summary judgment motions filed before the parties
have had adequate opportunity  to conduct discovery.”  Rogers v. McLane , No. 22CV130, 2022
WL 17418978, at *2 (N.D. Tex. Nov. 14, 2022) (collecting cases), R&R adopted by  2022 WL
17418016, at *1 (N.D. Tex. Dec. 5, 2022).
Based on the limited record befo re the court, and defendants ’ pending motion to dismiss,
defendants will likely interpose defenses and create issues of fact challenging those asserted in
Porter’s motion. Therefore, this case simply is not in a proper pos ture to consider plaintiff’s
motion.  For these reasons, the co urt finds that Porter’s motion for summary judgment must be
dismissed as premature.
