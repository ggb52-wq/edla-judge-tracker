II. Legal Standard
A. Relevance
Unless otherwise limited by court order, Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1) defines the
scope of discovery: “Parties may obtain discovery regarding any nonprivileged matter
that is relevant to any party ’s claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the
case.” A court assesses proportionality by considering (1) the importance of the issues
at stake in the litigation; (2) the amount in controversy; (3) the parties’ relative access
to relevant information; (4) the parties’ resources; (5) the importance of the proposed
discovery in resolving the issues; and ( 6) whether the burden or expense of the

1485-86 (5th Cir. 1990).
Under Rule 26(c)(1), courts may restrict discovery “for good cause...to protect a
party or person from annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden or
expense.” The burden rests on the party objecting to the discovery request to
demonstrate good cause exists to restrict discovery. See Cazorla v. Koch Foods of
Miss., L.L.C., 838 F.3d 540, 549 (5th Cir. 2016). Discovery rules are “ accorded broad
and liberal treatment to effect their purpose of adequately informing litigants in civil
trials.” Herbert v. Lando, 441 U.S. 153, 177 (1979) (citations omitted). Discovery rules,
though, “are subject to the injunction of Rule 1 that they ‘ be construed to secure the

B.  False Claims Act
“The False Claims Act prohibits, in relevant part, 1) the presentment of a false
claim to the Government, 2) the use of a false record or statement to get a false claim
paid, and 3) conspiracies to get a false claim paid.”  United States ex rel. Grubbs v.
Kanneganti, 565 F.3d 180, 183 (5th Cir. 2009) . “To aid the rooting out of fraud, the
Act provides for civil suits brought by both the Attorney General and by private
persons, termed relators, who serve as a ‘ posse of ad hoc deputies to uncover and
prosecute frauds against the government. ’” Id. at 184 (quoting United States ex rel.
Milam v. Univ. of Tex. M.D. Anderson Cancer Ctr., 961 F.2d 46, 49 (4th Cir. 1992)).
Complaints under the False Claims Act must meet the heightened pleading
standard of Rule 9(b). See id. at 185 (citation omitted). “[T]o plead with particularity
the circumstances constituting fraud for a False Claims Act § 3729(a)(1) claim, a
relator’s complaint” may allege the details of an actually  submitted false claim, but
it also “may nevertheless survive by alleging particular details of a scheme to submit
false claims paired with reliable indicia that lead to a strong inference that claims
were actually submitted. ” See id. at 190. Rule 9(b) ’s heightened pleading standard
thus acts as an initial “gatekeeper to discovery.” Id. at 185. But Rule 9(b) should not
“shoulder all the burden of policing abusive discovery.” Id.  at 191. Courts must

to the “case at hand.” See id.

B.    Proportionality
Defendants claim that Relator ’s discovery requests are disproportional since
they extend beyond Relator ’s explicit allegations. 13 As established, Relator has
consistently alleged that Defendants are committing an ongoing fraudulent scheme.
When an FCA  action relator sufficiently pleads an ongoing scheme, the relator is
entitled to discovery of information on all alleged instances of fraud in the scheme.
United States ex rel. Branch Consultants, L.L.C., 2010 WL 11627442, at *12.
Both the importance of the issues and the amount in controversy weigh heavily
in Relator’s favor. Relator ’s claims are that Defendants have fraudulently acquired
millions of dollars for skin substitutes. 14 See United States v. Umbrella Fin. Servs.,
LLC, No. 3:22-CV-2759-D, 2023 WL 4109697, at *2 (N.D. Tex. June 21, 2023) ( “As a
matter of public policy, allegations that a defendant has caused millions of dollars in
tax harm is of high public importance.”).

IV. Conclusion
Relator’s amended complaint alleges with particularity and specificity an
ongoing scheme, including facts suggesting that the scheme would remain in place
even after the filing of the complaint. Her recent declaration provides some
corroboration for this claim and undercuts the assertion that this is a “fishing
expedition.”
