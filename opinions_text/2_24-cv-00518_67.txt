A court has inherent power to recognize, encourage, and when necessary, enforce
settlement agreements reached by the parties when the court specifically retains jurisdiction to

when state law supplies the rule of decision in a diversity case, this case is a federal question case,
not a state law diversity case.  See ECF No. 1.  Where the substantive rights and liabilities of the
parties derive from federal law, “[q]uestions regarding the enforceability or validity of [settlement]
agreements are determined by federal law.”8  Federal law likewise applies to determine the validity

C. The Requirements to Enforce a Settlement Under Federal Law
Federal law governing settlement agreements applies the general common law principles
of contracts. 11  To form a settlement agreement, there must be an offer, an acceptance,
consideration, the inclusion of all material or essential terms, and a meeting of the minds among
the parties.12  An offer and acceptance “is judged by the parties’ overt acts and words, not by the
subjective or secret intent of the [parties].” 13  For there to have been a meeting of the minds, the
parties must have “knowingly and voluntarily” entered into the agreement.14
Unlike Louisiana law, federal law even allows oral settlement agreements.15  Thus, as long
as there is an offer, acceptance, consideration, the inclusion of all material or essential terms, and
a meeting of the minds among the parties, the federal requirements are met and the agreement need
not be reduced to writing.16  A settlement “‘is valid and enforceable even if it contemplates the

III. ANALYSIS
After a one-hour and thirty- seven minute settlement conference during which numerous
offers were made, the parties reached an agreed settlement and placed the material terms of that
settlement on the court record.  ECF No. 49.  At that time, the parties confirmed on the court record
that they understood they were entering into a binding settlement, they understood the settlement
terms recited into the record, and they were voluntarily entering into the settlement.
At the forty-seven minute hearing on this motion, the Court played the audio recording of
the settlement for the parties and discussed those terms with them.   ECF No. 62. The recording
reflects that the parties addressed several of the disputed issues during the settlement conference.
The parties recognized that certain disputed items were mooted after listening to the recording.
Some of the items were not discussed  at the settlement conference, rendering those terms
immaterial to the settlement reached.  The recording made clear that the scope of release included
any and all claims that were brought or could have been brought in this litigation in connection
with this loan.
 The Court will address Plaintiffs’ numbered items (ECF No. 65)  and Chase’s numbered
items (ECF No. 66) with reference to the draft Agreement attached as ECF No. 65-1:

 Plaintiffs object to the opening paragraph including Chase’s successors and
assigns, and Chase asserts same is needed due to mergers, acquisitions and
corporate transfers.

Recommendation:  Th e “successor and assigns” language in the opening paragraph is
unnecessary and should be deleted.  Chase’s successors and assigns are not parties to this
agreement, though they do receive the full benefit of the release in favor of Chase , as set
forth in ¶ 5 of this draft.

Plaintiffs also object to the Recitals in A and B indicating Plaintiffs
“executed and delivered” a note and “gave” a mortgage, which Plaintiffs dispute.

2008, Vladimir D. Thomas executed and delivered a  promissory note in the amount of
$141,200.00 (the “Note”)  and, as security , a mortgage (the “Mortgage”) on that certain

Regardless of  any dispute regarding validity, the note and mortgage are reflected in

2, 35-5.  This settlement resolves the loan at issue in this lawsuit, and there is no need to identify
same by account or loan number.
2. Recital C
Plaintiffs seek to require Chase to confirm its authority to settle and bind Fannie Mae.

Recommendation:  No changes to Recital C or  ¶ 5 are necessary.

Plaintiffs named Fannie Mae as a party to this litigation.  ECF No. 1.  Although the Court
granted Fannie Mae’s motion to dismiss before the settlement conference (and thus it did not
participate) (see ECF No. 31), the resolution of the matter resolved all claims that were brought or
could have been brought in this case.  Plaintiffs ’ claims against Fannie Mae are thus within the
scope of the agreed settlement and release, and Fannie Mae is properly included as a released party.
As to Chase’s authority, Chase explicitly agrees to cancel the mortgage in ¶ 3( b) and deliver
appropriate documentation reflecting satisfaction of the note in ¶ 3(d) .  Once the note is marked
satisfied and the mortgage is cancelled, there can be no further interest in the note by any party.

Recommendation:  Paragraph 1 already includes language reflecting that neither party

reflects Plaintiff disputes the debt.  The last sentence of ¶ 1 was not discussed during the

Paragraph 3 generally reflects the precise sequence of events as agreed.  However, the last
sentence of ¶ 3(a) should be revised to read:  “Within 3 days of delivery of the Chase
Payment, Customer shall tender to Chase the amount of $x , which reflects the pay -off of
the debt disputed by Customer.”  When reciting the terms of settlement into the court
record, Chase stated that Plaintiff would pay “to Chase the sum of [$x], which is the payoff

to make clear that Plaintiff disputes the debt.  No further modification is appropriate as the
“payoff of the loan” language was recited into the court record and is thus appropriately
included in this document memorializing same.

Paragraph 3(c) should be revised to read:  “Within thirty (30) days after Chase’s providing
documentation reflecting satisfaction of the note and cancellation of the mortgage, the
Parties will file a Joint Motion to Dismiss With Prejudice all claims and counterclaims filed
in this matter.”

4. Final Account Status

Plaintiffs seek to add a requirement that Chase be obliged to note in its
records that the Disputed Loan is closed with a zero balance and no remaining
servicing, collection, enforcement, or foreclosure rights, and correct its credit
furnishing.

Recommendation:  Chase regularly closes loans when paid, and how it denotes same in
its records is a n issue that was not discussed during the settlement conference.  It is not
proper to attempt to negotiate new obligations after having reached a binding settlement as
recited in the court record.

5. Note Disposition and Proof of Cancellation

Plaintiffs seek to require Chase to mark  the original Note as cancelled and satisfied or
provide a sworn affidavit, and to provide certified  recorded proof that the Mortgage
inscription was cancelled, and to correct any rejected, deficient, or ineffective filing.

Recommendation:  The requested revision is unnecessary and duplicative.  Chase is
already obliged to provide notice of satisfaction and cancellation in ¶ 3(b) and (d) .  Its
failure to do so would be a breach subject to summary enforcement in this Court.

6. Waiver and Scope of Release

Plaintiffs object to the waiver of appeal rights and requests reciprocal releases.

Recommendation:  A release, including any right to appeal, is consistent with a full and
complete release of all claims.  No change to ¶ 4 is necessary.

Paragraphs 5 and 6 appropriately include any potentially related entity or party.  The scope
of the releases should be combined into one paragraph and be mutual.  The full and
complete release was not limited to “by, through or under” the released parties.

7. Confidentiality and Tax Reporting

Plaintiffs ask for mutual confidentiality and disclosure of tax reporting.

Recommendation:  The parties explicitly agreed to a one-sided confidentiality obligation
owed by Plaintiffs in ¶ 17.  It is not appropriate to attempt to negotiate new terms in
documenting the agreed settlement.  Paragraph 16 reflects that no tax advice was provided,
and Chase will report this transaction as required by applicable law.  No change to ¶ 16 is
necessary or appropriate.

8. Representations, Assignments, Indemnities

Plaintiffs object to the representations and warranties, Chase’s right to assign its rights and
demand additional instruments as necessary, but not Plaintiffs.

Recommendation:  The warranty provisions in ¶ 7 and ¶ 20 and the survival provision of
¶ 12 are appropriate and necessary.  No changes should be made to these paragraphs.
Paragraph 11 is unnecessary, was not discussed, and should be deleted.  Paragraph 22
should be mutual so that each party is required to execute any documents necessary to
consummate the agreed settlement.

IV. CONCLUSION
  Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS RECOMMENDED that Plaintiffs’ Motion to Enforce Settlement (ECF No. 51 ) be
GRANTED IN PART AND DENIED IN PART as stated herein.
