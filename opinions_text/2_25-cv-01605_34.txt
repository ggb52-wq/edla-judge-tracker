II. LEGAL STANDARD
The F ederal Arbitration Act expresses a strong federal policy in favor of enforcing
arbitration agreements. 9 U.S.C. §§ 1 –16; see also Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. v. Byrd , 470 U.S.
213, 217–18 (1985); Hughes v. Uber Tech., Inc., 718 F. Supp. 3d 571, 578 (E.D. La. 2024). Under
the FAA, a written agreement to arbitrate “shall be valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon
such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract.” 9 U.S.C. § 2. This
provision reflects “both a liberal federal policy favoring arbitration, and the fundamental principle
that arbitration is a matter of contract.” AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion , 563 U.S. 333, 339
(2011) (internal quotations omitted). Section 3 of the FAA provides that if any suit is brought in
federal court “upon any issue referable to arbitration,” the court, “upon being satisfied that the
issue involved in such suit or proceeding is referable to arbitration . . . , shall on application of one
of the parties stay the trial of the action until such arbitration has been had in accordance with the
terms of the agreement.” 9 U.S.C. § 3. The Supreme Court has directed courts to “‘rigorously
enforce’ arbitration agreements according to their terms.” American Express Co. v. Italian Colors
Restaurant, 570 U.S. 228, 233 (2013) (quoting Dean Witter , 470 U.S. at 221). “[A]ny doubts
concerning the scope of an arbitration agreement should be resolved in favor of arbitration.” Safer
v. Nelson Financial Group, Inc., 422 F.3d 289, 294 (5th Cir. 2005).
When deciding whether to enforce an arbitration agreement, the court conducts a two-step
inquiry. Id. at 293. The first step is to determine whether the parties agreed to arbitrate the dispute
in question. Id. If so, the second step is determining “ ‘whether legal constraints external to the
parties’ agreement foreclosed the arbitration of those claims.’” Id. at 294 (quoting Webb v.
Investacorp, 89 F.3d 252, 258 (5th Cir. 1996)). Ordinarily both steps are questions for the court.
Kubala v. Supreme Production Services, Inc., 830 F.3d 199, 201 (5th Cir. 2016).

III. DISCUSSION
A. The Contractual Arrangements

The November 2021 warehouse receipt effected the delivery of the disputed crabmeat to
NO Cold Storage pursuant to the terms set forth in Louisiana Newpack’s  2020 term sheet. Gulf
Marine concedes that Louisiana Newpack is its “predecessor in interest .”30 Gulf Marine further
acknowledges Louisiana Newpack entered a deposit contract with NO  Cold Storage through the
November 2021 warehouse receipt .31 The issue presented is whether the arbitration clause is
enforceable against Gulf Marine, who didn’t own the crabmeat when the warehouse receipt was
issued and never signed a written agreement containing the arbitration clause.
In Louisiana, a rbitration agreements apply to nonsignatories, but “only in rare
circumstances.” Traders’ Mart, Inc. v. AOS, Inc., 52,592, p. 8 (La. App. 2 Cir. 4/10/19), 268 So. 3d
420, 427. One such circumstance is when a nonsignatory “embraces” a contract.  ERG Enterprises,
LLC v. Green Coast Enter., LLC, 2019-1104, p. 7 (La. App. 4 Cir. 5/13/20), 299 So. 3d 1194, 1199
(quoting Traders’ Mart, 52,592, p. 10, 268 So. 3d at 428).  A nonsignatory can “embrace” a
contract: “(1) by knowingly seeking and obtaining direct benefits from that contract; or ( 2) by
seeking to enforce the terms of that contract or asserting claims that must be determined by
reference to that contract.” Id., 2019 -1104, p. 7, 299 So. 3d at 1199 -200 (internal quotations
omitted).
In ERG Enterprises, the court enforced an operating agreement against nonsignatories. The
nonsignatories sued to enforce parts of the agreement, but claimed they did not consent to the
arbitration clause in the agreement. Id. at pp. 2-3, 299 So. 3d at 1197. Having benefitted from the

Although arbitration agreements must be in writing, the writ ing need not be signed if the
parties’ conduct confirms the agreement.  Marino v. Dillard’s, Inc. , 413 F.3d 530, 532 (5th Cir.
2005); see also In re Succession of Taravella , 98-834 (La. App. 5 Cir. 4/27/99), 734 So. 2d 149,
151; Hurley v. Fox, 520 So. 2d 467, 469 (La. App. 4 Cir. 1988). A party can accept an offer through
performance, “[u]nless the law prescribes a certain formality for the intended contract.” La. Civ.
Code art. 1927.
The 2020 term sheet expressly provided that delivery of the crabmeat to the NO Cold
Storage facility would be acceptance of its terms.34 Louisiana Newpack delivered 1,390 cases of
crabmeat to the Jourdan Road warehouse .35 Thus, Louisiana Newpack accepted the 2020 term
sheet and the arbitration clause included in its general terms. By delivering the crabmeat to NO
Cold Storage’s warehouse pursuant to the 2020 term sheet, Louisiana Newpack agreed to arbitrate.

By purchasing the crabmeat, leaving it in NO Cold Storage’s warehouse, and paying storage fees,

includes the agreement to arbitrate.
Gulf Marine argues that without a signature, there is no evidence any party consented to
the 2020 term sheet. 36 Louisiana Civil Code article 1947 presumes parties do not intend to be
bound until they sign a contract in a form they “contemplated.” 37 The 2020 term sheet did not
contemplate a signature to consent. In fact, it  expressly recognized delivery as a form of
acceptance.38 The Article 1947 presumption does not apply.
Lastly, Gulf Marine argues “all of [its] claims arise from alleged violations of the Louisiana
Civil Code ,”39 not Lineage’s 2020 term sheet.  But the complaint and Gulf Marine ’s conduct
demonstrate its reliance on the 2021 warehouse receipt and 2020 term sheet. Compare Jones v.
Singing River Health Services Foundation, 674 Fed. App’x 382, 387 (5th Cir. 2017) (“[Plaintiff’s]
claims are not directly dependent on the [contracts at issue]”. ). Without those documents and the
agreements contained in them , the crabmeat Gulf Marine purchased would not have been stored
at the Jourdan Road warehouse. Gulf Marine cannot accept the benefits of that storage and deny
the corresponding obligations.  The court finds Gulf Marine  embraced the 2020 term sheet. The
claims are referable to arbitration.
 B. The Court Must Stay the Case.
A court that compels arbitration “shall on application of one of the parties stay the trial of
the action until such arbitration has been had.” 9 U.S.C. § 3. Gulf Marine requested a stay. That
request forecloses dismissal. Smith v. Spizzirri, 601 U.S. 472, 478 (2024).  A stay is ordered.
