III. LAW AND ANALYSIS
The Mandamus Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1361, provides that “[t]he district courts shall
have original jurisdiction of any action i n the nature of mandamus to compel an
officer or employee of the United States or any agency thereof to perform a duty owed
to the plaintiff.” 28 The Fifth Circuit has recognized that “[t]he test for jurisdiction
[pursuant to the Mandamus Act] is whether mandamus would be an appropriate
means of relief.”29 Three elements are required before mandamus can issue: “(1) the

plaintiff must have a clear right to the relief; (2) the defendant must have a clear duty
to act; and (3) no other adequate remedy must be available.” 30 The petitioner must
carry the burden of showing that the “right to issuance of the writ is clear and
indisputable.”31 The Fifth Circuit has determined that federal district courts “lack[]
jurisdiction under § 1361 to compel state officials to perform their official duties.” 32
The All Writs Act authorizes federal courts to issues writs “necessary or appropriate
in aid of their respective jurisdictions and agreeable to the usages and principles of
law,” including the writ of mandamus requested here. 33  It functions to “fill the
interstices of federal judicial power when those gaps threate[n] to thwart the
otherwise proper exercise of federal courts’ jurisdiction.” 34  However, w ithout a
separate basis, like the Mandamus Act, for example, the All Writs Act “does not confer
an independent basis for subject matter jurisdiction.”35

To the extent Petitioner requests clarification of Judge Morgan’s June 13,
2017, findings regarding lawful street policing, jurisdiction, and authority in Orleans
Parish—specifically that “the AG’s office has limited statutory authority to
investigate crim es within certain areas, but it doesn’t have the ability to make
statewide arrests for violations of state law,”36 Petitioner lacks standing to seek this
clarification.37 There is nothing in the record to suggest that Petitioner was a party
to that case. It is well-settled that one who is not a party to a matter has no right to
appeal a judgment entered in that matter. 38 And, finally, as to this “request for
clarification,” this Court has no authority over Judge Morgan’s 2017 case. It would be
inappropriate for this Court to issue any clarification of another Court’s findings in a
separate case made almost a decade earlier. Lacking jurisdiction in both regards, the
Court need not, and declines to, address the merits of the underlying Motion for
Emergency Stay.39

Petitioner then seeks Writs of Mandamus against numerous state and city

Court has jurisdiction over an action seeking “to compel an officer or employee of the

However, this Court lacks jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1361 to compel state officials
to perform their official duties. 42 Therefore, this Court does not have jurisdiction to
issue a writ of mandamus pertaining to any of the actions described in (iii)(a) -(j) nor

IV.  CONCLUSION
IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that the Motion for Emergency Stay/For Writ of
Mandamus44 is DISMISSED for lack of jurisdiction.
