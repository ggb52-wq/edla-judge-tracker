ORDER AND REASONS
 Before the Court is Defendants Rexel USA, Inc. and Samuel Tesh’s (collectively,
“Defendants”) Motion to Extend Expert Disclosure Deadline .1 The instant motion was filed on
July 24, 2026, with the submission date set for August 19, 2026. 2 Defendants move the Court to
extend the expert disclosure deadline from July 24, 2026 to September 24, 2026 “due to ongoing
and outstanding unresolved discovery matters.”3 Specifically, Defendants aver that a central issue
in this matter is “whether Plaintiffs, Kori Williams and Khalilah Williams, were occupants of the
vehicle at the time of the accident.” 4  Defendants assert that they have “retained an expert
telecommunications engineer that specializes in cellular network technology to confirm the
locations of all three plaintiffs on the date of the accident.”5 Further, Defendants contend that “due
to unforeseen circumstances, including Plaintiffs’ failure to timely provide the properly-executed
authorizations to obtain the cellular telephone records, Defendants have been unable to obtain the

Defendants contend that thereafter AT&T objected to Defendants legal demand “because the
Name and Phone Number referenced do not match [their] records.”14 Thus, Defendants assert they
are “unable to meet the relevant scheduling order deadline[,]” and request that the Court continue
the current trial and pre-trial deadlines, and reset trial for the next available date.15
 Federal district courts have the inherent power to enforce their scheduling orders, 16 and
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 16(b) provides that a scheduling order “may be modified only for
good cause and with the judge’s consent.”17 Whether to grant or deny a continuance is within the
sound discretion of the trial court. 18 In deciding whether to grant a continuance, the Court’s
“judgment range is exceedingly wide,” for it “must consider not only the facts of the particular
case but also all of the demands on counsel’s time and the court’s.”19
Here, the Court finds that Defendants have established good cause for a continuance of the
expert disclosure deadline. However, the Court finds that Defendants have not established good
cause for a continuance of trial or the other pretrial deadlines  at this time . Defendants have
explained in sufficient detail why they require Plaintiffs’ relevant phone records from AT&T, that

new expert disclosure deadline requested by Defendants is over  one month away on September
24, 2026. Defendants fail to explain why they would be unable to obtain the relevant cellphone

IT IS HEREBY ORDERED  that Defendants’ Motion to Extend Expert Disclosure
Deadline20 is GRANTED IN PART AND DENIED IN PART . The motion is granted to the
extent it seeks a continuance of Defendants’ expert disclosure deadline. The motion is denied in
all other respects.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED  that Defendants’ expert disclosure deadline is continued
