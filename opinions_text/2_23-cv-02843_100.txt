A. Recusal
The Supreme Court has established an objective standard that requires recusal when “the
likelihood of bias on the part of the judge ‘is too high to be constitutionally tolerable.’” 13  “A
motion to recuse must be strictly construed for form, timeliness, and sufficiency in order to guard
against the danger of frivolous attacks on the orderly process of justice.”14  Motions to recuse
judges from district court proceedings may be sought under two statutes, 28 U.S.C. § 144
and  § 455.15

Different procedures govern recusals under § 144 and § 455.  Under § 144,16 a party must
file a timely and sufficient affidavit regarding the alleged personal bias or prejudice either against
him or in favor of any adverse party, which must “state the facts and the reasons for the belief that
bias or prejudice exists,” and the party must attach “a certificate of counsel of record stating that
[the motion] is made in good faith.”17  Section 455,18 on the other hand, does not require such an
affidavit.
1. Recusal Under § 144
Section 144 aims exclusively at actual bias or prejudice. 19  It requires a judge to recuse if
a party to the proceeding “makes and files a timely and sufficient affidavit that the judge before
whom the matter is pending has a personal bias or prejudice either against him or in favor of any
adverse party.” § 144.
The filing of a motion to recuse under § 144 does not automatically trigger the appointment
of another judge.20  A “judge must pass on the legal sufficiency of the affidavit”21 —that is, whether

B. E nforceability of Settlement
“Compromises of disputed claims are favored by the courts.” 41  For that reason, the Fifth
Circuit has repeatedly emphasized that “a settlement agreement once entered into cannot be
repudiated by either party and will be summarily enforced.” 42  When a party authorizes a
settlement and then changes her mind when presented with the settlement documents, that party
remains bound by the terms of the agreement.43
1. The Court Retains the Authority to Enforce the Settlement Agreement
A court has inherent power to recognize, encourage, and when necessary, enforce
settlement agreements reached by the parties when the court specifically retains jurisdiction to
enforce the settlement in the order of dismissal.44  The Court’s dismissal order in this case
specifically retained jurisdiction to enforce the parties’ settlement agreement.   ECF No. 50.
Further, interpreting an identically phrased Order of Dismissal on a motion to re-open and enforce
settlement, Judge Vance held that the court had jurisdiction to enforce the settlement agreement:
[T]he Court's order of dismissal expressly retained jurisdiction over enforcement
of the settlement agreement.   The Court's ancillary enforcement jurisdiction
extends beyond the sixty days within which a party could move to reopen the action.
Therefore, given plaintiff's failure thus far to consummate settlement, the Court has
jurisdiction to enforce the parties’ agreement.45

Federal law governing settlement agreements applies the general common law principles

consideration, the inclusion of all material or essential terms, and a meeting of the minds among

settlement agreement is generally present where the parties have agreed upon the monetary amount

negotiations cannot be considered material terms. 57  Disagreement about non- material terms not

not established personal bias or prejudice stemming from an extrajudicial source and resulting in
an opinion on the merits on some basis other than what the judge learned from participating in the
case.  In short, Plaintiff has not provided any facts or evidence to establish that a reasonable and
objective person, knowing all of the facts, would harbor doubts concerning the judge's impartiality

The parties entered into a binding settlement agreement when they read the material terms
of that settlement into the court record on February 11, 2025.  Although the parties remained free
to negotiation and agree on additional provisions, only the material terms recited into the court
record form the enforceable settlement agreement.  Here, t he parties agreed on the  payment of a
specified amount in exchange for mutual releases with a nominal amount allocat ed to the
confidentiality provision.  While the parties could agree on any other terms, their failure to do so
does not impact the enforceability of the agreed settlement that they recited into the court record
of this case.  Further, as the dispute regarding current allocation would not bind the IRS and was
not the subject of negotiation between the parties at the settlement conference, it cannot now be
characterized as “material” to the settlement.
Given that former counsel Minias is a party to a contingency- fee contract and served as
counsel for P laintiff in this case until last month, at this pleading stage, he has sufficiently
demonstrated an interest (i.e., contingency -fee interest) in this underlying action, that final
disposition may impair or impede his ability to protect that interest, and his interest is not
adequately represented by the existing parties.  Whether he can prevail on his intervention claim
is a merits issue for later resolution , but it is not properly considered on this preliminary motion
for leave to intervene.  The only issue on the motion for leave to intervene is whether he satisfies
the requirements of Rule 24, which he does.
