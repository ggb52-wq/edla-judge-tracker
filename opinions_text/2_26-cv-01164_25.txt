Plaintiff, Century Surety Company (“Century”), filed a Motion for Leave to
Amend its Complaint for Declaratory Judgment (R. Doc. 21 ). Defendants have not
filed an opposition and the time to do so has expired . H aving considered the
submissions of counsel , the record, and the applicable law, the Court grants the
Motion for Leave to Amend its Complaint for Declaratory Judgment.
On June 1, 2026, Century filed a lawsuit seeking a declaratory judgment as to
its rights and obligations under an insurance policy, number CCP 1291501, issued to
defendant JJMC1 LLC d/b/a/ Meineke Car Care Ce nter #2857 (“JJMC1”).1
Specifically, Century seeks a determination that it has no duty to defend or indemnify
defendants John Womack  or JJMC1 from claims asserted by defendants David
Burley and Susannah Burley (the “Burleys”) in an underlying state court action.2 On
August 3, 2026, the Burleys filed a motion to dismiss , requesting the Court find it
lacked subject matter jurisdiction under the Declaratory Judgment Act and

Under Rule 15(a)(2), if more than 21 days have passed after service of a
responsive pleading, then the party may amend its pleading only with the opposing
party’s written consent or the court’s leave. The court “should freely give leave when
justice so requires.”5 The language of Rule 15(a)(2) “evinces a bias in favor of granting
leave to amend.”  Chitimacha Tribe of Louisiana v. Harry L.L. Co.,  690 F.2d 1157,
1163 (5th Cir. 1982);  see also Mayeaux v. Louisiana Health Serv. & Indem. Co. , 376
F.3d 420, 425 (5th Cir. 2004). The Fifth Circuit has instructed that the “district court
must possess a ‘substantial reason’ to deny a request for leave to amend.”  Smith v.
EMC Corp., 393 F.3d 590, 595 (5th Cir. 2004). Although leave to amend is to be freely
given under Rule 15(a)(2) , “that generous standard is tempered by the necessary
power of a district court to manage a case.” Yumilicious Franchise, LLC v. Barrie, 819
F.3d 170, 177 (5th Cir. 2016).  Relevant factors to consider include “undue delay, bad
faith or dilatory motive on the part of the movant, repeated failures to cure
deficiencies by amendments previously allowed, undue prejudice to the opposing

Each of the Rule 15 factors weighs in favor of amendment. The parties are in
the early stages of litigation. A scheduling order has not yet been entered. And while
the proposed pleading adds both a new claim for restitution and new parties,
defendants will have ample time to exchange and review discovery such that there is
no undue prejudice. The new claim Century seeks to add address es issued raised by
the Burleys’ August 3, 2026, motion to dismiss, and Century filed its motion for leave
to file its opposition out of time on September 25, 2026.6 Century filed this Motion on
October 5, 2026.7 The undersigned finds no undue delay and futility is not at issue.

IT IS ORDERED that  the Motion (R. Doc. 21) is GRANTED. Century’s
