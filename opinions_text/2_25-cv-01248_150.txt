Before the Court is a  Motion to Review  the Magistrate Judge’s Order on Defendants’
Motion to Strike the Deposition Testimony of Michelle Johnson filed by Plaintiff Kathy Sherrod
(“Sherrod”). R. Doc. 122. Defendants oppose the Motion. R. Doc. 143.

Docs. 15, 89. Relevant here, on July 28, 2026, Sherrod unilaterally noticed Defendants of her
intention to depose  Johnson, a nurse and former employee of Defendant Marrero Healthcare

enter. R. Doc. 72-2 at 1. On July 29, 2026, the parties conferred via telephone conference and
discusse

Thereafter, on August 24, 2026, D efendants motioned to strike the deposition of Johnson
on the grounds that (1) Defendants did not have proper notice of Johnson’s deposition, and (2) Mr.
Garcia was disrespectful and threatening in the course of Johnson’s deposition. R. Doc. 90.
Magistrate Judge Dossier granted Defendants’ Motion and ordered the parties to retake
Johnson’s deposition. R. Docs. 106 , 145. Judge Dossier additionally awarded Defendants
reasonable fees and expenses incurred in connection with their Motion to Strike. R. Doc. 145 at
16. Judge Dossier rested h er decision on two principal findings . First, given the ongoing
communications about rescheduling Johnson’s deposition, Defendants reasonably concluded the
August 11 deposition would not go forward. Id. at 8. Sherrod’s counsel acted unprofessionally by
failing to confirm the deposition would proceed on August 11 and by failing to contact defense
counsel when they did not appear on that date. Id. at 8–9. Second, Mr. Garcia’s conduct during the
deposition was hostile, coercive,  unprofessional and undertaken in bad faith, id. at 13–15, and
retaking the deposition was an appropriate way to rectify the situation. Id. at 16.

the Motion to Strike pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 72(a)  and Local Rule 72.2. R.

 A magistrate judge may issue orders regarding nondispositive pretrial matters. Fed. R. Civ.
P. 72(a). Under Fed. R. Civ. P.  72(a), a magistrate judge’s ruling on a non -dispositive matter is
subject to reversal only where the decision is “clearly erroneous or contrary to law.” Fed. R. Civ.
P. 72(a); 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1)(A). A ruling is “clearly erroneous” only when “the reviewing court
on the entire evidence is left with the definite and firm conviction that a mistake has been
committed.” Anderson v. Bessemer City, N .C., 470 U.S. 564, 565 (1985) . A magistrate judge’s
discovery order “should be afforded substantial deference and be overturned only if found to be
an abuse of discretion.” Palmer v. Readers Digest Assoc., No. 84 Civ. 8397, 1995 WL 686737, *1
(S.D.N.Y. Nov. 20, 1995). As such, the party seeking reversal “bears a heavy burden.” Seacor
Marine, L.L.C. v. Cummins Mid-S., Inc., No. 09-383, 2010 WL 2292152, at *2 (E.D. La. June 3,
2010).
 Having reviewed the record , the Co urt finds no clear error in Judge Dossier’s decision.
Fed. R. Civ. P . 30(d)(2) vests Judge Dossier the authority to fashion  an “appropriate sanction—

including the reasonable  expenses and attorney’s fees incurred by any party—on a person who
impedes, delays, or frustrates the fair examination of the deponent.” “[T]he full scope of sanctions
available under Rule 30(d)(2) is not expressly described in the text of the rule.” Howell v. Avante
Servs., LLC, No. CIV.A. 12-293, 2013 WL 824715, at *5 (E.D. La. Mar. 6, 2013).
Judge Dossier’s finding that Sherrod failed to provide adequate notice of the August 11
deposition is supported  by the record:  although Sherrod issued a notice, the subsequent
communications between the parties left the date unsettled. Sherrod could have easi ly contacted
defense counsel when they failed to appear but elected not to do so,  and proceeded unilaterally.
Mr. Garcia’s unprofessional and improper behavior toward Johnson is amply supported by the
deposition transcript. Judge Dossier’s decision to have the parties retake the deposition represented
an appropriate response to the problems  presented. And, given the misconduct exhibited by
Sherrod’s counsel during the deposition, the decision to award attorney’s fees was not made in
clear error.
Accordingly;
IT IS ORDERED that Plaintiff’s Motion for Review of the Magistrate’s Order, R. Doc.
122, is hereby DENIED. The parties are ordered to retake Michelle Johnson's deposition
in accordance with Magistrate Judge Dossier's Order (R. Doc. 106).
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that Plaintiff's Motion to Expedite Consideration, R. Doc.
126, is hereby DENIED as moot.
