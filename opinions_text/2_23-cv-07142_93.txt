II. LEGAL STANDARD
Summary judgment is appropriate when “the movant shows that there is no
genuine dispute of material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter
of law.” FED. R. CIV. P. 56(a); see also Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322–23
(1986). “In reviewing the record, ‘the court must draw all reasonable inferences in
favor of the nonmoving party, and it may not make credibility determinations or
weigh the evidence.’” Vote.Org v. Callanen, 89 F.4th 459, 469 (5th Cir. 2023) (quoting
Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Prods., Inc. , 530 U.S. 133, 150 (2000)). The Court
“‘resolve[s] factual controversies in favor of the nonmoving party, but only where

time of assignment. Id. at 39. Scottsdale argues that, because the assignment contract between BMA
and JES was executed just three days after Hurricane Ida, no bad faith claims existed because the
requisite time delays had not yet run. Id. Additionally, the assignment did not specifically include any
claims for bad faith damages. Id.  at 40–42. Therefore, Scottsdale reasons that JES’s bad faith claims
must be dismissed. Id.  at 43. Finally, Scottsdale argues that JES cannot recover in its own right
because it is not a named insured under the policy.  Id. at 43–44. In Louisiana, claims under La. R.S.
§§ 22:1892 and 22:1973 are expressly reserved to first party claimants.  Id.  at 44. Therefore, because
JES is not a named insured under the Policy, it does not have a valid cause of action for claims outside
the contract as a third-party claimant. Id. Ultimately, the Court need not address these alternative
theories because, as discussed infra, the Court finds the summary -judgment record devoid of facts
showing Scottsdale acted in a manner that was arbitrary, capricious, and without probable cause.

there is an actual controversy, that is, when both parties have submitted evidence of
contradictory facts.’” Little v. Liquid Air Corp., 37 F.3d 1069, 1075 (5th Cir. 1994).
To succeed on a motion for summary judgment, the movant  need not produce
evidence negating the existence of material fact. Celotex, 477 U.S. at 323. Instead, the
moving party is only required to point out the absence of evidence supporting the
other party’s case. Id.; Fontenot v. Upjohn Co.,  780 F.2d 1190, 1195 (5th Cir. 1986).
Once satisfied, the nonmoving party must come forward with specific facts showing
that there is a genuine dispute of material fact for trial.  FED. R. CIV. P. 56(e); see
Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp.,  475 U.S. 574, 587 (1986) . The
nonmoving party may not rest upon the pleadings but must identify specific facts that
establish a genuine issue “such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the
nonmoving party.” Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc. , 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986).
However, the Court will not, “in the absence of any proof, assume that the nonmoving
party could or would prove the necessary facts.” Defrancesch v. Emps. Mut. Cas. Co.,
No. 06 -5920, 2008 WL 1930450, at *1 (E.D. La. Apr. 30, 2008)  (Engelhardt, J.)
(quoting Little, 37 F.3d at 1075). “If the nonmovant fails to meet its burden of showing
a genuine dispute for trial that could support a judgment in favor of the nonmovant,
summary judgment must be granted.” Talluri v. AIG Prop. Cas. Co., 732 F. Supp. 3d
518, 526 (E.D. La. 2024).

requiring Scottsdale to pay $681,520.31, along with bad faith penalties , because

receiving satisfactory proof of loss. Id. § 22:1892(A)(1) (2023). An insurer that refuses

This is not a case where an insurer was “sitting on its hands” and outright
denied a claim or refused to tender payment under the policy. See Gentilly, 2024 WL
5246606, at *5. To date, Plaintiffs have received $3,037,698.69 under the policy, and
Scottsdale has agreed to release all claims in exchange for the remaining policy limits.
Given these undisputed facts, it is difficult to see how Scottsdale acted in bad faith
by opening the claim, dispatching inspectors, and nearly exhausting the  full policy
limits. See Reed, 857 So. 2d at 1022 (distinguishing bad faith claims where an insurer
refuses to make any tender from cases where an insurer paid thirty percent of the
policy limits). It is the Plaintiffs’ burden to present “at least one  sworn averment” of
fact in support of their claim. See Lujan, 497 U.S. 871 at 888–89. Plaintiffs have failed
to satisfy this burden . Accordingly, the Court need not address Scottsdale’s
alternative arguments in support of its motion.
Moreover, Plaintiffs are not entitled to relief under  Rule 56(d). Under Rule
56(d), “[i]f a nonmovant shows . . . that, for specified reasons, it cannot present facts
essential to justify its opposition, the court may: (1) defer considering the motion or
deny it; (2) allow time to obtain affidavits or declarations or to take discovery; or (3)
issue any other appropri ate order.” F ED. R. CIV. P. 56(d). To obtain relief
under Rule 56(d), the party opposing the summary judgment, “may not simply rely
on vague assertions that additional discovery will produce needed, but unspecified,
facts.” SEC v. Spence & Green Chem. Co., 612 F.2d 896, 901 (5th Cir. 1980). Instead,
the party must “set forth a plausible basis for believing that specified facts,
susceptible of collection within a reasonable time frame, probably exist and indicate

21

how the emergent facts, if adduced, will influence the outcome of the pending
summary judgment motion.” Raby v. Livingston , 600 F.3d 552, 561 (5th Cir. 2010)
(quoting C.B. Trucking, Inc. v. Waste Mgmt. Inc., 137 F.3d 41, 44 (1st Cir. 1998)).
Plaintiffs argue that they cannot present facts to fully oppose the motion
because they had only thirteen days from the production of discovery until the date
their opposition was due. 90 However, Plaintiffs offer no reason to believe that the
discovery disclosures will yield facts supporting their bad faith claims.  Instead,
Plaintiffs ask generally for additional time to “examine” the evidence in the hopes
that it will produce evidence  sufficient to withstand a motion for summary
judgment.91 But without a plausible basis for believing that these specific facts exist,
Plaintiffs do not present a sufficient claim for relief under Rule 56(d).
Because Plaintiffs have failed to present specific evidence supporting their bad
faith claim, Scottsdale’s motion for partial summary judgment is GRANTED.
