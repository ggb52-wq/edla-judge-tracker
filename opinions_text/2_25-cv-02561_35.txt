I. Factual Summary
Plaintiff Deninah Goodwin (“ Goodwin”) filed the subject action alleging that she was
employed by the Department of Homeland Security where she worked a Ticket to Work program
from December 1990 until December 2025.  R. Doc. 1.  Plaintiff alleges that she performed the
duties satisfactorily.  She also  complains that on July 25, 2024, she became the victim of a
conspiracy which led to her termination.  Id.
Goodwin complains that sh e was retaliated ag ainst after requesting reasonable
accommodations regarding her status as a pastor.  Id.  It is unclear from the complaint whether
Goodwin was requesting time off on Sunday or what request was denied.  Id.  However, the conflict
escalated resulting in her termination.  Id.  She also contends that her termination resulted in her
loss of home. Id.   Goodwin seeks compensatory and puniti ve damages, lost pay, reinstatement,
and attorney’s fees and expenses.

A. Executed Summons
On June 17, 2026, a Show Cause Order was issued regarding Defendants Diaz, Filfault,
Chambers and Calcano. R. Doc. 25. This Show Cause Order directed Goodwin to state why she
was not prosecuting the case against these Defenda nts. Instead, Goodwin failed to comply but

This Court has the authority to dismiss a case for failure to prosecute. See FED. R. CIV. P.
41(b). Rule 41(b) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provides that a case may be involuntarily
dismissed if a Plaintiff “fails to prosecute or to comply with these rules or a court order.” See id.
Although Rule 41(b) expressly addresses a situation in which a de fendant moves to dismiss for
failure to prosecute, it has long been recognized that  a district court has the inherent authority to
dismiss for failure to prosecute sua sponte. See LeSane v. Hall's Sec. Analyst, Inc., 239 F.3d 206,
209 (2d Cir. 2001) (citing Link v. Wabash R.R. Co., 370 U.S. 626, 630 (1962)). The court's power
to dismiss for want of pros ecution should be used sparingl y, although it may be exercised sua

C. New Defendants Proposed
Goodwin also now seeks to allege claims against Markwayne Mullin, the Head of the

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15(a) govern s the amendment of pleadings and provides
that leave to amend pleadings “shall be free ly given when justice so requires.”  F ED. R. CIV. P.
15(a)(2).  Rule 15 and other federal rules “reject the approach that pleading is a game of skill in
which one misstep by counsel may be decisive to the outcome a nd accept the principle that the
purpose of pleading is to facilitate  a proper decision on the merits.” Conley v. Gibson , 355 U.S.
41, 48 (1957).
Thus, Rule 15(a) evinces a liberal amendment policy and a motion to amend should not be
denied absent a substantial reason to do so.  See Jacobsen v. Osborne, 133 F.3d 315, 318 (5th Cir.
1998).  Leave to amend is by no means automatic.  Addington v. Farmer’s Elevator Mut. Ins. Co.,
650 F.2d 663, 666 (5th Cir. 1981).  However, the deci sion to grant or deny a motion for leave to
amend lies within the sound discretion of the trial court.  Id.

A complaint is frivolous “if it lacks an arguable basis in law or fact.” Reeves v. Collins, 27
F.3d 174, 176 (5th Cir. 1994). In de ciding as to whether a claim is  frivolous, the Court has “not
only the authority to dismiss a claim based on an indisputably meritless legal theory, but also the
unusual power to pierce the veil of the complaint's fa ctual allegations and dismiss those claims
whose factual contentions are clearly baseless.” Neitzke v. Williams , 490 U.S. 319, 327 (1989);
Macias v. Raul A. (Unknown), Badge No. 153, 23 F.3d 94, 97 (5th Cir. 1994).
A complaint fails to state a claim on which relief may be granted when the plaintiff does
not “plead enough facts to state a cl aim to relief that is plausibl e on its face. Factual allegations
must be enough to raise a right to relief above the speculative level, on the assumption that all the
allegations in the complaint are true (even if doubtful in fact).”  In re Katrina Canal Breaches
Litig., 495 F.3d 191, 205 (5th Cir. 20 07) (citation, footnote, a nd quotation marks omitted), cert.
denied, 552 U.S. 1182 (2008).
In this case, Goodwin just s eeks to add the names of the go vernmental officials, but she
fails to set forth any facts establishing either their role in her dismissal or that they even knew her.
Therefore, Goodwin’s request to correct the Defendants by addi ng Markwayne Mullin, the Head
of the Department of Homeland Security, Davi d Courcelle, the U.S. Attorney for the Eastern
District of Louisiana, and the A ttorney General of the United States  should be denied for failure
to state a claim for which relief may be granted.
