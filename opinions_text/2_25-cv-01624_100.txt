LEGAL STANDARD
 Federal courts are courts of limited subject matter jurisdiction, and they possess
only the power authorized by the Constitution and by statute.36 Under 28 U.S.C. § 1441, a
defendant may remove an action from state court to federal court if the plaintiff could
have originally brought the action in federal court. 37 Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1332, a
federal court may exercise subject matter jurisdiction “where the matter in controversy
exceeds the sum or value of $75,000, exclusive of interests and costs,” and where there is
complete diversity of citizenship.38 The parties are completely diverse where no plaintiff
is a “citizen of the same State as any defendant.”39 Section 1441(b)(2) permits a defendant
to “remove a case from state court to federal court on the basis of diversity jurisdiction so
long as none ‘of the parties in interest properly joined and served as defendants is a citizen
of the State in which such action is brought.’”40 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c) allows “30 days after
the filing of the notice of removal” for a party to file a motion to remand a case “on the
basis of any defect other than lack of subject matter jurisdiction[.]”
 Jones Act suits are generally non -removable because the Jones Act incorporates
the general provisions of the Federal Employer Liability Act (“FELA”), including 28 U.S.C.

LAW AND ANALYSIS
I. Plaintiff’s Motion to Remand is untimely under 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c).

 An untimely motion to remand must be denied as procedurally defective. 28 U.S.C.
1447(c) provides, in part:
A motion to remand [a removed] case on the basis of any defect other than
lack of subject matter jurisdiction must be made within 30 days after the
filing of the notice of removal under section 1446(a).

In Elder v. Walmart Stores, Inc., a section of this court held that a motion to remand filed
31 days after defendant filed a notice of removal should be denied as a matter of law.43 In
that case, the plaintiffs served their motion to remand on the 31st day after the notice of
removal was filed, asserting that defendants notice of removal itself was untimely. 44 The
Court denied the motion to remand, stating, “the issue of a timely or improvident removal
has been waived and the Court need only look further to the issue of [determining whether
it had subject matter jurisdiction].” 45  The court held that “[P]laintiff’s failure to initiate
service of the motion to remand within the thirty -day period described by §1447(c) has
waived their objections to the alleged untimely and improvident removal of this case.”46

II. Plaintiff has waived his right to remand by participating in the
conduct of the action.

 Even if the Court were to consider the motion despite its procedural defect,
Plaintiff has taken affirmative action constituting waiver of his right to remand.
 In Johnson v. Odeco Oil and Gas Company , the Fifth Circuit found that a Jones
Act plaintiff’s participation in discovery and other pretrial litigation matters in federal
court following removal constitutes waiver of an objection to removal based on 28 U.S.C.
§ 1445(a).52 In that case, the plaintiff originally brought his Jones Act claim in state court,
which the defendant subsequently removed to federal court. 53 Discovery progressed in
the federal forum for nearly a year, at which time the defendant filed a motion for
summary judgment, and the plaintiff  responded with a motion to remand pursuant to §
