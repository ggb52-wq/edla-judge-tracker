II. LEGAL STANDARD
The Federal Rules of Civil Procedur e impose disclosure requirements
on proponents of expert testimony.  Fed. R. Civ. P. 26.  Experts retained by a
party to testify must provide an expert report pursuant to Rule 26(a)(2)(B).
For non-retained experts, the propounding party must provide a less fulsome
disclosure.  Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(C).
In its Scheduling Orders, the Court stated that it “will not permit any
witness, expert or fact, to testify or an y exhibits to be used unless there has
been compliance with this Order as it pertains to the witness and/or exhibits,
without an order to do so issued on motion for good cause shown.”14
Rule 16(b) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provides that “[a]
scheduling order may be modified only for good cause and with the judge’s
consent.”  Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(b)(4).  Th ere are four factors to consider when
determining whether good cause exists: “(1) the explanation for the failure to
timely comply with the scheduling order; (2) the importance of the
modification; (3) potential prejudice in allowing the modification; and (4)
the availability of a continuance to cure such prejudice.”  Squyres v. Heico
Cos., 782 F.3d 224, 237 (5th Cir.  2015) (citation modified); see also

III. DISCUSSION
A. First Factor: Explanation for the Failure
Plaintiff offers several explanations for his failure to meet his June 12,
2026 expert deadline.  First, he states that he was not aware until May 2026
that his treating physician, Dr. Mort eza Farr, had recommended him for a
surgery in March 2026. 15  From plaintiff’s memora ndum, it is unclear if
plaintiff himself did not know of this  recommendation or whether it was
merely plaintiff’s counsel who did not know of this recommendation.
Regardless, when plaintiff’s counsel was made aware of the recommendation
on May 20, 2026, plaintiff still ha d over three weeks to move for a
continuance of the expert deadline.  Plaintiff failed to do so.  This explanation
is unsatisfactory.
Second, plaintiff claims that he di d not separately move to extend his
expert deadline because he believed th at a continuance of trial would reset
this deadline.16  But plaintiff never moved to continue trial, and the motion
