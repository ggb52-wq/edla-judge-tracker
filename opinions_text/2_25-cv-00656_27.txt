II. LEGAL STANDARD
Rule 8(a)(2) requires “a short and plai n statement of the claim showing that
the pleader is entitled to relief.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 8(a)(2). A co mplaint that does not
meet Rule 8(a)(2)’s pleading standard should be dismissed for failing to state a claim
upon which relief can be granted. Fed. R. Ci v. P. 12(b)(6). “[T]he pleading standard
Rule 8 announces does not require ‘detailed factual allegations,’ but it demands more
than an unadorned, the-defendant-unlawfully-harmed-me accusation.” Ashcroft v.
Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009) (quoting Bell. Atl. Corp. v. Twombly , 550 U.S. 544,

555 (2007)). “A pleading that offers ‘labels and conclusions’ or ‘a formulaic recitations
of the elements of a cause of action will not do.’” Id. (quoting Twombly, 550 U.S. at

factual enhancement.’” Id. (quoting Twombly, 550 U.S. at 557).
Ultimately, “[t]o survive a motion to dismiss” under Rule 12(b)(6), “a complaint
must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to ‘state a claim to relief that
is plausible on its face.’” Id. (quoting Twombly, 550 U.S. at 570). “A claim has facial
plausibility when the plaintiff pleads factua l content that allows the court to draw
the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.” Id.
(citing Twombly, 550 U.S. at 556). Although courts “accept all well-pled facts as true,
construing all reasonable inferences in the complaint in the light most favorable to
the plaintiff, conclusory allegations, unwarranted factual inferences, or legal
conclusions are not accepted as true.” Allen v. Hays, 65 F.4th 736, 743 (5th Cir. 2023)

plaintiff’s reliance on each scheme. Assessing AXIS’s motion requires a close review

A. § 22:1892.2, as triggered by a violation of § 22:1892(I)(2)(b),  does
not apply because AXIS paid th e settlement amount within 60
days of receiving sufficient notice of payment due.

In 2024, Louisiana revised its bad-faith penalties statutes. After Hurricane Ida
in 2021, the Louisiana legislature amended th e laws applicable to insurance claims.
See La. Act 3 of 2024 (SB 323) (effective July 1, 2024). Specifically, it added La. R.S.
§ 22:1892.2, revised La. R.S. § 22:1892, and repealed La. R.S. § 22:1973, adding the
enumerated duties of § 22:1973 to § 22:1892(I). Id. See R & R Holdings Unlimited
LLC v. Scottsdale Ins. Co , No. 3:25-CV-00992, 2026 WL 2632346, at n.18 (W.D. La.
Aug. 3, 2026). The parties agree that the post-July 1, 2024 version of the statutes
applies.
Our analytical starting point is the newly-enacted § 22:1892(I)(1)(a), which
describes duties an insurer owes to an insured: a “duty of good faith and fair dealing,”
a duty to “adjust claims fairly and promptly,” and an obligation “to make a reasonable
effort” to settle claims with the insured. § 22:1892(I)(1)(a). Certain acts constitute a
breach of these duties. § 22:1892(I)(2). One such act is when an insurer “fail[s] to pay
a settlement within thirty days after an agreement is redu ced to writing.”
§ 22:1892(I)(2)(b). Here, AXIS does not dispute that it breached its duty by failing to
pay its legal settlement to Louisiana Realty within 30 days of their agreement’s
reduction to writing.

Section 22:1892.2 establishes penalties for violating § 22:1892(I). The very first
clause of § 22:1892.2, subsection (A)(1), reads, “An insurer shall not violate any
provision of [§ 22:1892(I)].” Again, Louisiana Realty alleges AXIS, an insurer, violated
§ 22:1892(I) by delaying payment of the settlement. The penalty for a violation of
Subsection A (that is, a violation of § 22:1892(I)) is found in Subsection B:
Failure to comply with Subsection A of this Section, when the failure is
found to be arbitrary, capricious, or without probable cause, shall
subject the insurer to a penalty payable to the insured, in addition to the
amount of loss, of only the greater of fifty percent of the amount found
to be due from the insurer to the insured . . . or two thousand five
hundred dollars, whichever is greater, together with, in either instance,
reasonable attorney fees and costs actually incurred.
§ 22:1892.2(B)(1).24
But this penalty is qualified. As a “condition precedent” to bringing an action
under § 22:1892.2, the insured must give th e insurer 60 days’ written notice of a
violation, known as a “cure period notice.” § 22:1892.2(C)(1). The cure period notice
requires the insured to provide a “formal written demand providing sufficient notice
of the facts and circumstances of the dispute.” § 22:1892.2(C)(2). If the insurer pays
the full amount due within 60 days after receiving a cure period notice, including any
expenses incurred and attorney’s fees not to exceed 20% of the amount due, “there
shall be no further cause of action pursua nt to [§ 22:1892.2] regarding that noticed
demand.” § 22:1892.2(C)(3).

B. § 22:1892(B)(1) does not apply to late-payment of settlement
agreements, nor would it apply in this case because the payment
involved a “catastrophic loss” arising under an insurance policy
for immovable property.

Louisiana Realty also seeks relief under the penalty pr ovision of
§ 22:1892(B)(1)(a). But this subsection does not apply for two reasons. First, this
subsection establishes penalties when an insurer fails to timely adjust or settle
claims, not when an insurer fails to timely pay out the settlement of a lawsuit. Second,
even if this subsection were construed to cover delayed payment of legal settlements,
in this case the delayed payment arose out of a claim of catastrophic loss to immovable
property, the penalties for which mani fest under § 22:1892.2 and not
§ 22:1892(B)(1)(a).

Under these subsections, the Louisiana Le gislature mandates the timely payment
and adjustment of property insurance claims. The question is whether these
subsections also mandate the timely payment of litigation settlements arising out of
insurance claims.
AXIS argues it does not.29  AXIS claims that recovery under § 22:1892(B)(1)(a)
is foreclosed in the case of an insurer’s untimely payment of a litigation settlement.
Indeed, in Katie Realty, Ltd. v. Louisiana Citizens Prop. Ins. Corp., a 2012 Louisiana
Supreme Court case cited by AXIS, the cour t held that penalties for alleged late
settlement payments are not available under § 22:1892(B)(1)(A). 2012-0588 (La.
10/16/12), 100 So. 3d 324. In that case, the court found that § 22:1892(A)(1) obligates
an insurer to timely pay the amount of a claim after receiving satisfactory “proof of
loss,” and that § 22:1892(B)(1) prescribes penalties for br each of that obligation. Id.
at 328. But according to the court a settlement agreement is not a “proof of loss,” nor
is it an insurance “claim” arising under a contract of insurance. Id. at 330–31. Rather,
a settlement agreement is a separate co mpromise resolving a dispute over an

IV. CONCLUSION
In summary, because Louisiana Realty alleges AXIS did not pay out its written
settlement within 30 days; the only penalty statute applicable to this type of claim is
§ 22:1892.2; that statute contains a conditio n precedent, which requires an insured
give an insurer a 60-day cure period after notice of the violation; and AXIS paid the
settlement minutes after Louisiana Realty reached out to inquire about the status of
