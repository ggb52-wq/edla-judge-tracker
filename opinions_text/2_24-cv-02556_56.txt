ORDER AND REASONS

 Before the Court are  cross Motions for Summary Judgment 1 filed by Plaintiff Robert J.
Sigillito (“Plaintiff”) and Defendant Augustar Life Assurance Corporation (“Augustar”). Plaintiff
alleges that Augustar breached a disability insurance policy by failing to pay Plaintiff Total
Disability benefits after an illness caused Plaintiff to cease his employment as an emergency room
physician.2 Augustar contends that Plaintiff’s continued work as a Chief Deputy Coroner
foreclosed a finding of T otal Disability but allowed for Residual Disability payment under the
policy.3 For the reasons stated herein, the Court finds that there are genuine issues of material fact
in dispute. Considering the motions, the memoranda in support and in opposition, the record, and
the applicable law, the Court denies both motions.

I. Background4
 On September 13, 2024, Plaintiff filed a complaint in the 22nd  Judicial District Court for
the Parish of St. Tammany. 5 Plaintiff was a board -certified emergency room physician. 6 On
October 21, 1996, Plaintiff obtained a disability benefits insurance policy from Augustar, bearing
policy number H6182337.7 The policy provides for a $3,500 (increased to $8,000 and adjusted for
inflation) monthly disability income benefit in the event that Plaintiff becomes totally disabled.8
 In the late 2000s, Plaintiff began to suffer from several degenerative conditions, and he
was eventually diagnosed with Hashimoto’s Disease, among other things, which slowly became a
disabling condition a ffecting Plaintiff’s eyesight and resulting in double vision. 9 The condition
caused Plaintiff to limit his practice as an emergency room physician while he continued to seek
effective treatment.10 On August 16, 2021, Plaintiff took a job as an Assistant Parish Coroner, and
thereafter transitioned into the role of Chief Deputy Coroner .11 Plaintiff stopped working as an
emergency room physician as of July 25, 2022.12

IV. Analysis
 Plaintiff seeks summary judgment, arguing that the policy provides Total Disability
benefits because his inability to continue practicing emergency medicine rendered him totally
disabled from his “Regular Occupation” as a board-certified emergency physician. Plaintiff further
contends that Augustar improperly redefined his occupation by treating his position as Chief
Deputy Coroner as his “present occupation,” contrary to the policy language, Louisiana law, and
the policy’s board-certified specialty provision.
 Augustar also seeks summary judgment, arguing that Plaintiff cannot establish entitlement
to Total Disability benefits because the policy defines “Regular Occupation” as the occupation or
occupations in which the insured was regularly engaged when he became totally disabled.
According to Augustar, Plaintiff continued working full time as Chief Deputy Coroner after
leaving emergency medicine and therefore continued performing substantial and material duties
of his regular occupation. Augustar maintains that Plaintiff was entitled only to Residual Disability
benefits while working as Chief Deputy Coroner, and those benefits ceased once Plaintiff stopped
working for reasons unrelated to his medical condition.
 The policy defines “Total Disability or Totally Disabled” as follows:
You are Totally Disabled or have a Total Disability if due to Injury or Sickness:
(a) you are not able to do the duties of any occupation which you may reasonably
be expected to engage in because of education, training or experience; and

care on a regular basis if you can show that further recovery is not expected for

 Viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to the nonmovant on each motion, the
Court concludes that several genuine disputes of material fact remain.  First, the record contains
conflicting evidence regarding the date on which Plaintiff became “Totally Disabled.” In his claim,

drawing factual inferences regarding Plaintiff’s occupational duties and his capacity to perform
the substantial duties of his occupation at the relevant time. These issues cannot be resolved at the
summary judgment stage.

 Second, genuine factual disputes remain concerning Plaintiff’s “Regular Occupation”

language and Louisiana law. Louisiana Revised Statute § 22:990(C) provides, “ A general

or becomes, qualified by reason of education, training, or experience and which provides him with
substantially the same earning capacity as his former earning capacity prior to the start of the
disability.”190 A determination of whether Augustar violated Louisiana Revised Statute
§ 22:990(C)  is dependent upon disputed factual issues concerning the onset of Plaintiff’s
disability, the scope of Plaintiff’s Regular Occupation at that time, and whether Plaintiff’s coroner
position constituted an occupation providing substantially the same earning capacity. Accordingly,
summary judgment is inappropriate.

V. Conclusion

 For the foregoing reasons, the Court concludes that neither party has demonstrated
entitlement to judgment as a matter of law. The record contains genuine disputes of material fact
regarding the application of the policy to Plaintiff’s circumstances. Specifically, factual disputes
remain concerning the scope of Plaintiff’s Regular Occupation, whether Plaintiff was regularly
engaged in one or multiple occupations at the time he allegedly became totally disabled, whether
Plaintiff’s visual impairment prevented him from performing the substantial and material duties of
his Regular Occupation, and whether Augustar properly classified Plaintiff’s claim as one for
Residual Disability benefits. These issues require the resolution of competing evidence, which is
reserved for the trier of fact. Accordingly, summary judgment is inappropriate for either party.
