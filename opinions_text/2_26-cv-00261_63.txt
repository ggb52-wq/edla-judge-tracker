II. Legal Standard
Rule 12(b)(6) permits dismissal when a party “fail[s] to state a claim upon
which relief can be granted.” In reviewing such a motion, the Court accepts all well -
pleaded facts as true and views them in the light most favorable to the non -moving
party.23 To survive dismissal, the pleading must contain sufficient factual matter to
state a claim that is plausible on its face.24 “‘Threadbare recitals of the elements of a
cause of action, supported by mere conclusory statements’ cannot establish facial
plausibility.”25 Generally, in deciding a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim,
if “matters outside the pleading are presented to and not excluded by the court, the
motion shall be treated as one for summary judgment.” 26 But “[d]ocuments that a
defendant attaches to a motion to dismiss are considered part of the pleadings if they
are referred to in the plaintiff’s complaint and are central to her claim.”27 A document
filed by a litigant  pro se must be “liberally construed ,” and “a pro se complaint,
however inartfully pleaded, must be held to less stringent standards than formal
pleadings drafted by lawyers.”28 Although the Court should be “appropriately lenient”
with pro se parties, the “right of self -representation does not exempt a party from
compliance with relevant rules of procedural and substantive law.”29

Subsection (c) makes it unlawful for any person employed by or associated with any

All of these provisions  require a plaintiff to allege a pattern of racketeering
activity. Section 1961(1) contains an exhaustive list of acts of racketeering, also

pleads litigation conduct, such as defending the State against his claims in state court
and receiving and responding to discovery requests on the State’s behalf, and assigns

Plaintiff failing to plead, specific intent to deprive another person of “money or
