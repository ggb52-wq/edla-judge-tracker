Before the Court is Plaintiffs’ Renewed Emergency Motion for  Temporary
Restraining Orde r Preserving the Status Quo Pending Ruling on Preliminary
Injunction.1 Also pending in this case is Plaintiff’s Motion for Preliminary Injunction,2
for which the Court held a hearing on October 8, 2026. Plaintiffs request this TRO to
maintain the status quo pending a ruling on the preliminary injunction, specifically,
to halt the Special Education Advisory Counsil meeting on October 14, 2026,3 and the
Washington Parish School Board meeting on October 15, 2026.4
To obtain a TRO, a plaintiff must show “(1) a substantial likelihood of success
on the merits, (2) a substantial threat of irreparable injury if the TRO is not issued,
(3) that the threatened injury if the TRO is denied outweighs any harm that will
result if the TRO is granted, and (4) that the grant of a TRO will not disserve the

To show immediate and irreparable harm, a plaintiff must demonstrate it is
likely it will suffer irreparable harm in the absence of preliminary relief. 8 However,
a “[s]peculative injury is not sufficient; there must be more than an unfounded fear
on the part of the applicant.”9 Plaintiffs argue that the Court should order Defendants
to stop implementing the reconfiguration while the Court considers whether to enjoin
it. Specifically, Plaintiffs state there is irreparable harm because “implementation
will extinguish parents’ right to participate before decisions are made and will subject
children with disabilities to transitions that may later be reversed.”10
The renewed motion fails to provide adequate reasoning as to why both
meetings in question will produce irreparable harm. Plaintiffs have still not shown
how meetings regarding implementation of the voted -on decision to reconfigure
Mount Herm on School create immediate, certain harm to Plaintiffs. Plaintiffs
acknowledge that the reconfiguration, if not enjoined, would not take place until the

The Court finds that Plaintiffs have failed to meet their burden in showing (1)
a substantial likelihood of success on the merits of one or more of their claims; (2) a
threat of irreparable harm , as the  actual reconfiguration  is not scheduled to take
effect until the following school year; (3) the alleged injury would outweigh any harm
that would result in a grant; and (4) the grant of this TRO would not disserve the
public interest. The Court finds this issue is better suited for review in the pending
motion for preliminary injunction.
Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that the Renewed Motion for Temporary Restraining Order
is DENIED.
