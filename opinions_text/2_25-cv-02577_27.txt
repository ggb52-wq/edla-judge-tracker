II. Standard of Review
“When a Rule 12(b)(1) motion is filed in conjun ction with other Rule 12 motions, the court
should consider the Rule 12(b)(1) jurisdictional attack before addressing any attack on the merits.”
See Ramming v. United States, 281 F.3d 158, 161 (5th Cir. 2001) (citing Hitt v. City of Pasadena,
561 F.2d 606, 608 (5th Cir. 1977) (per curiam)).  “On a Rule 12(b)(1) motion, the party seeking to
invoke federal jurisdiction has the burden [of proof].” Boudreaux v. La. State Bar Ass'n , 3 F.4th
748, 753 (5th Cir. 2021). The district court shou ld only grant a 12(b)(1) motion “if it appears
certain that the plaintiff cannot pr ove any set of facts in support of his claim that would entitle
plaintiff to relief.” Ramming, 281 F.3d at 161.
In the Fifth Circuit, the standa rd of review applicable to motions to dismiss under Rule
12(b)(1) resembles the standard of review for mo tions to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(6) but also
allows the Court to consider a broader range of materials. El v. United States , No. 21-968, 2021
WL 4148118, at *3 (E.D. La. Sept. 13, 2021) (citing Williams v. Wynne, 533 F.3d 360, 364-65 n.2
(5th Cir. 2008)). Under 12(b)(6), the district court “must accept all well-pleaded facts as true and
view those facts in the light most favorable to the plaintiff.” Schlesinger v. ES & H, Inc., No. 11-
294, 2011 WL 3900577, at *2 (E.D. La. Sept . 2, 2011) (Lemelle, J.) (citing True v. Robles, 571
F.3d 412, 417 (5th Cir. 2009)).
III. Analysis
Moore named Willie Williams, Courtney Williams, and Tybrysom Miller as Defendants in
this discrimination matter. R. Doc. 1.  However, David Steiner is the Postmaster General or Agency
head for the United States Postal Service.

Title 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-16(c) permits most federal employees to seek relief in federal court
from unlawful employment practices. Pacheco v. Mineta, 448 F.3d 783, 787-88 (5th Cir. 2006).
As in other Title VII cases, a federal employee mu st first exhaust her administrative remedies by
filing a charge of discrimination with the EEO division of the employing agency. Id. (citing Brown
v. Gen. Servs. Admin. , 425 U.S. 820 (1976);  Martinez v. Dep't of U.S. Army , 317 F.3d 511 (5th
Cir. 2003); 29 C.F.R. § 1614.105-107 (2005)).
 Title 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-16(c) also provides that the appropriate party defendant in a
discrimination lawsuit brought by a federal employee is “the head of the department, agency, or
unit.” In Hall v. Small Business Administration, the Fifth Circuit dismissed the employee’s claims
against the regional directors of  a government agency because Title VII requires that only the
agency head can be named as a proper defendant in Title VII cases against the federal government.
See Hall v. Small Bus. Admin., 695 F.2d 175, 180 (5th Cir. 1983) (“There is patently no cause of
action against the [agency], its regional director, or its district director.... Only the Administrator
[of the agency] was a proper defendant.”).
 Willie Williams, Courtney Williams, and T ybrysom Miller are indi vidual employees of
the Postal Service.  Therefore, they are not su bject to suit pursuant to Title VII.  The proper
Defendant is the Postmaster Gene ral David Steiner who is also al ready a party.  Therefore, the
claims against Willie Williams , Courtney Williams, and Tybrys om Miller are dismissed with
prejudice because the Court lacks jurisdiction over the asserted claims.
IV. Conclusion
Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that Defendant, David P. Steiner’s Motion to Dismiss Parties (R. Doc.
20)  is GRANTED.
