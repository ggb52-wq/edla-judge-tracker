REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

 Plaintiff Michelle Brown filed this lawsuit against Hilton New Orleans Riverside on May
22, 2025. She alleges that she experienced discrimination in violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights
Act of 1964, as amended, while she was employed with Hilton New  Orleans Riverside. She filed
proof of service into the record, which reflects that Scott Taylor served the Complaint and Summons
on Tiffany Jacobs, manager of Hilton New Orleans Riverside on May 26, 2025. Hilton New Orleans
Riverside has not made an appearance. The Clerk of Court entered default on September 19, 2025.
Ms. Brown now moves for Default Judgment (Rec. Doc. 10).
 Default Judgment is not appropriate in this case because Hilton New Orleans Riverside is
not an entity that is capable of being sued. Accordingly, it is RECOMMENDED that the Motion
for Default Judgment be DENIED and that Hilton New Orleans Riverside be DISMISSED.
Law and Analysis
1. Default Judgment
“When a party against whom a judgment for affirmative relief is sought has failed to plead
or otherwise defend, and that failure is shown by affidavit or otherwise, the clerk must enter the
party's default.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 55(a). If plaintiff’s claim is not for a sum certain that can be made
by certain computation, the plaintiff must apply to the court for a default judgment. Id.  R. 55(b).

2. Capacity to Be Sued
 For parties that are not individuals or corporations, Rule 17 provides that capacity to be sued
is determined by the law of the state where the court is located. Fed. R. Civ. P. 17(b)(3). Under
Louisiana law, “an entity must qualify as a juridical person” to be capable of being sued. Dugas v.
City of Breaux Bridge Police Dep't , 1999-1320 (La. App. 3 Cir. 2/2/00), 757 So. 2d 741, 743 . “A
juridical person is an entity to which the law attributes personality, such as a corporation or a
partnership.” La. Civ. Code art. 24.
 Ms. Brown has named Hilton New Orleans Riverside as the defendant and her employer.
She does not identify what kind of entity Hilton New Orleans Riverside may be. The Louisiana
Secretary of State’s database of business filings does not contain any record of a “Hilton New
Orleans Riverside.” The closest matches are Hilton New Orleans, LLC (a limited liability company
with a domicile address in Virginia); Hilton New Orleans Corporation (a corporation that  in 1998
merged into Park Place New Orleans, L.L.C ., a n entity that is itself now inactive ); and Hilton
Riverside, LLC (a limited liability company with a domicile address in Delaware).1
Louisiana law also provides that “[a] person who does business under a trade name is the
proper defendant in an action to enforce an obligation created by or arising out of the doing of such
business.” La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 736 . The comments note that the article was intended to

does not have the capacity to be sued. Ms. Brown’s Motion for Default Judgment should be denied
and Hilton New Orleans Riverside should be dismissed.
3. Service of Process
Even if Ms. Brown had named as defendant Hilton New Orleans, LLC, or Hilton Riverside,
LLC, as the entity operating under the name Hilton New Orleans Riverside,  Ms. Brown has not
properly served either entity. Courts have held that default judgment cannot be entered against a
party that has not been properly served. See Black v. Louisiana Dep't of Correction, No. 3:16-CV-
799-JWD-RLB, 2017 WL 11710636, at *2 (M.D. La. Nov. 17, 2017); Jones v. Becnel, No. CIV.A.
15-713, 2015 WL 4677543, at *3 (E.D. La. Aug. 6, 2015) . Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(h)
provides that:
Unless federal law provides otherwise or the defendant's waiver has been filed, a
domestic or foreign corporation, or a partnership or other unincorporated association
that is subject to suit under a common name, must be served:
(1) in a judicial district of the United States:
(A) in the manner prescribed by Rule 4(e)(1) for serving an
individual; or
(B) by delivering a copy of the summons and of the complaint to an
officer, a managing or general agent, or any other agent authorized

Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(h). Rule 4(e)(1) allows for the use of state law procedures  for service of process.
Under Louisiana law a limited liability company may be served by personal service on its registered
agent for service or process, La. Code Civ. Proc. Art. 1266. There is an alternative procedure if no
such agent has been designated or if the person attempting to make service certifies that they have
been unable to serve the designated agent after due diligence. Id.
Both Hilton New Orleans, LLC, and Hilton Riverside, LLC have designated registered
agents for service of process, as reflected in the Louisiana Secretary of State’s database of business
filings.2 Thus under state law, the registered agent must be personally served. But Ms. Brown did
not serve either registered agent (incidentally, both registered agents are Corporation Service
Company, 450 Laurel Street, 8th Floor, Baton Rouge, LA 70801).3 Nor is there any reason to believe
that manager Tiffany Jacobs is a managing or other agent authorized to receive service for Hilton
New Orleans, LLC, or Hilton Riverside, LLC, such that service would be appropriate under Federal
Rule of Civil Procedure 4(h) (1)(B). Accordingly, even had they been named, neither Hilton New
Orleans, LLC, nor Hilton Riverside, LLC, were properly served.
Conclusion
 Because defendant Hilton New Orleans Riverside is not an entity that is capable of being
sued, IT IS RECOMMENDED that (1) Ms. Brown’s Motion for Default Judgment (Rec. Doc. 10)
be DENIED and (2) Hilton New Orleans Riverside be DISMISSED.
