III. APPLICABLE LAW AND ANALYSIS
A. Required Elements of a § 1983 Claim
Section 1983 creates a damages remedy for the violation of federal constitutional or
statutory rights under color of state law:
Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or
usage, of any State . . . subjects, or causes to be subjected, any . . . person within
the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities
secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured in an action
at law . . . .15

“The purpose of § 1983 is to deter state actors from using their badge of authority to deprive
individuals of federally guaranteed rights and to provide relief to victims if such deterrence fails.”16
A plaintiff must satisfy three elements to establish § 1983 liability:

Because § 1983 merely provides a remedy for designated rights, rather than creating any
substantive rights, “an underlying constitutional or statutory violation is a predicate to liability.”18

acting under color of state law. 19  “The traditional definition of acting under color of state law
requires that the defendant in a § 1983 action have exercised power ‘possessed by virtue of state
law and made possible only because the wrongdoer is clothed with the authority of state law.’”20
Mere negligence, however,  is an insufficient basis to support a claim for denial of a
constitutional right:21  “[T]he Due Process Clause is simply not implicated by a negligent act of an

of contexts, courts have determined that allegations amounting to mere negligence cannot support

2. Immunity – Clerk of Court
Plaintiff’s claim against Jon Geggenheimer as Jefferson Parish Clerk of Court in his official
capacity is barred by absolute and/or qualified immunity.44  Absolute immunity extends to non -
judicial officers who perform judicial functions in accordance with a court directive or instruction,
including court clerks.45  This rule exists because “court personnel should not serve as a lightening
[sic] rod for harassing litigation.”46  Court clerks have absolute immunity from actions for damages
arising for actions they are specifically required to perform by court order or discretionary action,
and qualified immunity for routine duties not explicitly commanded by court decree or judic ial
instruction.47
Although Plaintiff does not detail the precise basis for his claim against the Clerk of Court,
it presumably relates to the redaction of court records, which is conduct that falls within the scope
of performing either judicial or routine functions.  Therefore, the official capacity claim against
defendant Jefferson Parish Clerk of Court Jon Geggenheimer is frivolous, seeks relief against an
immune defendant, and otherwise fails to state a claim for which relief can be granted and must
be dismissed pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e) and § 1915A.

IV. AVAILABILITY OF AMENDMENT
The court should freely grant leave to amend a pleading when justice so requires.62  Unless
it is clear that the plaintiff is unwilling or unable to amend in a manner that will avoid dismissal,
the court errs in not providing a pro se plaintiff with at least one opportunity to cure pleading
deficiencies before dismissing.63  When conducting a frivolousness review, the court may offer a
pro se litigant an opportunity to amend his complaint before it is dismissed if the court finds that
he has not had the opportunity to state his “best case.”64
Given Granger’s Complaint. Memorandum and Supplemental Memorandum, Granger has
had the opportunity to state his best case. Further, amendment would be futile given that he cannot
state a non -frivolous due process nor access to the courts claim  based on events in March 2024
when his AEDPA deadline for filing a habeas petition expired almost ten years earlier, in July
2014.
