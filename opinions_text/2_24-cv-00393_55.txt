ORDER AND REASONS

Before the Court is Defendant Jin Weiji’s (“Weiji”) Motion to Stay.1 This litigation arises
out of a  cryptocurrency scheme where Plaintiff  Jeysen Zivan Yogaratnam (“Plaintiff”)  was
allegedly defrauded of $294,255.2 Plaintiff originally filed this case on February 15, 2024, naming
numerous “John Doe” Defendants.3 On June 1, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff leave to file t he
Second Amended Complaint, which specifically named five individual defendants including Jin
Weiji.4 Plaintiff alleges that each of the named defendants have controlled, owned, or held
beneficial interests in wallet addresses that received Plaintiff’s stolen cryptocurrency. 5 The Court
also granted Plaintiff’s request to authorize alternative service of the defendants by email.6 Plaintiff
served the defendants with the Second Amended Complaint on June 8, 2026.7

On July 28, 2026, Weiji filed the instant Motion to Stay.8 In the motion, Weiji seeks a stay
of any default entered against him, and he seeks an additional 60 days to file responsive pleadings.9
On August 10, 2026, Plaintiff filed an opposition to the motion.10 Plaintiff contends that Weiji has
not shown good cause to excuse his failure to timely appear. 11 Plaintiff also contends that Weiji
has not carried his burden to justify the extraordinary relief of a stay.12 On August 11, 2026, Weiji
filed a reply brief, asserting that his delay in failing to appear was not willful. 13 He contends that
he did not understand the email service because he resides in China, does not read English, and
does not have counsel.14
Pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(a)(1), Weiji had 21 days after being served
with the summons and Amended Complaint to file responsive pleadings. Weiji was served on June
8, 2026, and he filed the instant motion on July 28, 2026,  nearly two months after he was served.
Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 6(b)(1)(B), “any grant of an extension that is filed after the
time for a response has expired may only be granted upon a finding of excusable neglect. ”15
“Excusable neglect”  is “an elastic concept.”16 This concept can apply for inadvertent delays and

“is not limited strictly to omissions caused by circumstances beyond the control of the movant.”17
The determination of what is “excusable” is “at bottom an equitable one, taking into account all
relevant circumstances surrounding the party’s omission.”18
The Court finds that Weiji has demonstrated excusable neglect sufficiently justifying an
extension of the time to file responsive pleadings.  Weiji represents that he resides in China, does
not read English, and does not have counsel. Although Weiji acknowledges receipt of the June 8,
2026 email serving him with a copy of the Second Amended Complaint, he states that he did not
understand its importance until after he had the email translated. Moreover, Plaintiff has not filed
a motion for entry of default, and no default was entered before Weiji filed the instant motion.
Therefore, the Court grants Weiji an additional 30 days to file responsive pleadings.
Weiji also requests that the Court stay this proceeding. In Landis v. North American Co.,
the Supreme Court recognized that “the power to stay proceedings is incidental to the power
inherent in every court to control the disposition of the causes on its docket with economy of time
and effort for itself, for counsel, and for litigants.”19 The Supreme Court noted that “how this can
best be done calls for the exercise of judgment, which must weigh competing interests and maintain
an even balance.”20 Therefore, a district court has “discretionary power to stay proceedings before
it in the control of its docket and in the interests of justice.”21 In determining whether a stay should
be granted, “courts consider the following factors as set out in Landis v. North American Co.: (1)

any hardship imposed on the moving party by proceeding with the action, (2) any prejudice to the

Considering Weiji’s Motion in light of the Landis factors, Weiji has not shown that a stay
is warranted. First, Weiji has not shown how proceeding with this action will cause any hardship
apart from the general constraints of litigation . Second, Plaintiff will be prejudiced by a stay of
proceedings, as it would delay adjudication of his claims. Third, it is in the interest of judicial
economy to resolve this matter . Therefore, all three Landis factors weigh against staying this
matter.
 IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that Defendant Jin Weiji’s Motion to Stay23 is GRANTED
IN PART and DENIED IN PART. The motion is GRANTED to the extent it requests additional
time to file responsive pleadings. The motion is DENIED in all other respects.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that responsive pleadings shall be filed within 30 days of
