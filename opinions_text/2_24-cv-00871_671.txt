ORDER AND REASONS
 Before the Court are two Motions to Strike Plaintiffs’ Air Monitoring Expert and Report 1
filed by Defendant Huntington Ingalls, Inc. (“Avondale”) 2 and Defendant Bayer CropScience,
Inc.’s (“Bayer”) 3 (collectively referred to herein as “Defendants”) . In this litigation, Plaintiffs
allege Decedent Michael P. Dandry, Jr. (“Decedent”), while an employee for Avondale, was
exposed to asbestos and asbestos -containing products manufactured, distributed, sold, and/or
handled by Avondale and other parties.4 Plaintiffs allege this exposure caused and/or contributed
to Decedent’s development of mesothelioma and, ultimately, his death. 5 On the other hand,
Defendants advance an alternat ive theory of asbestos exposure from other sources, including on
properties near Decedent’s childhood home and workplace.6

Nevertheless, Defendants have not shown that exclusion of the air monitoring is warranted.
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26(a)(2)(B) provides for the disclosure of expert reports.
Disclosures under Rule 26(a)(2)(D) must be made in accordance with the deadlines set in the
Court’s Orders, or, absent a stipulation or a court order, at least 90 days before trial.39 Any opinions
that are not properly disclosed in accordance with Rule 26(a) may be excluded “unless the failure
was substantially justified or is harmless.”40 However, exclusion is not mandatory or automatic.41
Instead, the matter left to the district court’s discretion. 42 In deciding whether to exercise its
discretion to exclude an expert witness for failure to comply with Rule 26(a)(2), the Court is guided
by four factors: “(1) the importance of the evidence; (2) the prejudice to the opposing party of
including the evidence; (3) the possibility of curing such prejudice by granting a continuance; and
(4) the explanation for the party’s failure to disclose.”43
 Considering the first factor, the air monitoring evidence is relevant to Plaintiffs’ response
to Defendants’ evidence suggesting that Decedent may have been exposed to asbestos at or near
the Dandry family property. Plaintiffs intend to use the air monitoring to rebut Defendants’ soil
sampling. The evidence therefore bears directly on a disputed issue in this litigation.
As to the second factor, Defendants have not shown that they will be prejudiced if the

could have anticipated the need for air monitoring earlier, the Court finds Plaintiffs’ explanation
