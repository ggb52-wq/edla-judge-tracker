genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a

action.”12 When assessing whether a material fac tual dispute exists, the Court considers
“all of the evidence in the record but refrain[ s] from making credibility determinations
or weighing the evidence.” 13 All reasonable inferences are drawn in favor of the
nonmoving party. 14 There is no genuine issue of ma terial fact if, even viewing the
evidence in the light most favo rable to the nonmoving party, no reasonable trier of fact
could find for the nonmoving party, thus en titling the moving party to judgment as a
matter of law.15

persuasion at trial, the moving party “m ust come forward with evidence which would
