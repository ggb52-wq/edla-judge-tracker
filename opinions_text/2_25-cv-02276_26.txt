I. BACKGROUND
Plaintiff, Maylois Bacot, sued multiple defendants, including attorneys, law
firms, corporations, and a former state court judge, over a real estate foreclosure .
The foreclosure was  litigated in Louisiana state court from 2020  to 2023.2  Bacot
makes many  claims and requests for relief under the federal and Louisiana state
constitutions, federal and state law and regulation s, and rules of ethical and
professional conduct by judges and lawyers. 3  The request s include a temporary
restraining order and preliminary injunction against the foreclosure proceeding.4
II. ANALYSIS
A. PHH Mortgage Corporation, Amanda Stout, and Citi Residential Lending,
Inc.

 PHH Mortgage Corporation and Amanda Stout seek dismissal arguing service
was not proper , and the court does not have personal jurisdiction. 5  Alternatively,
they request that Bacot amend her complaint to adequately state her claims and give
notice of the damages caused by PHH and Stout.6

B. Penny Daigrepont9

Penny Daigrepont seeks dismissal under Rules 12b( 6) and 12b(1).  Among
other arguments, Daigrepont contends (1) she is immune for all actions taken by her
as counsel for an opposing party in the state court proceedings, (2) there are no
independent acts by Daigrepont apart from her  acts as opposing counsel and (3)
Bacot fails to plead facts sufficient to support federal civil rights claims and tort
claims against Daigrepont.10
The only discernible allegations against  Daigrepont are that she  acted
unlawfully while representing Bacot’s adversary  in the state court proceedings.
Generally, Louisiana law does not permit negligence claims against adverse counsel.

In Re Gulf States Long Term Acute Care of Covington, LLC , Nos. 11-cv-1659, 13-

prohibition applies to acts by adverse counsel that violate the Louisiana Rules of
Professional Conduct and result in negligible injury.  Id. at *5.  While there is a
narrow path for suits against adverse counsel for intentional acts, such acts must
demonstrate “a specific malice or intent to personally inflict direct harm upon his
client's adversary and with full knowledge that his conduct would cause such harm.”
Id. at *3 (citation omitted).
Bacot makes no specific  allegation against Daigrepont . She alleges “all
defendant parties acted in concert” to conspire to deprive her of her residence and
commit fraud on the court in so doing.  “A plaintiff must allege facts that establish
each individual [d]efendant ’s liability for the misconduct alleged. ”  Hawkins v.
Sanders, No. 19 -13323, 2023 WL 2565403 , at *3 (E.D. La. March 17, 2023)
(citations and other quotation marks omitted).   A plaintiff cannot refer to all
defendants “who occupied different positions and presumably had distinct roles in
the alleged misconduc t without specifying which defendants engaged in what
wrongful conduct.”  Id.  (citation and internal quotation marks omitted).   A court
will dismiss a complaint that contains such “impermissible vague group pleading”.
Id. (citation omitted).

 Additionally, the law requires that f raud be alleged with specificity.   See

C. LOGS Legal Group, LLP

LOGS Legal Group, LLP moves for dismissal under Rule 12b(6) for failing
to state a claim.11  Bacot only mentions LOGS in a list of defendants who she seeks
to enjoin from selling her house and from whom she seeks relief for violating her
federal and state constitutional and legal rights.  It appears LOGS (under a former
name) was Deutsche Bank’s counsel in the state court foreclosure proceedings.12
The same failings noted against Daigrepont, above, apply to LOGS.
