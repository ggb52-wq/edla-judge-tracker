II. LEGAL STANDARD
Federal courts are courts of limited jurisdiction. 44  A defendant may remove
“any civil action brought in a State court of which the district courts of the United
States have original jurisdiction.”45  “The removing party bears the burden of proving
that federal jurisdiction exists and that removal was proper.”46  The removal statute
is strictly construed and any doubt as to the propriety of removal should be resolved
in favor of remand.47  Remand is proper if at any time the court lacks subject matter
jurisdiction.48

Federal district courts have original jurisdiction over “all civil actions arising
under the Constitution, laws, or treaties of the United States.” 49  A party seeking to
establish federal question jurisdiction over a state law claim with an embedded
federal issue must satisfy the four-part test laid out in Grable & Sons Metal Products,
Inc. v. Darue Engineering & Manufacturing .50  As explained by the Fifth Circuit,
“Grable applies if: (1) resolving a federal issue is necessary to resolution of the state-
law claim; (2) the federal issue is actually disputed; (3) the federal issue is
substantial; and (4) federal jurisdiction will not disturb the balance of federal and
state judicial responsibilities.”51  According to the Fifth Circuit, “[t]hese conditions
are difficult to meet,” and, “[l]ike most federal question doctrines, Grable is applied
in the shadow of the well-pleaded complaint rule.”52  Thus, courts look “to the face of
a plaintiff’s well -pleaded complaint to determine whether the issues it raises
implicate Grable.”53  The Fifth Circuit explained that, “[t]he category of cases that
satisfy these requirements is special and small ,” as, “[t]he type of claim that creates
a federal question under Grable is typically a state -law claim premised on some
component of federal law.” 54  “Furthermore, although the parties may ultimately
litigate a federal issue in their case, that fact does not show that the suit, that is, the

Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1333(1) , also known as the saving to suitors clause,
federal district courts  also “have original jurisdiction, exclusive of the courts of the
States, of: (1) Any civil case of admiralty or maritime jurisdiction, saving to suitors in
all cases all other remedies to which they are otherwise entitled.”56  “[A] party seeking
to invoke federal admiralty jurisdiction pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1333(1) over a tort
claim must satisfy conditions both of location and of connection with maritime
activity.”57  The locality test assesses “whether the tort occurred on navigable water
or whether injury suffered on land was caused by a vessel on navigable water.”58  This
test requires the Court to first determine whether the dispute “has a potentially
disruptive impact on maritime commerce,” and then determine “whether the general
character of the activity giving rise to the incident shows a substantial relationship
to traditional maritime activity.”59

III. ANALYSIS
A. Federal Question Jurisdiction, 28 U.S.C. § 1331
In their Notice of Removal, Defendants assert that the Court has subject
matter jurisdiction over Plaintiff’s claims under 28 U.S.C. § 1331. 65  In their
Opposition brief to Plaintiff’s Motion to Remand, Defendants contend that the Court
has federal question jurisdiction over Plaintiff’s state law claims  because Plaintiff
seeks a determination of the proper distribution of the Salvage Award issued by this
Court, which is governed by the Salvage Convention.   In Grable & Sons Metal
Products, Inc. v. Darue Engineering and Manufacturing, the Supreme Court held that
a state law claim does not “arise under” federal law simply because a state court must
interpret some aspect of federal law in reaching its decision.66  The Supreme Court in
Romero v. International Terminal Operating Co. likewise cautioned against the
“infusion of general maritime jurisdiction into the ‘federal question’ grant.”  67  As
recognized by Defendants, under  Grable & Sons , this Court can exercise federal
question jurisdiction under limited circumstances when “(1) resolving a federal issue
is necessary to resolution of the state -law claim; (2) the federal issue is actually
disputed; (3) the federal issue is substantial; and ( 4) federal jurisdiction will not
disturb the balance of federal and state judicial responsibilities."68

B. Federal Admiralty Jurisdiction, 28 U.S.C. § 1333(1)
Defendants also assert in their Notice of Removal that the Court has subject
matter jurisdiction over Plaintiff’s claims based upon federal admiralty jurisdiction,
28 U.S.C. § 1333.79  Although Defendants do not cite § 1333 in their Opposition brief,
they contend that Plaintiff’s marine salvage claims fall within the Court’s admiralty
jurisdiction and that removal was proper under the saving to suitors clause because
the Court has federal question jurisdiction over Plaintiff’s claims.80  Defendants argue
that Plaintiff’s lawsuit “seeks, above all, a determination of each salvors’ claim to the
global Salvage Award. That determination is to be made pursuant to federal law,
including the Salvage Convention.”81
 As previously discussed, the Fifth Circuit has held that the saving to suitors
clause in § 1333(1) “does not guarantee plaintiffs a nonfederal forum, or limit the
right of defendants to remove such actions to federal court where there exists some
