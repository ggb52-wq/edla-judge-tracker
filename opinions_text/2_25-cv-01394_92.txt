I. BACKGROUND
Plaintiff Quanda Edmond was injured in a motor vehicle collision on a
construction site in Plaquemines Pa rish, Louisiana, on May 22, 2024. 4  The

alleges that a vehicle operated by  an employee of defendant Zachry
Industrial, Inc. (“ZII”), rear-ended th e vehicle that plaintiff was driving,

II. LEGAL STANDARD
Courts are “afforded broad discre tion when deciding discovery
matters.”  Crosby v. La. Health Serv. and Indem. Co., 647 F.3d 258, 261 (5th
Cir. 2011).  Federal Rule of Civil Pr ocedure 26(c)(1) provides, in relevant
part:
A party or any person from whom discovery is sought
may move for a protective order in the court where
the action is pending—or as an alternative on matters
relating to a deposition, in the court for the district
where the deposition will be taken.  The motion must
include a certification that the movant has in good
faith conferred or attempted to confer with other
affected parties in an effort to resolve the dispute
without court action.  The court may, for good cause,
issue an order to protect a party or person from
annoyance, embarrassment,  oppression, or undue
burden or expense, including one or more of the
following:

(A) forbidding the disclosure or discovery;
(B) specifying terms, including time and place or
the allocation of expenses, for the disclosure or
discovery;
(C) prescribing a discovery method other than the
one selected by the party seeking discovery;
(D) forbidding inquiry into certain matters, or
limiting the scope of disclosure or discovery to
certain matters

Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(c)(1).

discovery.  Fujita v. United States, 416 F. App’x 400, 402 (5th Cir. 2011).  The

Plaintiff’s arguments that defendants’ representatives should be

therefore be open to all issues of liability; rather, the Court retains discretion
under Rule 26(c) to limit discovery to that which is “necessary to respond to
defendants’ motions,” as stated in the October Order.45
