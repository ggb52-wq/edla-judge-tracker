II. LEGAL STANDARD
Under Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 37, if a party “fails to obey an order to
provide or permit discovery,” the Court is authorized to impose sanctions, including
“dismissing the action or proceeding in whole or in part.” 26 The Rules also provide
that “[i]f the plaintiff fails to prosecute or to comply with [the Rules] or a court order,
a defendant may move to dismiss the action or any claim against it.”27 “In addition to
the authority granted by Rule 41(b), a federal district court possesses the inherent
authority to dismiss an action for want of prosecution, which it may exercise on its
own motion when necessary to maintain the orderly administration of j ustice.”28
Dismissal with prejudice “is warranted only where a clear record of delay or

“[I]t is not a party's negligence —regardless of how careless, inconsiderate, or

Specifically,
[S]everal factors [“ Conner factors”] must be present before a district
court may dismiss a case with prejudice as a sanction for violating a
discovery order: (1) “the refusal to comply results from willfulness or bad
faith and is accompanied by a clear record of delay or contumacious
conduct;” (2) the violation of the discovery order must be attributable to
the client instead of the attorney, (3) the violating party's misconduct
“must substantially prejudice the opposing party;” and (4) a less drastic
sanction would not substantially achieve the desired deterrent effect.32

Courts have found a clear record of delay “where there have been ‘significant
periods of total inactivity.’” 33 And courts have found that “[d]ismissal is warranted
when a court is faced with ‘[d]eliberate, repeated refusals to comply with discovery
orders.’”34

III. ANALYSIS
The Court first recognizes that dismissal with prejudice is an extreme sanction
that is not imposed lightly. Wi th that in mind , and after reviewing the record, the
Court finds that all Conner factors are present in this case, warranting dismissal.
First, the Court finds the Plaintiff’s refusal to comply results from willfulness
or bad faith and is accompanied by a clear record of delay or contumacious conduct.35
Plaintiff's repeated failure to comply with the Court ’s Orders without even
attempting to put forth an excuse demonstrates the kind of conduct that warrants
dismissal. Courts have repeatedly found that this type of behavior constitutes
contumacious conduct.36 Plaintiff has been warned several times of the consequences
that would result from his failure to comply with the Court's Orders and his discovery
obligations. The Court finds that this behavior is not merely negligent and  instead
shows an intentional refusal to abide by the Court’s Orders.
Second, the Court finds there is delay caused by the P laintiff himself and not

Fourth, the Court finds a less drastic sanction would not substantially achieve
the desired deterrent effect. 52 Several lesser sanctions are available to the Court:
“Assessments of fines, costs, or damages against the plaintiff or his counsel, attorney
disciplinary measures, conditional dismissal, dismissal without prejudice, and
explicit warnings are preliminary means or less severe sanctions that may be used to
safeguard a court's undoubted right to control its docket.” 53 Given the significant
periods of inactivity in this case, and the Plaintiff's disregard of the Court’s O rders,
no lesser sanction will serve justice here.  The Court has imposed sanctions in this
case including explicit warnings, which have been ignored. Plaintiff was warned
specifically on August 26, 2026 that his failure to comply with the Court’s Order
granting BP’s Motion to Compel and Motion to Enforce “may result in sanctions,
including a recommendation that his lawsuit be dismissed for failure to prosecute.”54
Despite this explicit warning, Plaintiff failed to abide by the Order.
Assessment of fines, costs or damages in this case are unlikely to spur Plaintiff
to comply with Court Orders or engage in the prosecution of his case. Plaintiff has
shown a complete unwillingness to participate in his litigation, which the Court finds
is unlikely to be remedied by imposing monetary penalties. A conditional dismissal
seems equally unlikely to persuade Plaintiff to comply with deadlines and Orders he
has previously ignored. Dismissal without prejudice is unavailable in this case
because the statutes of limitations have run on Plaintiff’s claims, thus rendering a

IV. CONCLUSION
The Court recognizes that dismissal with prejudice is an extreme sanction and
does not impose it lightly. However, the Court finds that little to no steps have been
taken in the prosecution of this case since its inception in 2019, and all aggravating
Conner factors are present. The Court is entitled to impose sanctions “to achieve the
orderly and expeditious disposition of cases .”56 Thus, the Court finds dismissal with
prejudice appropriate in this case.
Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that BP’s Motion to Dismiss as a Discovery Sanction 57 be
GRANTED and Plaintiff’s claims be dismissed with prejudice.
