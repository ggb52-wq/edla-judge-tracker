REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

While incarcerated at the Jefferson Parish Correctional Center (“JPCC”), p laintiff, Albert
Lewis, filed this  federal civil  action pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983. 1  Pursuant to its screening
authority, the Court dismissed plaintiff’s  claims against three of the defendants, Marc Macaluso,
Toni Nguyen, and Wilfred Garrison.2  The Court then ordered that summons issue with respect to
the remaining defendants, Joseph Lopinto, Derek Adams, and Daniel Lassus. 3  After those
remaining defendants filed an answer,4 the Court scheduled a preliminary conference in the case,
and the JPCC’s warden was ordered to produce plaintiff for that conference.5  However, the Court
was subsequently notified that the warden was unable to comply with that order because plaintiff
was no longer incarcerated that the JPCC.  Therefore, that conference was canceled.6
Plaintiff provided no alternate address in the complaint, and he has failed to notify the
Court of his current address despite being released or transferred from the JPCC.  That failure is a
direct violation of this Court’s Loc al Rules, which expressly provide:  “Each attorney and pro se
litigant has a continuing obligation promptly to notify the court of any address or telephone number

 I n light of the foregoing, it is appropriate to dismiss plaintiff’s c laims against the remaining
defendants for want of prosecution.  The authority of a federal trial court to dismiss a plaintiff’s
action because of failure to prosecute is clear.  Link v. Wabash R.R., 370 U.S. 626 (1962);
McCullough v. Lynaugh, 835 F.2d 1126 (5th Cir. 1988).  The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure
specifically provide that a court may, in its discretion, dismiss a plaintiff’s action for failure to
prosecute or for failure to comply with the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure or any order of the
court and that such a dismissal is considered to be an adjudication on the merits.  Fed. R. Civ. P.
41(b).  The Court’s power to dismiss for want of prosecution should be used sparingly, although
it may be exercised sua sponte whenever necessary to achieve the orderly and expeditious
disposition of cases.  Ramsay v. Bailey, 531 F.2d 706, 707 (5th Cir. 1976).
