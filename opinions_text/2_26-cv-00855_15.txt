When, as here, the motion to amend a pleading is filed beyond the scheduling
order’s permitted date, the movant must first establish good cause under 16(b)(4). 6
See e.g., S&W Enters., L.L.C. v. SouthTrust Bank of Alabama, NA, 315 F.3d 533, 535-
56 (5th Cir. 2003). Only upon a showing of good cause will the “more liberal standard
of Rule 15(a) apply.” Id.; Jackson v. Gray, 345 F.R.D. 597, 607 (E.D. La. Jan. 26, 2024)
(quoting Butler v. Taser Intern., Inc. , 535 F. App’x  371, 372 (5th Cir. 2013)). Courts
consider “(1) the explanation for the failure to timely move for leave to amend; (2) the
importance of the amendment; (3) potential prejudice in allowing the amendment;
and (4) the availability of a continuance to cure such prejudice.” Jackson, 345 F.R.D.
at 607 (citing Fahim v. Marriott Hotel Servs., Inc., 551 F.3d 344, 348 (5th Cir. 2008));
S&W Enters., L.L.C., 315 F.3d at 536.
 All four factors weigh toward amendment. The instant action is the result of a
telephone conference at which General Motors requested Gerry Lane be added as a
defendant.7 Considering Gerry Lane allegedly  sold and repeatedly repaired the
vehicle, the amendment is of clear import and without unfair prejudice. The Court
accordingly considers Rule 15’s more permissive standard.

Under Rule 15(a)(2), if more than 21 days have passed after service of a 12(b)
motion and no scheduling order has been entered, then the party may amend its
pleading only with the opposing party's written consent or the court's leave. The court
should free ly give leave when justice so requires.  Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a)(2).  The
language of Rule 15(a)(2)  “evinces a bias in favor of granting leave to amend.”
Chitimacha Tribe of Louisiana v. Harry L.L. Co., 690 F.2d 1157, 1163 (5th Cir. 1982);
see also Mayeaux v. Louisiana Health Serv. & Indem. Co., 376 F.3d 420, 425 (5th Cir.
2004). The Fifth Circuit has instructed that the “district court must possess a
‘substantial reason’ to deny a request for leave to amend.”  Smith v. EMC Corp., 393
F.3d 590, 595 (5th Cir. 2004).  Although leave to amend is to be freely given under
Rule 15(a)(2) , “that generous standard is tempered by the necessary power of a
district court to manage a case.” Yumilicious Franchise, LLC v. Barrie, 819 F.3d 170,
177 (5th Cir. 2016) (quoting Schiller v. Physicians Res. Grp. Inc., 342 F.3d 563, 566
(5th Cir. 2003)).
Relevant factors to consider under Rule 15(a) include “undue delay, bad faith
or dilatory motive on the part of the movant, repeated failures to cure deficiencies by
amendments previously allowed, undue prejudice to the opposing party by virtue of
allowance of the amendment, and futility of the amend ment.” Schiller, 342 F.3d at
566. Denial of leave to amend is reviewed for abuse of discretion. Mayeaux, 376 F.3d
at 425.  “[A]bsent a ‘substantial reason’ such as undue delay, bad faith, dilatory
motive, repeated failures to cure deficiencies, or undue prejudice to the opposing
party, ‘the discretion of the district court is not broad enough to permit denial.’”  Id.

 Each Rule 15 factor weighs toward amendment. Trial is more than four months
away, this is the Complaint’s first amendment, and the current defendant concurs
that the new party’s addition  is appropriate . Further, there is no indication that
amendment would be unduly prejudicial or futile. Plaintiff discovered the basis for
his amendment during a telephone conference that, although Plaintiff does not
provide a date for, appears to have occurred after the amendment deadline.8 Plaintiff
then originally sought leave to amend on October 7, 2026; the undersigned does not
find undue delay.
 Accordingly,
 IT IS ORDERED that Plaintiff’s Motion (R. Doc. 14) is GRANTED.
 IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that Plaintiff’s request for further modification
of the Scheduling Order is DENIED WITHOUT PREJUDICE to being re- urged
before the District Court.
