II. LEGAL STANDARD
A. Review of the Magistrate Judge’s Report and Recommendation
 Under 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1), the district court may refer dispositive matters to
a magistrate judge, who then issues a report and recommendations.   The district
court must review de novo those portions of the report and recommendations to which
an objection is made. 63  The Court reviews all other portions of the report and
recommendations for clear error to determine whether they are contrary to law.64  A
decision is clearly erroneous or contrary to law “when the reviewing cou rt on the
entire evidence is left with the definite and firm conviction that a mistake has been
committed.”65  Here, Defendant only objects to the Report and Recommendation to
the extent that it does not address his request for attorney’s fees.  Accordingly, the
Court will review Defendant’s purported request for attorney’s fees de novo, while
reviewing the remainder of the Report and Recommendation for clear error.
B. Attorney’s Fees
There are three avenues by which a federal court may award attorney’s fees
against a party who obstructed a litigation proceeding in bad faith: (1) Federal Rule
of Civil Procedure 11; (2) 28 U.S.C. § 1927; and (3) the court’s inherent power.66  Rule
11 permits the Court to award reasonable attorney’s fees and expenses as a sanction
for documents filed for an improper purpose, “such as to harass, cause unnecessary
