II. APPLICABLE LAW
Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6) provides that an action may be dismissed “for failure to state a
claim upon which relief can be granted.” “To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain
sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to ‘state a claim for relief that is plausible on its face.’”
Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009) (quoting Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly , 550 U.S. 544,
570 (2008)). “Factual allegations must be enough to raise a right to relief above the speculative
level.” Twombly, 550 U.S. at 556. A claim is plausible on its face when the plaintiff has pled facts
that allow the court to “draw a reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct
alleged.” Id. at 570. Although a court must liberally construe the complaint in light most favorable
to the plaintiff, accept the plaintiff’ s allegations as true, and draw all reasonable inferences in
favor of the plaintiff, Baker v. Putnal , 75 F.3d 190, 196 (5th Cir. 1996), courts “do not accept as
true conclusory allegations, unwarranted factual inferences, or legal conclusions .” Arias-Benn v.
State Farm Fire & Cas. Co., 495 F.3d 228, 230 (5th Cir. 2007) (quoting Plotkin v. IP Axess Inc. ,
407 F.3d 690, 696 (5th Cir. 2005)).
If a complaint fails to state a plausible claim, a district court should generally grant leave
to amend under Fed. R. Civ. P. 15 before dismissing the action. Carroll v. Fort James Corp., 470
F.3d 1171, 1175 (5th Cir. 2006) (Rule 15 “evinces a bias in favor of granting leave to amend. The
policy of the Federal Rules is to permit liberal amendment . . .”). A court may deny leave, however,
when amendment would be futile. Villarreal v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., 814 F.3d 763, 766 (5th
Cir. 2016) (explaining that amendment is futile when “the amended complaint would fail to state
a claim upon which relief could be granted”).

III. DISCUSSION
The Court will begin by discussing Plaintiffs’ state law claims before continuing to discuss
Plaintiffs claims brought under § 1983.
A. State Law Claims
Louisiana Revised Statute 9:5628(A) provides a maximum prescriptive period of three
years from the date of the alleged act, omission, or neglect for the filing of medical malpractice
claims. Section 9:5628 applies to claims arising from patient care regardless of whether the alleged
conduct is characterized as intentional or unintentional. Montagino v. Canale, 792 F.2d 554, 556–
57 (5th Cir. 1986). Section 9:5628’s three -year deadline applies to survival actions by family
members of a decedent in the medical malpractice context, but not to wrongful death actions.
Taylor v. Giddens, 618 So. 2d 834, 840–842 (La. 1993). “[T]he commencement and running of [a
wrongful death action’s] prescriptive period is controlled by the one year liberative period
applicable to delictual actions[.]” Id. at 841.
In this case, it is plain that Plaintiffs did not file their claims against Turn Key within three
years of Alexzander’s death. Accordingly, Plaintiffs’ state law claims are prescribed unless they
relate back to Plaintiffs original, timely-filed complaint, or the prescriptive period was interrupted
as to Turn Key by Plaintiffs’ filing of their original complaint, despite Turn Key not being named
therein.
In support of their argument that these claims are not prescribed, Plaintiffs invoke the
general rule that timely suit against one joint tortfeasor interrupts prescription against all joint
tortfeasors. R. Doc. 39 at 10–11. However, the Louisiana Supreme Court has expressly held that
the general code article providing for relation back, La. C.C.P. art. 1153, does not apply to claims
governed by § 9:5628(A). Warren v. La. Med. Mut. Ins., 2007-0492, p. 4 (La. 12/2/08), 21 So. 3d

186, 207–08, on reh'g (June 26, 2009). Nor does the general code article providing for interruption
of prescription, La. C.C. art. 3462, for if these general code articles did apply, “then the
prescription and suspension provisions provided in the Medical Malpractice Act will be written
out.” LeBreton v. Rabito, 97-2221, p. 8 (La. 7/8/98), 714 So. 2d 1226, 1230.
As the Louisiana Supreme Court has explained, “[t]he [Medical Malpractice] Act has no
rules allowing relation back of pleadings for medical malpractice claims.” Warren , 21 So. 3d at
207. On this basis, Louisiana courts have held that amended medical malpractice pleadings naming
new defendants after the three-year prescriptive period cannot relate back to the original pleading.
See Santiago v. Tulane Univ. Hospital & Clinic, 2012-1095, (La. App. 4 Cir. 4/24/13), 115 So. 3d
675, 687–88; Wright v. State on Behalf of Alleyn, 2019-0499, pp. 4–6 (La. App. 4 Cir. 8/5/20), 364
So. 3d 312, 316–18;  Cook v. Rigby, 2019-1475, pp. 5–8 (La. App. 1 Cir. 11/25/20), 316 So. 3d
482, 486–88. Moreover, the Louisiana Supreme Court has held that the three-year period for filing
medical malpractice claims is peremptive and “therefore not susceptible of suspension,
interruption, or renunciation for any reason” even in the case of joint tortfeasors. Borel v. Young,
2007-0419, p. 10 (La. 11/27/07), 989 So. 2d 42, 49, on reh'g  (July 1, 2008) . Accordingly,
Plaintiffs’ claims against Turn Key arising from medical malpractice cannot be render ed timely
by relation back to the original complaint or interruption based on the timely filing of the original
complaint.
As to Plaintiffs’ claim for wrongful death, La. C.C.P. art. 1153 provides: “When the action
or defense asserted in the amended petition or answer arises out of the conduct, transaction, or
occurrence set forth or attempted to be set forth in the original pleading, the amendment relates
back to the date of filing the original pleading.” However, the Louisiana Supreme Court has
established several criteria “for determining whether art. 1153 allows an amendment which

Nor can interruption save Plaintiffs’ wrongful death claim from its one -year prescriptive
period. Plaintiffs argue that their timely initial complaint interrupted prescription of their wrongful
death claim under Louisiana Civil Code article 2324(C) , which states that “[i] nterruption of
prescription against one joint tortfeasor is effective against all joint tortfeasors. ” Where, as here,

prescription was interrupted. Terrebonne Par. Sch. Bd. v. Mobil Oil Corp., 310 F.3d 870, 878 (5th
Cir. 2002). Plaintiffs thus bear the burden of showing that Turn Key and the other named
defendants are joint tortfeasors such that their filing of the original complaint against the other
named defendants interrupted the running of the prescriptive period of its wrongful death claim
against Turn Key. Plaintiffs assert only that the “defendants are liable jointly, severally and in

B. Section 1983 Claims
Turn Key argues that Plaintiffs ’ § 1983 claims against it must also be dismissed. R. Doc.
34. It points out that Plaintiffs ’ complaint “does not identify a Turn Key policy that required or
encouraged the denial of prescription medication or appropriate medical care. Instead, Plaintiffs
allege in a conclusory fashion that Turn Key maintained policies or customs involving inadequate
staffing, delayed or withheld medical care, inadequate screening and monitoring, and the
containment of treatment within correctional facilities to save money.” R. Doc. 34-1 at 3–4. Turn
Key is correct that Plaintiffs’ conclusory allegations are inadequate to state a plausible claim under
§ 1983 at this time. However, Plaintiffs request leave to amend their complaint to supplement it
with “additional facts concerning similar incidents, prior corporate knowledge, or local
implementation[.]” R. Doc. 39 at 7. Given Rule 15’s bias in favor of liberally permitting
amendment when it is not plain that amendment would be futile, the Court will grant Plaintiffs
leave to amend their complaint as to these claims to more fully factually support their allegations.
IV. CONCLUSION
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons;
IT IS ORDERED that Turn Key’s Motion, R. Doc. 34, is GRANTED IN PART as to
Plaintiffs’ state law claims, which are hereby DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE.
