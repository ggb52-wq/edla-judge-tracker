II. LEGAL STANDARD
Counterclaimants’ request is governed by the Supplemental Admiralty and
Maritime Claims Rule E(7)(a), which provides:
When a person who has given security for damages in the original action
asserts a counterclaim that arises from the transaction or occurrence
that is the subject of the  original action, a plaintiff for whose benefit
such security has been given must give security for damages demanded
in the counterclaim unless the court, for cause shown, directs otherwise.
Proceedings on the original claim must be stayed until  this security is
given, unless the court directs otherwise.

The purpose of this rule is “to place the parties on an equality as regards
security.”25 “Although the language of the rule is automatic, it is not absolute; the
original seizing complainant may be excused by the court ‘ for cause shown. ’”26 The
determination of “for cause shown” is at the  discretion of the trial court, 27 but the
Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals has provided guidance as to what may constitute
“cause.” Factors to consider include whether the countersecurity is in an amount so
great that the original plaintiff is forced to give up his initial claim , whether a party
is financially unable to post countersecurity , and whether the counterclaim is
frivolous or lacking in meri t.28 The Fifth Circuit has further held that another
consideration is “the potential injustice of requiring [ one party ] to post security
without affording reciprocal protection.”29 “The exercise of the trial court's discretion
to order countersecurity is to be guided by the essential and equitable purposes of the
rule.”30

Requiring Plaintiffs post countersecurity would deprive Plaintiffs  of their right to
prosecute their initial claim, and essentially demand the indefinite stay or dismissal
of Plaintiffs’ case. The Court acknowledges that the purpose Rule E(7) is “to be guided
by the essential and equitable purposes of the rule,” 32 but finds that requiring
security from the Plaintiffs would be inequitable.
Second, there is not a parallel security concern between the Plaintiffs  and
Counterclaimants. The purpose of vessel seizures and required security is to allow
courts to continue to exercise jurisdiction  over parties who are “ mobile and able to
flee the jurisdiction of the court. ”33  The Plaintiffs and their vessel, the BRINLEY
MARIE, are Louisiana residents, and their property is located within the district. 34
The BRINLEY MARIE is presently located on land in Slidell, Louisiana .35 There is
not a reciprocal concern that Plaintiffs and their vessel will flee  the jurisdiction of
this Court.

holistically,36 the Court finds that good cause exists to deny ordering countersecurity
