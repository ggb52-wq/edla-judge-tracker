ORDER & REASONS
Before the Court is a motion to dismiss defendant Huntington Ingalls Inc.’s (“Avondale”)
second supplemental crossclaim and third-party complaint filed by defendant Philip Morris USA
Inc. (“PM USA”).1  Avondale responds in opposition,2 and PM USA replies in further support of
its motion.3
PM USA argues that Avondale’s second supplemental crossclaim and third -party
complaint (the “crossclaim and third-party complaint”) should be dismissed for the same reasons
it articulates in its motion to dismiss plaintiffs’ second amended complaint or for a more definite
statement4 and b ecause the crossclaim and third -party complaint  does not  assert any factual
allegations against PM USA “but merely incorporates” the allegations as stated in the second
amended complaint.5  In response, Avondale “adopts and incorporates by reference [the p]laintiffs’
[o]pposition to [PM USA’s] motion to dismiss their claims ” and contends that “[t]he law clearly
allows for incorporated allegations when the allegations give the party fair notice of the claims.” 6
Avondale insists that it gave PM USA fair notice of its claims by expressly incorporating the

“The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure specifically allow for incorporation by reference in
… pleadings.”  Carroll v. Ft. James Corp., 470 F.3d 1171, 1176 (5th Cir. 2006); see also Fed. R.
Civ. P. 10(c) (“A statement in a pleading may be adopted by reference elsewhere in the same
pleading or in any other pleading or motion. ”).  But “such incorporation must be done ‘with a
degree of specificity and clarity which would enable the responding party to easily determine the
nature and extent of the incorporation.’”  See Carroll, 470 F.3d at 1176 (quoting Wolfe v. Charter
Forest Behav. Health Sys., Inc., 185 F.R.D. 225, 228-29 (W.D. La. 1999)).  Having considered the
parties’ memoranda, the record, and the applicable law, the Court grants the motion in part and
denies it in part for the reasons stated in the Court’s November 5, 2026 Order & Reasons.9
Furthermore, the crossclaim and third -party complaint provides sufficient notice for PM
USA to “determine the nature and extent of the incorporation ,” id., because it specifically states
which paragraphs and allegations it incorporates from the second amended complaint.  But because
the Court has ordered that the plaintiffs provide a more definite statemen t, which will result in a
third amended complaint , Avondale will have to file an amended crossclaim  and third -party
complaint to that pleading so as to clearly place PM USA on notice of its claims against PM USA,
which it may do by again incorporating the operative complaint.

IT IS ORDERED that PM USA’s motion to dismiss Avondale’s second supplemental
crossclaim and th ird-party complaint (R. Doc. 1 75) is GRANTED IN PART and DENIED IN
PART.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that the motion is GRANTED IN PART to the extent the
second supplemental crossclaim and third-party complaint incorporates the plaintiffs’ negligence
claims that PM USA’s advertisements, promotions, or labels should have included additional or
more clearly stated warnings after July 1, 1969, which claims are  DISMISSED WITH
PREJUDICE.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that the motion is DENIED IN PART to the extent the second
supplemental crossclaim and third-party complaint incorporates the plaintiffs’ negligence claims
that PM USA’s advertisements, promotions, or labels should have included additional or more
clearly stated warnings before July 1, 1969.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that the motion is DENIED AS MOOT to the extent the
second supplemental crossclaim and third -party complaint incorporates any fraud claim ag ainst
PM USA because Avondale disavows any allegations based on intentional tort.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that  Avondale must provide a more definite statement by
filing an amended crossclaim and third-party complaint no later than 10 days after the plaintiffs
file a third amended complaint.
