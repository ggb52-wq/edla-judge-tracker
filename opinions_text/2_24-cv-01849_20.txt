REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

Before the Court is the Motion for Attorney Fees Pursuant to the Equal Access to Justice
Act filed by Plaintiff Ashley Cupit (Rec. Doc. 19) and automatically referred to the undersigned.
For the following reasons, it is RECOMMENDED that Plaintiff’s Motion for Attorney Fees
Pursuant to the Equal Access to Justice Act be GRANTED.
I. Background
 On July 25, 2024, Ms. Cupit filed a Complaint to review the decision of the Commissioner
of the Social Security Administration denying her application for Supplemental Security Income.
On January 3, 2025—after Plaintiff filed her motion for summary judgment—Defendant, Carolyn
Colvin, Acting Commissioner of Social Secu rity, filed a con sent motion moving to reverse and
remand the case for further administrative proceedings. The motion was granted by the district
judge. Ms. Cupit now seeks her attorney fees of $8,190.00 pursuant to the Equal Access to Justice
Act (“EAJA”), 28 U.S.C. § 2412. The Commissioner does not object.
II. The EAJA and Hourly Rate
  Under the EAJA, the Court shall award fees and other expenses to the prevailing party in
a civil action against the United States, unless the position of the United States was substantially

justified or special circumstances make an award unj ust. 28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(A). When the
district court remands a Social Security appeal to the ALJ for further findings, the Social Security
claimant qualifies as a prevailing party for purposes of fees under the EAJA. See Breaux v. U.S.
Dep’t of Health & Human Servs., 20 F.3d 1324 (5th Cir. 1994) (per curiam); Thibodeaux v. Astrue,
914 F. Supp. 2d 789, 791 (E.D. La. 2012). There is no dispute that Ms. Cupit is a prevailing party
entitled to an award of fees under the EAJA.
Ms. Cupit proposes an hourly rate of $ 234.00 for the attorney fees award, and the
Commissioner does not oppose this rate.  Attorney fees awarded pursuant to the EAJA are capped
at $125 per hour “unless the court determines that an increase in the cost of living or a special
factor, such as the limited availability of qualified attorneys for the proceedings involved, justifies
a higher fee.” 28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(2)(A). The United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit
posts a schedule of the maximum EAJA each year accounting for “[a]pprop riate cost-of-living
increases,” which it calculates “by multiplying the $125 statutory rate by the annual average
consumer price index figure for all urban consumers ( ‘CPI–U’) for the years in which counsel ’s
work was performed, and then dividing by the CPI–U figure for March 1996, the effective date of
EAJA’s $125 statutory rate.” Thangaraja v. Gonzales, 428 F.3d 870, 876–77 (9th Cir. 2005). For
work performed in 2024, the posted maximum hourly rate is $251.84. See Statutory Maximum
Rates Under the Equal Access to Justice Act, United States Courts for the Ninth Circuit ( March
28, 2025), https://www.ca9.uscourts.gov/content/view.php?pk_id=0000000039. In considering
the maximum hourly rate, c ourts in this dis trict have also considered the increase in the cost of
living using the consumer price index . Kobrock v. Soc . Sec. Admin. , No. 23 -256, 2024 WL
1285242, at *2 (E.D. La. Mar. 26, 2024) (noting that federal courts in this district “routinely apply

III. Hours Expended
 Ms. Cupit claims that h er counsel spent 38.9 hours in preparing h er case. She s eeks
reimbursement for 3 5 of those hours.  An itemization of h er counsel’s time is included  in the
motion. The Court finds the time spent to be reasonable . The Court notes that the Commissioner
does not challenge the reasonableness of the hours spent by Ms. Cupit’s counsel. In addition, courts
in this jurisdiction often find that  35 hours, or more, expended on a Social Security lawsuit are
appropriate. See, e.g., Smith v. O’Malley, No. 24-244, 2024 WL 5159280, at *3 (E.D. La. Dec. 18,
2024) (finding 45.6 hours reasonable ); Kobrock, 2024 WL 1 285242, at *2  (finding 39.6 hours
reasonable); Saucier v. Kijakazi, No. 20-2373, 2022 WL 2132281, at *1 (E.D. La. June 14, 2022)
(finding 35 hours reasonable). Accordingly, the Court will approve the expenditure of a total of 35
hours of work performed on behalf of Plaintiff.
IV. To Whom the Award is Payable
The Commissioner correctly points out in its response that an EAJA fee award is payable
to the prevailing litigant, not the attorney. See Astrue v. Ratliff, 560 U.S. 586, 589, 591-92, 599. In
line with this precedent, Plaintiff indicates in its proposed order that, “Plaintiff is the sole payee of

any EAJA payment.” However, Plaintiff requests that the payment be  sent to the Law O ffices of
Chermol and Fishman , LLC at  11450 Bustelon Avenue, Philadelphia, PA 19116. The
Commissioner does not object, and instead indicates that “[a]s a matter of practice, an EAJA fee
made payable to Plaintiff may properly be mailed to Plaintiff ’s counsel.” Rec. Doc. 19 at p. 2.
Accordingly, the Court finds that so long as the payment is  made payable directly to Ms. Cupit,
such payment may be sent to Plaintiff’s counsel.
Additionally, because the award is pay able to the litigant  and is “therefore subject to a
Government offset to satisfy a pre-existing debt that the litigant owes the United States,” Astrue,
560 U.S. at 589, the Court explicitly notes that any payment is subject to an offset to satisfy any
pre-existing debt owed by Ms. Cupit to the government.
Recommendation
 For the foregoing reasons, it is RECOMMENDED that Plaintiff’s Motion for Attorney’s
Fees Pursuant to the Equal Justice Act (Rec. Doc. 17) be GRANTED and that Plaintiff be awarded
$8,190.00 payable to  Plaintiff and mailed to her attorney David F. Chermol , Law Offices of
Chermol & Fishman, LLC at 11450 Bustleton Avenue, Philadelphia, PA 19116, with such award
subject to offset for any outstanding debt Plaintiff owes the United States.
