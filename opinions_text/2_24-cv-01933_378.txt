    I.        LEGAL STANDARD

         According to the Fifth Circuit, the purpose of a motion in limine is to prohibit

opposing counsel “from mentioning the existence of, alluding to, or offering evidence

on matters so highly prejudicial to the moving party that a timely motion to strike or

an instruction by the court to the jury to disregard the offending matter cannot

overcome its prejudicial influence on the jurors’ minds.” 24 Under Federal Rule of

Evidence 401, evidence is relevant if it has any tendency to make a fact more or less

probable than it would be without the evidence, or if the fact it seeks to prove is of

consequence in determining the action. 25 While all relevant evidence is admissible,

the Court may exclude relevant evidence if its probative value is substantially

outweighed by danger of “unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury,

undue delay, wasting time, or needlessly presenting cumulative evidence.” 26

         However, the Fifth Circuit has held that, “[t]he exclusion of evidence under

Rule 403 should occur only sparingly.” 27 “An order in limine excludes only clearly

inadmissible evidence; therefore evidence should not be excluded before trial unless

it is clearly inadmissible on all potential grounds.” 28 Instead, “[i]f the evidence is not

    II.       ANALYSIS

          In the instant Motion, Plaintiffs seeks enforcement of the parties’ Joint Exhibit

reservations in the Revised Pretrial Order, and preclusion of admitted exhibits from

“being represented as establishing disputed execution, authorization, attribution,

receipt, understanding, actual rental use, knowledge of the disputed transaction or

terms at the relevant time, adoption, confirmation, or ratification without an

appropriate evidentiary basis for the particular proposition asserted.” 30

    i.        Requests by Plaintiff to Limit Exhibits

          Plaintiffs seek to limit the use of several other categories of evidence. Plaintiffs

argue that “joint exhibit status does not convert disputed facts into stipulations;” 31

“authentication or business record status does not, without more, establish

attribution to plaintiffs;” 32 rental value, underwriting, and commercial loan records

should not be used as direct proof of actual rental use, intent, or receipt of rent

without foundation;” 33 and “later refinance and electronic signature records do not by

themselves establish knowledge or ratification of the 2020 transaction.” 34

          Defendants argue that the use of Motion in Limine to categorically exclude

otherwise admissible evidence in advance of trial is improper. 35 Further, they argue
