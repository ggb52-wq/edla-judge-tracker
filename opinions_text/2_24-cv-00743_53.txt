Pending before me is a Motion to Enforce Compromise filed by Plaintiffs Michael J. Berry
and Cheryl Berry.  ECF No. 49.  Defendant Ross Erstling filed an Opposition Memorandum.  ECF
No. 52.  Plaintiffs seek to enforce or an oral agreement to settle, and Defendant contends  the
agreement is not enforceable because it was neither written nor recited on the court record.  The
Honorable Wendy B. Vitter referred Plaintiffs’ Motion to Enforce to the undersigned magistrate
judge pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 636.  ECF No. 51.1
  Although the parties dispute the legal issue regarding the enforceability of the settlement
agreement, they have no fact dispute necessitating an evidentiary hearing.  No party requested oral
argument under Local Rule 78.1, and the Court agrees that oral argument is unnecessary.
Considering the record, the submissions, and applicable law, and for the following reasons,
the undersigned RECOMMENDS that the Motion to Enforce Compromise be GRANTED.

I. BACKGROUND
Plaintiffs Michael J. Berry and Cheryl Berry filed a petition for damages in state court
against Defendants Titan Solar Power LA, Inc., Goodleap, LLC, Solar Match Florida, LLC and
Ross Erstling, alleging violations of the Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. § 1681b), Truth in
Lending Act (15 U.S.C. § 1638), the Louisiana Unfair Trade Practices Act (L A. R EV. S TAT.
§ 51:1405), and a variety of state law claims arising out of installation of solar power cells on their
home.  ECF No. 1-1 ¶¶ 25-45.  Defendant Goodleap removed the case based on federal question
jurisdiction, with supplemental jurisdiction over the state law claims.  ECF No. 1 ¶¶ 8 -13.  The
removal did not invoke diversity of citizenship or even specify the parties’ citizenships.  See
generally ECF No. 1.
The undersigned held a settlement conference with all parties on November 12, 2024.  ECF
No. 36.  Discussions were productive , but the parties needed additional time to assess certain
proposed settlement terms.  Id.  The undersigned resumed settlement discussions with the parties
on November 13, 2024, during which terms were proposed and Plaintiffs requested time to assess
the implications of the proposed terms.  ECF No. 37.  The parties continued settlement discussions,
and later reached an agreement as reflected via email.  ECF Nos. 49-2.  Defendant Erstling initially
agreed to pay $5,000, with $2,000 paid at execution and $500 per month for  the next six months,
but later requested that he be allowed to make a single lump sum of $4,000, which Plaintiffs
accepted.  ECF Nos. 49-3, 49-4, 49-5, 49-6, 49-7, 49-8.  After Plaintiffs filed a notice of settlement,
the Court issued a conditional order of dismissal, retaining jurisdiction to enforce the compromised
agreed upon by the parties.  ECF Nos. 41, 42.
Plaintiffs requested a status conference with the undersigned after Defendant Erstling failed
to execute the settlement agreement  and make any payment .  ECF No. 44.  Plaintiffs alleged

II. APPLICABLE LAW AND ANALYSIS
A. This Court Retains the Authority to Enforce the Settlement Agreement
A court has inherent power to recognize, encourage, and when necessary, enforce
settlement agreements reached by the parties when the court specifically retains jurisdiction to

enforce the settlement in the order of dismissal. 2  The court ’s dismissal order in this case
specifically retained jurisdiction to enforce the parties’ settlement agreement.  ECF No. 42.
The court may summarily enforce a settlement agreement if no material facts are in dispute,
but when material facts are in dispute, an evidentiary hearing is required. 3  Whether there is a
disputed issue  of material fact as to the validity of the settlement agreement is similar to the
standard the court relies upon in ruling on a motion for summary judgment.4
In this case, the parties do not dispute any of the material facts.  The only dispute is whether
or not, based on the undisputed material facts, the parties’ settlement agreement is enforceable.
B. F ederal, Not State, Law Governs the Enforceability of the Settlement Agreement
Although the assumption was that this case is a diversity case and thus Louisiana law
applied to determine the enforceability of the settlement agreement, Defendant Goodleap actually
removed this case based on federal question jurisdiction, not diversity , citing to Plaintiffs’ claims
under the Fair Credit Reporting Act and Truth in Lending Act.  ECF No. 1 ¶ 11; see ECF No. 1-1
¶¶ 25-34.  This case is thus before the Court based on federal question jurisdiction.
“Questions regarding the enforceability or validity of [settlement] agreements are
determined by federal law . . . where the substantive rights and liabilities of the parties derive from
federal law.” 5  Federal law likewise applies to determine the validity and enforceability of a

C. T he Requirements to Enforce a Settlement Under Federal Law
Federal law governing settlement agreements applies the general common law principles
of contracts. 7  To form a settlement agreement, there must be an offer, an acceptance,
consideration, the inclusion of all material or essential terms, and a meeting of the minds among
the parties.8  An offer and acceptance “is judged by the parties’ overt acts and words, not by the
subjective or secret intent of the [parties].” 9  For there to have been a meeting of the minds, the
parties must have “knowingly and voluntarily” entered into the agreement.10
Unlike Louisiana law, federal law even allows oral settlement agreements.11  Thus, as long
as there is an offer, acceptance, consideration, the inclusion of all material or essential terms, and
a meeting of the minds among the parties, the federal requirements are met and the agreement need
not be reduced to writing.12  When a party in a case governed by federal law authorizes a settlement
and then changes his mind when presented with the settlement documents, that party remains
bound by the terms of the agreement.13
