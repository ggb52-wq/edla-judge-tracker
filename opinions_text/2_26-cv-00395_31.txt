II. LAW & ANALYSIS
 This motion addresses the relationship between the saving -to-suitors clause and the
Shipowners’ Limitation of Liability Act  (the “Limitation Act”).  Under the Limitation Act, “the
liability of the owner of a vessel for any claim, debt, or liability described in subsection (b) shall
not exceed the value of the vessel and pending freight.”  46 U.S.C. § 30523(a).  Claims subject to
limitation include “any loss, damage, or injury by collision, or any act, matter, or thing, loss,
damage, or forfeiture, done, occasioned, or incurred, without privity or knowledge of the owner.”
Id. § 30523(b).  When a shipowner properly files a limitation action in federal court, “the limitation
court stays all related claims against the shipowner pending in any forum,” and all claimants must
file their claims with the limitation court.   In re Magnolia Marine Transp. Co. , 964 F.2d 1571,
1575 (5th Cir. 1992) ; Fed. R. Civ. P. Supp. Rule F(3).  The federal court maintains exclusive
jurisdiction to determine whether and to what extent a shipowner is entitled to limitation under the
Act.  In re Magnolia Marine Transp. , 964 F.2d at 1575.  But this exclusivity in jurisdiction  is
limited by the “saving-to-suitors” clause of 28 U.S.C. § 1333.  Odeco Oil & Gas Co. v. Bonnette,
74 F.3d 671, 674 (5th Cir. 1996).
The saving-to-suitors clause reserves a claimant’s right to file his claim in a state or federal
forum “so long as the vessel owner’s right to seek limitation of liability is protected .”  Lewis v.
Lewis & Clark Marine, Inc., 531 U.S. 438, 454-55 (2001); see 28 U.S.C. § 1333(1).  The “clause
evinces a preference for jury trials and common law remedies in the forum of the claimant ’s
choice.”  Odeco Oil & Gas, 74 F.3d at 674.  Therefore, in limitation proceedings, the Fifth Circuit

has held that “a district court must allow a [claimant’s] state court action to proceed” outside of
the limitation action, “when all claimants stipulate that the federal court has exclusive jurisdiction
over the limitation proceeding, and that the claimants will not seek to enforce a damage award
greater than the value of the ship and its freight until the shipowner ’s right to limitation has been
determined by the federal court. ”  Odeco Oil & Gas , 74 F.3d at 674 (first emphasis added).
Because Claimants assert state-law tort claims, their claims are cognizable in both admiralty and
law.  Claimants may pursue their claims outside the instant limitation action if the  amended
limitation stipulations comply with “the requirements set forth in Odeco Oil” to protect MARS’
rights under the Limitation Act.   See In re N&W Marine Towing, L.L.C. , 31 F.4th 968, 972 (5th
Cir. 2022).
The Court finds that they do.  Claimants acknowledge that “Draco LaFleur is a claimant.”29
As such, and because no other parties have filed claims against MARS in this action, all claimants
have joined the amended limitation stipulations.30  The amended limitation stipulations also clearly
state that this Court has exclusive jurisdiction regarding all issues related to the instant limitation
action and that, because of Claimants’ waiver of res judicata, this Court will not be “bound by any
other court’s findings.” 31  And, finally, they provide that Claimants will not seek to enforce a
finding by “any court or forum of any type” – including liability – arising from the August 24,
2025 incident and exceeding the limitation fund until after this Court determines MARS’ right to
limitation.32  Because the amended limitation stipulations meet the requirements set out in Odeco
Oil, the Court finds that they adequately protect MARS’ rights under the Limitation Act .
Therefore, Claimants’ state-court action must be allowed to proceed.

III. CONCLUSION
Accordingly, for the foregoing reasons,
IT IS ORDERED that Claimants’ motion to dissolve injunction and permit prosecution of
state court action upon filing of limitation stipulations (R. Doc. 11) is GRANTED.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that in no event may Claimants seek to enforce any judgment
or recovery insofar as it may expose limitation plaintiff MARS to liability in excess of the value
of the vessel known as the M.A.R.S. Loki and her freight, pending adjudication of the verified
complaint for exoneration from or limitation of liability in this Court.
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that the Clerk of Court shall administratively close this
limitation of liability action without prejudice to the right of any party to file a motion to reopen
the proceedings at such time, if any, it shall become necessary for this Court to determine issues
relevant to limitation and/or exoneration.  This order shall not be considered a dismissal or
disposition of this matter, and should further proceedings become necessary or desirable, any party
may file such a motion to reopen the case.
