REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION
Before the Court is Plaintiff Nathan Durapau’s Motion for Leave to File a First
Amended Complaint (R. Doc. 36). Plaintiff also filed a reply memorandum in support
of his motion.1 While Plaintiff anticipated his motion would be opposed, no defendant
filed an opposition. 2 Because the undersigned finds both good cause to extend the
deadline and that justice requires allowing the amendment, the undersigned
recommends that the District Court modify its scheduling order and grant Plaintiff
leave to amend his complaint.
The deadline set by the District Court for amendments to pleadings passed on
August 8, 2024.3 When, as here, a party’s request to amend pleadings is beyond the
scheduling order deadline, the moving party must establish good cause to extend that
deadline under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 16(b)(4). S&W Enterprises, L.L.C. v.
SouthTrust Bank of Alabama, NA, 315 F.3d 533, 535 (5th Cir. 2003). “Only after the
Court finds that the good cause standard is satisfied under Rule 16(b) will the ‘more

Rule 15(a)(2) directs that courts “should freely give leave [to amend a
complaint] when justice so requires.”  Because the language of this rule “‘ evinces a
bias in favor of granting leave to amend[,]” the United States Court of Appeals for the
Fifth Circuit has instructed that a “district court must possess a ‘substantial reason’
to deny a request for leave to amend.”  Smith v. EMC Corp ., 393 F.3d 590, 595 (5th
Cir. 2004) (quoting Lyn–Lea Travel Corp. v. Am. Airlines, 283 F.3d 282, 286 (5th Cir.
2002)). Courts must consider five factors in determining whether such a substantial
reason is present: “1) undue delay, 2) bad faith or dilatory motive, 3) repeated failure
to cure deficiencies by previous amendments, 4) undue prejudice to the opposing
party, and 5) futility of the amendment. ” Id. (citing Rosenzweig v. Azurix Corp., 332
F.3d 854, 864 (5th Cir.  2003)); see also Foman v. Davis , 371 U.S. 178, 182 (1962)).

“Absent any of these factors, the leave sought should be ‘ freely given.’” Id. (quoting
Foman, 371 U.S. at 182)).
Plaintiff’s delay in moving to amend his complaint was not undue, and there is
no indication the amendment would  prejudice any defendant. Nor is there any
indication of bad faith or dilatory motive. Plaintiff has not been afforded a previous
opportunity to amend his complaint.  And the amendment, which would dismiss from
this action one uninvolved defendant and name two allegedly involved defendants, is
not futile. Thus, no substantial reason supports denying Plaintiff’s unopposed request
for leave to amend his complaint. Plaintiff’s motion should be granted. Accordingly,
IT IS RECOMMENDED that the District Court’s modify its scheduling order,
that Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to File a First Amended Complaint (R. Doc. 36) be
GRANTED, and that Plaintiff’s proposed First Amended Complaint 10 be filed into
the record.
