III. DISCUSSION
The Court finds that bifurcating the issues here achieves the purposes
of Rule 42(b).  The notice issues ar e a threshold matter: if there was not
sufficient notice, the parties and the Court will not need to engage in fact-
intensive discovery and trial of damages to the 50 properties at issue.  And
the parties have stated that the notice issues can be resolved collectively for
all of the claimed losses, without a sepa rate analysis as to each property. 18
Thus, bifurcation would “expedite and economize” the proceedings.  Fed. R.
Civ. P. 42(b).  Further, bifurcation wi ll not prejudice the parties.  As the
parties have confirmed, the notice issues are distinct and separable from the
issues concerning the damage to the properties, and the parties have waived
their right to a jury trial on the notice issues.  19  Accordingly, bifurcation is
proper here under Rule 42(b).
