Here, these factors weigh in favor of a finding of excusable neglect.
On the first factor, there is little da nger of prejudice to defendants in

allege prejudice insofar as the delay wi ll force them to stand trial or face
discovery burdens. 24  The brief delay in allo wing plaintiff to oppose

risk of prejudice to defendants.  Thus, the first factor weighs in favor of
granting leave.

C. Motion for Relief Under Rule 56(d)
Third, plaintiff seeks relief under Rule 56(d).  Rule 56(d) provides that,
if “a nonmovant shows by affidavit or declaration that, for specified reasons,
it cannot present facts e ssential to justify its o pposition, the court may:
(1) defer considering the motion or deny it; (2) allow time to obtain affidavits
or declarations or to take discover y; or (3) issue any other appropriate
order.”  Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(d).
The Fifth Circuit has held that a party is entitled to relief under Rule
56(d) only if they “(1) requested extended discovery prior to the court’s ruling
on summary judgment; (2) placed the di strict court on notice that further
discovery pertaining to the summary judgment motion was being sought;
and (3) demonstrated to the district court with reasonable specificity how the
requested discovery pertained to the pending motion.”  Enplanar, Inc. v.
Marsh, 11 F.3d 1284, 1291 (5th Cir. 1994).   As to the third requirement, the
