II. ANALYSIS
A. Rule 12(c) Review Standard
 “Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(c) permits any party to move for a
judgment on the pleadings, provided the motion is made early enough to avoid
delaying trial.”  NAZ, LLC v. Philips Healthcare, a Div ision of Philips Electronics
North America Corp., No. CV 17-2882, 2018 WL 1202570, at *5 (E.D. La. Mar. 8,
2018) (citing Great Plains Trust Co. v. Morgan Stanley Dean Witter & Co., 313 F.3d
305, 312 (5 th Cir. 2002)).  “A court may grant a Rule 12(c) motion only if the
pleadings evince no disputes of genuine material fact and questions of law alone
remain.”  Id. (citing id.). “A motion brought pursuant to Fed.  R. Civ. P. 12(c) is
designed to dispose of cases where the material facts are not in dispute and a
judgment on the merits can be rendered by looking to the substance of the pleadings

B. Attachments Supplied by DOW and Union Carbide
DOW and Union Carbide attach to the ir motions excerpts from the
“Agreement For Services” between PSC and DOW dated November 22, 2021.  They
also attach a “Hold Harmless-Responsibility-Insurance Contract”, entered between
PSC and DOW dated October 28, 2021.  No one has disputed that the parties were
operating pursuant to the service agreement at the time of the April 25, 2025 incident.
Dillon does not attach or incorporate by reference either the services agreement or
the hold harmless agreement in his complaint.  Compare Craft v. Max Access LLC,
No. 6:22-cv-5899, 2023 WL 5029056, at *4 (W.D. La. Aug. 7, 2023) (allowing for
the consideration of a master services agreement due to the complaint’s direct
referral to the agreement)  with Rampart Allstars, LLC v. Sonder USA Inc. , No. 24-
cv-1767, 2024 WL 4818273, at *3 (E.D. La. Nov. 18, 2024) (denying a motion to
dismiss but noting that “ at least some of [defendant]’s arguments appear to have

The documents attached to defendants’ motions may be dispositive of the
“statutory” employer issue .  Consequently, pursuant to Rule 12(d), the court will
treat this matter as one for summary judgment under Rule 56.  Though not required
to provide explicit notice to the parties  when converting to  a Rule 56 motion , the
court permits Dillon, DOW, and Union Carbide to supply materials they deem
necessary for the court’s consideration of the motions.  See Hodge v. Engleman, 90
F.4th 840, 845 (5th Cir. 2024) (“A court need not advise either party of its intention
to convert the motion … The only requirement is that the non-moving party have at
least ten days in which to submit its own evidence  … That ten-day period begins
running when the non-movant is first put on notice that, based on its acceptance of
evidence outside the pleadings, the court could convert the Rule 12(b)(6) motion
into a summary judgment.”) (internal quotation marks and citations omitted).
