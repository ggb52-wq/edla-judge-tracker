I. LEGAL STANDARD
Rule 16(b) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provides that “[a]
scheduling order may be modified only for good cause and with the judge’s
consent.”  Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(b)(4).  The “good cause standard requires the
party seeking relief to show that the deadlines cannot reasonably be met
despite the diligence of the pa rty needing the extension.”  S&W Enters.,
L.L.C. v. SouthTrust Bank, NA , 315 F.3d 533, 535 (5th  Cir. 2003) (citation
modified).  Whether to grant or deny  a continuance is within the sound
discretion of the trial court.  United States v. Alix, 86 F.3d 429, 434 (5th Cir.

II. DISCUSSION
In his motion to continue, plainti ff argues that his medical condition
will not be sufficiently evaluated and treat ed prior to trial.  As a result, the
extent of damages will not be known by the time of trial, and expert witnesses
will not be able to fully evaluate plai ntiff’s medical condition and level of
disability.2  Plaintiff states that his medica l provider anticipates that he will
reach maximum medical improvement within the next six to sixteen
months.3

continuance of trial.  Because plaintiff’s medical condition will be uncertain
at the time of trial, the parties cannot reasonably meet the deadlines in this
case, despite their diligence.  The Cou rt therefore grants the motion to
