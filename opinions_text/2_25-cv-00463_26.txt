ORDER AND REASONS

Before the Court is the Report and Recommendation of United States Magistrate Judge
Michael B. North.1  The Report and Recommendation addresses three motions that were filed in
the above-captioned matter: [1] the Motion to Dismiss Pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6) for
Failure to State a Claim and Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(1) for Lack of Subject Matter Jurisdiction filed
by Defendants Michael Tregre, Sheriff of St. John the Baptist Parish, Deputy Lionel Luchion, and
Deputy Daniel Reed;2 [2] the Motion to Dismiss Pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6) for Failure
to State a Claim filed by Defendant Deputy Bobby Bradberry;3 and [3] the Motion for Plaintiff to
Reply to Defendants’ Answer Pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 7(a) filed by Defendants Deputy Lionel
Luchion, and Deputy Daniel Reed.4
Pro se  Plaintiff Tyrone James Davis, III filed this action against Sheriff Treg re in his
official capacity and Deputies Bradberry, Luchion, and Reed (“Deputy Defendants”) in their
individual and official capacities. 5 Davis brings claims against the  Deputy Defendants under 42
U.S.C. § 1983 based on allegations that the Deputy Defendants violated Davis’s Fourth
Amendment rights by subjecting Davis to excessive force, unlawful search and seizure, and

Davis filed an Objection to the Report and Recommendation,8 and Defendants filed a Reply
to Davis’s Objection. 9  Davis’s Objection begins with a section titled “Partial Agreement and
Intent to Amend,” wherein Davis states that he partially agrees with certain recommendations by
the Magistrate Judge and intends to file an amended complaint to (1) expand upon the Monell
claim with greater specificity regarding the pattern of unconstitutional conduct, including a newly
documented second incident involving Davis and other officers, resulting again in dismissed
charges under similar circumstances, and (2) refine the false arrest allegations with additional
details, legal citations, and factual assertions supported by newly obtained evidence, including full
dash cam and body cam footage. Davis then proceeds to make several objections to the Report and
Recommendation.
First, Davis argues that the Magistrate Judge erred in dismissing his false arrest claim
because the Magistrate Judge misapplied the law in finding that there was probable cause to arrest
Davis. Relying on the video footage from Davis’s cellphone, the Magistrate Judge concluded there
was probable cause to arrest Davis for violating La. R.S. § 14:108(B)(1)(f), which provides, in
relevant part, that a person commits the offense of resisting an officer by “[f]ail[ing] to provide or
display the person’s state issued driver’s license or identification on the officer’s request when the
person is an operator of a motor vehicle, the person has been lawfully detained for an alleged
violation of a law, and the officer has exhausted all resources at his disposal to verify the identity

of the person.”10 Davis contends the statute requires him to “provide or display” his identification,
not both, and Davis argues that because the video recording shows that he displayed his
identification, he complied with the law and, thus, there was no probable cause to arrest him.
Davis’s argument fails because Davis ignores the clause in the statute that says “on the officer’s
request,” and the video footage from Davis’s cell phone shows that Deputy Bradberry did not
merely request Davis to display his identification; he asked Davis, repeatedly, to physically
provide it to him. By failing to do so, Davis gave the officers probable cause to arrest him for
violating La. R.S. § 14:108(B)(1)(f).
Next, Davis argues that newly obtained “dash cam and body cam” footage, which he has
attached to his Objection, confirm there was no probable cause to arrest him. But the motions that
are currently pending and presently before the Court challenge the sufficiency of the claims
pleaded in Davis’s Complaint. In considering a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim, the
Court is limited to the contents of the Complaint and the attachments thereto. 11 Thus, the Court
may not consider this new video evidence  in determining, at this stage and based on the current
Complaint, whether Davis has adequately pleaded a claim for false arrest. Nevertheless, Davis
may do as he has stated he intends to and file an amended complaint to refine his false arrest
allegations with additional details based on this newly obtained evidence.12
Next, Davis argues that the Magistrate Judge erred because there is no qualified immunity
for knowingly unlawful arrests and use of excessive force. But the Magistrate Judge’s Report and
Recommendation does not contain any finding or recommendation regarding qualified

Next, Davis argues th at th e Magistrate Judge recommended dismissal of the Monell

Lastly, Davis asks the Court to supplement the record with the newly obtained body cam
and dash cam footage pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 72(b)(3). Under this Rule, in resolving objections

But on a Rule 12(b)(6) motion, the Court’s review is generally limited to the pleadings and the
attachments thereto. 15 Because this new evidence was not attached to Davis’s pleading (his
Complaint), and because the evidence does not fall within any other exception upon which the
Court could consider evidence outside of the Complaint at this stage,16 the Court may not receive

For these reasons ,  Davis’s objections to the Magistrate Judge’s Report and
Recommendation are overruled. The Court will adopt the Magistrate Judge’s recommended
disposition, and dismiss with prejudice Davis’s claims against the Deputy Defendants in their
official capacities and for violations of his right to due process under the Fourteenth Amendment.
The Court will dismiss without prejudice the following claims: Davis’s claims against the Deputy
Defendants for false arrest under the Fourth Amendment ; Davis’s claims against the Deputy
Defendants for unlawful search and seizure under the Fourth Amendment; Davis’s claim against
Sheriff Tregre under Monell; and Davis’s claim for injunctive relief.  Davis will be granted 30
days, or until September 30, 2026, to file an amended complaint to cure the insufficiencies in his
pleading as to these claims that are being dismissed without prejudice. Failure to file an amended
complaint by the deadline will result in dismissal with prejudice of these claims. Davis’s claims
against the Deputy Defendants for excessive force under the Fourth Amendment and his claim
against all Defendants for intentional infliction of emotional distress remain pending at this time.
