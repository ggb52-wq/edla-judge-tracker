II. APPLICABLE LAW
A. HIDTA and the ONDCP
 HIDTA is a federal program organized under the Office of National Drug Control Policy
(“ONDCP”).  21 U.S.C. § 1706(a)(1).  The High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area (HIDTA)
program was created by Congress through the Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1988 for the purpose of
coordinating and assisting federal, state, local, and tribal law enforcement areas to address regional
drug threats in an effort to reduce drug trafficking and production in the United States.8  HIDTA is
administered by ONDCP, a component of the Executive Office of the President  tasked with
“lead[ing] the national drug control effort.”9  “HIDTA is not a federal agency or organization itself,
but rather a coordination umbrella that enables  . . . law enforcement agencies to combine and
leverage resources and capabilities to address drug -trafficking and drug related crime.” 10
Accordingly, “HIDTAs and their Executive Boards are not considered legal entities under Federal
law”11 and there is no agency relationship between individual high intensity drug trafficking areas
and the federal government.  21 U.S.C. § 1706(e)(4).  And as the Fifth Circuit recently held in

D. Failure to State a Claim
To survive a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss, a plaintiff must plead enough facts “to state
a claim for relief that is plausible on its face.”37  A claim is “plausible on its face” when the pleaded
facts allow the court to “draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the
misconduct alleged.”38  A court must accept the complaint’s factual allegations as true and must
“draw all reasonable inferences in the plaintiff's favor.”39  The court need not, however, accept as
true legal conclusions couched as factual allegations.40  To be legally sufficient, a complaint must
establish more than a “sheer possibility” the plaintiff’s claims are true.41  If it is apparent from the
face of the complaint that an insurmountable bar to relief exists, and the plaintiff is not entitled to
relief, the court must dismiss the claim. 42  The court’s review is limited to the complaint and any
documents attached to the motion to dismiss that are central to the claim and referenced by the
complaint.43

III. ANALYSIS
A. HIDTA
 The ONDCP’s HIDTA program  is not an agency, 44 nor is it a “ legal entity capable of
employing individuals” or being sued.45 Therefore, any claims against HIDTA must be dismissed
for failure to state a clai m.  Even if Plaintiff could assert a claim against ONDCP’s HIDTA
program, the United States’ waiver of sovereign immunity for claims asserted under the FTCA
“only applies against Government employees of federal agencies.” 46  Thus, the claim would be
barred by sovereign immunity.
B. The ONDCP

3. Claims under Title II of the ADA and § 504 of the Rehabilitation Act
 Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act, like § 504 of the Rehabilitation Act,
proscribes exclusion of an individual with a disability, by reason of such disability, from
participation in or denial of the benefits of the services, programs, or activities of a public entity,
or from being subjected to discrimination by any such entity. 42 U.S.C. § 12132; 29 U.S.C. § 794.
Both statutes are enforceable through an implied private right of action.64  “ The ADA and the
Rehabilitation Act generally are interpreted in pari materia.”65
 A plaintiff states a claim for relief under Title II of the ADA or § 504 of the Rehabilitation
Act, which prohibit discrimination in services, programs, or activities of a public entity, if he
alleges: (1) that he has a qualifying disability; (2) that he is being denied the benefits of services,
programs, or activities for which the public entity is responsible, or is otherwise discriminated
against by the public entity; and (3) that such discrimination is by reason of his disability.66
 Title II specifically proscribes “public entities” from engaging in disability discrimination.
As defined in 42 U.S.C. § 12131, the term “public entity” means (a) any State or local government;
(b) any department, agency, special purpose district, or other instrumentality of a State or States or
local government; and (c) the National Railroad Passenger Corporation, and any other commuter
