Pro se Plaintiff Menette Adams Hambrick filed a Motion for Leave to File
Second Amended Complaint (R. Doc. 20). Defendan ts do not oppose the Motion. The
Motion clarifies the roles of the two defendants and expands Plaintiff’s factual
allegations.1 Having considered the Motion , the record, and the applicable law, the
Motion (R. Doc. 20) is GRANTED.
Plaintiff filed the instant action on August 11, 2026; Defendants removed it to
federal court on September 22, 2026. 2 Plaintiff pursues state and federal claims to
include breach of duty of care, public accommodation discrimination, and disability
discrimination against “Walgreen Co.” and “Walgreens Louisiana Co. Inc.”3 Plaintiff
alleges that on or about June 4, 2025, Defendants’ pharmacist filled her pain
medication prescription but warned Plaintiff that she would not fill future
prescriptions unless Plaintiff obtained a Louisiana driver’s license. 4 Plaintiff alleges
that on August 26, 2025, Defendants’ pharmacist refused to dispense controlled pain

Under Rule 15(a)(2), if more than 21 days have passed after service of a
responsive pleading, then the party may amend its pleading only with the opposing
party’s written consent or the court’s leave. The court “should freely give leave when
justice so requires.”8 The language of Rule 15(a)(2) “evinces a bias in favor of granting
leave to amend.”  Chitimacha Tribe of Louisiana v. Harry L.L. Co.,  690 F.2d 1157,
1163 (5th Cir. 1982);  see also Mayeaux v. Louisiana Health Serv. & Indem. Co. , 376
F.3d 420, 425 (5th Cir. 2004). The Fifth Circuit has instructed that the “district court
must possess a ‘substantial reason’ to deny a request for leave to amend.”  Smith v.
EMC Corp., 393 F.3d 590, 595 (5th Cir. 2004). Although leave to amend is to be freely
given under Rule 15(a)(2) , “that generous standard is tempered by the necessary
power of a district court to manage a case.” Yumilicious Franchise, LLC v. Barrie, 819
F.3d 170, 177 (5th Cir. 2016).  Relevant factors to consider include “undue delay, bad
faith or dilatory motive on the part of the movant, repeated failures to cure

Each of the Rule 15 factors weighs in favor of amendment. The parties are in
the early stages of litigation. The proposed pleading clarifies the separate roles of the
defending parties, and Defendants do not object to the proposed amendment. Nor is
there any indication that amendment would be unduly prejudicial or futile. The
amended complaint was filed approximately two weeks after removal from state
court. The undersigned finds no undue delay.
For the reasons set forth above,
