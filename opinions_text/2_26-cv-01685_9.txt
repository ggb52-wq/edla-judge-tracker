II.  LAW AND ANALYSIS
A. Removal Generally
“It is axiomatic that the federal courts have limited subject matter jurisdiction
and cannot entertain cases unless authorized by the Constitution and legislation.”
Coury v. Prot, 85 F.3d 244, 248 (5th Cir.1996). A defendant “may remove a civil action
brought in state court to federal court if the action is within a federal court’s original
jurisdiction.” Pace v. Cirrus Design Corp., 93 F.4th 879, 888 (5th Cir. 2024) (citing 28
U.S.C. § 1441(a)). “A federal district court has subject matter jurisdiction over a state
claim when the amount in controversy is met and there is complete diversity of
citizenship between the parties.” Mumfrey v. CVS Pharmacy, Inc., 719 F.3d 392, 397
(5th Cir. 2013) (citing 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)).

“Because removal raises significant federalism concerns, the removal statute
is strictly construed and any doubt as to the propriety of removal should be resolved
in favor of remand.” Gutierrez v. Flores, 543 F.3d 248, 251 (5th Cir.  2008) (internal
quotation and citation omitted). The removing party bears the burden of establishing
“that federal jurisdiction exists and that removal was proper.” Manguno v. Prudential
Prop. & Cas. Ins. Co. , 276 F.3d 720, 723 (5th Cir. 2002)  (citations omitted) .
Accordingly, a federal court must remand a case back to state court (1) if there was a
procedural defect rendering removal improper and the moving party raises the defect
within thirty days of removal or (2) if the court lacks subject matter jurisdiction. 28
U.S.C. § 144 7; see also BEPCO, L.P. v. Santa Fe Minerals, Inc. , 675 F.3d 466, 470
(5th Cir. 2012).
B. Removal Procedure
28 U.S.C. § 1446 governs removal procedure , setting forth various
requirements such as timing, deadlines, notice, and consent. The removing defendant
generally must file its notice of removal within 30 days of service. 28 U.S.C. § 1446(a)-
(b)(1). In cases that are not initially removable, the 30-day period begins to run “after
receipt by the defendant, through service or otherwise , of a copy of an amended
pleading, motion, order or other paper from which it may first be ascertained that the
case is one which is or has become removable.” 28 U.S.C. § 1446(b)(3).
Section 1447(c) provides that “[a] motion to remand the case on the basis of
any defect other than lack of subject matter jurisdiction must be made within 30 days
after the filing of the notice of removal under section 1446(a).” The Johnsons’ motion

1. The Rule of Unanimity
Where a removed case has multiple defendants , the rule of unanimity is
triggered: “all defendants who have been properly joined and served must join in or
consent to the removal of the action.” See 28 U.S.C. § 1446(b)(2)(A).9 This rule of
unanimity requires that , within 30 days of service of the state court petition on a
defendant, each served defendant  must either sign the notice of removal , or “there
must be some timely filed written indication from each served defendant, or from
some person or entity purporting to formally act on its behalf in this respect and to
have authority to do so, that it has actually consented to such action.” See Getty Oil
Corp. v. Ins. Co. of N. Am., 841 F.2d 1254, 1262-63 n.11, 12 (5th Cir. 1988); Powers v.
United States, 783 F.3d 570, 576 (5th Cir. 2015) (citing Getty Oil Corp., Div. of Texaco.
Inc. v. Ins. Co. of N. Am. , 841 F.2d 1254, 1262 n. 11 (5th Cir. 1998))  (“The rule of
unanimity requires that all defendants to an action either sign the original petition
for removal or timely file written consent to the removal.”).
In the Fifth Circuit, a  mere allegation by the removing defendant that its co -
defendant consents to removal is insufficient to indicate unanimity or withstand a
motion to remand. See, e.g., Baker v. Amazon Logistics, Inc. , No. 23-2078, 2023 WL

2. The One-Year Bar for Diversity-Jurisdiction-Based Removal
“[T]he defendant in a diversity case has one year following the commencement
of an action to remove it.” Hoyt v. Lane Construction Corp. , 927 F.3d 287, 292 (5th
Cir. 2019) (citing 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)). The single “exception to this time bar” is where
“the district court finds that the plaintiff has acted in bad faith in order to prevent a
defendant from removing the action.” Id. A plaintiff's bad faith can be evidenced by
his “deliberate[ ] fail[ure] to disclose the actual amount in controversy to prevent
removal.” 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1)(B). “Ultimately, the burden of showing that plaintiff[
] acted in bad faith to prevent removal lies with the removing defendant.” Jones v.
Ramos Trinidad , 380 F. Supp. 3d 516, 521 ( E.D. La.  2019) (Brown, J.) (citations
omitted).
Plaintiffs move to remand because removal was effected after the one-year bar:
Plaintiffs filed their lawsuit on May 15, 2025 but Southeastern Freight did not
remove it until July 31, 2026, more than two months beyond the one-year removal
bar. In its notice of removal, Southeastern Freight alleges that Plaintiffs acted in bad
faith to prevent timely removal. Having failed to respond to the motion to remand,
however, Southeastern Freight fails to carry its burden sufficient for this Court to
find that Plaintiffs acted in bad faith to prevent removal.
Southeastern Freight ’s allegations regarding bad faith  fail to contend with
Plaintiffs’ arguments advanced in their motion to remand. For their part, Plaintiffs
indicate that they encountered issues with service by the sheriff during the service
timeframe, including a ransomware cyberattack that shut down the Orleans Parish

Sheriff’s Office. Furthermore, Plaintiffs contend that Southeastern Freight’s removal

III.  CONCLUSION
 In removing the Johnsons’ lawsuit, Southeastern Freight failed to comply with
the rule of unanimity and effected removal  after the one -year deadline, without
establishing that Plaintiffs acted in bad faith to prevent timely removal. For either of
these independent reasons, removal was procedurally defective. Accordingly, mindful
that “any doubt as to the propriety of removal should be resolved in favor of remand,”
Gutierrez v. Flores, 543 F.3d 248, 251 (5th Cir. 2008) (citation omitted),
 IT IS ORDERED  that Plaintiffs’ unopposed motion13 to remand is
GRANTED . This case is REMANDED  to the Civil District Court for the Parish
of Orleans, State of Louisiana, under 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c).
