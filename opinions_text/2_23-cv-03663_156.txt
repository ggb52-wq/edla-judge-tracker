II. STANDARD OF LAW
Rule 54(b) provides that “[w]hen an action presents more than one claim for
relief . . . or when multiple parties are involved, the court may direct entry of a final
judgment as to one or more, but fewer than all, claims or parties only if the court
expressly determines that there is no just reason for delay.” FED. R. CIV. P. 54(b). This

rule “is an exception to the general rule that a final judgment is appealable only after
the adjudication of the rights and liab ilities of all parties to a proceeding.” Akeem v.
Dasmen Residential, LLC, No. 19-13650, 2021 WL 4806913, at *1 (E.D. La. Oct. 14,
2021) (Ashe, J.). In order to properly certify a Rule  54(b) final judgment, two
prerequisites must be met.
First, the court must have fully disposed of “one or more . . . claims or parties.”
Eldredge v. Martin Marietta Corp., 207 F.3d 737, 740 (5th Cir. 2000) (quoting FED. R.
CIV. P. 54(b)). When a district court dismi sses with prejudice all claims against a
defendant, that defendant is no longer a party to the litigation and thus meets the
first requirement of Rule 54(b). Id. This requirement is jurisdictional. Id.
Second, the court must determine that there is no just reason for delay.
Eldredge, 207 F.3d at 740, n.2; Akeem, 2021 WL 4806913, at *1. “Rule 54(b)
judgments are not favored and should be awarded only when necessary to avoid
‘hardship or injustice through delay,’ and ‘should not be entered routinely as a
courtesy to counsel.’” Krutz v. Huntington Ingalls Inc.,  No. 20-1722, 2021 WL
5893980, at *1 (E.D. La. June 30, 2021) (Vance, J.) (quoting PYCA Indus. v. Harrison
Cnty. Waste Water Mgmt. Dist., 81 F.3d 1412, 1421 (5th Cir. 1996)). In considering a
Rule 54(b) motion, the district court must “weigh the inconvenience and costs of
piecemeal review on the one hand and the danger of denying justice by delay on the
other.” Road Sprinkler Fitters Loc. Un ion v. Cont’l Sprinkler Co., 967 F.2d 145, 148
(5th Cir. 1992) (quoting Dickinson v. Petroleum Conversion Corp., 338 U.S. 507, 511
(1950)) (internal quotations omitted). Because a district court must “take into account

III. ANALYSIS
Here, the first prerequisite is met: the Court’s Orders12 granting Rule 12(b)(6)
dismissal of the JPDA and JPCO defendants disposed entirely of these defendants as
parties in the litigation. 13 But the second prerequisite is not met. The dismissed
defendants have not adequately shown that  an immediate appeal avoids dangers of
hardship or injustice, nor have they show n that any hardship they face outweighs
judicial administrative interests in avoiding piecemeal appeals.
1. The dismissed defendants have not proven sufficient hardship or
injustice warranting Rule 54(b) certification.
The dismissed defendants complain of the burden they would endure if
required to wait and monitor the trial of Parria’s claims against the JPSO
Defendants, and then further await Parria’s decision whether to appeal the Court’s
12(b)(6) decisions.14 But the defendants’ burden of monitoring this litigation, as non-
participating outsiders, does not present the type of hardship or injustice that would
justify operation of Rule 54(b). If “wait[ing] until the end of litigation and monitor[ing]
the docket to see if an appeal is eventu ally taken” was “reason enough to grant
