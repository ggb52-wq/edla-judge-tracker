II. Standard of Law
Plaintiffs rely solely on a theory of specific personal jurisdiction.  “Specific
jurisdiction ‘focuses on the relationship among the defendant, the forum, and the
litigation.’” Seville v. Maersk Line, Ltd. , 53 F.4th 890, 895 (5th Cir.
2022) (quoting Walden v. Fiore, 571 U.S. 277, 284 (2014) ). Exercising personal
jurisdiction must be consistent with a State’s long-arm statute and the Constitution.
When, as in Louisiana, a State ’s long-arm statute is co -extensive with the limits of
the Due Process Clause of the 14 th Amendment, only the constitutional question
remains.
Determining whether the exercise of specific personal jurisdiction is
constitutional requires a three-step approach.
[Courts] consider “(1) whether the defendant has minimum contacts
with the forum [S]tate, i.e., whether it purposely directed its activities
toward the forum [S]tate or purposefully availed itself of the privileges
of conducting activities there.” Monkton Ins. Servs., Ltd. v. Ritter, 768

III. Analysis
The District Court’s ruling confirms that some jurisdictional discovery is
necessary, but the Plaintiffs’ proposal goes too far.8
A.   General Limits on Jurisdictional Discovery
Certain parameters apply to the discovery sought regardless of topic. First,
jurisdictional discovery is confined to the Product. Plaintiffs seek information about
virtually all contacts with Louisiana, 9 including those that do not relate to the
Product. This approach, however, conflates specific and general jurisdiction. See Ham
v. La Cienega Music Co., 4 F.3d 413 (5th Cir. 1993).

Second, jurisdictional discovery extends only to Grupo Navis and CPE.
Attempts to pursue discovery from other individuals or entities are inconsistent with
Plaintiffs’ position in the District Court and the procedural posture of the case.
Third, the geographic scope of the discovery is largely (although not entirely)
focused on Louisiana. Plaintiffs allege that general “knowledge of the Products’ sale
nationwide” may provide the requisite reasonable expectation of sales in Louisiana.10
Plaintiffs cite Ainsworth v. Moffett Eng’g, Ltd. , 716 F.3d 174, 179 (5th Cir. 2013) , to
support this proposition. In Ainsworth, the Fifth Circuit concluded that nationwide
sales of certain equipment used in poultry production were relevant to the
defendant’s knowledge of sales in Mississippi.  Id. To support this conclusion,
however, the Fifth Circuit highlighted that Mississippi was one of the primary states
involved in poultry production. Here, there is no contention that the market for the
Product in Louisiana is similarly special. Thus, this case perhaps is analogous to Zoch
v. Magna Seating (Germany) Gm bH, in which the F ifth Circuit rejected an attempt
to impute Texas contacts from the mere fact of nationwide distribution. 810 F. App’x.
285, 293 (5th Cir. 2020). To allow the parties to develop a record to litigate this issue,
some discovery as to nationwide sales and distribution is permissible  as set forth
below.

Fourth, a temporal limit applies to jurisdictional discovery. Plaintiffs allege
exposure from April 2023 to September 2023.11 Grupo Navis and CPE represent that
the Product was first sold in November 2022.12 The Product was recalled in October
2023.13 Perhaps marketing and distribution efforts relevant to personal jurisdiction
preceded Plaintiffs’ exposure. And perhaps events that occurred after the recall shed
some light on whether Plaintiffs’ exposure in Louisiana was foreseeable.  To account
for these possibilities, the relevant period for jurisdictional discovery is September 1,
2022, through December 31, 2023.14
Fifth, discovery requests must be propounded in a form recognized by the
Federal Rules of Civil Procedure . While the relevance analysis  herein will not be
revisited absent good cause, the parties remain free to raise relevance objections to a
particular interrogatory, for example, consistent with this ruling.
Sixth, Plaintiffs’ request to proceed with depositions is denied as premature .
Courts routinely address specific personal jurisdiction based on written discovery and
affidavits or declarations. Grupo Navis and CPE are invited to continue providing
information via declaration or affidavit. Should Grupo Navis and CPE decline this
opportunity, it is more likely that depositions will be necessary.  With these
parameters in place, the Court establishes topical limits on jurisdictional discovery.

B. Authorized Topics
Oral argument confirmed that Plaintiffs recognize their discovery is confined
to the scope urged before the District Court, subject to further refinement by the
undersigned. Thus, the topics proposed to the District Court and listed below frame
the analysis.
 1. Contracts
Plaintiffs propose discovery as to “contracts or agreements with other
Defendants or other entities involved in the manufacture, marketing, distribution,
development, sale or regulatory affairs related to the defective products.” 15 This
inquiry goes too far. Plaintiffs may seek discovery from Grupo Navis and CPE relative
to contracts or agreements with one another or other entities that relate to the
manufacture, marketing, distribution, development, and sale  of the Product  in
Louisiana16 between September 1, 2022, and December 31, 2023.
2. Organization Structure and Membership
Plaintiffs propose discovery as to the “organizational structure and membership
of CPE and Grupo Navis and in relation to the other Defendants or other entities they
owned that were involved with the defective products .”17 Confining this inquiry to
Plaintiffs’ theory of specific personal jurisdiction requires certain general limits. Thus,
