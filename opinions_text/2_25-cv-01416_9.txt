REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

Pro se plaintiff Preston Lewis has filed this civil action against Judge Nannette Jolivette
Brown, Magistrate Judge Michael B. North, Magistrate Judge Donna Phillips Currault, Judge
Darrel James Papillion, Attorney General Jeff Landry, and Orleans Parish Criminal District Court.
Lewis alleges that the defendants have violated his civil rights. He seeks declaratory judgment,
compensatory and punitive damages, and injunctive relief.
For the following reasons, it is RECOMMENDED that this lawsuit be DISMISSED for
failure to state a claim and for seeking monetary relief against a defendant who is immune from
suit under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(ii-iii).

subject to a competing motion for sanctions for abusing the judicial process. He alleges that Judge

Law and Analysis
There exists no absolute right to proceed in forma pauperis in federal civil matters; instead,
it is a privilege extended to those unable to pay filing fees when it is apparent that the claims do
not lack merit on their face. See Startii v. United States, 415 F.2d 1115, 1116 (5th Cir.1969); see
also Adepegba v. Hammons, 103 F.3d 383, 387 (5th Cir. 1996) (noting that the revocation of the
privilege of proceeding in forma pauperis is not new).
 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B) provides for summary dismissal sua sponte, should the Court
determine that a case is frivolous.  Section 1915(e)(2)(B) provides in pertinent part as follows:
(2) Notwithstanding any filing fee, or any portion thereof, that may have been paid,
the court shall dismiss the case at any time if the court determines that –
*   *   *
  (B) the action or appeal –
  (i) is frivolous or malicious;
  (ii) fails to state a claim on which relief may be granted; or
(iii) seeks monetary relief against a defendant who is immune from
such relief.

28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(i) -(iii) (emphasis added).  A claim is "frivolous" where it lacks an

complaint fails to state a claim upon which relief may be granted when it does not contain
“sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.”
Rogers v. Boatright, 709 F.3d 403, 407 (5th Cir. 2013)  (quoting Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662,

1. Claims Against Judges
“Judicial officers are entitled to absolute immunity from claims for damages arising out of
acts performed in the exercise of their judicial functions.” Boyd v. Biggers, 31 F.3d 279, 284 (5th
Cir. 1994). “A judge will not be deprived of immunity because the action he took was in error,
was done maliciously, or was in excess of his authority; rather, he will be subject to liability only
when he has acted in the ‘clear absence of all jurisdiction. ’” Stump v. Sparkman, 435 U.S. 349,
356–57 (1978)  (quoting Bradley v. Fisher , 80 U.S. 335, 351  (1871)). “ This broad scope of
immunity is afforded to judges for actions taken within their jurisdiction because their role in the
judicial system requires that they enjoy freedom to determine the law unfettered by the threat of
collateral attacks against the judge personally.” Mays v. Sudderth, 97 F.3d 107, 110–11 (5th Cir.
1996). “[A] district court may appropriately consider the possible applicability of the doctrine of
absolute immunity as a threshold matter in making a section 1915(d) determination.” Krueger v.
Reimer, 66 F.3d 75, 76 (5th Cir. 1995) ; see Knight v. Bennet , No. CIV.A. 07 -1276, 2007 WL
1217727, at *2 (E.D. La. Apr. 23, 2007) (dismissing claims against judge on § 1915 review where
only relief sought was monetary damages and all alleged actions were taken in judge’s role as a
judicial officer).

 The Court finds that  Mr. Lewis’ claims against Judges Brown, North, Currault, and
Papillion are barred by the doctrine of judicial immunity. Mr. Lewis challenges their actions in
handling cases before them. All actions alleged were taken in the judges’ capacity as judicial
officers in issuing rulings and recommendations, admitting evidence, and making comments in
court. As the United States Supreme Court held in Stump, it matters not whether these judges made
errors, acted in excess of their authority, or even acted maliciously. 435 U.S. 356–57.
 In his response to this Court’s Show Cause Order, Mr. Lewis claims that judicial immunity
is inapplicable to claims under the Civil Rights Act of 1866 because legislators at the time intended
the Act to apply to judges. But the legislative history of that Act does not guide the court here. The
Civil Rights Act of 1866 is presently codified as 42 U.S.C. § 1981 and § 1982. See Runyon v.
McCrary, 427 U.S. 160, 170 (1976) . Mr. Lewis invokes § 1981 1 in his Complaint. But an action
under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 is the exclusive remedy for claims that a state actor has violated § 1981.
Felton v. Polles, 315 F.3d 470, 482 (5th Cir. 2002). It is well settled that judicial immunity bars an
action for damages under § 1983. Pierson v. Ray, 386 U.S. 547, 554 (1967) ; Stump, 435 U.S. at
355-56. Indeed, the Supreme Court instructs that Section “1983 is to be read in harmony with
general principles of tort immunities and defenses rather than in derogation of them.” Imbler v.
Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409, 418 (1976) . Furthermore, civil rights actions against federal officials
(acting under color of federal, and not state, law)  like the judges named by Mr. Lewis are further
limited to cases of  “unreasonable searches and seizures in violation of the Fourth Amendment,”

clearly concern actions taken in the judges’ judicial capacity. Accordingly, they are entitled to
judicial immunity from Mr. Lewis’ claims for damages.
 It does not appear that Mr. Lewis’ claim for injunctive relief implicates the judges. He asks
that his property be returned to him. But no facts alleged suggest that any of the judges named
have the authority or ability to do so. In any event, injunctive relief against a judicial officer is
unavailable “unless a declaratory decree was violated or declaratory relief was unavailable. ” 42
U.S.C. § 1983; see Doris v. Van Davis , No. CIV.A. 08 -4138, 2009 WL 382653, at *3 (E.D. La.

2. Claims against State Court
 Mr. Lewis also names the Civil District Court as a defendant. But Louisiana state courts
are not juridical persons or entities capable of being sued. See Dupree v. Fourth Jud. Dist. Ct., No.
3:12-CV-1442, 2012 WL 5438955, at *2 (W.D. La. Sept. 24, 2012), report and recommendation
adopted, No. 3:12-CV-1442, 2012 WL 5438948 (W.D. La. Nov. 7, 2012) ;  Moity v. Louisiana
State Bar Ass'n, 414 F. Supp. 180, 182 (E.D. La. 1976) (“[T]he jurisprudence seems clear that state
courts are not considered ‘persons' within the meaning of s 1983.”). Therefore, Mr. Lewis cannot
state a claim against the Civil District Court.
3. Claims Against Attorney General
Prosecutors are also  absolutely immune from  a civil rights suit for damages. Imbler v.
Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409, 428–29. The scope of this immunity depends not on the defendant's status
as a prosecutor, but on “the functional nature of the activities” of which the plaintiff complains.
McGruder v. Necaise, 733 F.2d 1146, 1148 (5th Cir. 1984) . Courts have held that prosecutorial
immunity extends “ to a prosecutor's actions in initiating, investigating and pursuing a criminal
prosecution.” Cook v. Houston Post , 616 F.2d 791, 793 (5th Cir. 1980) . But “[a]  prosecutor's
administrative duties and those investigatory functions that do not relate to an advocate's
preparation for the initiation of a prosecution or for judicial proceedings are not entitled to absolute
immunity.” Buckley v. Fitzsimmons , 509 U.S. 259, 273 (1993) . Importantly, as with judicial
immunity, civil rights actions under §1983 are subject to the principle of prosecutorial immunity.

Imbler, 424 U.S. at 420 (“[A] prosecutor enjoys absolute immunity from § 1983 suits for damages
when he acts within the scope of his prosecutorial duties.”).
Here, the only allegation as to former Attorney General Jeff Landry is that he ignored Mr.
Lewis’ civil rights complaints. To the extent Mr. Lewis alleges that Mr. Landry should have
initiated a criminal prosecution related to Mr. Lewis’ complaint, Mr. Landry is entitled to
prosecutorial immunity and Mr. Lewis’ claims against him must be dismissed. There is no support
for Mr. Lewis’ argument that the legislative hist ory of the Civil Rights Act of 1866 precludes
prosecutorial immunity. As discussed above, Section “1983 is to be read in harmony with general
principles of tort immunities and defenses rather than in derogation of them.” Imbler, 424 U.S. at
418.
Additionally, “[i]n most cases, Eleventh Amendment sovereign immunity bars private suits
against nonconsenting states in federal court.” City of Austin v. Paxton , 943 F.3d 993, 997 (5th
Cir. 2019) . Sovereign immunity “ prohibits suits against state officials or agencies that are
effectively suits against a state.” City of Austin v. Paxton, 943 F.3d 993, 997 (5th Cir. 2019). The
case of Ex Parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908) provides an exception:  “a federal court may enjoin
a state official in his official capacity from taking future actions in furtherance of a state law that
offends federal law or the federal Constitution.”  Healthy Vision Ass'n v. Abbott , 138 F.4th 385,
396 (5th Cir. 2025) (quoting Moore v. Louisiana Bd. of Elementary & Secondary Educ., 743 F.3d
959, 963 (5th Cir. 2014)). “For Young to apply, three criteria must be satisfied: (1) A plaintiff must
name in dividual state officials as defendants in their official capacities; (2) the plaintiff must
allege[ ] an ongoing violation of federal law; and (3) the relief sought must be properly
characterized as prospective. Green Valley Special Util. Dist. v. City of Schertz, Texas , 969 F.3d
460, 471 (5th Cir. 2020) (citations and internal quotation marks omitted).

In challenging Mr. Landry’s actions or inactions as Attorney General, Mr. Lewis
effectively attempts to sue the State of Louisiana, implicating sovereign immunity. The Court finds
that the Ex Parte Young exception is not implicated here because Mr. Lewis does not seek to enjoin
Mr. Landry from enforcing a state law in contravention to federal law or the federal Constitution.
Indeed, Mr. Landry is no longer the Attorney General of Louisiana and could not be ordered to
take or stop taking future actions in that capacity. Thus, Mr. Lewi s’ claims against Mr. Landry
must also be dismissed as barred by sovereign immunity.
Conclusion
 For the foregoing reasons, Mr. Lewis’ claims should be dismissed for failure to state a
claim and for seeking monetary relief against a defendant who is immune from suit under 28
U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B)(ii-iii).
