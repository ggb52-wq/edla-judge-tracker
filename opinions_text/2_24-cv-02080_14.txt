REPORT AND RECOMMENDATION

 Plaintiff filed this lawsuit on August 23, 2024, seeking judicial review of a final decision
by the Commissioner of Social Security denying her application for benefits. Before the Court is
the defendant’s Motion to Dismiss. (Rec. Doc. 5). Because plaintiff’s Complaint is untimely and
she has not established that she is entitled to equitable tolling, IT IS RECOMMENDED that the
Motion to Dismiss be GRANTED and that Plaintiff’s Complaint be dismissed with prejudice.
Background
 An administrative law judge (“ ALJ”) denied plaintiff’s claim for disability insurance
benefits on January 10, 2024. Unhappy with her counsel’s representation through that point,
plaintiff fired her attorney on or about January 15, 2024. Without the assistance of counsel, plaintiff
timely appealed, and the Appeals Council denied her request for review on May 21, 2024. It mailed
her notice of its action on the same day.
The Appeals Council notice  explicitly informs plaintiff that she has 60 days from her
receipt of the notice to file suit, stating that “[y]ou have 60 days to file a civil action (ask for court
review)” under a bolded heading “Time To File a Civil Action.” (Rec. Doc. 5 -5, at 3). The letter
notes that the 60 days starts the day after she receives the letter and that “[w]e assume you received
this letter 5 days after the date on it unless you show us that you did not receive it within the 5-day

Law and Analysis
1. Limitations Period for Social Security Appeals
The Social Security Act provides that an individual may obtain review of a final decision
of the Commissioner of Social Security “ by a civil action commenced within sixty days after the
mailing to him of notice of such decision or within such further time as the Commissioner of Social
Security may allow.” 42 U.S.C. § 405(g); see 20 C.F.R. § 422.210 (c). The regulations provide that
“the date of receipt of notice of denial of request for review of the presiding officer’s decision or
notice of the decision by the Appeals Council shall be presumed to be 5 days after the date of such
notice, unless there is a reasonable showing to the contrary .” 20 C.F.R. § 422.210(c). “[I]f the
[claimant] successfully rebuts the presumption, the burden is then placed upon the Secretary to
establish that the [claimant] received actual notice.” McCall v. Bowen, 832 F.2d 862, 864 (5th Cir.
1987). Additionally, the regulations also provide that the Commissioner can extend the 60 -day
period “upon a showing of good cause.” Id.
The Secretary here has chosen not to extend the limitations period. Nonetheless, the
Supreme Court has held that courts may apply the principle of equitable tolling if appropriate. See
Bowen v. City of New York, 476 U.S. 467, 480  (1986); see also Barrs v. Sullivan, 906 F.2d 120,

122 (5th Cir. 1990). “Generally, a litigant seeking equitable tolling bears the burden of establishing
two elements: (1) that he has been pursuing his rights diligently, and (2) that some extraordinary
circumstance stood in his way.” Pace v. DiGuglielmo, 544 U.S. 408, 418 (2005). In Social Security
cases, courts have applied equitable tolling where “the claimant (i) was unaware that there had
been a violation giving rise to a claim, (ii) rebutted the presumption that notice of the Appeals
Council denial was received within five days, (iii) received conflicting information about the filing
deadline, o r (iv) was unable to comprehend the appeal process because of an impediment.”
Robison v. Comm'r of Soc. Sec. Admin., No. 9:19-CV-0037, 2019 WL 2745644, at *2 (E.D. Tex.
July 1, 2019) (quoting Cole-Hill ex rel. T.W. v. Colvin, 110 F. Supp. 3d 480, 484–85 (W.D.N.Y.
2015)).
Equitable tolling applies only in rare cases. See Barrs 906. F2d at 122. In Barrs v. Sullivan,
the Fifth Circuit held that plaintiff had failed to establish that “his case falls within the narrow class
of cases in which the equities in favor of tolling the limitations period are so great that deference
to the agency's determination is inappropriate.” 906 F.2d at 122. There was no impropriety by the
government, the plaintiff had not filed any medical documentation to support his claim tha t brain
damage excused him from timely filing, and plaintiff had waited more than nine months after the
Appeals Council sent notice to file his action in federal court. Id.
In Moore v. Astrue, the claimant filed suit 63 days after the date she was presumed to have
received the Commissioner’s decision. No. CIV .A. 10-1380, 2011 WL 3738976, at *2 (W.D. La.
July 18, 2011), report and recommendation adopted,  No. CIV .A. 10-1380, 2011 WL 3739048
(W.D. La. Aug. 22, 2011). The court found equitable tolling was not warranted where she alleged
that she did not have counsel to assist her in filing suit, that the Clerk of Court told her she had
time to continue processing her complaint, and that “ her alleged disability causes her body to

Id. The court found that she had not supported her allegation regarding the statements of the Clerk

“every pro se plaintiff who appeals an unfavorable disability decision is suffering from an

2. Analysis

The decision of the Appeals Council is dated May 21, 2024. Under the regulations, it is
presumed that plaintiff received the decision on May 26, 2024. She was required to file this civil
action by July 25, 2024. She did not file this action until almost a month later on August 23, 2024.
Her action is untimely.
Plaintiff argues first that she did not receive the Appeals Council decision unt il June 17,
2024. She presents no evidence to support this assertion such as an affidavit, sworn declaration, or
other documentation. Even if she could show that she received the decision on June 17, 2024, it is
undisputed that her filing was still untimel y. Using the June 17 date, she was required to file this
civil action by August 16, 2024. She did not do so until a week later.
Plaintiff argues that this Court should nonetheless allow her action to proceed as timely
under the doctrine of equitable tolli ng. She asserts that she “suffers from mental disabilities that
impair her focus and memory.” (Rec. Doc. 7, at 2). She submits that she was “unable to
comprehend the necessity of filing her action within the prescribed time.” Id. In her motion,
plaintiff does not provide any further information about her mental disabilities or any evidence to
support the existence or impacts of the alleged disabilities. In her complaint, though, she explains

that she is suffering from and/or being treated for depressive dis order, post traumatic stress
disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, and insomnia. (Rec.
Doc. 1-1, at 15). She alleges that these conditions cause, among other symptoms, poor focus,
inability to pay attention, shor t attention span, and forgetfulness. Id. Although she has attached
medical records to her Complaint, none are for mental health treatment.
Plaintiff argues further that the Social Security Administration was negligent in obtaining
her medical records. She claims this contributed to her inability to present her case effectively and
understand the implications of the Appeals Council’s decision.  She does not explain how this
alleged failure impacted her ability to timely initiate her action. She argues that her inability to
meet the deadline should be viewed in the context of the Social Security Administration’s failures.
The Court finds that pl aintiff has failed to establish circumstances that warrant equitable
tolling here. First, she has not presented any evidence to rebut the presumptive receipt date. Using
that date, her filing is approximately one month late. Even using the purported date o f receipt,
plaintiff filed a week too late.
Plaintiff claims that her mental impairments prevented her from timely filing suit. The
Court finds that her generalized assertions of not understanding that she had to comply with the
deadline are insufficient. The Appeals Council notice is incredibly clear. There is no suggestion
that plaintiff is unable to read, understand English, or that she has any cognitive impairment that
would impact her ability to understand the instruction in the notice. Indeed, her Complaint and her
other filings in this case, all drafted without the assistance of counsel, reflect a satisfactory ability
to read and write. As the court in Moore pointed out, all claimants challenging a denial of benefits
claim a disability. To allow equi table tolling under the circumstances here would mean that any
person with an impairment or medication side effect impacting their ability to focus and pay

attention who claimed they did not understand the necessity of timely filing suit would not be
subject to the statutory limitations period.
Finally, the Court finds no evidence of any action that the Social Security Administration
provided conflicting information or otherwise hindered plaintiff from timely filing her civil action.
Whatever deficiencies may have transpired in obtaining records during the application and hearing
process1 have no bearing on the issue now before the Court.
For the foregoing reasons, the Court finds that plaintiff has not established that equitable
tolling is warranted here. Accordingly,
IT IS RECOMMENDED that Defendant’s Motion to Dismiss (Rec. Doc. 5) be GRANTED
and that plaintiff’s Complaint be dismissed with prejudice.
