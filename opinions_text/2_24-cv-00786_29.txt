Plaintiff Tomarcus Porter filed a Motion for Summary Judgment (ECF No. 28) on the issue
of the liability of defendants Russell Roberts and Major Andria Johnson for the alleged violations
of his constitutional rights as asserted in his complaint brought pursuant to 42 U.S.C. § 1983.  The
motion was referred to a United States Magistrate Judge to conduct a hearing, including an
evidentiary hearing, if necessary, and to submit proposed findings and recommendations for

applicable, 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(c)(1) and(2).  Upon review of the record, the Court has determined
that the motion can be disposed of without further proceedings.

II. S TANDARDS OF REVIEW FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT
The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provide  that “[t] he court shall grant summary
judgment if the movant shows that there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the
movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” 2  The initial burden falls on the movant, who
must show that there is an “absence of a genuine issue as to any material fact.” 3  “The movant
satisfies this burden by showing that a reasonable jury could not find for the nonmovant, based on
the burdens that would apply at trial.”4  When the movant meets th is initial burden, “the burden
shifts to the nonmoving party to produce evidence or designate specific facts showing the existence
of a genuine issue for trial.  Doubts are to be resolved in favor of the nonmoving party, and any
reasonable inferences are to be drawn in favor of that party.”5
Therefore, where, as here, a motion for summary judgment is filed by a plaintiff, the
plaintiff is essentially taking the position “that he is entitled to prevail as a matter of la w because
the opponent has no valid . . . defense to the action . . .”6

District courts are permitted to dismiss a motion for summary judgment without prejudice
as premature if it is filed before any party is served and/or when no party has filed an answer.7  In
this case, when granting Porter leave to proceed as a pauper, the court withheld service of summons
pending completion of the statutory frivol ousness review.  See  ECF No. 6.  Thus, not only have
the defendants not been served with summons, no defendant has filed or has had an opportunity to
file an answer or responsive pleading.  B ased on the limited record before the court, 8 it appears
that an y answer by a defendant would interpose defenses an d likely create issues of fact
challenging those asserted in the Porter’s motion.  The motion therefore was filed prior to service
of summons and prior to the filing of an answer.
Porter’s case also is still in the preliminary stages , with the pending Report and
Recommendation that will, if adopted, narrow the claims that survive to be served on a defendant.9
