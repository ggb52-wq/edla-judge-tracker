LEGAL STANDARD
 Courts have a duty to screen initial filings to independently assess the merits of a
complaint filed by a litigant requesting to proceed in forma pauperis  and to dismiss
claims as frivolous that have no arguable basis in law or fact. 25 A claim lacks an arguable
basis in law when it is “based on an indisputably meritless legal theory.”26
Pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(b)(6), a district court may dismiss
a complaint, or any part of it, for failure to state a claim upon which relief may be granted
if the plaintiff has not set forth factual allegations in support of his claim that would entitle
him to relief.27 “To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain sufficient factual
matter, accepted as true, to ‘state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.’”28 “A claim
has facial plausibility when the plaintiff pleads factual content that allows the court to
draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.” 29
The court, however, does not accept as true legal conclusions or mere conclusory
statements, and “conclusory allegations or legal conclusions masquerading as factual

II. Meta is not liable for secondary copyright infringement  because
Plaintiff did not comply with the provisions of 17 U.S.C. § 512.

Plaintiff does not specify whether she sues Meta for direct copyright infringement
or for secondary copyright infringement, either in her original complaint or her response
to the show cause order. However, she clarifies that she is suing Meta under a theory of
secondary copyright infringement in her objection to the Magistrate Judge’s Report and
Recommendation.50 This Court will conduct a de novo review of the Magistrate Judge’s
finding that Plaintiff’s claim for copyright infringement should be dismissed for failure to
state a claim.
The Digital Millenium Copyright Act (“DMCA”) , 17 U.S.C. § 512, prohibits
Plaintiff’s claim. It provides, in relevant part,
“A service provider shall not be liable for monetary relief . . . for infringement of
copyright by reason of the storage at the direction of a user of material that resides
on a system or network controlled or operated by or for the service provider, if the
service provider

(i) does not have actual knowledge that the material or an activity using
the material on the system or network is infringing;

(ii) in the absence of such actual knowledge, is not aware of facts or
circumstances from which infringing activity is apparent . . .”51

Plaintiff never notified Meta that her online persona “Slimdabodylast” was a registered
copyright. In her objection to the Magistrate Judge’s Report and Recommendation,
Plaintiff admits that she did not use Meta’s designated copyright report mechanism and
that she did not include the word “copyright” in her demand letter to Meta.52  Thus, Meta
had no actual knowledge that Plaintiff  claimed her copyright was being infringed, and
Defendant alleges no facts that suggest Meta should be aware of facts of circumstances to

put it on constructive notice of such copyright infringement. Furthermore, the DMCA also
provides, in relevant part, that any “ notification of claimed infringement must be a
written communication provided to the designated agent of a service provider ,” and that
a “notification from a copyright owner or from a person authorized to act on behalf of the
copyright owner that fails to comply ” with this designated agent requirement “ shall not

In this case, Plaintiff admits that she did not file her reports to Meta via its designated

that her demand letter notice gave Meta either actual or constructive knowledge of the
copyright infringement. This Court will thus adopt the Magistrate Judge’s conclusion that
Plaintiff has failed to state a claim for copyright infringement.55
