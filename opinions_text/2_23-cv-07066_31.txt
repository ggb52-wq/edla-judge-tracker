II. LAW AND ANALYSIS
“The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure specifically provide that a court may, in
its discretion, dismiss a plaintiff’s action for failure to prosecute or for failure to
comply with the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure or any order of the court and that
such a dismissal is considered to be an adjudication on the merits.” 22  Nonetheless,
“The Court’s power to dismiss for want of prosecution should be used sparingly.”23
Where dismissal without prejudice will likely bar future litigation due to the
applicable statute of limitations, dismissal is reviewed under the  heightened
standard used to review a dismissal with prejudice .24  Before an action can be
dismissed with prejudice, three factors must be present: 25 (1) there is a clear record
of delay or contumacious conduct by the plaintiff; (2) lesser sanctions would not serve
the best interests of justice; (3) the Court finds that (a) delay was caused by the
plaintiff, as opposed to plaintiff’s  attorney , (b) the defendant suffered actual
prejudice, or (c) the delay was caused by intentional conduct .26  The third factor is
considered an aggravating factor.  The Fifth Circuit has held that “ aggravating
factors must ‘usually’ be found,” not that they “must ‘always’ be found.”27  Dismissal
with prejudice is appropriate only in “cases where the plaintiff's conduct has

To succeed in their Motion, the Defendants first must show that there is a clear
record of delay or contumacious conduct by the Plaintiff. 29  There is a clear record of
delay when there are significant periods of total inactivity that persist for longer than
just a few months.30  A clear record of contumacious conduct exists when the plaintiff
has deliberately exhibited a “stubborn resistance to authority.”31  Defendants argues
that Plaintiff’s failure to comply with the Court Order compelling arbitration and
Plaintiff’s acknowledgement that Defendants did “not receive timely responses from
Plaintiffs counsel” constituted contumacious conduct. 32  The Court find s that
Defendants have not provided enough evidence for the Court to find that Plaintiff
acted with a “stubborn resistance to authority.”  While prior to the filing of the instant
Motion, there is evidence that  Plaintiff’s counsel did not timely respond to the
Defendants regarding arbitration matters, it appears that the parties’ contract
included procedures to follow  in the event that  Plaintiff failed to participate in the
arbitrator selection process. 33  It appears that the arbitration process is currently
pending, and the Organization Meeting, where preliminary matters will be discussed,

Thus, the Court finds that no aggravating factors are present and thus this is
not an unusual case where  dismissal is warranted without the finding of an
aggravating factor.  Dismissal with prejudice is an extreme sanction that deprives a
litigant of the opportunity to pursue his claim.  The sanction of a dismissal with
prejudice “is reserved for the most egregious of cases.”  The Court finds that the
Plaintiff’s actions in this case do not meet the high bar required for dismissal with
prejudice.

For the foregoing reasons,  IT IS HEREBY ORDERED  that Defendant’s
Motion to Dismiss for Failure to Prosecute is DENIED.
