II. LEGAL STANDARD
A Rule 12(b)(6) motion challenges the sufficiency of a plaintiff’s allegations.  A complaint,
or any part of it, may be dismissed for failure to state a claim upon which relief may be granted if
plaintiff fails to set forth factual allegations that would e ntitle him to relief.   Bell Atl. Corp. v.
Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007); Cuvillier v. Taylor, 503 F.3d 397, 401 (5th Cir. 2007).
To survive, a complaint must contain sufficient facts, accepted as true, to state a claim for
relief that is plausible on its face.  Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009).  “A claim has facial

plausibility when the plaintiff pleads the factual content that allows the court to draw the
reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.”  Id.  Determining
whether a complaint states a plausible claim for relief is a context -specific task requiring the
application of judicial experience and common sense.  Id. at 679.
While detailed factual allegations are not required, a complaint must offer more than labels,
legal conclusions, or formulaic recitations of the elements of a cause of action.  Iqbal, 556 U.S. at
678.  “The complaint is construed in the light most favorable to plaintiff, accepting as true all well-
pleaded factual allegations and drawing all reasonable inferences in plaintiff’s favor.”  Lovick v.
Ritemoney Ltd., 378 F.3d 433, 437 (5th Cir. 2004) (citation omitted).  However, the court will not
accept “conclusory allegations, unwarranted factual inferences, or legal conclusions” as true.
Ferrer v. Chevron Corp., 484 F.3d 776, 780 (5th Cir. 2007) (quoting Plotkin v. IP Axess Inc., 407
F.3d 690, 696 (5th Cir. 2005)).  If factual allegations are insufficient to raise a right to relief above
the speculative level, the claim should be dismissed.  Twombly, 550 U.S. at 555.
III. DISCUSSION
Damond’s claims under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, 42 U.S.C. § 1981, Title VI , the Fourteenth
Amendment and the Eighth Amendment fail because he does not allege facts indicating intentional
racial discrimination.  Damond “believes” Dr. Tatford  administered inadequate anesthesia and
otherwise failed to treat him appropriately . But, he alleges no facts suggesting Dr. Tatford was
racially motivated. He alleges Dr. Tatford said not to touch him while he performed a toenail
procedure. That is a racially neutral request. He alleges Dr. Tatford said numbing cream was
unavailable. That is a racially neutral observation . Damond does not allege numbing cream was
available or was offered only to white patients. There is no allegation Dr. Tatford refused to treat
Damond because of his race, nor did he use racial epithets. The only support for Damond’s claims

is his subjective feelings, his belief. That is not evidence of intentional discrimination. Mandawala,
16 F.4th at 1151.
Damond alleges a violation of  Title VII . Title VII serves to  “provide[] remedies to
employees for injuries related to discriminatory conduct and associated wrongs by employers.”
Body by Cook, Inc. v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., et al., 869 F.3d 381, 391 ( 5th Cir. 2017)
(quoting Univ. of Tex. Sw. Med. Ctr. v. Nassar , 570 U.S. 338, 342 (2013)) (emphasis added) .
Damond failed to allege an employment relationship . Without an  employment relationship ,
Damond has no standing under Title VII.
Damond’s claims for medical malpractice,15 negligence, intentional infliction of emotional
distress, punitive damages, and vicarious liability  are before the Court based upon supplemental
jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1367. Having dismissed all original jurisdiction claims, the court
declines to exercise supplemental jurisdiction over the state law claims and dismisses them,
without prejudice. See Royal Canin U.S.A., Inc. v. Wullschleger , 604 U.S. 22, 32 (2025)
(reaffirming the district court “may decline to exercise supplemental jurisdiction . . . if the district
court has dismissed all claims over which it has original jurisdiction”) (internal quotations
omitted).
After reviewing his opposition, the court finds Damond fails to state what material facts he
would include in an amended complaint to save his federal law claims. The court does not grant
another opportunity to amend.

IV . CONCLUSION
