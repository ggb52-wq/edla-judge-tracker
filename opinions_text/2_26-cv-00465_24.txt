 ORDER AND REASONS
Before the Court is  a Motion to Dismiss pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure
12(b)(6) filed by Defendants Women with a Vision (“WWAV”), Deon Haywood, Shaquita
Borden, and Tabitha Pearl Hicks (the “Individual Defendants”) . R. Doc. 16. P laintiff Whitney
Nicole Gibson has not filed an opposition. See  LR 7.5. Accordingly, this motion is deemed to be
unopposed. Defendants have filed a reply in support of their motion. R. Doc. 17.
BACKGROUND
 Plaintiff filed this pro se action alleging employment discrimination under the Americans
with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) against her alleged former  employer, Women with a Vision
(“WWAV”), and three WWAV employees, Executive Director  Deon Haywood, Chief Financial
Officer Shaquita Borden, and Tabitha Pearl Ricks. R. Doc. 4 at 2-3. Plaintiff identifies WWAV as
the sole employing entity. R. Doc. 4 at 3. Defendants have filed a Motion to Dismiss the claims
against the individual employees. R. Doc. 16.
LAW and ANALYSIS
Pursuant to the Eastern District of Louisiana ’s Local Civil Rule 7.5, a party opposing a

motion “must file and serve a memorandum in opposition to the motion with citations of authorities
no later than eight days before the noticed submission date.”  Nevertheless, a motion such as one
for summary judgment cannot be granted simply because there is no opposition, even if the failure
to oppose violated a local rule. John v. La. (Bd. of Trustees for State Colleges & Universities), 757
F.2d 698, 709 (5th Cir.1985). The movant retains the burden of establishing the absence of a
genuine issue of material fact and, unless he has done so, the court may not grant the motion,
regardless of whether any response was filed. Id. at 708. That same rationale applies with respect
to a Motion to Dismiss pursuant to F.R.C.P. Rule 12(b)(6). Here, although Plaintiff has not
opposed the motion, Defendants have otherwise established that their Motion to Dismiss the
Individual Defendants has merit and should be granted.
 Rule 12(b)(6) allows a defendant to seek dismissal of a complaint based on the “failure to
state a claim upon which relief can be granted.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6). “To survive a motion to
dismiss, a complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to ‘state a claim to
relief that is plausible on its face.’” Ashcroft v. Iqbal , 556 U.S. 662, 678, 129 S.Ct. 1937, 173
L.Ed.2d 868 (2009) (quoting Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 570, 127 S.Ct. 1955, 167
L.Ed.2d 929 (2007)). “A claim has facial plausibility when the plaintiff pleads factual content that
allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct
alleged.” Id. A court “do[es] not accept as true conclusory allegations, unwarranted factual
inferences, or legal conclusions.” Plotkin v. IP Axess Inc., 407 F.3d 690, 696 (5th Cir. 2005). Pro
se pleadings are afforded a more liberal construction. Clark v. Nicholls State Univ., No. CV 19-
6054, 2020 WL 360511, at *2 (E.D. La. Jan. 22, 2020).  Nevertheless, a pro se litigant’s complaint

must satisfy the plausibility standard. Id. (citations omitted).
 The Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) does not permit suits against individual
employees. See 42 U.S.C. § 12131(1). The Fifth Circuit has  made this clear, explaining that the
ADA imposes liability only on a covered employer, not on supervisors, managers, or co‑workers.
Kemp v. Holder, 610 F.3d 231, 234 (5th Cir. 2010).
 As Defendants point out, Plaintiff  in her Compl aint does not describe the Individual
Defendants as anything other than employees. She identifies Deon Haywood as “Executive
Director,” Shaquita Borden as “Chief  Financial Officer,” and Tabitha Ricks as a co‑worker. R.
Doc. 4 at 2- 3. Nowhere does Plaintiff allege that any of these defendants individually employed
her, controlled her compensation or hours, or acted as her “ statutory” employer under the ADA.
The Complaint alleges various performance-related disagreements, supervisory communications,
and inte rnal personnel discussions, which do not create indivi dual liability  under the ADA.
Therefore, Plaintiff has failed to allege any plausible claims against the Individual Defendants, and
such claims will be dismissed with prejudice. Accordingly,
IT IS ORDERED that  that the Motion to Dismiss pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil
Procedure 12(b)(6) is G RANTED and t he claims asserted against Defendants Deon Haywood,
Shaquita Borden, and Tabitha Pearl Hicks are DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE.
