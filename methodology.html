EDLA Analyzer

How we count

Independent project, not affiliated with the court. Not legal advice. The numbers describe docket activity only and say nothing about whether any ruling was right.

What the site counts

This site counts docket events in civil cases in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Louisiana and attributes each to the district judge assigned to the case, except that a written opinion signed by a different district judge (one entered before the case was reassigned) is credited to the judge who signed it. It covers the court's 16 active district judges. Rows for magistrate judges presiding by consent, and for visiting or senior judges, are kept in the data but not charted.

The counts describe docket activity. They do not measure the quality or correctness of any ruling. Judges are listed alphabetically unless a reader sorts a table.

Sources

  • Written opinions, 2022 through 2026: the PACER Written Opinions Report for the court, run by hand by the site's owner (it carries no fee) for each of 2022, 2023, and 2024, Jan. 1 to Dec. 21, 2025, and Jan. 1 to Oct. 20, 2026, with each opinion's docket text, signing judge, and case details. Counts begin with the earliest report period, now Jan. 1, 2022. No report on file covers Dec. 22 to 31, 2025, so opinions from those days are mostly missing; those days are left out of the working days behind each per-week and per-month rate, and are drawn with a dashed outline in the by-day chart.
  • Civil orders, Sept. 17 to Oct. 16, 2026: the PACER Docket Activity Report, event category "order."
  • Opinion PDFs and text: GovInfo's United States Courts collection (Government Publishing Office), which publishes the documents the court flags as written opinions, and CourtListener. Each GovInfo PDF is matched to the docket by the "Document" number CM/ECF stamps on every page.
  • Calendar: the court's seven-day calendar, docket text that sets proceedings, and minute entries (see Calendar).
  • Docket activity: every civil entry in the court's RSS feed, kept for 14 days (see Docket activity).
  • Judges' requirements and chambers contact: each judge's page on the court's website, read daily (see Judges' requirements).
  • Since Oct. 17, 2026: the court's public CM/ECF RSS feed, checked every 15 minutes around the clock, matched against CourtListener (Free Law Project), which builds docket records from the same feed and adds the assigned judge and full event type. Documents CourtListener marks as free on PACER are the entries on PACER's Written Opinions Report; the site asks CourtListener for newly marked ones every two hours. New written opinions also come from GovInfo, which posts the same documents, usually within a few days: each PDF's CM/ECF stamp gives the case, document number, filing date, and judge, and its title and opening paragraph give the motions decided. When the docket text arrives later (from CourtListener or a Written Opinions Report), it replaces what was read from the PDF.

Definitions

  • Civil order: any docket event in the court's "order" category, including orders on motions, scheduling orders, text orders, judgments, hearing settings, transfers, and magistrate judges' reports and recommendations, counted once per docket entry.
  • Written opinion: an entry on PACER's Written Opinions Report. Each chambers decides which of its orders to flag, so chambers flag different shares of their rulings. In a Sept.–Oct. 2026 check, about two-thirds of rulings on Rule 12 and Rule 56 motions appeared on the report, and the share varied widely by judge.
  • Merits ruling: a written opinion resolving a motion under Rule 12 (dismissal, judgment on the pleadings), Rule 56 (summary judgment), or a trial or post-trial matter (findings of fact and conclusions of law, judgment as a matter of law, new trial, altering a judgment).
  • Working days: weekdays other than court holidays, including Mardi Gras. A judge who joined the court during the period is counted from that judge's first day on the bench; Judge Papillion took the bench on June 2, 2023, Judge Long on Dec. 21, 2023, Judge Crain on Dec. 22, 2025, and Judge St. John on April 13, 2026.
  • Rates: opinions per week divide a judge's written opinions, other than magistrate R&Rs, by working days ÷ 5; merits per month divide merits rulings by working days ÷ 21; orders per working day divide orders by working days.
  • Ruling type (opinion search): each written opinion falls in exactly one type, checked in this order: magistrate judge's report and recommendations; Rule 12 ruling; Rule 56 ruling; trial or post-trial ruling; other opinion. An entry that rules on both a Rule 12 and a Rule 56 motion counts as Rule 12 here, so these counts can differ by a few entries from the Rule 12 and Rule 56 columns of the court tables, which count such an entry in both.
  • Written opinions by month: each month's written opinions, split into merits rulings and everything else (other opinions and magistrate R&Rs).
  • Share of orders by weekday: each weekday's share of a judge's civil orders, beside the same share for all judges' orders combined. It rests on a few weeks of orders, so one heavy week can tilt it.
  • Time of entry: the share of civil orders with a recorded entry time that were entered in each clock hour, Central. Orders without a recorded time are left out. Entry times come from the RSS feed or CourtListener's record of when it read the entry, and show when the clerk entered an order, which may differ from when the judge signed it.
  • Period: "All", "90D", and "30D" in the top bar. The last two count back 90 or 30 calendar days from the data-through date, and every figure, including working days, is recomputed for that window.
  • Median judge: the middle value among the 16 judges; with 16 judges, the average of the 8th and 9th.

Outcomes

For each written opinion that rules on a motion, the site records what the order did to the motion. Outcomes describe the motion, not a party: "granted" means the motion was granted, whichever side filed it. Magistrate judges' reports and recommendations get no outcome.

GrantedThe motion was granted.
Granted, leave to amendThe motion was granted and the order gave leave to amend. Counted with "granted" in shares and bars; shown separately in the opinion search and in bar tooltips.
Granted in partThe motion was granted in part and denied in part, or granted as to some relief and denied as to the rest.
DeniedThe motion was denied.
Denied without prejudiceThe motion was denied without prejudice to re-urging it. Counted with "denied" in shares and bars; shown separately in the opinion search and in bar tooltips.
MixedThe order decided several different motions in different ways.
MootThe motion was denied or dismissed as moot.
OtherAny other disposition, entered by hand.
Not determinedThe order was read and no outcome could be found in it. These rulings count as not coded.

Outcomes come from three places, and the opinion search shows which one each outcome came from when you hover over it:

  • Docket text. When the site adds a new opinion, it reads the clerk's docket text for the entry (for example, "ORDER granting 12 Motion to Dismiss"). Where the docket text and a reading of the order disagree, the docket text is used.
  • The order. For opinions already in the data, the update script reads the order's text from GovInfo or CourtListener a few opinions at a time and looks at its "IT IS ORDERED" paragraphs, or its concluding paragraph when there are none. Only paragraphs naming the motion the opinion is counted for are read; for a Rule 12 or Rule 56 ruling, that is the motion to dismiss or for summary judgment.
  • By hand. The site owner can record or correct an outcome. A hand entry always replaces an outcome read from the docket text or the order.

Outcome bars show the shares of coded rulings that were granted, granted in part, denied, and mixed, moot, or other. They count only rulings whose outcome has been coded and say how many those are ("n"). A bar resting on fewer than five coded rulings is drawn in outline. The thin bar under each row on a judge's page is the same breakdown for all 16 judges' rulings pooled together. "Granted" columns give the share granted in full.

Automatic reading is a first pass. It can misread orders written in an unusual form, and it cannot tell which of several motions to dismiss a single sentence addresses.

Motion categories

The judge pages and the opinion search group motion rulings by the motion's name in the docket event. Rule 12 and Rule 56 come from the merits flags above. The rest are checked in this order and take the first match: remand; in limine; Daubert / expert (Daubert, exclusion, or an expert); discovery (motion to compel, protective order, motion to quash, discovery, deposition, subpoena, interrogatories, production); injunction / TRO; reconsideration; stay; default judgment (including entry of default and setting aside a default); attorney fees; and other motions for everything else. The grouping reads the docket event, not the motion papers, so a motion the clerk entered under a general event such as "Motion for Miscellaneous Relief" lands in "other motions."

Time to ruling

Calendar days from the date the motion was docketed to the date the written opinion deciding it was entered, reported as a median. The motion's docket number comes from the text the outcome was read from ("granting 12 Motion to Dismiss", or "(R. Doc. 12)" beside the motion's name in the order); the update script then reads that docket entry's date from CourtListener. Filing dates accumulate as rulings are read, so each figure states how many rulings it rests on. A judge's headline median appears once five rulings have dates; until then it reads "Not yet" and says how many are dated. Where four or more rulings have dates, the motion-outcome table also gives the middle half (the 25th to 75th percentile range) on hover. The measure runs from docketing, not from the date the motion was set for submission or fully briefed, so it includes any continuances the parties sought.

Hearing set before ruling

For a motion ruling with a filing date, the site checks whether any order in the same case set a hearing between the motion's docket date and the ruling. The check uses the civil orders on file, so it is asked only when both dates fall on or after the start of the orders data; earlier motions show no figure. It cannot tell whether the hearing concerned that motion, so read it as "a hearing was set in the case while the motion was pending," not as "the motion was argued."

Opinion length

Pages in the opinion's PDF, for opinions whose PDF the site has found. Medians are shown with the number of opinions they rest on; a judge's headline median appears once five PDFs have page counts. Page counts measure length only.

Judge page charts and facts

Each judge's page has charts and a list of facts computed from the same data, for the period chosen in the top bar. Where a chart shows all judges, it pools their rows into one figure.

  • Written opinions by day: one cell per weekday from the start of the opinion count, or 12 months before the data-through date if that is later, to the data-through date, shaded by the number of the judge's written opinions (magistrate R&Rs included) dated that day, in fixed classes (0, 1, 2, 3, 4–5, 6 or more) that are the same on every judge's page. Court holidays are outlined; days no Written Opinions Report on file covers have a dashed outline. Opinions dated on a weekend are left out of the grid and counted in its key.
  • Civil orders by weekday and hour of entry: the judge's civil orders with a recorded entry time, by weekday and clock hour (Central), shaded relative to the judge's busiest cell. The columns above give each hour's share of the judge's timed orders; the gray mark is the same share for all judges' orders combined. Entry time is when the clerk docketed the order (see Time of entry).
  • Written opinions by ruling type and month: one small chart per ruling type (see Ruling type under Definitions), each month's count, all on one scale so the charts can be read against each other.
  • Motion types decided: the judge's motion rulings among written opinions, by motion category, as a count and a share of the judge's motion rulings. The gray mark is the share of all judges' motion rulings in that category. Categories are listed in a fixed order.
  • Days from filing to ruling: each ruling with a filing date on file as one dot, in calendar days from the motion's docket date to the ruling (see Time to ruling), grouped by motion category. The samples are the same ones the case pages use: written opinions with a filing date, and motions seen in the RSS feed with a later order matched to them. The dark tick is the judge's median once a category has five rulings; the gray tick is the median for all judges' rulings in that category once it has five.
  • Opinion length: the share of the judge's written opinions with a page count that fall in each page range, beside the same share for all judges' opinions.
  • Cases by year filed: the judge's cases (every case in which the judge entered a written opinion or civil order in the period) by the year the case was filed, read from the docket where CourtListener supplied a filing date and otherwise from the case number. The first column gathers every older year. The gray mark is the share for all judges' cases.
  • Nature of suit: the same set of cases, by the nature of suit on the docket where it is on file; the eight most frequent for the judge are listed and the rest are combined. The gray mark is the share of all judges' cases with a nature of suit on file.
  • Hearings and trials: proceedings in the judge's cases from 30 days before today to 30 days after, from the court's calendar, docket entries that set a proceeding, and minute entries (see Calendar), one row per kind: trials, hearings (including oral argument), conferences, and other proceedings. Blue marks a setting; gray marks a proceeding recorded as held in a minute entry. The court's calendar shows only a week at a time and the feed is kept for 14 days, so this is not a complete record.
  • Fifth Circuit decisions: decisions read from the Fifth Circuit's opinions feed in appeals from the judge's cases (see Submission dates, notices, and appeals), listed one by one with the disposition as the decision states it. The site does not total or rate them by judge.

Facts from the data are single items taken from the same rows. Where several rows tie, the most recent one is named.

  • Longest written opinion and opinions of 20 pages or more: from PDF page counts.
  • Most written opinions in a day and in a month: the date or month with the largest number of the judge's written opinions.
  • Longest run of days with an opinion: the most consecutive working days on each of which the judge entered at least one written opinion.
  • Case with the most opinions, cases with a written opinion, and oldest case with an opinion (by year filed, as above).
  • Most common motion decided: the motion category, other than "other motions," with the most rulings among the judge's written opinions.
  • Most civil orders in a day, earliest order entry time, and latest order entry time: from civil orders and their recorded entry times (when the clerk docketed the order).
  • Most common nature of suit and magistrate judge most often referred: among the judge's cases with that detail on the docket.

Written opinions and short orders

For the period with every civil order on file, a judge's page sets that judge's written opinions beside the judge's total civil orders: the share of orders flagged for the Written Opinions Report, the share entered as text orders (the docket event begins "Text Order"), and the same comparison for orders whose event names a motion. The docket does not distinguish a reasoned written order that chambers did not flag from a minute entry, so the figure measures flagging practice, not how often a judge writes reasons.

Comparing with other judges

Each headline figure on a judge's page carries the median judge's figure and a strip with one tick per judge, a taller tick at the median, and a dot for the judge on the page. The strip shows where the 16 figures fall, from zero to the highest; it is not a ranking. For time to ruling and opinion length, only judges with five or more measured rulings or PDFs appear on the strip.

Compare judges

The comparison page places up to four judges' figures side by side. Its "All judges" column gives the median judge for counts and rates, and, for motion outcomes, all 16 judges' coded rulings pooled together. Outcome shares carry the number of coded rulings they rest on; a share resting on fewer than five is a weak basis for comparison.

Submitted motions

Under this court's Local Rule 7.2, a motion is noticed for submission on a set date, after which it is before the judge. The "Motions" page lists civil motions recorded from the court's public CM/ECF feed, which names each entry's event type ("Motion to Dismiss"), case, and document number but not the docket text. The feed shows only its latest entries, so the list begins on the day the site started recording motions and does not reach further back. Responses, replies, and orders that mention a motion are not motions and are not listed.

The submission date is read from the motion's docket text ("Motion(s) will be submitted on 11/4/2026") when the site's periodic CourtListener search indexes the entry, so a motion can appear for a few hours before its date is known. A motion is marked ruling found when a later order or written opinion in the same case names the same type of motion, or when an outcome on file cites the motion's docket number. That is a match on docket events, not a link between documents: a motion that was withdrawn, decided under a different event, or ruled on by minute entry can still show as awaiting. Past submission means the submission date is before the data-through date and no ruling was found. Days past count calendar days from the submission date. The counts describe the docket; they say nothing about why a ruling is pending.

Docket activity

The Activity page lists civil docket entries newest first. Its main source is the court's public CM/ECF RSS feed, which the updater reads about every 15 minutes; the site keeps every civil entry the feed shows for 14 days. The feed gives each entry's case number, case name, document number, docket event ("Motion to Dismiss," "Response/Memorandum in Opposition to Motion"), and time of entry, but not the docket text, so entries are described by event name only. Before the site began keeping the feed, and for anything the feed missed, the page also lists the civil orders, written opinions, and motions already in the site's data for the same 14 days.

Each entry gets one label, checked in this order: Opinion (an entry on the Written Opinions Report, with its outcome and a link to the document where a free copy exists); Order (an event in the court's order category, the same test used to count civil orders); Motion (a motion's own entry, not a response, reply, or order on it; the feed often names a motion by its relief alone, such as "Continue" or "Reconsideration," and those count as motions); New case (a complaint, notice of removal, or petition, other than an amended complaint); Hearing (an event naming a hearing, conference, trial, minute entry, or oral argument); and Filing for everything else. The judge shown is the district judge on the case in the site's data; a case the site has not yet tied to a judge shows a dash. Times are Central and show when the clerk entered the item.

Calendar

The Calendar shows each judge's proceedings by day, from four sources refreshed every 15 minutes:

  • The court's calendar. The court posts a seven-day calendar at laed.uscourts.gov/court-calendar, listing each judge's trials, hearings, and criminal settings with times and courtrooms. The site copies each district judge's entries as written and re-reads the page every update, so an entry taken off the court's calendar comes off here too. The court notes that some proceedings are left off its calendar. Cases appear there in short form ("24-1933"); the site matches a short number to a civil case it already knows, and leaves others, including criminal cases, as written.
  • Docket entries that set a proceeding. When a docket entry's text says that a proceeding is "set for" or "reset for" a date ("Status Conference set for 11/4/2026 10:00 AM"), the site records that proceeding on that date. The text comes from the CourtListener records the updater already reads, so these appear as those records are read, and reach further ahead than the court's seven days. A later entry setting the same kind of proceeding in the same case replaces an earlier setting that has not yet happened.
  • Minute entries. A docket entry recording that a conference, hearing, oral argument, or trial took place appears on the day it was entered, marked "held." The time shown is when the clerk entered it, not when the proceeding began.
  • Motions set for submission (optional): the count of civil motions the site has recorded as set for submission before each judge that day.

The court's RSS feed gives a docket entry's event name but not its text, so an entry that sets a hearing ("Order Set Hearings," "Scheduling Order") shows that something was set without saying when. The page lists those entries from the last two weeks separately, under "date not on file yet," until the date is read from the docket text or the proceeding reaches the court's calendar. Weeks beyond the court's seven-day calendar show only proceedings set in docket text the site has read, so an empty day does not mean a judge has nothing scheduled. A proceeding that appears on the court's calendar and in a docket entry is shown once.

Judges' requirements

Each district judge has a page on the court's website. Some of those pages set out the judge's practice requirements under tabs such as "Chambers Communication," "Courtroom Rules," "Motions & Oral Argument," and "Pretrial/Trial Procedure," and link to standing orders, sample documents, and forms; others give only the section and chambers contact. The site reads every judge's page once a day and copies the text of those tabs word for word, with their links, onto the judge's page here and into the requirements search. It does not summarize, rephrase, or interpret them, and it does not open the linked documents; it lists them with links to the court's copies. It keeps the section letter, chambers room, phone number, chambers email address, and Federal Judicial Center biography link, and leaves out the names of chambers staff.

When a tab's text changes, the tab is marked Updated, and a document link the site has not seen before is marked New, for 60 days. Nothing is marked on the first day the site read the pages. Each judge's page here says when the court's page was last checked. The court's page, and any order in the case, controls.

The requirements search finds passages that contain every word searched, in singular or plural ("copies" also finds "copy"), across all judges' pages and the titles of the documents they post.

Case lookup

The search box finds a judge by name or section ("Section G"), a case by number ("2:25-cv-01750", "25-cv-1750", or "1750") or by party name, and opinions by keyword. A case found opens its case page (see Case pages); a well-formed case number not in the data opens an empty case page that can be watched. A case is listed only if it appears in the site's data: an order since Sept. 17, 2026, a written opinion on file (from January 2022), or a motion since recording began. The judge shown is the district judge on the case's most recent entry in that data.

Case pages and My cases

Each civil case has a page with the judge and the judge's posted requirements, the case's motions, its settings on the calendar, the judge's written rulings on similar motions, and the docket entries on file. The docket entries are those the site has seen (every civil entry in the court's feed for the last 14 days, and orders, written opinions, and motions since the site began recording each); the page is not the full docket. When the site does not yet know a case's judge, the reader can pick one; that choice is kept only in the reader's browser.

Past ruling times. Each motion without a ruling found shows how many days have passed since it was filed, beside how long past rulings on the same kind of motion took. A motion's kind comes from the relief it names: motions to dismiss and other Rule 12 motions; summary judgment; discovery (compel, quash, protective order); Daubert and expert; in limine; injunction and TRO; remand; reconsideration; stay; default judgment; attorney fees; scheduling and procedural motions (continuances, extensions, leave to file, counsel changes, sealing); and other motions. A past ruling time is the number of calendar days from a motion's filing to the order deciding it, from two sources: written opinions whose decided motion has a known filing date (see Time to ruling), and motions recorded from the feed with a later order in the same case on the same relief. The page shows the median and the middle half (25th to 75th percentile) of those times for the case's judge when the judge has at least five on that kind of motion, and otherwise for all judges together, marked as such. With fewer than five in all, it shows no comparison. The figures describe past rulings; they do not predict when a motion will be decided.

My cases. Watched cases, and the time of your last look at each, are stored in your browser only; clearing site data clears them. Entries on file after the last look are marked new; for a case added from a shared link, or watched before anything was on file, that count starts when it was added. "Share list" copies a link that adds the same cases in another browser. "Add all to calendar" downloads an .ics file of a case's upcoming settings.

Submission dates, notices, and appeals

Civil motion submission dates. Each section of the court posts its calendar of civil motion submission dates (under Local Rule 7.2, a motion is noticed for one of them). The site reads every district judge's list once a day and shows the next dates on the Calendar page (as "Civil submission day" with the Motion submissions switch on), on each judge's page, and on case pages. The court's list controls.

Docket details. When the site's periodic CourtListener search returns a case, the site keeps the details CourtListener lists for it: the magistrate judge, nature of suit, cause, jury demand, filing and termination dates, and the parties, attorneys, and firms. CourtListener has parties and counsel only for dockets someone has bought from PACER with the parties included, so many cases show none. CourtListener does not say which attorney represents which party in these lists. A change in a case's district judge between searches is shown as a reassignment, with the date the site saw it.

Court notices. The court's news feed and its General Orders page, read every six hours, each item linked to the court's copy. Items first seen in the last 60 days are marked New.

Fifth Circuit decisions. The Fifth Circuit's opinions feed is read on every update. For each Louisiana appeal, the site reads the opinion's first page for the district and the district court case number ("Appeal from the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Louisiana, USDC No. 2:24-CV-1933") and the opinion's text for the judgment as the court states it in capitals ("AFFIRMED," "We REVERSE and REMAND"). Decisions in civil cases from this court appear on the Court notices page and on the case's page. The site shows each decision on its case only; it does not total decisions by district judge. The opinion controls; a judgment the reader cannot find is shown as "Decided."

Links to opinions

The opinion search and the judge pages link each written opinion to the best copy the site has found. When GovInfo or CourtListener holds the PDF, the case number opens it and carries a PDF mark. When CourtListener lists the docket entry but has no PDF, the case number opens the docket entry. Until an address is found, the case number opens a CourtListener search for the case. The update script looks up addresses for earlier opinions a few at a time and rechecks weekly for PDFs not yet posted. A document can appear on GovInfo or CourtListener after the site counts it, and some documents never appear there.

Known limits

  • Order counts follow the judge currently assigned to the case, so an order entered before a reassignment can be credited to the new judge. Written opinions on the Written Opinions Report are credited to the district judge who signed them. Entries dated before a judge's first day are excluded.
  • The RSS feed shows only the latest entries and does not name the judge. Entries reach the site after CourtListener records them, usually within minutes. If the feed or CourtListener is unavailable, entries can arrive late; the update log notes low-coverage days.
  • Oral rulings, minute entries outside the order category, and sealed entries are not counted.

Data and code

The data files and the update script are public at github.com/ggb52-wq/edla-judge-tracker. Corrections are welcome through that repository.

The time at the top of every page ("Checked 10:46 AM") is the updater's last read of the court's feed, in Central time. Checks run every 15 minutes, around the clock, whether or not the feed has anything new; the mark turns amber when 45 minutes pass without one. An open page picks up new data on its own within about five minutes of a check. Order and opinion counts run through the "Data through" date: today once CourtListener's order search has read the day in full, otherwise the last working day before today once a CourtListener check for written opinions has finished. New entries appear on the Activity page as soon as a check finds them.
